The owners had **refused** to vacate the property and instead undertook **unauthorized** repairs in the half-decade in between. While the fall of the Jilani building, which was old but not on a 'dangerous' list, was a failure of **anticipation**, that of the Kohinoor building, which was on such a list and fell mid-repairs, implies a failure of **enforcement**. Evacuation orders are also hard to **enforce** without police support, while allowing the corporation to claim that legal **liability** has been **discharged** on paper; **demolitions** are politically **fraught**. The post-2000s construction **boom** only **outpaced** maintenance work and administrative **oversight**, with the latter having **fragmented** further since. In mid-2024, roofs and canopies collapsed at airports in Delhi, Jabalpur, and Rajkot, while Bihar witnessed a spate of collapsing bridges through 2024-26. Roads have frequently caved in and basements have flooded in tier-I cities, including in the Rajinder Nagar tragedy. This year, similar collapses have also been reported in Kolkata and Pune.\n\nDespite the availability of technologies to continuously **monitor** structures and issue early warnings, **urbanisation** has **outrun** municipal capacity, with most of the resulting failures involving a known **hazard**. The **apparent** rise in the frequency of these incidents indicates a lack of attention to construction quality as much as greater media coverage and there being more infrastructure to fail. But in place of the state's almost **instinctive** responses in the form of legal and **disciplinary** action, inquiries and promises of audits, and victim **compensation**, India's buildings need **widespread** **rehabilitation** work. Nothing short of an urban **renewal** would suffice to prevent further tragedies.\n\nExperts argue that the real crisis lies not in the absence of building codes but in their weak **implementation**. Municipal bodies remain understaffed and underfunded, unable to conduct regular **structural** audits across thousands of ageing properties. Meanwhile, the political costs of ordering mass demolitions or **resettlement** often outweigh the perceived benefits for local administrations, leading to a cycle of **negligence** dressed up as bureaucratic process.
