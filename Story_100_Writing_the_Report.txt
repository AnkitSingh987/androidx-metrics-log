Title: Writing the Report
Category: Beginner

It was Sunday afternoon. The weekend was almost over. Emma and Sarah were sitting at the desk in Emma's bedroom. The desk was covered in papers, colored pencils, and their two blue notebooks from the museum trip. They had chosen their topic, and now it was time for the hard work. They had to write the report. 

"Okay," Emma said, tapping her black pen against her chin. "Mr. Harrison said the report must be three pages long. We should make a plan first."
"Good idea," Sarah agreed. "How about page one is about the solar system and the planets? Page two is about astronauts and space suits. And page three is about the moon and the moon rock we saw."
"That is a perfect plan," Emma smiled. "I will start writing the text for page one, and you can start drawing a picture of Jupiter and its red spot."

They worked quietly for the first hour. Emma looked at her messy notes from the dark museum room. She read the facts carefully and turned them into neat, complete sentences. 
She wrote: *The solar system is very large. There are eight planets that circle around the Sun. Jupiter is the largest planet. It is a gas giant, and you could fit one thousand Earths inside it.*

While Emma wrote, Sarah used her colored pencils to draw a beautiful, detailed picture of Jupiter in the middle of the page. She used orange, brown, and yellow pencils to make the swirling stripes, and she drew a bright red spot near the bottom. 

"Look at this," Sarah said, showing Emma the drawing. 
"That looks amazing," Emma praised her. "It looks exactly like the glowing model we saw."

They moved on to the second page. Emma wrote about the thick, white astronaut suit. She explained how the suit provided oxygen and protected the astronauts from the freezing cold of outer space. Sarah drew a picture of the suit, carefully adding the glass helmet and the heavy boots. 

The work took a long time. Their hands began to ache from writing and drawing, but they did not stop. They wanted the report to be perfect. 

Finally, they reached the third page. This was Emma's favorite part. She wrote about the small, grey rock sitting in the glass box. 
She wrote: *In 1969, brave astronauts traveled all the way to the moon. They walked on the dusty surface and picked up rocks to bring back to Earth. We saw a real moon rock at the City Museum. It looked like a normal grey stone, but it was very special because it came from outer space.*

"Finished the text!" Emma announced, putting her pen down and rubbing her tired hand. 
"Finished the drawing!" Sarah said, putting down her grey pencil. She had drawn a picture of the moon rock in its glass case, with a small spotlight shining on it. 

Emma gathered the three pieces of paper. She read the entire report from beginning to end. She checked for spelling mistakes, but she did not find any. The sentences were clear, the facts were correct, and Sarah's colorful drawings made the pages look fantastic. 

"We need a title page," Sarah suggested. 
She took a fresh piece of paper. She used a thick black marker to write 'JOURNEY TO THE STARS' in large, neat letters. Under the title, she wrote, 'By Emma and Sarah'. 
Emma used a stapler to attach the title page to the front of their report. 

They laid the finished project on the desk and looked at it. They felt incredibly proud. They had spent their Saturday walking around a huge museum, taking notes like real scientists, and they had spent their Sunday creating a beautiful piece of work. 

"I think Mr. Harrison will give us an A," Emma said confidently. 
"I think he will give us an A plus," Sarah corrected her with a huge smile. 

They packed the precious report carefully into Emma's school bag, ready for Monday morning. The project had been a lot of work, but it was also a lot of fun. They had learned that history and science were not just boring words in a textbook. They were real things you could see, touch, and write about, and they were waiting to be discovered in the great City Museum.
