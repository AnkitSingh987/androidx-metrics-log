Title: Learning New Words
Category: Beginner

Maria is a student. She is ten years old. She is learning a new language. She is learning English. English is not her first language, but she likes it very much. She wants to speak English well. 

Every day, Maria goes to her English class. Her teacher is Mr. Smith. Mr. Smith is a very good teacher. He speaks English slowly and clearly. 
In class, Maria listens to Mr. Smith. She reads her English book. She talks to her classmates in English. 

But Maria knows that class is not enough. She must study at home too. 
Maria has a special notebook for her English study. It is a small yellow notebook. She carries it in her bag every day. This notebook is for new words. 

It is evening. Maria is sitting at her desk in her bedroom. She opens her yellow notebook. 
Today in class, she heard three new words. She did not know what they meant. She wrote them down quickly on a piece of paper. Now, she wants to learn them. 

The first word is 'beautiful'. 
Maria looks at the word. B-e-a-u-t-i-f-u-l. It is a long word. 
She takes her dictionary. A dictionary is a big, thick book. It has all the words in the English language. It explains what the words mean. 
Maria finds the letter 'B' in the dictionary. She looks down the page. Ban, bar, bat... bea... beautiful! She finds it. 

She reads the meaning. 'Very pretty. Nice to look at.'
"Ah," Maria says. "Beautiful."
She takes her blue pen. She writes the word 'beautiful' in her yellow notebook. Next to it, she writes the meaning in her own language. Then, she writes an English sentence to help her remember. 
She writes: 'The flower is very beautiful.'

She looks at the second word. The word is 'delicious'. 
She opens her dictionary again. She finds the letter 'D'. She looks down the page. Day, dog, door... del... delicious! 
She reads the meaning. 'Tastes very good. Very nice to eat.'
"Delicious," she repeats. 
She writes 'delicious' in her yellow notebook. She writes the meaning. She thinks of a sentence. 
She writes: 'My mother's chocolate cake is delicious.'

The third word is 'difficult'. 
Maria finds the letter 'D' again. She finds 'difficult'. 
The meaning is: 'Not easy. Hard to do.'
She writes 'difficult' in her yellow notebook. She writes the meaning. 
She writes a sentence: 'The math test was very difficult.'

Now, Maria has three new words in her yellow notebook. Beautiful, delicious, difficult. 
But writing them is only the first step. Now she must learn how to say them correctly. She must practice her pronunciation. 
Maria has a computer on her desk. She goes to the internet. She opens a dictionary website. The website has a small button with a picture of a speaker. 
She types the word 'beautiful'. She clicks the speaker button. 
A clear voice from the computer says, "Beautiful."
Maria listens. Then, she says it. "Beautiful."
She clicks the button again. "Beautiful."
She says it again. "Beautiful."

She types the word 'delicious'. She clicks the button. 
The computer says, "Delicious."
Maria repeats it. "Delicious." She tries to make her mouth move the same way. 
She types the word 'difficult'. She clicks the button. 
The computer says, "Difficult."
Maria repeats it. "Difficult."

She practices the three words for ten minutes. She says them loud. She says them quiet. She says them fast. She says them slow. 
She feels confident. She knows the meaning of the words. She knows how to spell the words. She knows how to say the words. 

She closes her dictionary. She closes her yellow notebook. She turns off the computer. 
Tomorrow in English class, she will try to use her new words. 
If she sees a nice picture on the wall, she will say, "That picture is beautiful."
If she eats a good apple at recess, she will say, "This apple is delicious."
If she has a hard question, she will say, "This question is difficult."

Maria smiles. Learning new words is like collecting small treasures. Every new word makes her English a little bit better. She puts her yellow notebook safely in her bag. She is ready for tomorrow.
