Title: The Broken Chair
Category: Beginner

Mr. Clark is an old man. He lives in an old house. He has many old things. He likes his old things. They hold many memories for him. 
In his dining room, he has a big wooden table. Around the table, there are six wooden chairs. The chairs are very old. They belonged to his grandfather. They are made of dark brown wood. They have nice carvings on the back. 

It is evening time. Mr. Clark is preparing his dinner. He cooks some soup and makes some bread. He puts a bowl of hot soup on the dining table. He puts a piece of bread on a small plate. He brings a glass of water. 
He is ready to eat. 
He walks to the table. He pulls out a chair. It is the chair near the window. He likes to sit near the window and look at the garden. 

Mr. Clark sits down on the chair. 
Suddenly, he hears a noise. 'Crack!' 
It is a loud noise. It comes from under him. 
The chair shakes. 
'CRACK!' 
The noise is louder this time. One of the chair legs is breaking. 

Mr. Clark is surprised. He tries to stand up quickly. But he is an old man. He is not very fast. 
Before he can stand up, the leg of the chair breaks completely. The wood splits in two. 
The chair falls to the right side. 
Mr. Clark falls with the chair. He tumbles onto the floor. 
"Ouch!" he says. 

He sits on the floor. He is a little shocked. He feels his arm. He feels his leg. He is not hurt. He did not hit his head. He is okay. 
He looks at his dinner. The soup is still on the table. The bread is still on the table. Nothing spilled. He is lucky. 

He looks at the chair. The chair is on the floor next to him. It looks sad. One of the front legs is broken. The wood is sharp where it broke. 
Mr. Clark slowly stands up. He dusts off his trousers. He looks at the broken chair again. 
"Oh dear," he says. "My grandfather's chair. This is very sad."

He cannot eat his dinner now. He must do something about the chair. 
He picks up the broken leg. He holds it against the chair. 
"Can I fix it?" he wonders. 

He goes to his garage. His garage is a place where he keeps his tools. He has many tools. He finds a bottle of wood glue. It is strong glue for wood. He finds a hammer. He finds some small nails. 
He brings the tools back to the dining room. 

He puts the chair upside down on the table. He puts the glue on the broken leg. He puts the glue on the broken part of the chair. He presses the two parts together. He holds them tight for a few minutes. 
The glue is very sticky. But the break is very bad. The wood is old and dry. The glue is not enough to hold it. 

"I need to use nails," Mr. Clark says. 
He takes a small nail. He places it on the wood. He hits it with the hammer. 'Tap, tap, tap.' 
But the old wood is brittle. It splits more. Another crack appears in the wood. 
"Stop, stop," Mr. Clark says. He puts the hammer down. 

He realizes he cannot fix the chair himself. If he uses the chair again, it will break again. Someone might get hurt. It is dangerous. 
He feels a little sad. He touches the smooth wood on the back of the chair. 
"You are a very old chair," he says softly. "You have done a good job for many years. But now you are tired. You are broken."

He takes the broken chair off the table. He carries it to a corner of the room. He puts it there safely. Nobody will sit on it in the corner. 
He cleans up the small pieces of wood from the floor. He puts the glue and the hammer away. 

Now, he is very hungry. His soup is not hot anymore. It is warm. 
He goes back to the dining table. He pulls out a different chair. This chair feels strong and solid. 
He sits down carefully. He listens. There is no cracking noise. This chair is safe. 
He starts to eat his soup. He eats his bread. He looks at the broken chair in the corner. 

"Tomorrow," he thinks, "I will take the chair to the carpenter in the town. The carpenter is a man who works with wood. He is a professional. Maybe he can fix the old chair properly."
If the carpenter cannot fix it, Mr. Clark knows what to do. He will keep the chair in the corner. He will put a nice plant on it. It will not be a chair for sitting anymore. It will be a small table for a plant. 
That is a good idea. He smiles. He finishes his dinner in his quiet dining room.
