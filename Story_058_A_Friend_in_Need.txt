Title: A Friend in Need
Category: Beginner

It is lunchtime at school. The bell rings, and all the children run out of their classrooms. They run to the cafeteria, or they run to the playground. They are hungry and excited. 

David and his best friend, Mark, walk to the playground. They like to eat their lunch outside on a wooden bench when the weather is nice. Today, the sun is shining, and the sky is a clear blue. 

David opens his school bag. He takes out his green lunch box. He opens it and sees a delicious sandwich, a red apple, and a small packet of cookies. His mother makes very good lunches. 
He looks at Mark. Mark is searching in his bag. He is pulling out books, pencils, and a sweater. But he does not have a lunch box in his hand. 

Mark stops searching. He looks into his empty bag. His shoulders drop, and he looks very sad. 
"What is wrong, Mark?" David asks. 
"I forgot my lunch," Mark says quietly. "I left my lunch box on the kitchen table this morning. I was in a hurry because I could not find my shoes."
"Oh no," David says. 

Mark sits down on the wooden bench. He watches the other children eating their sandwiches and drinking their juice. 
"I am very hungry," Mark says. "My stomach is making noises. But I have no money to buy food in the cafeteria."
"You cannot sit here and eat nothing," David says firmly. 

David looks at his green lunch box. He looks at his big sandwich. It is a chicken and salad sandwich. He looks at his red apple. He looks at his packet of cookies. There are four chocolate cookies in the packet. 

"We are best friends," David says. "And friends share their things."
Mark looks up. "What do you mean?"
David takes his sandwich. He carefully breaks it into two equal pieces. He hands one piece to Mark. 
"Here," David says. "You can have half of my sandwich."

Mark's eyes open wide. "Are you sure, David? Won't you be hungry?"
"I will be fine," David smiles. "Half a sandwich is better than no sandwich."
Mark takes the piece of sandwich. "Thank you so much, David," he says gratefully. "You are a very good friend."

They sit on the bench and eat their halves of the sandwich. The chicken is tasty, and the bread is fresh. 
When they finish the sandwich, David takes the red apple. 
"I cannot break an apple in half easily," David says. "But I have an idea."
He takes a small plastic knife from his lunch box. His mother put it there for cutting fruit. 
He cuts the apple straight down the middle. He gives half of the apple to Mark. 
"Thank you again," Mark says. 

They eat the sweet, crunchy apple. 
Finally, David opens the packet of cookies. There are four cookies. This is the easiest math problem of the day. 
"Two for you, and two for me," David says. He gives two chocolate cookies to Mark. 
They eat the cookies slowly. They are their favorite kind. 

Mark wipes his mouth with a napkin. His sad face is gone. He looks happy and satisfied. 
"I am not hungry anymore," Mark says. "That was a very good lunch. Thank you for sharing everything with me."
"You are welcome," David replies. He closes his empty green lunch box. 

"If you forget your lunch tomorrow, I will share mine with you," Mark promises. 
David laughs. "I hope I do not forget my lunch. But if I do, I will remember your promise."

They stand up from the wooden bench. They have fifteen minutes of playtime left before the bell rings for the afternoon classes. 
"Do you want to play basketball?" David asks. 
"Yes, let's go!" Mark says. 

They run to the basketball court together. David feels very good inside. He is a little bit hungry because he only ate half a lunch. But his heart feels very full. He knows that helping a friend is much more important than a whole chicken sandwich. A friend in need is a friend indeed, and David is happy he could be a good friend today.
