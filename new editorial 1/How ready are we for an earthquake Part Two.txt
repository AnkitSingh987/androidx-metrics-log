Title: How ready are we for an earthquake Part Two

How ready are we for an earthquake?

Once the shaking stops, check for immediate dangers. If the building is damaged or unsafe, evacuate calmly using the stairs, not lifts, and move to a safe open area away from buildings, walls, poles and wires. If the building is safe, remain there and stay alert for aftershocks. Early warning systems deserve a prominent place in this strategy.

Countries like Japan and Mexico use sensor networks that give residents a few seconds of warning, just enough time to duck under a desk. These systems work by detecting the faster, weaker P-waves before the destructive S-waves arrive. India currently has an early-warning system for parts of the Himalayas, which urgently needs national expansion.

Additionally, the moment the ground shakes, people should be alerted through messaging so that they can be ready for aftershocks, if any. The National Disaster Management Authority has established SACHET, a Common Alerting Protocol-based integrated alert system designed for near-real-time dissemination of geo-targeted warnings through multiple channels. India has also tested a nationwide cell-broadcast system capable of rapidly sending disaster alerts to mobile phones.

Promoting the SACHET app should be a national priority. In January 2023, six-year-old Mustafa in Lucknow survived the collapse of a five-storey apartment building by recalling earthquake-safety lessons from the cartoon Doraemon. When the building began shaking, he remembered the advice to take shelter under a bed and did so instead of running.

Though he survived with minor injuries, his mother and grandmother tragically died. The incident demonstrates how simple, age-appropriate disaster education can turn knowledge into instinctive action, and save lives. By SRINIVAS MADHAV (The writer is a transparency and equality advocate and co-founder, 51ABI Foundation)
