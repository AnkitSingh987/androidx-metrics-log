Title: Monsoon of discontent

Monsoon of discontent

Four weeks, 28 pending Bills, and a House that adjourned within hours of convening: the Monsoon Session has begun with a storm Parliament's Monsoon Session, running from July 20 to August 13 with nineteen sittings, carries a packed legislative docket: Bills on income tax, MSME reform, foreign contributions, births-and-deaths registration, and a new higher-education regulator to replace the UGC and AICTE. But one Bill will dominate everything else - the Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill, which proposes automatically removing a Prime Minister, Chief Minister or minister from office after thirty days in judicial custody on serious charges. The Joint Parliamentary Committee reviewing it has voted to retain this clause even as formal adoption of its full report remains stalled, with safeguards against misuse still under negotiation.

Government MPs frame this as plain accountability; Opposition parties  -  several of whose leaders have faced arrest in recent years  -  call it a constitutional weapon dressed up as reform, warning that it tramples the presumption of innocence and unsettles the federal balance. Expect this to be the session's defining constitutional argument. Politically, though, the Opposition's energy is aimed elsewhere.

A privilege motion against Defence Minister Rajnath Singh  -  accused of telling the House that no soldiers died in Operation Sindoor, only for six names to later appear on the National War Memorial  -  has reopened a wound from last year's military campaign. Equally combustible is the NEET-UG paper leak, a scandal serious enough to force the cancellation of this year's medical entrance examination and trigger CBI arrests; activist Sonam Wangchuk's hunger strike, tying exam reform to Ladakh's long-pending statehood demand, and police action against students marching on Parliament have already produced chaotic scenes on the floor. A third flashpoint  -  allegations of theft from Ram Temple donation collections in Ayodhya  -  adds a religious dimension that the Opposition hopes will travel beyond party lines.

Underneath these headline rows runs a steadier current: the Election Commission's Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, now expanding nationwide, with enumeration deadlines falling squarely within the session in states such as West Bengal, Tamil Nadu and Kerala that vote later this year. The Opposition calls it disenfranchisement by paperwork; the government insists it is a neutral, constitutionally sanctioned clean-up. Add rising prices and continuing unease over the India-US tariff deal, which critics say was struck at the expense of Indian agriculture, and the ingredients for a turbulent four weeks are complete.

What is less certain is whether any of this gets debated rather than merely shouted about. Day one ended, as these sessions often do, in adjournment before Question Hour properly began. Parliament has serious arguments to have this Monsoon  -  about liberty and accountability, about examination integrity, about who gets to vote.

Whether the well of the House proves more useful than the aisles around it remains, as ever, the real test.
