Title: EPR in Asia Pacific Japan sorts its plastic Part Three

EPR in Asia Pacific: Japan sorts its plastic better than any country. It still recycles very little of it

A single body. JCPRA gives every company one place to register, one price list and one audit trail. That is why compliance is high and free-riding is rare. 2. Open bidding for recycling contracts, which has kept costs down for twenty years. 3. A published fee table for every material. Anyone can look up what a kilogram costs.

That third point leads somewhere uncomfortable. A Japanese company pays about Rs 35 a kilogram for plastic packaging. An Indian company meeting the same obligation through EPR certificates pays a lot less. The government's own notified ceiling is Rs 7.90 (Category III). The actual cost to a city is much higher than this, a gap CSE's latest report, Cost of Managing Plastic Packaging in India, sets out city by city.

Three things are worth refusing 1. A flat fee based on weight. It pushes companies towards laminates, and India's waste stream is already full of multi-layer sachets. 2. A rule that covers only household waste. 3. A definition of recovery that counts incineration, cement kiln burning and export as recycling.

Japan's numbers look remarkable until you ask what "effectively utilised" includes. India will face the same temptation to write a flattering definition and then meet it. By Vanhishikha Vyas, Tribhuwan Singh Bisht, Siddharth Ghanshyam Singh
