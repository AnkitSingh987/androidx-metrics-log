Title: Baking the Cake
Category: Beginner

It was Friday afternoon, the day before Mia's fifteenth birthday party. The kitchen was warm and smelled strongly of vanilla and sugar. Mia and her mother were wearing aprons and standing at the kitchen counter. It was time to bake the birthday cake. 

"Are you ready for the great baking challenge?" her mother joked, holding up a large mixing bowl. 
"I am ready," Mia smiled, tying her apron tight. "I want to bake it myself this year. You can just supervise and give me instructions."
"Deal," her mother agreed. She opened an old, worn cookbook and found their favorite family recipe for rich chocolate cake. 

"Okay, first things first," her mother instructed. "We need to preheat the oven. Turn the dial to 180 degrees Celsius."
Mia walked over to the oven and turned the heavy plastic dial to the correct number. A small red light turned on to show it was heating up. 
"Now, you must prepare the cake pans," her mother continued. "Rub a little bit of butter on the inside of the two round pans, and then sprinkle a tiny bit of flour over the butter. This will stop the cake from sticking to the metal."
Mia did this carefully, ensuring every corner was covered. 

"Now for the fun part: mixing the ingredients," her mother said, pointing to the recipe book. "We always mix the dry ingredients first. Measure out two cups of white flour, two cups of sugar, and three-quarters of a cup of cocoa powder."

Mia took a plastic measuring cup. She scooped the white flour from a large paper bag and poured it into the mixing bowl. She added the sweet sugar and the dark, bitter cocoa powder. 
"Don't forget the baking powder and a pinch of salt," her mother reminded her. "The baking powder makes the cake rise and become fluffy."
Mia added the powders and used a large wooden spoon to stir everything together until the mixture was an even, light brown color. 

"Next, the wet ingredients," her mother read from the book. "In a separate jug, mix one cup of milk, half a cup of vegetable oil, and two large eggs."
Mia cracked the two eggs perfectly, without dropping any shell into the jug. She poured in the milk and the oil, and used a metal whisk to beat them together rapidly. 

"Okay, pour the wet mixture into the dry mixture," her mother directed. 
Mia poured the yellow liquid into the brown powder. She took the wooden spoon and started to stir. It was very difficult at first. The mixture was stiff and dry. But as she continued to stir, it slowly turned into a thick, smooth, dark brown batter. 
"Keep stirring," her mother encouraged her. "You want to make sure there are no lumps of flour left."

After five minutes of hard stirring, Mia's arm was tired, but the batter looked perfect. It was glossy and smelled exactly like dark chocolate. 
"It looks delicious," Mia said, tempted to dip her finger in. 
"Wait," her mother laughed. "There is one final, secret ingredient. The recipe calls for one cup of boiling water. It sounds strange, but it makes the cake incredibly moist."

Her mother poured the boiling water from the kettle into the batter. Mia stirred it in very carefully so she did not splash the hot water. The batter became very thin and liquid. 
"Are you sure this is right?" Mia asked, looking at the thin mixture doubtfully. 
"I promise you, it is right," her mother smiled. "Now, divide the batter evenly between the two prepared cake pans."

Mia carefully poured the dark liquid into the two round pans. Her mother opened the hot oven door, and Mia slid the pans onto the middle shelf. 
"We bake them for thirty-five minutes," her mother said, setting the kitchen timer. 

While the cakes were baking, they did not rest. They had to make the chocolate frosting. They mixed soft butter, powdered sugar, and more cocoa powder with an electric mixer until it was thick and creamy. 

When the timer finally rang, the kitchen smelled heavenly. Her mother used thick oven gloves to pull the cakes out. They had risen perfectly and looked soft and springy. 
"We have to let them cool completely before we put the frosting on," her mother explained. "If the cakes are hot, the butter in the frosting will melt, and it will be a disaster."

An hour later, the cakes were cool. Mia placed one cake on a large plate. She spread a thick layer of chocolate frosting over the top. Then, she carefully placed the second cake on top, making a tall, two-layer cake. Finally, she used a flat knife to cover the entire outside of the cake with the rest of the rich frosting. 

She stepped back and admired her work. It was not as perfect as a cake from a professional bakery, but it looked homemade and delicious. 
"I am very proud of you, Mia," her mother said, putting an arm around her shoulder. "You followed the recipe perfectly. Your friends are going to love it tomorrow."
Mia smiled. Baking the cake was hard work, but knowing she had made it herself made it very special. Now, everything was ready for the big day.
