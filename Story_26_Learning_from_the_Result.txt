Title: Learning from the Result

Category: Intermediate

David was a graphic design student at a small college in Chicago. He was in his second year and loved creating digital art. He spent hours every day in front of his computer, trying to make his designs look perfect. David was a perfectionist. He believed that if he worked hard enough, his work would be flawless. He did not like making mistakes, and he did not like taking risks.

One day, his professor, Mr. Evans, gave the class a new assignment. "Next week, our college is having an art exhibition," Mr. Evans announced. "Your assignment is to design a poster for the event. The best poster will be printed and placed all around the city. I want you to be creative. Do not be afraid to try something different."

David was excited. He wanted his poster to be chosen. He immediately started thinking about his design. He decided to use a very clean, modern style. He chose two simple colors: dark blue and silver. He used a very elegant font for the text. He wanted it to look professional. 

For the next five days, David worked on his poster. He checked every detail. He moved the text a few millimeters to the left, then a few millimeters to the right. He adjusted the colors over and over again. He did not sleep much. He just wanted the poster to be perfect. 

On Wednesday afternoon, his friend Sarah came to visit him. Sarah was also in the graphic design class. Her style was completely different from David's. She loved bright colors and messy, energetic designs. 

"How is your poster going?" Sarah asked. She looked at David's computer screen. "Wow, that looks very neat and professional."

"Thank you," David said proudly. "I have spent forty hours on it. I think it is perfect. There are no mistakes. What about yours?"

"Oh, mine is crazy," Sarah laughed. "I used bright yellow and pink. I drew the letters by hand. It is not perfect, but it has a lot of energy. I had fun making it."

David smiled politely, but he thought Sarah's idea sounded unprofessional. He was sure his clean, perfect design would win. 

On Friday morning, the students presented their posters to the class. Mr. Evans looked at all the designs. There were many good posters. Then, it was David's turn. He showed his dark blue and silver poster. 

"This is very nice, David," Mr. Evans said. "It is very clean. The text is easy to read. You have excellent technical skills."

David felt very happy. He was sure he had won. 

Next, Sarah showed her poster. It was bright yellow with big, pink letters. There were drawings of paint splatters and abstract shapes. It looked a little messy, but it grabbed everyone's attention immediately. The colors were so bright that you could not stop looking at it.

Mr. Evans smiled broadly. "Sarah, this is fantastic!" he said. "It is bold and energetic. It makes me want to go to the exhibition. It is full of life."

David felt a cold feeling in his stomach. He knew what was going to happen. 

At the end of the class, Mr. Evans announced the winner. "All the designs were good," he said. "But the poster that will be printed is Sarah's."

David was shocked. He could not believe it. He had worked so hard. His poster had no mistakes. Sarah's poster was messy. Why did she win? 

After class, David went to speak to Mr. Evans. "Professor," David said quietly. "Can I ask you a question? Why did you choose Sarah's poster? My poster was perfect. I checked every detail. Her poster had uneven letters."

Mr. Evans looked at David kindly. "Sit down, David," he said. "Your poster was technically perfect. You did a great job with the alignment and the colors. But a poster needs to do more than look perfect. It needs to make people feel something. It needs to grab their attention."

"But I spent forty hours on it," David said. 

"Time and perfection are not always the most important things," Mr. Evans explained. "Your design was safe. You were afraid to make a mistake, so you created something that looked like a bank advertisement. It did not look like a fun art exhibition. Sarah took a risk. Her design was not perfect, but it had emotion. Sometimes, a safe, perfect design is boring. Sometimes, a messy design with emotion is better."

David walked home slowly. He thought about what Mr. Evans had said. He realized that his professor was right. When David looked at his own poster, he felt nothing. It was just shapes and text. When he looked at Sarah's poster, he felt excitement. 

That weekend, David decided to do an experiment. He opened a new file on his computer. He did not use blue and silver. He chose bright orange and green. He did not use straight lines. He drew crazy shapes. He did not try to be perfect. He just tried to have fun. 

He worked for only three hours. When he finished, the design looked wild and energetic. There were some mistakes. The lines were not perfectly straight. But David loved it. It was the best thing he had ever made. 

On Monday, he showed his new design to Sarah. 

"Wow, David!" she said. "This is amazing! It looks so different. It has so much energy."

"I am learning from the result," David smiled. "I learned that trying to be perfect can stop you from being creative. I need to take more risks."

From that day on, David changed his way of working. He still used his technical skills, but he stopped worrying about being flawless. He learned to play with colors and shapes. He learned that mistakes can sometimes lead to beautiful results. He became a much better designer because he stopped trying to be perfect and started trying to be interesting. 

His next project was a logo for a local coffee shop. He did not make a safe, boring logo. He created something bold and unique. The coffee shop owner loved it immediately. David realized that sometimes, the best way to succeed is to stop being afraid of failure. The result of his lost competition was actually the best lesson he had ever learned.
