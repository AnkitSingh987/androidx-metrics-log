Title: Five More Minutes
Category: Beginner

The alarm clock rings. It is loud. Beep, beep, beep! It is six-thirty in the morning.
Sam is in his bed. He is sleeping. He hears the noise. He does not like the noise. He opens one eye. He looks at the clock. It is time to wake up.
But Sam is very tired. He went to bed late last night. He watched a movie on television. Now he wants to sleep more. His bed is very warm. His pillow is very soft. He does not want to get up.

Sam reaches out his hand. He touches the alarm clock. He pushes a button. The noise stops. The room is quiet again.
"Five more minutes," Sam says to himself. "I will sleep for five more minutes. Then I will get up."
He closes his eyes. He goes back to sleep. It is very easy to go back to sleep. 

The clock ticks. Tick, tock, tick, tock. One minute passes. Two minutes pass. Five minutes pass. Ten minutes pass. 
Sam is dreaming. He is dreaming about a big park. He is running with a dog. He is happy. 

Suddenly, the door opens. It is his older sister, Emma. She is already dressed. She is wearing her school clothes. She looks at Sam. He is still in bed.
"Sam!" Emma says. Her voice is loud. "Wake up! It is late!"
Sam opens his eyes. He is confused. "What time is it?" he asks.
"It is seven o'clock!" Emma says. "You slept for thirty more minutes. Not five minutes!"
Sam sits up fast. "Seven o'clock?" he says. "Oh no! I am late!"

Sam jumps out of bed. He is in a hurry. He runs to the bathroom. He washes his face quickly. He does not use soap. He just uses water. He brushes his teeth very fast. Up and down, up and down. He does not brush them well. 
He runs back to his bedroom. He opens his closet. He takes out his school uniform. He puts on his shirt. He buttons it wrongly. He puts on his trousers. 
He looks for his socks. Where are they? He finds one sock under the bed. He finds the other sock on a chair. They do not match. One is blue and one is black. But he has no time. He puts them on. 

He runs to the kitchen. His mother is there. She is drinking coffee. She looks at Sam. 
"Good morning, Sam," she says. "You are very late today."
"I know, Mother," Sam says. "I wanted to sleep for five more minutes. But I slept for thirty minutes."
His mother shakes her head. "That is why you must get up when the alarm rings," she says. 

"I have no time for breakfast," Sam says. "I must go."
"You must eat something," his mother says. "You cannot go to school with an empty stomach."
She gives him a piece of bread. "Here. Eat this on the way."
Sam takes the bread. "Thank you, Mother," he says. He takes a big bite. It is dry, but he eats it. 

He runs to the front door. He looks for his shoes. Where are his shoes? He sees them near the door. He puts them on. He does not tie the laces well. 
He looks for his school bag. He cannot find it. 
"Emma!" he calls out. "Where is my bag?"
"It is in the living room!" Emma says. 
Sam runs to the living room. He finds his bag. It is open. His books are not inside. They are on the table. He puts the books in the bag very fast. He closes the bag. 

He runs back to the door. "Goodbye, Mother!" he says. 
"Goodbye, Sam," his mother says. "Have a good day. And please tie your shoes."
Sam looks down. His shoe laces are loose. He bends down. He ties them quickly. 

He opens the door. He runs outside. The sun is shining. It is a nice day. But Sam cannot enjoy it. He is running to the bus stop. The bus stop is at the end of the street. 
He sees the bus. It is already there. The doors are open. People are getting on the bus. 
"Wait!" Sam shouts. "Wait for me!"
He runs faster. His bag is heavy. It bounces on his back. His unmatched socks feel funny. His shirt is buttoned wrongly. But he must catch the bus.

He reaches the bus stop. He is breathing hard. The bus driver looks at him. 
"You are just in time," the driver says. 
"Thank you," Sam says. He gets on the bus. He pays the money. He walks down the aisle. He finds an empty seat. He sits down. He is very tired. 
He looks at his clothes. He sees his wrong buttons. He sees his blue and black socks. He sighs. 

Emma is on the bus too. She sits in the front. She looks back at him. She smiles and shakes her head. 
Sam fixes his shirt. He buttons it right. He cannot fix his socks. He will have mismatched socks all day. 
He learns a lesson today. "Five more minutes" is a bad idea. When the alarm rings, he must get up. He will not be late tomorrow. He will get up on time. It is much better to have a slow, quiet morning than a fast, crazy morning.
