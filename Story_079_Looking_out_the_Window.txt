Title: Looking out the Window
Category: Beginner

The silver train moved very fast. It left the busy city and the quiet suburbs far behind. Now, it was traveling deep into the countryside. 
Thomas closed his comic book. He was tired of reading about superheroes. He turned his head and looked out of the large glass window next to his seat. His sister, Sophie, was already staring outside, her nose almost touching the cold glass. 

The view was completely different from what they saw every day. In the city, they only saw tall grey buildings, busy roads, and hundreds of cars. Here, everything was green and open. 

The train rushed past enormous fields of green grass. There were no buildings to block the view. The sky looked much bigger here. 
"Look over there, Thomas!" Sophie pointed excitedly. 
Thomas looked where she was pointing. In the distance, he saw a large group of black and white cows standing in a field. They were eating the grass slowly. 
"They look so small from here," Thomas said. 
"That is because they are far away," his father explained from the aisle seat. "If you stood next to them, they would be very big."

The scenery changed rapidly as the train continued its journey. The green fields disappeared, and the train entered a dark, thick forest. The tall pine trees grew very close to the train tracks. The sunlight came through the branches in thin golden lines. The forest looked mysterious and a little bit spooky. 
Suddenly, the forest ended, and the train crossed a very long, high bridge. 

"Wow!" both children said together. 
They looked down from the window. Far below them, a wide, blue river flowed rapidly over grey stones. Small boats were tied to wooden docks near the edge of the water. 
"That is the Silver River," Mr. Miller told them. "Your grandfather used to fish in that river when he was a young man."
"Can we go fishing with him this weekend?" Thomas asked eagerly. 
"Maybe," his father replied. "We will have to ask him if he has enough fishing rods."

After crossing the river, the train passed through several small towns. They were very quiet towns with narrow streets. The houses were small and made of old red bricks. Many houses had smoke coming out of their chimneys, rising straight up into the cold November air. 

At a railway crossing, the train blew its loud horn. 'HONK! HONK!'
Thomas saw a few cars waiting patiently behind a red and white barrier for the train to pass. He saw a young boy sitting in the back seat of a blue car. The boy waved his hand at the train. 
Thomas smiled and waved back, even though the train was moving too fast for the boy to see him clearly. It was fun to connect with a stranger for just one second. 

The three hours passed surprisingly quickly when there was so much to see. The window was like a giant television screen, showing a never-ending movie of the countryside. They saw old farms with red barns, horses running in fences, and large flocks of black birds flying across the sky. 

Finally, the train began to slow down. The loud, fast 'clack-clack' sound of the wheels became slower and quieter. 
"Are we there yet?" Sophie asked, turning away from the window. 
Mrs. Miller looked at her watch. "Yes, I think so. It is almost eleven o'clock. We are arriving at the village station."

The children felt a rush of excitement. They watched eagerly as the train moved past a few small buildings and finally stopped next to a long, concrete platform. The journey was over, and the real visit was about to begin. They had spent three hours looking out the window, but now it was time to step outside and explore this beautiful, green world for themselves.
