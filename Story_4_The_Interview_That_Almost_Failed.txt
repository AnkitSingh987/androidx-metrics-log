Title: The Interview That Almost Failed

Category: Intermediate

Maria stood in front of her bathroom mirror, carefully adjusting the collar of her light blue blouse. She took a deep breath and practiced her smile. Today was the most important day of her professional life. She had an interview for a senior graphic design position at Creative Spark Media, one of the most famous advertising agencies in the city. 

She had wanted to work for this company since she graduated from university. For the past two weeks, she had spent every evening preparing. She had updated her resume, practiced answering common interview questions, and, most importantly, perfected her digital portfolio. Her portfolio contained all her best design work on her laptop, showcasing her skills in website design, logo creation, and digital illustration.

"You are ready," she whispered to herself in the mirror. She grabbed her leather bag, making sure her laptop was safely inside, and left her apartment with plenty of time to spare. 

Because she was feeling slightly nervous, Maria decided to stop at a small cafe near the office building to buy a cup of chamomile tea to calm her stomach. The cafe was crowded with morning commuters rushing to work. 

As Maria turned away from the counter with her hot tea, a man walking too fast bumped directly into her shoulder. 

"Oh, excuse me!" the man said quickly, continuing to walk toward the exit. 

Maria gasped. A small splash of dark tea had spilled right onto the front of her light blue blouse. It wasn't a huge stain, but it was definitely noticeable. Panic rushed through her body. She ran to the cafe's bathroom and tried to wash the stain with cold water and paper towels, but it only made the wet spot larger and more obvious. 

She looked at her watch. It was 8:30 AM. Her interview was at 9:00 AM. She didn't have enough time to go all the way back home to change her clothes. 

"This is a disaster," she thought, feeling tears form in her eyes. "I look completely unprofessional."

But Maria was a problem solver. She ran out of the cafe and looked down the street. She saw a small clothing boutique that had just opened its doors. She hurried inside and quickly bought a beautiful, dark blue silk scarf. She tied the scarf elegantly around her neck, completely hiding the tea stain. It actually made her outfit look more stylish and creative. 

She sighed with relief and walked into the tall glass office building. She approached the front desk to ask for directions. 

"Creative Spark Media? They are on the twelfth floor," the security guard told her, pointing to the elevators. 

Maria rode the elevator to the twelfth floor. She stepped out, feeling confident again. However, when she walked through the glass doors, she realized something was wrong. The walls were painted green, and there were large screens displaying financial data. 

"Can I help you?" a receptionist asked.

"I'm here for an interview at Creative Spark Media," Maria said.

"Oh, you are on the wrong floor," the receptionist smiled kindly. "This is the accounting department. Creative Spark is on the tenth floor. The elevator takes a long time, so you should probably use the stairs."

Maria's heart started racing again. She ran to the emergency stairs and hurried down two flights. She burst through the doors on the tenth floor, breathing heavily. It was exactly 8:58 AM. She walked to the correct reception desk and announced her arrival. 

Five minutes later, she was invited into a large, modern meeting room. A serious-looking man with gray hair was sitting at the end of a long table. 

"Good morning, Maria. I am Mr. Harris, the creative director," the man said, shaking her hand. "Please, take a seat. I've read your resume, and you have some excellent experience. Now, I would love to see your portfolio."

"Thank you, Mr. Harris," Maria said, smiling confidently. "I have prepared a digital presentation of my recent projects."

She opened her bag and pulled out her laptop. She pressed the power button. Nothing happened. 

She pressed it again, holding it down. The screen remained completely black. Suddenly, she remembered that she had left her laptop unplugged on her bed the night before while she was reviewing her work. The battery was completely dead. And her charger was sitting on her kitchen table at home.

For a moment, Maria felt like the floor was going to open up and swallow her. All her preparation, her beautiful digital slides, her complex animations—they were all gone. She was sitting in the most important interview of her life with a dead computer. 

Mr. Harris raised an eyebrow. "Is there a problem with the presentation?"

Maria closed her eyes for a brief second. She could apologize, cry, and ask to reschedule. But she knew they wouldn't give her a second chance. She had to adapt. 

"Unfortunately, Mr. Harris, my laptop battery has died, and I do not have my charger," Maria said clearly, looking him directly in the eye. 

Mr. Harris frowned slightly. "That is highly unfortunate. Being prepared is a crucial part of working in an agency."

"You are absolutely right," Maria agreed without hesitation. "However, a designer's true tool is not the software; it is the creative process. If you don't mind, I would like to show you my work another way."

Before Mr. Harris could object, Maria reached into her bag and pulled out her thick, black sketchbook. She always carried it with her to draw ideas when she was on the train or at the park. It wasn't clean and polished like her digital portfolio. It was filled with messy pencil drawings, crossed-out words, color tests, and sticky notes. 

She opened the sketchbook and pushed it across the table toward Mr. Harris. 

"This is the foundation of every project I have ever created," Maria explained with passion in her voice. She turned to a page filled with rough sketches of a logo. "For example, this was for a local bakery. You can see how I started with basic shapes to represent wheat, and how the idea evolved over time. I tested different typography here in the margins before choosing the final, friendly font."

Mr. Harris leaned forward, putting on his glasses. He looked at the messy pages with interest. 

For the next twenty minutes, Maria didn't use a screen. Instead, she used her hands and her words. She talked enthusiastically about color theory, user experience, and how she solved visual problems. She explained the stories behind her designs, pointing to the original ink drawings in her book. 

Mr. Harris asked several difficult questions about her process, and because Maria knew her work so deeply, she answered them confidently and intelligently. 

When the interview finally ended, Maria closed her sketchbook. She felt exhausted, but also proud. She hadn't given up. 

Mr. Harris stood up and shook her hand again. "I must admit, Maria, when your computer died, I thought this interview was over. We rely heavily on technology here." He paused, a small smile appearing on his face. "But technology fails. It always does eventually. What I need are designers who can think on their feet, adapt to bad situations, and still deliver a clear message. Your ability to communicate your ideas without relying on a screen was very impressive."

"Thank you, Mr. Harris," Maria smiled, feeling a huge wave of relief. 

Three days later, Maria received a phone call. It was the human resources department at Creative Spark Media, offering her the senior graphic design position. She had learned a valuable lesson: careful preparation is important, but the ability to stay calm and find creative solutions when everything goes wrong is what truly leads to success.
