Title: Tom the Boy and the Flower

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tom. He lived in a small house with his mom, dad, and his pet cat, Fluffy. Tom loved to play with Fluffy all day long. One day, Tom and Fluffy were playing near a row of beautiful flowers. Fluffy jumped and knocked over a brilliant red flower. Tom was sad and worried that his mom would be mad. Tom told his mom about the flower. She looked at him and said, "I forgive you, Tom. I know it was an accident." Tom felt happy that his mom was not mad. He gave Fluffy a big hug, and they went back to play together.

Anna likes to play with her guitar. She has a big guitar that her dad gave her. She wants to learn how to make music with it. She watches her dad play his guitar and tries to copy him. One day, Anna is playing with her guitar in her room. She hears a knock on the door. It is her brother Tom. Tom is nosy. He always wants to see what Anna is doing. "Can I play with your guitar?" Tom asks. "No, it is mine. Go away!" Anna says. "But I want to learn too. Please let me play!" Tom begs. Anna does not want to share her guitar. She likes it too much. She thinks Tom will break it or lose it. She tells him to find his own toy. Tom is sad. He does not have a guitar. He likes music too. He thinks Anna is mean. He goes to his room and cries. Anna hears Tom crying. She feels sorry for him. She remembers that her dad taught her to be kind and share. She thinks maybe she can teach Tom how to play her guitar. She picks up her guitar and goes to Tom's room. "Tom, I am sorry. Do you want to play with my guitar?" Anna asks. Tom looks up and sees Anna with her guitar. He smiles. He wipes his tears and nods. "Thank you, Anna. You are nice. Can you teach me how to play?" Tom asks. Anna nods. She sits next to Tom and shows him how to hold the guitar. She teaches him how to strum the strings and make sounds. They play together and have fun. They learn how to make music with the guitar. They are happy.

Gus went out one day to go jogging. He was skipping along, feeling happy and carefree when suddenly he tripped and fell onto the stage. He looked around, hoping nobody had noticed his stupid mistake. But to his dismay, a group of children had stopped to watch. Gus blushed and whispered to them, “Don’t say anything about this, please.” But the children just laughed and began to sing a silly song about his fall. Gus felt embarrassed and angry. He tried to jog away as quickly as he could, but the children kept following and singing. Finally, in a fit of rage, Gus turned around and shouted at them, “Stop it! That’s enough!” But the children only laughed at him harder and kept following, singing louder and louder. Desperate, Gus ran faster and faster until he collapsed, exhausted and embarrassed. No matter how hard he tried, he couldn't shake the children off, and they kept singing all the way home. Gus never went jogging again.

Once upon a time, there was a sheep named Sam. Sam was an interesting sheep because he could supply soft wool to make warm clothes. Sam lived on a farm with many other animals. One day, Sam met a talking bird named Bob. Bob said, "Hello, Sam! I want to help you supply wool." Sam was surprised and happy. They became good friends and worked together. One day, a big wind came and blew away all the wool Sam had made. Sam was sad, but Bob had a plan. Bob called all his bird friends, and they flew high to find the wool. They brought the wool back, and Sam was happy. Sam and Bob knew that they could always help each other.

Once upon a time there lived an old man. He lived deep in the forest and was very wise. One day a young boy wanted to come to him and ask him a question. So, he went through the forest and arrived in front of the old man's door. He knocked on it. The old man opened the door and said: "Hello, come inside young boy. What can I do for you? The boy asked: "What's more interesting: coal or water?" The old man nodded and said: "That's a very interesting question, but I'm afraid it doesn't have an easy answer. Neither is more interesting than the other." He continued. "The most important thing is to find a balance between the two. Both are necessary and valuable." The boy thanked the old man for the answer and returned home with this valuable lesson. He was thankful for the wise advice he had recieved and realised that nothing in life should be taken too seriously.
