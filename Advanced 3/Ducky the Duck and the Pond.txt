Title: Ducky the Duck and the Pond

Once upon a time, there was a little duck named Ducky. Ducky loved to splash in the water all day. One day, Ducky went to the pond and saw a big cold puddle. Ducky was so excited to splash in the cold water. Near the pond, there was a naughty fox named Foxy. Foxy saw Ducky having fun and wanted to join. But Foxy was naughty and did not ask Ducky if he could play. Foxy jumped in the cold puddle and splashed water all over Ducky. Ducky was sad and wet. Ducky told Foxy that he should ask before joining someone's play. Foxy felt bad and said sorry to Ducky. From that day on, Foxy always asked before playing with others. They both learned that it was important to be kind and ask before joining someone's fun. And they became good friends who loved to splash in the cold water together.

Once upon a time, there was a big bookshelf. It was in a room with many toys. The toys liked to play and have fun. They liked to make things too. One day, they wanted to see who could make the best thing. They all became competitive. Teddy made a tower with blocks. Dolly made a pretty dress. Car made a race track. They all looked at the bookshelf. They wanted to make something with it. They thought and thought. Then, they had an idea. They worked together to make a big castle with the bookshelf. They used books, blocks, and blankets. It was so big and fun! The toys were happy. They learned that working together was better than being competitive. And they all played in the big castle they made.

Sara and her mom went to the shop. They wanted to buy some food for dinner. Sara liked the shop. It had many things to see and touch. "Can I help you?" a man asked. He worked at the shop. He wore a blue shirt and a name tag. "Yes, please. We need some milk, eggs, and bread," Sara's mom said. She gave the man a list. "OK, follow me. I will show you where they are," the man said. He smiled at Sara and her mom. Sara and her mom followed the man. He took them to a big fridge. It was cold inside. He opened the door and took out a bottle of milk. He gave it to Sara's mom. "Here you go. This is mild milk. It is good for you," he said. "Thank you," Sara's mom said. She put the milk in a basket. Sara looked at the fridge. She saw many other bottles of milk. Some were big, some were small. Some were white, some were pink. Some had pictures of cows, some had pictures of strawberries. "Mom, can I have some pink milk?" Sara asked. She pointed at a bottle of strawberry milk. "Maybe another time, Sara. We have to fill our basket with other things first," Sara's mom said. She took Sara's hand and walked to the next aisle. Sara nodded. She liked pink milk, but she also liked eggs and bread. She wondered what they would make for dinner. She hoped it would be yummy. She was hungry. She liked going to the shop with her mom.

Once upon a time, there was a happy little boy named Tim. He loved to build things with his toy blocks. One day, he decided to build something big and new. He wanted to build a cabinet for his room. Tim started to build the cabinet with his blocks. He put one block on top of another, making it taller and taller. He was very careful not to let it fall. His mom and dad watched him and smiled. They were so proud of their little boy. When Tim finished building the cabinet, he was very happy. He put his toys and books inside it. His mom and dad clapped their hands and gave him a big hug. Tim felt very proud of his new cabinet. And they all lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a big sunflower. The sunflower was in a garden. The sunflower was fearful because it felt alone. One day, a nice bee came to visit. The bee said, "Hello, sunflower! I am here to deliver something for you." The sunflower was happy to see the bee. The bee gave the sunflower a seed. The sunflower felt less fearful. The sunflower planted the seed in the ground. Soon, a new sunflower grew. The two sunflowers were happy together. The bee came back and said, "See, I told you I would bring something good." The sunflowers thanked the bee, and they were not fearful anymore.
