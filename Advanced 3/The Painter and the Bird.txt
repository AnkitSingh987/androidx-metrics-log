Title: The Painter and the Bird

Once upon a time, there was a happy painter. He liked to paint all day. One day, he met a hungry bird. The bird was very sad. The happy painter said, "Why are you sad, little bird?" The bird said, "I am hungry. I need food." The painter wanted to help, so he went to feed the bird. He gave the bird some food. But the food was not good for the bird. The bird ate the food and got sick. The happy painter was sad now. The bird could not fly anymore. The painter learned to be careful when feeding birds.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to play with her toys in her room. She had a big bottle of shampoo that she liked to use to wash her toys. Sue thought it was fun to make her toys all clean and shiny. One day, Sue was playing with her toys and she wanted to wash them with her shampoo. But, she could not open the shampoo bottle. Sue tried and tried, but it would not open. She felt very stupid for not being able to open the bottle. So, she decided to break the bottle to get the shampoo out. When Sue broke the bottle, something unexpected happened. The shampoo inside the bottle was not shampoo at all! It was a magic potion that turned her toys into real animals! Sue was so surprised and happy. She played with her new animal friends all day long. And from that day on, she never felt stupid again.

Anna and Ben are friends who like to play with blocks. They have many blocks of different colors and shapes. They can build houses, towers, cars and animals with their blocks. One day, they decide to play a game. They take turns to pick a block from a big box and add it to their building. The rule is that they have to say the color and shape of the block they pick. If they say it wrong, they have to give the block back to the box. Anna goes first. She picks a red square block and says, "Red square." She puts it on her house. Ben goes next. He picks a blue circle block and says, "Blue circle." He puts it on his car. They keep playing like this, having fun and being honest. But then, Ben sees a yellow star block in the box. He likes yellow stars very much. He wants to have it for his car. He picks it and says, "Green triangle." He hopes Anna will not notice. But Anna does notice. She says, "Ben, that is not a green triangle. That is a yellow star. You are not being honest. You have to give it back to the box." Ben feels ashamed. He knows he did something wrong. He says, "I'm sorry, Anna. You are right. I was not honest. I wanted the yellow star for myself. But that is not fair. Here, you can have it. I will pick another block." Anna smiles. She says, "Thank you, Ben. That is very kind of you. But you can keep the yellow star. I have enough blocks for my house. You can use it for your car. It will look nice." Ben is happy. He says, "Thank you, Anna. You are a good friend. I will not lie again. I will be honest with you and with myself." They hug and continue to play with their blocks. They learn that being honest is important and makes them happy. They also learn to share and be generous with their supply of blocks. They have a lot of fun and make beautiful buildings.

Once upon a time there was a little girl named Clara. She was a very special girl with a very unique diary. Every day she would go to the park and look for interesting things. One day, Clara noticed a pretty butterfly flying past and decided to follow it. She partened with the butterfly and eventually came to a pond. On the pond was a large tree with a huge hole. Curious, Clara decided to take a look and saw that inside there were lots of other diaries, just like hers. She was so excited to find more of them and checked them one by one. As she ran her fingers through the pages, they all looked very familiar. Clara realized that all the diaries were filled with her own words, thoughts and memories. She had written down many things that she had forgotten in the past, but it was all coming back to her now. Surrounded by all of the diaries, Clara realized that she was truly unique. Her diary was special and each story and memory was just hers. She smiled, happy to have found her own special place.
