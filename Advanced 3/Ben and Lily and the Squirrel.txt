Title: Ben and Lily and the Squirrel

Ben and Lily were playing in the park. They saw a squirrel on a tree. The squirrel had a big tail and small eyes. It looked harmless. "Let's go and say hello to the squirrel," Ben said. "OK, but be careful," Lily said. They walked slowly to the tree. The squirrel saw them and made a noise. It sounded like a chirp. "Hello, squirrel. We are Ben and Lily. Do you want to play with us?" Ben asked. The squirrel did not answer. It jumped from the tree and ran away. "Come back, squirrel. We are friendly. We won't hurt you," Lily called. But the squirrel did not come back. It hid in another tree. Ben and Lily felt sad. They wanted to be friends with the squirrel. "Maybe the squirrel is scared of us. Maybe we are too big and loud for it," Ben said. "Maybe we should have stayed away from the tree. Maybe we disturbed the squirrel's home," Lily said. They looked at each other. They both felt regret. They decided to leave the squirrel alone. They went to play on the swings instead. They hoped the squirrel would forgive them. They hoped they would see the squirrel again.

One day, a boy named Tim found a big jar of jelly. He was very happy. Tim loved jelly. He wanted to eat it all. Tim took a spoon and began to eat the jelly. After a while, Tim felt very tired. He rubbed his eyes and said, "I need to sleep." Tim went to his bed and fell asleep. While he was asleep, a magic fairy came into his room. The fairy saw the jar of jelly and said, "Oh, Tim ate too much jelly. I will help him." The fairy waved her wand and something unexpected happened. The jelly turned into a big, soft pillow. When Tim woke up, he saw the pillow and said, "Wow! My jelly is now a pillow!" Tim hugged the pillow and felt better. From that day on, he knew not to eat too much jelly.

Once upon a time there was a green rubber. One day, the rubber came alive and asked a little girl, "Please give me a ride?" The little girl was surprised, but said yes. She took the green rubber in her hand and ran off in the park. The little girl and the green rubber had lots of fun. The girl let the rubber jump on the grass and slide down the slide. They giggled and laughed together. Then, the girl thought of a special game. She said to the rubber, "Let's have a race!" The rubber agreed and the race began. The rubber jumped, hopped and stretched better than the girl. The girl laughed and said, "You win!" Suddenly, it became time for the girl to go home. Before saying goodbye, the girl gave the rubber a big hug. Then she gave the rubber a final gift. She rubbed the rubber gently and said, "Goodbye, my rubber friend!" The green rubber was forever thankful for the little girl and her special gifts.

One day, a little girl was walking around the park, carrying a sack. She was humming to herself and enjoying the nice, regular day. Suddenly, she heard a voice behind her. "Hey! Is that your sack?" The little girl turned around and saw a little boy smiling at her. She smiled back and replied "Yes. What's in your sack?" The little boy opened his sack and she saw it was filled with rocks. "Wow, that's so cool, why did you bring rocks?" she asked. The little boy grinned and replied, "I collect them every day so that when I get bigger I can build a castle." The little girl was amazed, and she said "That's a really great idea. I think I'll start collecting rocks too." The little boy smiled. "Let's collect them together. It'll be more regular that way!" The girl agreed, and the two of them continued their walk, chatting and collecting rocks along the way.

Jack was embarrassed. He wanted to keep his blanket close, but everyone was watching him. His blanket was his favorite; it was soft and warm. He softly hugged it to his chest, feeling the warmth seep into his body. Everyone started to laugh. Jack was so embarrassed he didn't know what to do. He started to get mad. He was determined to keep his blanket no matter what they said. He clung to his blanket and refused to let go, no matter how many times they asked him to. Suddenly, his mom appeared. She said, “Jack, let go of the blanket.” Jack shook his head and held it even tighter. His mom smiled sadly and said, “It’s ok. I’ll keep it safe and warm until you’re ready to let it go.” Jack felt relieved. He let his mom take his blanket and watched as she carefully folded it up and put it in her bag. Jack smiled and everyone stopped laughing. Jack was no longer embarrassed. He knew that his mom would always protect his favorite blanket.
