Title: The Independent Bird and the Tree

Once upon a time, there was an independent bird who wanted to fly away. He peeked out of his nest and looked at the sky. He was so excited! He wanted to fly yesterday but his mom said he must wait. But he was independent and wanted to do things his own way. So he spread his wings and flew away. He flew higher and higher and kept going until he couldn't find his way home. The little bird tried to find his family but he was lost. He flew for days, asked for help, but nobody knew how to get him home. Finally, he was too tired and hungry and had to rest in a tree. But tragedy struck. The next day, there was a strong wind that blew the poor little bird out of the tree and off into the sky, far away from home. The moral of the story is that sometimes being too independent can be dangerous. It's important to listen to those older and wiser, and to wait for the right time.

Once upon a time, there was a little pigeon. The pigeon was very lucky. Every day, the pigeon would fly and count things it saw. One day, the pigeon saw a big tree. It flew to the tree and saw a little squirrel. The squirrel said, "Hi, pigeon! Do you want to play with me?" The pigeon said, "Yes, let's play! But first, let's count the nuts I found." The pigeon and the squirrel counted the nuts. There were ten nuts. They were so happy. They played and shared the nuts. They became good friends. From that day on, the lucky pigeon and the squirrel played together every day. They would count things they found and share them with each other. And they lived happily ever after.

One day, a little girl named Lucy found an old pencil on the floor. She picked it up and saw that it had a funny face on it. She thought it was a toy, so she started to chew on it. "Ouch!" said the pencil. Lucy was so surprised that she stopped chewing. "Please don't chew on me," the pencil said. "I am not a toy. I am an old pencil, and I can help you draw pretty pictures." Lucy was happy to have a new friend. She took the old pencil and they started to draw together. They drew a big sun, a house, and a happy family. Lucy loved her old pencil friend and promised never to chew on it again.

"Look!" shouted Bobby. He pointed to a golf ball in the grass. Bobby's mother looked and said, "That's a golf ball! Would you like to play with it?" Bobby nodded and his mother took him to a nearby golf course. Bobby was excited to see all the green grass and dead trees. Bobby's mother handed the golf ball to him and Bobby started to tremble. He had never seen a golf ball before, let alone held one. Bobby's mother said, "It's okay. Here, let me help you." She showed Bobby how to swing the club just right. After a few tries, Bobby hit the ball with a loud thwack. He smiled and said, "Did you see that, Mommy? I hit the golf ball!" Bobby and his mother laughed, and they played until the sun went down.

Once upon a time, there was a small boy named John. He loved to explore the world. One day, he wanted to go somewhere special. He asked his Mom if he could go explore. She said yes, but reminded him to stay safe. So, John put on his shoes and went outside. He was amazed to see a huge billboard in the front of the building. He wanted to see it up close. John carefully crossed the street. He walked up to the billboard. It had pictures of happy people on it. John liked the pictures very much. He decided to sort them by color. When he was done, the pictures had been grouped together. It was time to go back home. John was happy because he had stayed safe during his adventure. He was also happy because he had sorted the pictures on the billboard.
