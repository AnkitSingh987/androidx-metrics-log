Title: Bear and the Bunny

Once upon a time, there were two friends named Bear and Bunny. They were very happy and always playing together. One day, Bunny said to Bear, "Let's bow down and touch our paws together like this". Bear nodded and they started to bow. After a few minutes, Bear said, "That's so fun, Bunny!" But then Bear started to feel a little dizzy. He said, "Whoa, why do I feel so dizzy like this?" Bunny smiled and said, "That's because when you bow, it helps your body get used to upside-down positions". "Ohh" said Bear, "That's so cool!" So they kept bowing until they felt better, then they went off to play with their other friends. And they never felt dizzy again!

Once upon a time, there was a little yellow house. In the house, lived a girl named Sue and her cat, Tom. They loved to play together all day long. One day, Sue wanted to play a new game. She said, "Tom, let's play hide and seek!" Tom meowed and nodded his head. Sue started to count, "One, two, three," while Tom ran to hide. Sue opened her eyes and called, "Ready or not, here I come!" Sue looked all around the house for Tom. She even knelt down to look under the bed. Then, she saw a tail near the window. She tiptoed and said, "I found you, Tom!" Tom came out from behind the curtain and meowed. They both laughed and played hide and seek until it was time for bed.

One day, a little elephant named Elly went to play with her friends. They were playing with a ball near a big tree. Elly's mommy told her, "Elly, please behave and play nice with your friends." While they were playing, Elly saw something yucky on the ground. It was a big pile of disgusting mud. Elly thought it would be funny to play a trick on her friends. She picked up the mud with her trunk and threw it at her friend, Tim the turtle. Tim said, "Elly, that's not nice! You made me dirty with that disgusting mud!" Elly felt bad and said sorry to Tim. She helped him clean up the mud. From that day on, Elly always remembered to behave and be a good friend to everyone.

Once there were two friends, Sally and Jessica. They cared a lot about each other. Every Saturday, Sally and Jessica went to the golf course together. Sally asked Jessica, "What will we do?" Jessica replied, "Let's play golf! I'm sure we can be successful." The two friends had a wonderful time playing golf. They laughed and cheered each other on when they made successful golf shots. When they were done, they hugged each other and promised to come back again next Saturday. Both friends had a great time at the golf course. They felt proud of themselves for being successful at golf. And they knew they could always count on each other to care.

Lily liked to watch cartoons on TV. She liked the funny animals and the bright colors. She laughed when they did silly things. Sometimes, she asked her mom what they were saying. "Mom, what does 'share' mean?" Lily asked, pointing at the TV. "It means to give some of your things to someone else, so you can both be happy," Mom said, smiling. "Like when I give you a hug?" Lily asked. "Yes, like that. Or when you let your brother play with your toys," Mom said. Lily nodded. She understood. She liked to share with her mom and her brother. She thought sharing was nice. One day, Lily saw a new cartoon. It was about a big dragon and a little girl. The dragon was angry and loud. He breathed fire and smashed things. The little girl was scared and sad. She tried to talk to the dragon, but he did not listen. "Mom, why is the dragon so mean?" Lily asked, frowning. "He is mean because he is lonely and hurt. He does not have any friends. He does not know how to share," Mom said, hugging Lily. "Can the girl be his friend?" Lily asked. "Maybe. If she is brave and kind. If she can help him calm down and understand his feelings," Mom said. Lily watched the cartoon. She hoped the girl and the dragon would be friends. She thought they could share hugs and toys and stories. She thought that would make them happy. She understood. She liked to be happy.
