Title: Daisy and the Veil

Daisy was feeling excited. Today she was going to the big fancy shop to pick out a veil for her cousin's wedding. As Daisy walked up to the shop, she could see many beautiful veils. When Daisy stepped inside, the shopkeeper greeted her with a big smile. "What can I help you with today?” Daisy blushed and explained, “I’m looking for a veil for a wedding.” The shopkeeper pointed to the wall, “Look at all the veils! Pick whichever one you want.” Daisy chose a veil that was soft and white. She asked the shopkeeper to cut it so it would fit her cousin. The shopkeeper got to work and soon had the veil cut to size. Daisy thanked him and the shopkeeper handed her a sour candy. Daisy laughed and said, “Thank you!” She was happy to have the perfect veil for her cousin’s wedding.

Once upon a time, in a big green forest, there lived a rabbit. This rabbit was very unusual. It had big, colorful ears that made it special. All the other animals liked to look at the rabbit's ears and talk about them. One day, a big, mean fox saw the rabbit with the colorful ears. The fox wanted to catch the rabbit and make it surrender. The rabbit was scared but decided to be brave. The rabbit ran fast, and the fox chased it. They ran and ran until they reached a big tree. The rabbit climbed the tree and hid in a hole. The fox could not climb the tree, so it had to give up. The other animals saw the rabbit's bravery and cheered. The rabbit learned that it's important to be brave, even when things are scary. And the other animals learned to never give up and always help each other.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was a lucky boy because he had a big bag of candy. He loved his candy very much. One day, he took his candy to the park to play with his friends. At the park, his friends saw the candy and wanted some too. Tim did not want to share his candy with his friends. This made his friends sad and mad. They all left Tim alone in the park. Tim felt bad and knew he should share his candy. Tim wanted to say sorry to his friends. He had an idea. He went to each friend's house and gave them some candy. His friends were happy and forgave Tim. They all went back to the park to play together. While playing, Tim tripped and fell. All his candy fell on the ground too. Tim felt embarrass because everyone was looking at him. But his friends helped him up and picked up the candy. They all laughed and shared the candy together. From that day on, Tim learned to share his lucky candy with his friends. They all had fun and played together every day. Tim was happy to have such good friends who helped him when he was embarrass. And they all lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a big dragon. The dragon was very dry. He lived in a cave near a small town. The dragon was not happy because he was so dry. He wanted to find water to feel better. One day, the dragon met a little girl. The girl knew how to number things. She told the dragon, "I can help you find water. Let's number the steps to the water." They walked together, counting the steps. One, two, three, four, and so on. As they walked, something unexpected happened. They found a big pool of water, but it was not just water. It was ice cream! The dragon was so happy. He ate the ice cream and felt better. The girl and the dragon became best friends, and they shared the ice cream with the whole town.

One day, a cat and a dog were playing in the park. They were having lots of fun. The cat was very fit and fast. The dog was big and strong. They wanted to have a race to see who was faster. "Ready, set, go!" said the cat. They both started to run very fast. The cat was in the lead, but the dog was close behind. Suddenly, the cat saw a big tree in front of her. She started to panic. She did not know what to do. The dog saw the cat panic and said, "Jump over the tree!" The cat jumped very high and went over the tree. The dog also jumped over the tree. They both kept running. In the end, the cat won the race. The dog was not sad. He was happy for the cat's victory. They both laughed and played some more. They learned that helping each other was more important than winning.
