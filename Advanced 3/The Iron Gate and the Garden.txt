Title: The Iron Gate and the Garden

Once upon a time, there was a big iron gate. The gate was very old and had started to rot. One day, a little boy named Tim and his dog, Spot, were walking by the gate. They saw a big hole in the gate. Tim said to Spot, "Look, Spot! We can go through the hole." As they went through the hole, they found a beautiful garden with lots of flowers and trees. Tim and Spot were very happy to play in the garden. They ran and played all day. But then, they saw a sad man sitting by a tree. The man looked very sorry. Tim went to the man and asked, "Why are you sorry?" The man said, "I am sorry because I made the hole in the gate. I didn't know it would rot." Tim smiled and said, "That's okay. Now we have a beautiful garden to play in!" The man smiled too, and they all played in the garden together.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was very adventurous. He liked to play with his toys and pretend he was driving a big car. One day, Tim found a dishwasher in the kitchen. He wanted to see if he could drive it like a car. He climbed inside and tried to turn it on. But, the dishwasher did not move like a car. It started to make loud noises and water came out. Tim was scared. Tim's mom heard the noise and came to see what was happening. She saw Tim inside the dishwasher and quickly turned it off. She told Tim that he should not play inside the dishwasher. Tim was sad, but he learned that not everything can be driven like a car. From that day on, Tim played with his toys and had many more adventures, but he never tried to drive the dishwasher again.

Tommy was an adventurous three year old boy, who loved going on safaris. He would often go and explore the wild corners of his backyard, ever daring himself to find new and exciting things. One day, his mom and dad invited him to take a real safari with them. Tommy was excited and a little bit scared when they went to the zoo. He started compaining to his mom, "I want to go see the tigers. Dare me Mommy!" His mom could see he was feeling a little bit troubled and so she said, "Sure, if you're brave enough. But you have to be careful and listen to Daddy." Tommy's mom, dad and little brother soon met up with the zookeeper and soon Tommy was on his very own safari. He was filled with joy as he saw all the wild animals around him, and he was filled with bravery as he finally he saw the tigers who were so close. The zookeeper smiled and said, "You're a brave one, Tommy. Not many children would dare to come this close to tigers!" Tommy beamed with pride and answered, "I'm brave, I'm strong and I'm ready for more safaris!"

Ben and Lily were playing in the backyard. They liked to pretend they were brave explorers, looking for treasure and adventure. They had a flashlight, a map, and a backpack with snacks and toys. "Let's go to the cave!" Ben said, pointing to the big bush that looked like a dark hole. "Maybe we'll find some gold or jewels!" "Okay, but be careful. It might be scary or dangerous in there," Lily said, following her brother. She held his hand and the flashlight. They crawled under the bush and saw a tunnel that led deeper into the ground. They decided to go in, shining the flashlight ahead of them. They saw some rocks, some roots, and some bugs. They also heard some noises, like squeaks and growls. "What's that?" Lily asked, feeling scared. "I don't know. Maybe it's a monster or a dragon!" Ben said, trying to sound tough. He wanted to protect his sister. He said, "Don't worry, Lily. I'll rescue you if anything happens." They kept going, hoping to find the end of the tunnel and the treasure. But then, something bad happened. The flashlight went out. It was very dark and they couldn't see anything. "Ben, I can't see! I'm scared!" Lily cried. "Me too, Lily. Me too," Ben said. He hugged his sister and tried to find the flashlight. He hoped it was just the batteries that were dead. He wished they had brought another flashlight or a candle. But then, they heard a voice. It was their mom. She had followed them to the bush and saw them go into the tunnel. She had another flashlight and she called their names. "Ben! Lily! Where are you? Are you okay?" Mom said. "Mom! We're here! We're here!" Ben and Lily shouted. They were so happy to hear her voice. They followed the sound and saw the light. Mom found them and hugged them. "Thank goodness you're safe! What were you doing in there?" Mom asked. "We were exploring. We were looking for treasure. But the flashlight went out and we got scared," Ben said. "Well, you're very brave, but also very silly. You shouldn't go into dark and dangerous places without telling me. You could have gotten hurt or lost," Mom said. "I'm sorry, Mom. We won't do it again," Lily said. "Me too, Mom. I'm sorry. I wanted to rescue Lily, but I couldn't," Ben said. "You don't have to rescue Lily. You just have to stay with her and help her. And I'll always rescue both of you, because I love you," Mom said. She took them out of the tunnel and back to the backyard. She gave them some juice and cookies and checked them for scratches and bites. She told them they could play, but only in the safe and sunny areas. She also told them they could keep the flashlight, but only for fun and not for trouble. Ben and Lily agreed. They thanked Mom and hugged her. They decided they had enough adventure for one day. They played with the flashlight, making shadows and shapes on the wall. They also shared their snacks and toys. They were happy and safe. They were also proud of themselves. They had been tough explorers, but they had also learned a lesson. They had learned to be careful, to listen to Mom, and to stick together. They had learned that the best treasure was their family.
