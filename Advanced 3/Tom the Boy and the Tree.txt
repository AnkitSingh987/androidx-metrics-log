Title: Tom the Boy and the Tree

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tom. Tom was an adventurous boy who loved to jump and play. One day, Tom found a vest in his room. It was a pretty vest with many colors. He put on the vest and went outside to play. Tom saw his friend, Sue. "Look at my new vest!" he said. Sue looked at the vest and smiled. "It's very pretty, Tom!" she said. "Let's go on an adventure!" Tom and Sue went to the big tree near their house. They liked to jump from the tree and play. Tom and Sue climbed the big tree. Tom wanted to jump first. He looked down and felt scared. Sue said, "Be careful, Tom!" But Tom was too adventurous. He jumped from the tree. But the jump was too big, and Tom fell down. He got hurt and cried. The pretty vest got dirty too. Tom and Sue learned that being too adventurous can sometimes lead to a bad ending.

Once upon a time, in a friendly little town, there lived a boy named Tim. Tim loved to eat apples. Every day, he would go to the big apple tree in his yard and pick the reddest, juiciest apple he could find. One sunny day, Tim went to the apple tree and saw that there were no more apples left. He was very sad. Tim decided to pray for more apples to grow on the tree. He closed his eyes and said, "Please, let more apples grow on my tree." The next morning, Tim ran to the tree and saw that it was full of big, red apples! He was so happy! Tim picked an apple and shared it with his friends. They all thanked him and prayed together for more apples to grow on the tree, so they could enjoy them every day.

Once upon a time, there was a charming little girl named Lily. She had a big box in her room. Lily loved her box because it was full of toys. But one day, she could not open the box. It was locked! Lily was sad and wanted to play with her toys. She looked for a way to unlock the box. She tried to push and pull, but the box would not open. Lily started to cry. Then, her big brother came into her room. He saw Lily crying and asked what was wrong. Lily told him about the locked box. Her brother found a key and helped her unlock the box. Lily was so happy to see her toys again. She hugged her brother and they played together all day.

Once upon a time there was a little girl named Dodi. She was very happy. One day while she was sitting in her chair, she heard a noise outside her window. Dodi went to look out the window to see what was making the noise. Much to her surprise, she saw a squirrel sitting on the lawn. Dodi was so excited she ran outside to get a closer look at the squirrel. She watched the furry creature with delight. Suddenly the squirrel stopped and looked right at Dodi. Dodi smiled, but the squirrel just stared. She waved at it, but the squirrel just kept staring. Dodi wasn't sure what to do next and she didn't understand. She turned to her mom for help. "Mommy, why doesn't the squirrel understand me?" Dodi asked. Her mom smiled and said, "The squirrel doesn't understand because it doesn't speak our language. But I'm sure it can tell you are a very happy girl." Dodi smiled and ran off to play, happy to know that all animals, even the squirrels, can tell when she is happy.

Once there was a little girl named Lydia who loved to skip. She could skip through the park and around her house better than anyone else. One day, Lydia was skipping through the park and saw a yellow tomato. She thought it was so funny, she had never seen a yellow tomato before. Lydia ran over to pick it up and it was so heavy that it almost threw her off balance. She had to use both of her hands to finally hold onto it. Suddenly, her neighbor, Miss Ethel, spoke from behind her. "Lydia, that is not your tomato," said Miss Ethel. "But I picked it up first!" Lydia exclaimed. Mrs. Ethel smiled and said, "It does not matter who found it first, it is not yours. We must all respect others' belongings." Lydia thought about it for a moment, then nodded her head and said, "You are right, Mrs. Ethel. I will put it back." So Lydia put the yellow tomato back down and skipped away, remembering the important lesson Mrs. Ethel had taught her.
