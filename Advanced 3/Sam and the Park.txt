Title: Sam and the Park

Sam was carrying his lollipop. It was a wide lollipop, almost as big as his hand. He was so excited! He was walking to the park with his daddy. "Daddy, look at my lollipop!" Sam said. Daddy smiled. "That's a nice one, Sam. Let's go to the park and have some fun!" Sam was so excited. They reached the park, and there were a lot of cool things to explore. Sam saw a swing and he ran over to it. Daddy carried the lollipop and watched as Sam had fun. Suddenly, Sam remembered his lollipop. "Daddy, can I have my lollipop now?" "Sure, here's your lollipop," Daddy said as he handed Sam the wide lollipop. Sam was so happy that he had a lollipop. He finished eating it and said to Daddy, "Thanks for carrying my lollipop for me!"

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Emma. She wanted to bake a cake. She set down all the things she needed: flour, eggs, and sugar. The next day she set the oven just the way her mom taught her. Then she mixed all the ingredients together. Finally she poured the mixture into the cake pan and put it into the oven. After a while the cake was ready. Emma set the cake on the table and she was very excited. She brought her friends to try it but they said it was so boring. They asked her, 'What did you put in this cake?' Emma said, 'Just the regular things, why?' Her friends said, 'You need to add some sprinkles or candy to make this cake better. Otherwise it's too boring.' So Emma did exactly what her friends said and added the sprinkles and candy to the cake. And it was delicious! Her friends were so happy and they said it was the best cake they had ever tasted.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a mighty king. The king had a very pretty gem. He loved the gem so much that he would call it his best friend. The gem was shiny and bright, and everyone in the town liked to look at it. One day, a little boy came to the king. The boy said, "I have a gift for you." The king was happy and asked, "What is it?" The boy showed the king a small stone. It was not shiny like the gem, but the boy said it was special. The king did not think the stone was special, but he took it anyway. That night, the king put the stone next to his gem. When he woke up the next morning, the gem was gone! The king was very sad and called for help. The little boy came back and showed the king that the stone was now shiny like the gem. The king was happy again and learned that even small things can be special too.

Once upon a time, in a big green park, there was a little circle. The circle was very happy. It would roll around the park all day long, playing with its friends. The circle had many friends like the square, the triangle, and the star. One day, the circle was playing with its friends when it saw an angry bird. The bird was mad because it could not find a place to rest. The circle wanted to help the bird, so it rolled over to the bird and said, "Don't be angry, bird. You can rest on me." The bird was very happy and thanked the circle. It rested on the circle and soon fell asleep. The circle was happy that it could help its new friend. From that day on, the circle and the bird became best friends and played together in the park every day.

Once upon a time, in a wide green park, there lived a tiny insect named Buzz. Buzz was a happy insect who loved to play and make new friends. One sunny day, Buzz found a shiny ring on the ground. The ring was very pretty, and Buzz wanted to keep it. Buzz showed the ring to his friend, Hop, the bunny. Hop told Buzz that the ring belonged to someone else, and they should try to find the owner. But Buzz did not want to give the ring back. He thought it would make him happy to keep it. Hop and Buzz went on a search to find the owner of the ring. They asked all their friends if they had lost a ring. Finally, they found the owner, a little girl who was very sad because she lost her ring. When Buzz gave the ring back to the girl, she was so happy, and she thanked Buzz and Hop. Buzz felt good inside for doing the right thing. The moral of the story is that it is better to be honest and kind, rather than keeping something that does not belong to you.
