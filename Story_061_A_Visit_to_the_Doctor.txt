Title: A Visit to the Doctor
Category: Beginner

It is Tuesday morning, but ten-year-old Sam is not getting ready for school. He is lying in his bed. He feels very hot, and his head hurts. His throat is sore when he swallows. 
His mother comes into the bedroom. She touches his forehead with her hand. 
"You are very warm, Sam," she says. "You have a fever. You cannot go to school today."
"My head hurts," Sam whispers. His voice sounds scratchy. 
"I know," his mother says gently. "We must go and see the doctor."

Sam does not like going to the doctor, but he knows it is a good idea. He feels terrible. 
His mother helps him put on his clothes. He puts on comfortable sweatpants and a warm sweater. They get into the car and drive to the local health clinic. 

The clinic is busy today. Many people are sitting in the waiting room. Some people are coughing. Some children are crying. 
Sam and his mother go to the reception desk. 
"Good morning," Sam's mother says to the receptionist. "My son is sick. Can he see Dr. Evans?"
The receptionist looks at her computer. "Yes, Dr. Evans has an open appointment in twenty minutes. Please sit down and wait."

Sam sits on a plastic chair next to his mother. He closes his eyes because the bright lights in the waiting room make his headache worse. He rests his head on his mother's shoulder. 
After a long twenty minutes, a nurse opens a door and calls his name. "Sam Miller?"
"That is us," his mother says. 

They follow the nurse down a short hallway. They go into a small, clean room. There is a bed with white paper on it, a desk, and some medical equipment. 
A few minutes later, Dr. Evans walks into the room. He is a tall man with friendly brown eyes and a white coat. 
"Hello, Sam," Dr. Evans says with a smile. "I hear you are not feeling well today. What is the problem?"

"My head hurts a lot," Sam says quietly. "And my throat is very sore."
"I see," the doctor says. "Let's take a look. Please open your mouth wide and say 'Ahhh'."
Sam opens his mouth wide. "Ahhh," he says. 
Dr. Evans uses a small wooden stick and a bright light to look inside Sam's mouth. 
"Your throat is very red," the doctor says. "It looks like you have a throat infection."

Next, the doctor uses a stethoscope. He puts the cold metal circle on Sam's chest. 
"Take a deep breath," Dr. Evans says. 
Sam breathes in deeply. He breathes out. 
"Your lungs sound clear," the doctor says. "That is good."
Then, the doctor uses a thermometer to check Sam's temperature. 
"You have a fever of thirty-eight degrees," Dr. Evans tells Sam's mother. "It is a normal fever for a throat infection."

Sam feels a little scared. "Am I very sick?" he asks. 
"No, you are not very sick," Dr. Evans says kindly. "You have a common infection. But you need to rest, and you need some medicine to help your body fight it. I will give you a prescription for some antibiotics."
The doctor sits at his desk and writes on a small piece of paper. He gives the paper to Sam's mother. 
"Take this paper to the pharmacy," he says. "They will give you the medicine. Sam needs to take it twice a day for a week. And he needs to drink plenty of water."

"Can he go to school tomorrow?" his mother asks. 
"No," Dr. Evans answers. "He should stay home tomorrow and rest. If his fever is gone on Thursday, he can go back to school."
"Thank you, Doctor," his mother says. 
"You are welcome," Dr. Evans says. He looks at Sam. "Get well soon, Sam. Drink your water and get some sleep."

"I will," Sam promises. 
They leave the small room and walk out of the clinic. Sam's head still hurts, but he feels a little better because he knows what is wrong. The doctor was nice, and the doctor knows how to fix the problem. Now, they must go to the pharmacy to get the medicine. Sam hopes the medicine does not taste terrible.
