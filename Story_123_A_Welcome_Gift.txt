Title: A Welcome Gift
Category: Beginner

It was late in the afternoon. The moving truck was gone, and the mountain of boxes on the new neighbor's porch had slowly disappeared inside the house. 

In Lily's kitchen, it was very warm, and the air smelled wonderfully sweet. Lily's mother was pulling a large, flat metal tray out of the oven. On the tray were two dozen perfectly round chocolate chip cookies. The chocolate chips were melted and gooey, and the edges of the cookies were golden brown. 

"They look perfect," Lily said, leaning over the counter and inhaling the sweet smell. "Can I have one?"
"Not yet," her mother laughed, placing the hot tray on a cooling rack. "These are not for us. These are a welcome gift for Mrs. Davis and Jack."
"That is a great idea, Mom," Lily agreed. "Jack said they drove for six hours today. They are probably too tired to cook a real dinner tonight."

"Exactly," her mother said. "When we moved into this house, the neighbors brought us a hot casserole. It is a very nice tradition to welcome new people with food. It makes them feel comfortable in a strange place."

They waited twenty minutes for the cookies to cool down slightly. Her mother found a beautiful white ceramic plate with blue flowers painted on it. She carefully arranged the chocolate chip cookies in a neat pile on the plate. Then, she wrapped the whole plate in clear plastic wrap and tied a small yellow ribbon around the top. It looked like a very professional, expensive gift from a bakery. 

"Okay, Lily," her mother said, handing her the plate. "The welcome gift is ready. Let's go over and officially introduce ourselves."

They walked out of their front door, crossed the grass, and walked up the driveway of the new house. The house was quiet. The curtains were not hanging in the windows yet, so Lily could see tall stacks of brown boxes sitting in the living room. 

Her mother knocked politely on the front door. They waited for a moment. Then, they heard footsteps, and the door opened. 
Mrs. Davis stood in the doorway. She looked tired, and there was a smudge of dust on her cheek, but she smiled brightly when she saw them. 

"Hello again, Lily," Mrs. Davis said. "And you must be Lily's mother."
"Yes, I am," Lily's mother smiled warmly, holding out her hand. "My name is Sarah. I live right next door. We just wanted to come over and officially welcome you to the neighborhood."
"It is wonderful to meet you, Sarah," Mrs. Davis said, shaking her hand. "I am Laura Davis. Please excuse the mess. We are surrounded by a million boxes right now."

"We understand completely," Sarah sympathized. "Moving is chaotic. That is exactly why we brought you this."
Lily held out the plate wrapped in clear plastic and yellow ribbon. 
"We baked you some fresh chocolate chip cookies," Lily explained. "We thought you might need some energy after carrying all those boxes today."

Mrs. Davis looked at the plate of warm cookies, and her smile grew even wider. She looked genuinely touched by the gesture. 
"Oh, that is incredibly thoughtful of you both," she said gratefully, taking the plate from Lily. "Thank you so much. To be honest, Jack and I were just wondering what we were going to do for dinner because I haven't unpacked the kitchen pots and pans yet. These cookies are a lifesaver."

"Jack! Come to the door!" Mrs. Davis called over her shoulder into the house. 
A moment later, Jack appeared in the hallway. He also looked tired and dusty. 
"Look what our new neighbors brought for us," his mother said, showing him the cookies. 
Jack's eyes lit up immediately. "Chocolate chip! Those are my favorite. Thank you, Lily!"
"You are welcome," Lily smiled. "Consider it payment for the heavy lifting we did earlier."

"We will not keep you any longer," Lily's mother said politely, stepping back from the door. "We know you have a lot of unpacking to do. But please, if you need anything at all—if you need to borrow a tool, or if you just need to know where the closest supermarket is—do not hesitate to knock on our door."
"I really appreciate that, Sarah," Mrs. Davis said. "You have made our first day here feel very welcoming."

Lily and her mother walked back to their house, feeling very happy. The simple act of baking a plate of cookies had instantly created a bridge between the two houses. It was a small gift, but it carried a big message: you are welcome here, and you are not alone in your new home.
