Title: The Refund Problem

Category: Intermediate

Sarah stood in front of the customer service desk at "Macy & Co," the large department store in the center of the shopping mall. In her hands, she held a heavy plastic shopping bag containing a dark blue winter coat. She was feeling incredibly frustrated. She had purchased the expensive coat just three days ago, hoping to wear it during her upcoming trip to the mountains. However, a major problem had ruined her plans.

When she had tried to wear the coat for the first time yesterday, she pulled the zipper up to her neck, and the entire metal track split apart. The zipper was completely broken, leaving the coat useless against the cold wind. It was clearly a manufacturing defect.

Behind the counter stood a young customer service assistant with a name tag that read "Chloe."

"Hi," Sarah said, placing the bag on the counter. "I need to return this coat. I bought it on Tuesday, and the zipper broke the very first time I tried to put it on."

Chloe smiled politely and pulled the coat out of the bag. She examined the broken zipper and nodded. "Oh, I see the problem. That is definitely defective. I am so sorry about that. Do you have the receipt?"

Sarah’s heart sank slightly. "Well, that is part of the problem. I threw the paper receipt away. I didn't think I would need it because the coat fit me perfectly in the fitting room. But I have the transaction record right here on my phone."

Sarah unlocked her smartphone, opened her banking application, and showed the screen to Chloe. "You see? It shows a charge for one hundred and twenty dollars at Macy & Co on Tuesday afternoon."

Chloe looked at the screen, but her polite smile faded into an expression of apology. "I'm sorry, ma'am, but I cannot accept a bank statement as proof of purchase. Our store policy strictly requires the original printed receipt for all cash or credit card refunds."

"But the charge is right there," Sarah argued, trying to keep her voice calm. "It clearly proves that I bought the coat here, and it proves how much I paid for it. The coat also still has the store tags attached to the sleeve!"

"I understand," Chloe said sympathetically, "and I can see the tags. But without the barcode on the original receipt, our computer system will not let me process a refund back to your credit card. The system simply blocks the action."

"That is ridiculous," Sarah sighed, resting her hands on the counter. "I bought a defective product from your store. I am entitled to a refund. If the computer system won't let you do it, can I please speak to a manager?"

"Of course, I will call him right now," Chloe said. She picked up a telephone and spoke quietly for a moment.

A few minutes later, a man in a sharp grey suit walked over. "Good morning. I'm David, the floor manager. Chloe explained the situation to me. You have a damaged coat but no receipt, correct?"

"Yes," Sarah explained again. "The zipper is completely broken. I just want my money back so I can buy a working coat before my trip."

David inspected the coat carefully. "It is definitely our merchandise, and the damage is our fault. The manufacturer used a cheap zipper on this batch. However, Chloe is correct about the store policy. Without the receipt, we cannot issue a direct refund to your bank account."

Sarah felt her frustration growing. "So, what are you saying? Because of a piece of paper, I just lost one hundred and twenty dollars on a broken coat?"

"Not at all," David replied quickly, raising his hands to calm her down. "We want to make this right for you. While I cannot give you a cash refund, I can offer you a few different solutions."

"What kind of solutions?" Sarah asked, crossing her arms defensively.

"First," David explained, "I can offer you an exact exchange. If we have this same blue coat in your size in the store, we can simply swap the broken one for a new one. I will personally test the zipper on the new one before you leave to guarantee it works."

Sarah thought about it. She really liked the color and the style of the coat.

"And if you don't have my size?" she asked.

"Then I can issue you a store credit," David said. "I can put one hundred and twenty dollars onto a Macy & Co gift card. You can use it today to buy a different coat, or you can save it and use it anytime in the future. It never expires."

Sarah wasn't thrilled about the store credit. She preferred to have her money back in her bank account, giving her the freedom to shop somewhere else. However, she recognized that David was trying to find a compromise, and she knew she had made a mistake by throwing the receipt away.

"Let's try the exchange first," Sarah finally agreed. "If you have the exact same coat in a medium, and the zipper works perfectly, I will take it."

"Excellent," David smiled. "Chloe, please check the system for a medium blue winter coat."

Chloe typed rapidly on her keyboard. "We have two left in stock in the back room. I'll go get one right now."

While they waited, David apologized again for the inconvenience. "We are trying to update our software so we can search for purchases using credit card numbers, but the new system isn't ready yet. I appreciate your patience."

A minute later, Chloe returned with a brand new coat. David placed it on the counter and carefully pulled the zipper up and down five times. It moved smoothly and securely.

"It works perfectly," David said, handing the coat to Sarah. "Because of the trouble you experienced today, I am also going to include a ten percent discount coupon for your next purchase."

Sarah inspected the new coat and felt the smooth zipper. The anger she had felt earlier completely disappeared. Although the store's strict refund policy was annoying, the staff had been polite and focused on solving her problem.

"Thank you, David. And thank you, Chloe," Sarah said, putting the new coat into her bag. "I appreciate your help. And I promise I will keep the receipt this time."

With a functional winter coat and a discount for her next visit, Sarah left the store feeling satisfied with the compromise.
