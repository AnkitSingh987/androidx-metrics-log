Title: The Space Room
Category: Beginner

Emma and Sarah climbed the grand marble staircase to the fourth floor of the City Museum. Their legs were a little tired, but their curiosity kept them moving fast. The sign at the top of the stairs pointed to a dark hallway. The sign said, 'Journey to the Stars'. 

As they walked down the hallway, the normal lights disappeared. The walls and the ceiling were painted completely black. Small, bright LED lights sparkled on the ceiling, looking exactly like distant stars in the night sky. Soft, mysterious music played quietly from hidden speakers. 

"It feels like we really are in space," Sarah whispered. The atmosphere was completely different from the bright, noisy dinosaur room. 

They entered the main exhibition room. It was a large, circular room. In the very center of the room, hanging from the dark ceiling, was a massive, glowing model of the Earth. It rotated slowly. Emma could see the blue oceans, the green continents, and the white clouds swirling around the globe. 

"That is beautiful," Emma said, staring up at the glowing Earth. 
But the Earth was not the only thing in the room. Scattered around the dark space were models of all the other planets in the solar system. 
Emma took her notebook out again. 

"Let's find the biggest planet," she said. 
They walked around the circular room until they found an enormous sphere painted with swirling stripes of orange, brown, and white. There was a large red spot near the bottom. 
"Jupiter," Sarah read from the glowing screen next to the model. "It is a gas giant. It is so big that you could fit one thousand Earths inside it."
Emma wrote quickly. 'Jupiter. Gas giant. 1,000 times bigger than Earth. Has a big red spot.'

Next, they found a planet with wide, beautiful rings made of ice and rock. 
"Saturn," Emma said without reading the sign. She had seen pictures of it before. "The rings look solid, but they are actually made of millions of tiny pieces of ice."
She wrote that fact down, too. 

In the corner of the room, there was a glass display case. Inside the case was a thick, white suit with a large glass helmet and heavy boots. 
"Is that a real astronaut suit?" Sarah asked, walking closer. 
"Yes," her mother answered, reading the small card on the glass. "This suit was worn by an astronaut who went to the International Space Station. The suit protects them from the extreme cold and gives them oxygen to breathe when they walk outside the spaceship."

Sarah took out her notebook and quickly sketched a picture of the bulky white suit and the round glass helmet. 

Finally, they found a small, rough, grey rock sitting inside a very secure glass box. A spotlight shone brightly on it. 
"What is that?" Emma asked. "It just looks like a boring stone."
Sarah leaned in and read the sign. She gasped. 
"Emma, it is not a boring stone," she said excitedly. "It is a moon rock! Real astronauts brought this rock back to Earth from the moon in 1969!"

Emma looked at the grey rock with new respect. It was amazing to think that the small stone had come from the bright white moon they saw in the sky every night. She wrote down the date: 1969. 

They spent another hour in the dark, quiet room, reading about black holes, distant galaxies, and hot, burning stars. The universe seemed incredibly huge and mysterious. It made the giant dinosaur bones seem very small and close to home. 

When they finally walked out of the dark exhibition and back into the bright, normal hallway of the museum, both girls felt a little dizzy. They had traveled back in time sixty-five million years, and they had traveled millions of miles across the solar system, all in one morning. 

"Well," Emma's mother said. "You have seen the dinosaurs, and you have seen space. Have you decided which topic you will choose for your school project?"
Emma and Sarah looked at each other and smiled. They did not even need to discuss it. 
"Space," they said together. "We want to write about the stars."
