Title: A Good Friendship
Category: Beginner

Two weeks had passed since the massive white moving truck parked in the driveway next door. The 'For Sale' sign had been completely removed from the front lawn. The empty, quiet house was now full of life. There were bright curtains in the windows, a welcome mat on the front porch, and a bicycle leaning against the garage wall. 

It was Friday afternoon. Lily and Jack were sitting under the large, green oak tree in Lily's backyard. The weather was still warm, but there was a slight chill in the air, hinting that autumn was just around the corner. 

They were sitting on a red blanket, doing their homework together. School had started three days ago. It had been a busy and exciting week for both of them. 

"I cannot solve this math problem," Jack groaned, throwing his pencil onto his notebook. "It makes absolutely no sense to me. Why do we need to learn algebra?"
Lily laughed and leaned over to look at his book. "It is not that difficult, Jack. You just missed a step. Look here, you have to multiply this number before you subtract."
She took her pencil and quickly wrote the correct equation on his paper. 
Jack looked at it for a second. "Oh, I see it now. Thank you, Lily. You are a lifesaver. I am terrible at math."
"And I am terrible at history," Lily reminded him. "So, you can help me remember all those dates for the test tomorrow."

They were a very good team. Over the past two weeks, they had spent almost every afternoon together. They threw the football in the yard, they explored the neighborhood on their bicycles, and they walked to school together every morning. 

For Jack, moving to a new town was not the scary, lonely experience he had feared. Having Lily as a neighbor made everything much easier. On the very first day of school, Lily introduced Jack to all her friends in the cafeteria. She showed him where his locker was, and she told him which teachers were strict and which teachers were funny. Because of her, he didn't feel like an outsider; he felt like he belonged. 

"Are you ready for the football tryouts on Monday?" Lily asked, closing her math book. 
Jack nodded, a confident smile on his face. "I am. We practiced throwing in the yard so much this week that my arm feels very strong. I am definitely going to make the team."
"I know you will," Lily said sincerely. "And I will come and watch your first game from the bleachers."

Just then, Lily's mother walked out the back door, carrying a plate of fresh sandwiches and two glasses of lemonade. 
"How is the homework going, you two?" she asked, setting the plate down on the red blanket. 
"It is finished, Mrs. Clark," Jack said politely, taking a sandwich. "Lily is a genius at math."
"Well, teamwork always makes the job easier," Lily's mother smiled. "You two have become quite the dynamic duo."

As her mother walked back into the house, Lily looked at Jack. He was eating his sandwich and looking up at the leaves of the oak tree. 
Lily thought about the day the moving truck arrived. She remembered how curious she was about the empty house and the new people. Now, she couldn't imagine her street without Jack living next door. 

In just two weeks, they had built a very strong connection. They shared the same sense of humor, they helped each other with their weaknesses, and they supported each other's goals. They were no longer just the boy and the girl who lived next door to each other. 

"Hey, Jack," Lily said, holding her glass of cold lemonade. 
"Yeah?" Jack answered, looking over. 
"I am really glad you moved into that house," Lily expressed her feelings honestly. "It has been a very fun two weeks."
Jack smiled, a genuine, happy smile. "Me too, Lily. It was the best move we ever made."

They clinked their glasses together in a small toast. The sun shone down through the green leaves, and the afternoon was perfect. It was the end of a busy summer, the beginning of a new school year, and most importantly, the start of a very good, lasting friendship.
