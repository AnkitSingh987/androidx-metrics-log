Title: The Fine Dolphin and the Shiny

Once upon a time, in a big sea, there lived a fine dolphin. The dolphin loved to swim and play all day. He was very happy and had many friends. One day, the dolphin found a shiny thing in the water. He did not know what it was, but he wanted to keep it. So, he decided to attach the shiny thing to his fin. Now, the dolphin had a pretty shiny thing on his fin and he was very proud of it. The dolphin showed his shiny thing to all of his friends. They all liked it very much and thought the dolphin looked even more fine with it. They all played and swam together, and the dolphin with the shiny thing was the happiest of them all.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play in the park on Wednesday. They have a big slide, a swing, and a sandbox. Anna likes the slide, but Ben likes the swing. One Wednesday, they go to the park with Mom. Anna runs to the slide and climbs up the ladder. Ben runs to the swing and pushes it high. He feels the wind in his hair and laughs. "Look, Mom, I'm flying!" he shouts. Anna slides down and sees Ben. She wants to try the swing too. She runs to Ben and says, "Ben, let me have a turn!" Ben shakes his head. "No, Anna, this is my swing. You have the slide." Anna feels angry. She pulls Ben's leg and tries to make him stop. Ben kicks Anna and tries to make her go away. "Stop it, Anna, you're hurting me!" he cries. "Stop it, Ben, you're being mean!" she yells. Mom hears them and comes over. She sees them fighting and feels sad. "Anna, Ben, what are you doing?" she asks. "Why are you not sharing?" Anna and Ben stop and look at Mom. They feel sorry. "Sorry, Mom," they say. Mom hugs them and says, "It's okay, but you have to be nice to each other. You are twins, you are friends. You can both play with the swing and the slide. How about you take turns?" Anna and Ben nod. They say sorry to each other and hug. They decide to take turns. Anna goes on the swing first, and Ben helps her push it high. Then Ben goes on the slide, and Anna helps him reach the ladder. They have fun and laugh. Mom smiles and says, "That's better. I'm proud of you, Anna and Ben. You are good twins, good friends."

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was an old theater. The theater had big doors and many seats. People loved to watch shows there. But one day, the theater started to break. The roof had holes, and the walls had cracks. A little girl named Lily wanted to save the theater. She loved the shows and wanted everyone to keep watching them. So, she asked her friends to help her. They all said yes. They wanted to save the theater too. Together, they fixed the roof and the walls. They painted the theater with bright colors. Everyone was happy to see the old theater looking new again. They all went inside, sat on the seats, and watched a show. Lily and her friends saved the theater, and everyone clapped for them.

Once upon a time, there was a lonely cobweb in a corner of a dark basement. The cobweb was old and sad because no one ever visited. One day, the cobweb noticed a little mouse creeping around. The cobweb was so excited to see a friend. The cobweb said "hello" to the mouse. But suddenly, the mouse disappeared! The cobweb was sad again and felt lonely. The cobweb thought hard about how to find its friend. Finally, the cobweb had an idea! The cobweb made a new cobweb right next to where the mouse had been. The next day, the mouse reappeared and the cobweb was overjoyed! The mouse and the cobweb became good friends and never disappeared again.

Once upon a time there were two friends, a boy and a girl. Every day they played together in the park. One day, the girl pulled out a roll of tape and said to the boy, “Let’s pretend this is a special rope that can take us anywhere we want to go.” The boy replied with excitement, “What a great idea!” So they ran around the park, imagining all the places the “rope” could take them to. Every once in a while the tape would get tangled up and the two friends would laugh and try to untangle it. After a while, the girl said, “I’m so proud of how humble and kind you’ve been throughout our entire game.” The boy smiled back at her and said, “That’s because I understand that we’re pretending. I know that being humble and kind is important in real life too.” The moral of the story is that pretend games can teach us important lessons!
