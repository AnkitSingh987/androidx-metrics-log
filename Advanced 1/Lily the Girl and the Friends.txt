Title: Lily the Girl and the Friends

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She loved to play with her toys and have fun with her friends. Lily was a very happy girl. One day, her big sister wanted to put makeup on her face. But Lily did not want that. She said, "No, I refuse!" Lily did not like makeup. She wanted to play outside and be with her friends. So, Lily went outside and played with her friends. They ran, laughed, and had a great time. Lily was very happy. She knew she did not need makeup to have fun. And she lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there was a girl named Amy. She had a pretty velvet dress that she loved to wear. One day, her dress got a big rip. Amy was very sad. Her mom said she would fix it for her. In her room, Amy had a big box of toys. She liked to keep her toys organized. She knew that if she kept her toys neat, she could find her favorite toy when she wanted to play with it. Amy thought that maybe if she helped her mom with the house and kept everything neat, her mom would fix her dress faster. So, Amy started to clean the house. She put away her toys and helped her mom with the dishes. Soon, the house looked very tidy. Her mom saw how hard Amy worked and smiled. She knew that Amy was trying to be helpful so that she would fix her dress. Finally, Amy's mom took out her sewing kit and fixed the big rip in the velvet dress. Amy was so happy! She put on her pretty dress and twirled around. From that day on, Amy always kept her things organized and helped her mom with the house. And her mom knew that she could count on Amy to be a helpful girl.

Once upon a time, there was a little bird named Billy. Billy loved to sing. Every day, he would sit on a tree branch and sing happy songs. All the animals in the forest liked to listen to Billy's songs. One day, it was Billy's birthday. His friends, the animals, gave him a present. It was a harmless toy. Billy was very happy and thankful for his present. He played with his toy and shared it with all his friends. The animals learned that sharing made everyone happy. They started sharing their things with each other, too. The forest was full of happy animals who sang and played together. The moral of the story is that sharing with others brings happiness to everyone.

Sara and Tom were friends who liked to play outside. One day, they found a big hole in the ground. It was a cave. Sara wanted to go inside and see what was there. Tom was afraid of the dark and the bugs. "Come on, Tom, let's go in the cave. It will be fun," Sara said. "No, Sara, I don't want to go in the cave. It is scary and dirty," Tom said. Sara was envious of Tom. He had a new bike and a big toy car. She did not have those things. She thought he was lucky and brave. But now she saw that he was not brave at all. He was a chicken. "Fine, Tom, you can stay here. I will go in the cave by myself. Maybe I will find something cool and you will be sorry," Sara said. She walked into the cave and left Tom behind. Tom felt sad and lonely. He wanted to play with Sara, but he did not want to go in the cave. He wished he was brave like her. Sara went deeper into the cave. It was dark and cold. She heard strange noises and saw creepy shadows. She started to feel scared and sorry. She wished she had not refused to play with Tom. She wanted to go back to him. She turned around and ran to the exit. But she could not find it. She was lost in the cave. She cried and shouted for help. But no one heard her. Tom heard Sara's cries. He felt sorry for her. He wanted to help her. He was still afraid of the cave, but he loved his friend more. He took a flashlight and a rope and went into the cave. He followed Sara's voice and found her. He hugged her and said, "Don't worry, Sara, I am here. I will get you out of the cave." Sara was happy to see Tom. She thanked him and said, "I am sorry, Tom, I was mean to you. You are not a chicken. You are a hero." Tom smiled and said, "I am sorry, Sara, I was scared of the cave. You are not envious. You are adventurous." They tied the rope to a rock and climbed out of the cave. They were glad to be safe and together. They learned to respect each other's feelings and choices. They were still friends who liked to play outside. But they never went back to the cave.
