Title: Two and the Frog

Two frogs were hopping down the road when they suddenly heard a loud noise. One of them asked the other, "What was that?” The other one replied, “I don't know, let's go find out!” They hopped down the road and saw a copper bag in the middle of the road. One of the frogs cried out, “Incredible! Let's go see it up close.” The frogs hopped over to the bag and suddenly they heard the noise again. This time, they heard a voice say, “Freeze!” Both frogs paused, then the first frog said, “We'd better not, it's someone else's bag. That's not polite. We need to be respectful of other people's belongings.” The two frogs then hopped away and continued on their journey. Moral: The moral of the story is to be respectful of other people's belongings and don't take what isn't yours.

Once upon a time, there was a little squirrel named Sam. Sam loved to find acorns and hide them for later. One day, Sam found a big acorn under a tree. He was very glad. He thought, "I will hide this acorn and eat it when I am hungry." Sam dug a hole and put the acorn in the hole. Then he covered the hole with dirt. He looked around to make sure no one saw him. Sam said to himself, "I will remember this spot." He did not want to forget where he hid the acorn. The next day, Sam was hungry. He went back to the tree to find his acorn. But he could not remember where he hid it. Sam felt sad. He looked and looked but could not find the acorn. Then, he saw a little bird named Betty. Betty said, "I saw you hide the acorn. I can help you find it." Sam was glad. They looked together and found the acorn. From that day on, Sam and Betty were friends. Sam learned to repeat where he hid his acorns, so he would not forget again.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. She loved to play in her garden with her toys. One sunny day, Amy found a big pick in the garden. She thought it would be fun to dig a hole with it. So, she started to dig and dig. While digging, Amy found a box in the hole. She opened the box and saw many colorful stones inside. She had an idea to add the stones to her garden to make it look pretty. So, she put the stones all around her flowers. But soon, she started to feel uncomfortable. The stones were not soft to sit on, and they hurt her when she played. Amy's mom saw her looking sad and asked what was wrong. Amy told her mom about the stones and how they made her uncomfortable. Her mom smiled and said, "Sometimes, things that look pretty can make us feel bad. It is important to choose what makes us feel good and happy." Amy learned that it is not just about how things look, but how they make us feel. So, Amy and her mom removed the stones from the garden and replaced them with soft grass. Now, Amy could play happily in her garden again.

Once upon a time, there was a delicate woman. She lived in a small house near the woods. She loved to play with her ball in the yard. One sunny day, the woman went outside to play catch with her ball. She threw the ball high into the air and caught it with her hands. She laughed and clapped, happy to play her favorite game. As she played catch, a soft wind blew. It took the ball away from the woman. She ran after the ball, trying to catch it. The ball rolled into the woods, and the woman followed it. She found her ball and went back home, smiling. The delicate woman had a fun day playing catch.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to play outside. He loved to run, jump, and play with his toys. Tim also liked to search for bugs and rocks in the grass. Today, he found a pretty red ball. He was so happy! While playing with the ball, Tim accidentally kicked it too hard. The ball flew into the air and hit a big tree. The ball got a small tear and air started to come out. Tim felt guilty for breaking the pretty red ball. Tim went to his mom and told her about the ball. His mom smiled and said, "Don't worry, Tim. The ball was a surprise gift for you!" Tim was so surprised and happy. His mom fixed the tear with a special patch. Now, Tim could play with his new ball and be more careful.
