Title: The Tidy Butterfly and the Leaves

Once there was a tidy butterfly. She liked to fly around, collecting leaves in the garden. One day, she flew to a bright yellow flower. There, she saw a little girl. The butterfly flapped her wings and said, "Hello!" The little girl smiled and said, "Hi, butterfly!" She reached out her hand to touch it, but the butterfly flew away. She couldn't help it, she was too excited! The little girl giggled. She said, "Come back, butterfly! I want to play with you!" The butterfly flew back and stayed still. The little girl reached out her hand again and this time she was able to touch it. She said, "I'm glad you came back!" The butterfly smiled and flew away. She was happy to have made a new friend and had a great adventure that day.

Dave was a small boy with big dreams. Every day he liked to go to the beach and surf. He had been doing this for a long time and he thought he was the best! One day, Dave decided to suggest to his dad that he teach him how to surf better. His dad liked the idea, so he agreed. The two of them went surfing every day. His dad taught him different techniques and gave him pointers. Dave was very excited and felt like he was getting better with each wave. But then one day, on a particularly large wave, Dave became scared. He started to panic and his dad suggested he just “let the wave take you”. So Dave decided to try it. He stayed on the board and he surfed the wave. But it was too strong and he was caught in its power! He had to be rescued by lifeguards and was taken back to the beach. The moral of the story is that it’s important to think about the risks we take and be careful of the suggestions people make. Something that seems long and harmless could have dangerous results.

Lila and Tom were friends who liked to write. They had big crayons and paper. They wrote their names, animals, and shapes. They wrote on the floor, on the wall, and on the table. They had fun writing. One day, they saw something unusual outside. It was mist. Mist was like water, but in the air. It made everything look fuzzy and white. They wanted to write on the mist. They ran outside with their crayons. They tried to write on the mist, but it did not work. The crayons did not make any marks. The mist was too wet and soft. Lila said, "How can we write on the mist?" Tom said, "Maybe we can use our fingers." They tried to use their fingers, but it did not work either. The mist was too thin and moved away. They could not see their fingers. Lila said, "This is hard. I want to write on the mist." Tom said, "Me too. Maybe we can use something else." They looked around and saw a stick. They picked up the stick and tried to write on the mist with it. It worked! The stick made lines and shapes on the mist. They wrote their names, animals, and shapes. They wrote on the mist. Lila said, "This is fun. I like to write on the mist." Tom said, "Me too. The mist is unusual, but we can write on it." They smiled and kept writing on the mist. They had fun writing.

Once there was a little boy. He was 3 years old. His name was Steven. Steven had a towel. It was very soft. He liked it so much and carried it everywhere with him. One day Steven went to the park with his towel. He got to a big, soft seat and sat down on it. He put the soft towel on the seat and made himself comfortable. He stayed there, playing with his towel and enjoying the sun. After a while, he felt tired, so Steven curled up on the seat with the soft towel. His towel was so warm and cosy, and he quickly fell asleep. When he woke up, Steven couldn't believe how long he had been sleeping. He said goodbye to the seat and picked up his soft towel. He smiled as he walked back home, thinking about how comfortable the seat had been with his towel.
