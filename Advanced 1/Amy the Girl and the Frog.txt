Title: Amy the Girl and the Frog

One day, a girl named Amy went for a walk. She saw a small hut in the woods. The hut was made of sticks and leaves. Amy was curious, so she went to look inside. In the hut, Amy found a big pot of spicy soup. It smelled very good. A funny frog was sitting next to the pot. The frog said, "Hi Amy! Do you want to try my spicy soup?" Amy stared at the frog, surprised that it could talk. She said, "Yes, I would like to try your soup." Amy took a small sip of the spicy soup. Suddenly, she felt very strange. She looked down and saw that she had turned into a frog! The talking frog laughed and said, "Now we can be friends and live in the hut together!" Amy was not happy, but she decided to make the best of it. She and the talking frog became good friends and had many fun adventures together in the woods.

Once upon a time, in a tiny town, there lived a tidy cat named Tom. Tom loved to gather his toys and keep them nice and clean. One day, the weather was sunny and warm, so Tom went outside to play. While playing, Tom saw a big dog named Max. Max was not tidy. He liked to make a mess. Max saw Tom's toys and wanted to play too. Tom did not want Max to mess up his toys, so he said, "No, you cannot play with my toys." Max was sad but he wanted to be friends with Tom. So, Max decided to learn how to be tidy. He watched Tom and learned how to keep things clean. Soon, Max was just as tidy as Tom. Tom saw that Max had changed and let him play with his toys. They became best friends and played together every day. The town was happy to see them getting along. The moral of the story is that if we try to change for the better, we can make new friends and be happy.

Once upon a time there was a little girl called Beatrice. She was three years old and loved celebrating. One day, she found a shiny copper coin. She was so excited and decided to celebrate by going for a picnic. Beatrice ran to her small kitchen and grabbed a bottle of juice and some bread. She began walking to the park, humming quietly to herself. Suddenly, Beatrice heard a loud cry. She saw a dog trapped in a ditch. The dog was covered in mud and looked so sad. Beatrice quickly put down her bag and ran to save him. She carefully pulled the dog out and gave it a hug. Beatri ce then opened her bag and saw the copper coin was missing. Her shoulders drooped and she felt very sad. She realized she had to share the copper with the dog and couldn't celebrate anymore. The moral of this story is that sharing your blessings with others should be more important than celebrating. Even if it's just a small copper coin, it can still make a big difference.

It was raining outside, and Susie was sitting in her room, feeling quite bored. But then she had an idea! She ran to the window and opened it, and stuck her head out. A hedge in the garden was getting wet, and Susie had an idea. She turned to her mommy and asked, "Mommy, can I help the hedge?" Her mommy examined the hedge and said, "It looks like it needs some help. Of course you can. What do you want to do?" Susie smiled and said, "I want to give it a hug!" Her mommy smiled as well and said, "That's very compassionate of you." She helped Susie climb out of the window and over to the hedge. Susie hugged the hedge tightly, and her mommy said, "There you go! That should help make it feel better." Susie smiled and said, "Thank you, Mommy!" And soon, the hedge looked drier and happier.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue had a pretty skirt that she loved to wear. One day, Sue went for a walk in the park. She saw a small bird on the ground. The bird looked confused and lost. Sue started to worry about the bird. She wanted to help the bird find its way home. Sue walked closer to the bird, but the bird was scared and flew away. Sue felt sad and looked at her skirt. She got an idea. Sue took off her skirt and made a soft nest on the ground. She called the bird and waited. The confused bird saw the skirt and flew back to Sue. The bird landed on the skirt and felt safe. Sue was happy she could help the bird. From that day on, Sue and the bird became best friends.
