Title: A Lesson from My Manager

Category: Intermediate

Alex was twenty-one years old and very excited about his new job. He had just started working as a waiter at The Silver Spoon, a very popular and busy Italian restaurant in the city center. The restaurant was famous for its fresh pasta, loud atmosphere, and excellent service. Alex wanted to do a good job. He wanted to prove that he was fast, efficient, and professional.

His manager was a woman named Elena. Elena had worked in the restaurant business for fifteen years. She was strict, but fair. She walked quickly around the restaurant, watching everything with sharp eyes. She noticed if a glass was empty, if a customer looked unhappy, or if a table was dirty.

During his first week, Alex tried to do everything as fast as possible. He ran between the kitchen and the dining room. He carried three plates at once. He took orders quickly without writing everything down. He thought speed was the most important thing for a waiter.

One Friday evening, the restaurant was completely full. The noise of talking and laughing was very loud. People were waiting outside for a table. Alex was sweating, running from table to table.

He walked up to Table Number Seven. A nice couple was sitting there, looking at the menu.

“Good evening! Are you ready to order?” Alex asked quickly, holding his small notepad but not really looking at it.

“Yes,” the man said. “I will have the spaghetti carbonara, please.”

“Great choice,” Alex smiled. “And for you, madam?”

“I would like the seafood risotto,” the woman said. She looked directly at Alex. “But please, this is very important. I have a severe allergy to peanuts and tree nuts. Can you make absolutely sure that there are no nuts in the risotto, and that it is not cooked near any nuts?”

“Of course, no nuts at all. Seafood risotto, no nuts. I got it,” Alex said quickly. He wanted to move to the next table because Table Number Nine was waving at him. He didn’t write down the allergy on his notepad. He just kept it in his memory.

He ran to the computer system in the corner to send the order to the kitchen. He typed quickly: Table 7 - 1 Spaghetti Carbonara, 1 Seafood Risotto. In his rush, he completely forgot to press the special button that sent an "ALLERGY ALERT" message to the chefs.

Twenty minutes later, the kitchen bell rang. “Order for Table Seven ready!” the chef shouted.

Alex picked up the two hot plates and carried them carefully to the couple. “Here you are,” he said brightly. “Spaghetti carbonara, and seafood risotto. Enjoy your meal!”

He turned around to help another table. Suddenly, he felt a strong hand on his shoulder. It was Elena, the manager. Her face was pale, and her eyes were wide.

“Alex, come with me right now,” she said in a low, serious voice. She didn't wait for him to answer. She walked quickly back to Table Seven.

“Excuse me, madam,” Elena said to the woman, who was just picking up her fork. “Please, do not eat that risotto.”

The woman looked surprised and a little scared. “Why? What is wrong?”

Elena quickly picked up the plate of risotto. “I am so sorry, but there has been a mistake. The kitchen used a pesto sauce for the bread garnish on this plate, and that pesto contains walnuts. I will have a completely fresh meal made for you immediately in a safe pan.”

The woman gasped and put her hand to her chest. “Oh my goodness. Thank you. I told the young man about my severe nut allergy.”

Elena turned to look at Alex. Her look was fierce. “I apologize deeply on behalf of the restaurant, madam. We will take care of this right away, and your meal will be free of charge tonight.”

Elena walked back to the kitchen, carrying the plate. Alex followed her, his heart beating very fast. He felt terrible. His face was hot with embarrassment and fear.

When they were inside the kitchen, away from the customers, Elena put the plate down. She turned to Alex. She didn't shout, but her voice was very firm.

“Alex, what did you do?” she asked.

“I... I forgot to type the allergy into the computer,” Alex stammered. “She told me, but I was in such a hurry to get to the next table, I just forgot.”

Elena took a deep breath. “Alex, look at me. In this job, speed is good, but accuracy is everything. You almost sent a woman to the hospital tonight. A food allergy is not a small preference. It is a matter of life and death.”

“I am so sorry, Elena,” Alex said, looking down at the floor. “I just wanted to be fast.”

“Being fast means nothing if you do the wrong thing,” Elena explained. “When a customer tells you about an allergy, you must stop what you are doing. You write it down clearly. You type it into the computer carefully. And then, you go into the kitchen and speak to the chef personally to make sure they understand. You never rush an allergy order.”

“I understand,” Alex nodded. “I feel awful.”

“Feeling awful won't fix it,” Elena said. “Learning from it will. I saw you rushing around tonight. You are trying to do too many things at once. You don't need to run. You need to walk purposefully. You need to listen carefully to the customers. Do you know why I caught your mistake?”

Alex shook his head. “No.”

“Because I check the computer tickets,” Elena said. “I know Table Seven asked questions about the menu earlier. I saw you take their order very quickly without writing much down. When the ticket printed without an allergy warning, I felt something was wrong. So, I went to check the plate before she ate it.”

Elena placed her hand on his shoulder. It was a gentle gesture this time. “You are a smart young man, Alex. You have a lot of energy. But you must learn to control that energy. Take a breath. Write things down. Check your details. The restaurant will not fall down if a customer waits one extra minute for you to take their order correctly.”

“Thank you, Elena,” Alex said quietly. “I promise I will never make that mistake again.”

“Good,” she nodded. “Now, go back out there. Apologize to Table Seven yourself, pour them some fresh water, and tell them their new food will be ready in ten minutes.”

Alex did exactly as he was told. He went to the table, apologized sincerely, and made sure they were comfortable. For the rest of the evening, Alex changed his style. He stopped running. He walked calmly. When he took an order, he looked at the customer, listened carefully, and wrote everything down on his notepad. He repeated the order back to them.

It was a hard lesson to learn, but Alex never forgot it. He learned that taking responsibility for details is much more impressive than simply being fast. From that day on, he became one of the most reliable and careful waiters at The Silver Spoon.
