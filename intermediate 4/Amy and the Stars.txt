Category: Intermediate
Title: Amy and the Stars

Amy was a little girl who wanted to measure the dark. She walked outside and looked up at the night sky. She could see a few stars, but it was mostly dark. She wanted to measure how high the dark was. So she got a long piece of string and a ruler. She attached the end of the string to the ruler and held the other end in her hands. Then she carefully measured how high the dark was. She stretched the string up towards the sky and stretched till the string was fully stretched out. When she was finished, Amy was surprised to find out that the dark was very high. She had never seen anything so high before. She also noticed that the dark was full of stars, just like she had seen in the book her mom had read to her. Amy smiled. She found out that the dark was really high. She couldn't believe it. She decided that the next time she wanted to measure the dark, she was going to bring some friends and have a fun time together.

Once upon a time, there was a bright little girl named Lily. She loved to learn new things. One day, she wanted to learn how to draw a cat. So, she went to search for a tutor to teach her. Lily found a kind tutor named Mr. Fox. Mr. Fox said, "I can teach you, but you must be patient." Lily was excited and agreed to be patient. They started to practice drawing cats every day. One day, Lily felt sad because her cat didn't look good. Mr. Fox reminded her, "Remember to be patient." Lily kept trying, and soon her cat looked great! She thanked Mr. Fox for his help. The moral of the story is to be patient when learning new things.

Sara likes to write in her journal every day. She draws pictures of her family, her friends, and her toys. She also writes some words that she knows, like her name, cat, and love. She keeps her journal under her pillow, so no one can see it. One day, her big brother Tom finds her journal. He thinks it is funny to read what Sara writes. He laughs at her pictures and her words. He says, "Sara, you are so silly. You can't write. You only scribble. And look at this picture. Is this supposed to be me? I am not fat. You are fat." Sara hears Tom and runs to her room. She sees him holding her journal. She feels angry and sad. She says, "Tom, give me my journal. It is mine. You are mean. You scare me. You are not my brother. You are a monster." Tom sees Sara crying and feels sorry. He says, "Sara, I am sorry. I was just joking. I don't mean to scare you. You are not fat. You are cute. And your journal is nice. You can write and draw very well. You are smart. You are my sister. I love you." He gives her back her journal and hugs her. Sara stops crying and hugs him back. She says, "Tom, thank you. I am sorry too. You are not a monster. You are my brother. I love you too." They smile and play together. Sara shows Tom her journal and Tom shows Sara his comics. They share their stories and have fun. They are happy.

Tom and Lily were playing in the garden. They liked to water the flowers with the hose. But the hose was too long and heavy for them. They had an idea. "Let's use the faucet in the kitchen," Lily said. "It is easier to turn on and off." "OK, but we have to be careful," Tom said. "Mommy said we should not make a mess. Promise you will not splash water everywhere." "I promise," Lily said. "You promise too." They ran inside and found the faucet. They turned it on and filled a small bucket with water. Then they ran back outside with the bucket. They took turns pouring water on the flowers. They were having fun. But then Lily saw a big butterfly. She wanted to catch it. She dropped the bucket and ran after the butterfly. The bucket tipped over and water spilled on the ground. "Oops!" Lily said. "I forgot about the bucket." Tom saw the water and got worried. He ran inside and turned off the faucet. He hoped Mommy would not notice the wet floor. He was clever. He found a towel and dried the floor. He also dried the bucket and put it away. He ran back outside and joined Lily. They played with the butterfly for a while. Then they heard Mommy calling them. "Time for lunch, kids!" Mommy said. "Come inside and wash your hands." They ran inside and washed their hands. Mommy did not see the towel or the bucket. She smiled and hugged them. "You are such good kids," Mommy said. "You did not make a mess in the kitchen. I am proud of you." Tom and Lily smiled. They were happy. They did not tell Mommy about the faucet. They kept their promise.
