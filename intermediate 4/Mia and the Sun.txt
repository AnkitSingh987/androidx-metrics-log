Category: Intermediate
Title: Mia and the Sun

Mia likes to learn new things. She has a tutor who comes to her house every week. Her tutor is lovely. She teaches Mia how to read and write and count. Mia likes to show her tutor what she can do. One day, Mia and her tutor are playing with water and paint. They make colorful pictures on paper. Mia dips her brush in the water and then in the paint. She makes a big red circle on her paper. She smiles and says, "Look, I made a sun!" Her tutor smiles and says, "That's very good, Mia. You are very creative. What else can you make?" Mia thinks for a moment. She dips her brush in the water and then in the blue paint. She makes a wavy line under the sun. She says, "This is the sea. The sea is blue and wet." Her tutor nods and says, "That's right, Mia. The sea is blue and wet. And what is the opposite of wet?" Mia thinks harder. She remembers a word that her tutor taught her. She says, "The opposite of wet is dry. When something is wet, it has water on it. When something is dry, it has no water on it." Her tutor claps and says, "That's very clever, Mia. You are learning a lot. Can you show me something that is dry?" Mia looks around. She sees her paper with the sun and the sea. She touches it with her finger. It is still wet. She says, "My picture is not dry yet. It needs more time." Her tutor says, "That's true, Mia. Your picture is not dry yet. But it will be soon. Do you know what will make it dry?" Mia thinks again. She remembers another word that her tutor taught her. She says, "The sun will make it dry. The sun is hot and bright. It makes water go away." Her tutor says, "That's right, Mia. The sun will make it dry. You are very smart. Why don't we put your picture outside, where the sun can see it?" Mia likes the idea. She picks up her picture carefully. She follows her tutor outside. They find a sunny spot on the grass. They lay the picture down. Mia says, "Bye bye, water. Hello, sun. Please make my picture dry." Her tutor says, "That's a good way to say it, Mia. Now let's go back inside and play some more. Maybe we can make another picture later." Mia says, "Okay. I like to play with you. You are a lovely tutor." Her tutor says, "Thank you, Mia. You are a lovely student."

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lucy. She loved to draw with pastels. One day, she found a big, shiny pastel in her room. It was so pretty, and she couldn't wait to use it. Lucy started to rub the pastel on the paper, and something magical happened! A talking bird appeared on the paper. The bird said, "Hi, Lucy! I am Dizzy, the dizzy bird. I am here to help you draw." Lucy was so happy to have a new friend. She asked Dizzy, "What should we draw together?" Dizzy replied, "Let's draw a big, pretty tree with lots of flowers!" So, Lucy and Dizzy rubbed the pastel on the paper and drew a beautiful tree. After they finished the tree, Lucy and Dizzy drew more fun things, like a sun, clouds, and a rainbow. They had a great time drawing together. At the end of the day, Lucy said, "Thank you, Dizzy. I had so much fun today!" Dizzy smiled and said, "You're welcome, Lucy. I had fun too! Remember, I am always here to help you draw." And with that, Dizzy went back into the pastel. Lucy knew she would always have a magical friend to draw with, and she was very happy.

Once upon a time, there was a tough painter named Bob. Bob had his own painting studio, and every day, he would spin his paintbrushes and mix paints of all colours. Bob worked very hard, spending hours spinning his brushes. One day, a three-year-old boy named Joe, who liked to play in Bob's studio, asked him, "Why do you spin your paintbrushes, Bob?" Bob smiled. "It's very important. When I spin my paint brushes, it's like giving them a workout, so they will stay strong for all the painting I do." Joe was amazed, "Wow! You're so tough! Can I spin your brushes too?" Bob laughed, "Sure! Let's give it a try." Bob held Joe's hands as he spun the brush with him. Joe was very proud, and he said, "Bob, I'm a tough painter too! Just like you." Bob smiled. "Yes, Joe, you are quite a tough painter! Who knows, maybe someday you'll have your own painting studio just like me!"
