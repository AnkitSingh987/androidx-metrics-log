Title: Tim the Player and the Ball

Once upon a time, there was a player named Tim. Tim loved to play with his ball. He played with it every day. But one day, Tim saw a fake ball. It looked like his ball, but it was not real. Tim was sad because he did not want the fake ball. He wanted his real ball. He knew he had to value his real ball more. So, Tim looked and looked for his real ball. At last, he found his real ball under his bed. Tim was so happy! He played with his real ball all day long. From that day on, Tim always valued his real ball and never played with the fake one again.

Once upon a time, there was a big red umbrella named Umbi. Umbi liked to help people when it rained. One day, it rained a lot, so Umbi was very alert. Umbi knew that he had to keep people dry. Umbi saw a girl named Lily who needed help. She was wet from the rain. Umbi went to Lily and she held him. Now, Lily was dry and happy. They walked together in the rain, and Umbi kept her dry. Later, they saw a big puddle. Lily wanted to print her foot in the puddle. She put Umbi down, and jumped in the puddle. Splash! Lily laughed, and Umbi was happy to see her have fun. They went home, and Lily said, "Thank you, Umbi!" The big red umbrella smiled, knowing he had done a good job.

Once upon a time, there was a little horse named Horsy. Horsy lived in a stable with his friends. They liked to play and run around all day. One day, Horsy and his friends were playing a game. Horsy was running very fast and accidentally bumped into his friend, Cowy. Cowy fell down and started to cry. Horsy felt sorry and said, "I am sorry, Cowy. I did not mean to hurt you." Cowy replied, "It's okay, Horsy. I know you didn't mean it. Let's relax and sit down for a while." So, Horsy and Cowy sat down in the grass, and they talked and laughed. They were happy to be friends and enjoy the day together. After a while, Horsy and Cowy went back to the stable. They told their friends about their day and how they had learned to say sorry and relax. All the animals in the stable were happy to have such good friends, and they all lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there were two friends named Bob and Jack. Each day, Bob would take his cooler out to the park to have lunch. Whenever he did this, Jack would be so envious that he would just sit and watch. One day, Bob had taken something extra special to eat. He asked Jack if he wanted some, but Jack just continued to stare. That's when Bob said to Jack, “Why don't you come and have a taste?” “No”, replied Jack, “I don't have a cooler like yours.” “That's okay”, Bob said with a smile, “We can share my cooler. I'll even make a joke. Ready? It’ll be cooler together!" Jack laughed and agreed to have lunch with Bob from then on. They had the best lunches ever, and Bob and Jack were cooler together.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to the park. He loved to imagine he was a big, strong lion. Tim would run around the park, roaring like a lion. He felt happy when he played this game. Tim met a little girl named Sue. Sue liked to dance to the rhythm of the music. She would move her feet and clap her hands. She was having a lot of fun. Tim thought her dance was harmless and wanted to join her. "Can I dance with you?" Tim asked Sue. "Yes, let's dance together!" Sue said with a big smile. They danced and laughed together, enjoying their new friendship.

One day, a boy named Tim went to the store with his mom. They saw a long line of people waiting to buy toys. Tim saw a big, expensive toy car that he really wanted. He asked his mom, "Can I have that toy car, please?" His mom looked at the price and said, "No, Tim. That toy car is too expensive. We cannot buy it today." Tim was sad but he knew his mom was right. As they were leaving the store, Tim tripped and started to fall. His hands reached out to catch something. He grabbed the expensive toy car and it fell too. The toy car broke into pieces on the floor. The store owner was angry and made Tim's mom pay for the broken toy. They went home with no new toy and less money. Tim felt very sorry for what he had done.
