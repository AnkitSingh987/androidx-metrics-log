Category: Intermediate
Title: Fin the Fish and the Ball

Once upon a time, there was a little fish named Fin. Fin loved to swim in the big blue sea. One day, while swimming, Fin saw something new. It was a big red ball. Fin was very excited and wanted to touch it. Fin swam closer to the big red ball to touch it. But, when Fin touched the ball, it was very spicy! Fin's fin felt hot and he did not like it. Fin swam away fast, trying to find something to make his fin feel better. Fin saw his friend, a big green turtle named Tilly. Tilly knew what to do. She told Fin to swim in the cool water. Fin did as Tilly said and swam in the cool water. Soon, his fin felt better. Fin and Tilly swam together, happy to have helped each other. And Fin knew to be careful when touching new things in the sea.

Once upon a time, there was a colorful bird. The bird loved to fly and sing. It had many friends in the forest. One day, the bird saw a sad monkey. The monkey had a hurt arm. The bird wanted to help the monkey feel better. The bird gave the monkey a massage. The monkey felt happy and loved. The monkey's arm felt better too. They became good friends. The monkey learned to give massages too. They helped other animals in the forest feel better. The animals in the forest learned to help each other. They all felt happy and loved. The forest was a beautiful place to live. The moral of the story is to help others and share love.

Tom and Lily are in the car with Mom and Dad. They are going to the park to play. Tom is happy, but Lily is not. She hates the park. She hates the swings, the slides, and the sand. She wants to stay home and watch TV. "Look, Lily, there is a big billboard with a picture of a clown!" Mom says, pointing out the window. "Do you like clowns?" Lily shakes her head. She hates clowns too. They are scary and loud. She does not want to see them. "I like clowns!" Tom says. "They make funny faces and jokes. They are fun!" Lily does not answer. She looks away from the billboard. She sees a clear sky with white clouds. She wishes she could fly away like a bird. "Are we there yet?" Tom asks. He is excited to get to the park. "Almost, honey," Dad says. "Just a few more minutes." Lily sighs. She hopes the park will be closed. She hopes there will be no other kids. She hopes there will be no clowns. She hates the park. She hates everything.

One day, Amy was exploring in the woods. She noticed a quick, fierce animal. Scared, she stepped back and started to run. But then she heard a voice calling out. "Wait! Where are you going?" Amy looked back and saw a kind old man walking towards her. She stopped and waited. The man smiled and said, "Don't be scared. That was just a fox. See... it is gone now." Amy looked back and noticed the fox had run away. The old man said, "You should be careful when exploring in the woods. There are lots of quick and fierce animals here. Looks like you're safe now. Have a nice day!" Amy thanked the man and skipped off, looking forward to more exploring.

Timmy was in his backyard with his mom. He looked at the big blue sky, which was very clear and bright. "Mom, can I go surfing?" Timmy asked. "Well, Timmy," she said with a smile, "the water looks a little too thick for you right now. Maybe later when it's a bit thinner." Timmy frowned, and his mom gave him a hug. "Don't worry," she said, "you can still practice here on the grass. Maybe you can even make a surfboard out of a cardboard box." Timmy brightened up and nodded. He ran to the garage, grabbed a thick cardboard box, brought it to the backyard and started to make a surfboard. He was having lots of fun and his mom was encouraging him the whole time. Finally, Timmy's surfboard was ready. He took it to the beach and asked his mom if he could try it out. "Of course," she said with a smile, "just be careful!" Timmy got on the surfboard and paddled out into the water. His mom cheered for him as he rode the waves and had the time of his life.
