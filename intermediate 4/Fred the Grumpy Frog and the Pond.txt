Category: Intermediate
Title: Fred the Grumpy Frog and the Pond

Once upon a time, there was a grumpy frog named Fred. Fred lived in a small pond with other frogs. He was always sad and never liked to play with his friends. One day, a little bird named Betty flew to the pond. She saw Fred and asked, "Why are you so grumpy, Fred?" Fred said, "I want to jump very high, but I can't. I try and try, but I don't succeed." Betty wanted to help Fred. She said, "Don't give up, Fred! Let's try together." So, they practiced jumping all day. Fred jumped and jumped, and with each try, he got a little bit higher. Finally, Fred succeeded! He jumped so high and was very happy. He thanked Betty for helping him. From that day on, Fred was not grumpy anymore. He played with his friends and had fun every day.

Lily liked to collect yellow things. She had a big box full of yellow things. She had yellow balls, yellow flowers, yellow books, yellow hats and more. She was very happy with her yellow things. One day, she went to the park with her mom. She saw a boy playing with a yellow car. She wanted the yellow car. She ran to the boy and said, "Give me your yellow car. I collect yellow things." The boy was angry. He said, "No, this is my yellow car. You can't have it. Go away." Lily was sad. She said, "But I love yellow things. Yellow is the best color. You have a lot of other things. Why can't you share?" The boy said, "Because this is my favorite car. It is different from your things. It can move and make sounds. It is special to me. You can't take it." Lily and the boy started to cry. They both wanted the yellow car. They both thought it was the best thing. Mom saw them crying. She came to them and said, "What is wrong? Why are you crying?" Lily and the boy told her what happened. Mom said, "I see. You both like the yellow car. But you can't take something that belongs to someone else. That is not nice. You have to ask first. And you have to respect their answer. Maybe you can play together with the yellow car. You can take turns and share. That way, you can both have fun and be friends." Lily and the boy thought about it. They said, "Okay, we can try that. We can play together with the yellow car. We can share." Mom smiled. She said, "That's good. I'm proud of you. You are learning to be kind and fair. And you are learning that different things can be good too. Yellow is a nice color, but there are many other colors that are nice too. You can collect yellow things, but you can also enjoy other things. You can have your own things, but you can also share with others. That is how you grow and learn." Lily and the boy nodded. They said, "Thank you, mom. You are right. We can grow and learn. We can enjoy different things. We can be friends." They hugged mom and then hugged each other. They played with the yellow car and had a lot of fun. They also looked at each other's things and found some other things they liked. They learned to collect and share. They learned to be happy.

Once upon a time, there was a big, dry tree. It was near a gate. The gate was old and it would tremble when the wind blew. One day, a little boy named Tom went to the gate. He wanted to play near the tree. He saw the tree shake and heard the gate tremble. He was scared. But then, a little bird came out of the tree. The bird was making the tree shake and the gate tremble. Tom was not scared anymore. He played with the bird and they became friends.

Once upon a time, there was a patient named Joe. One day, Joe was feeling very curious and he had many questions. But suddenly, he realized that he was in a dangerous situation and he was scared. Joe quickly looked around and saw a policeman standing close by. Joe had a brave heart and he decided to ask the policeman some questions. "Officer, am I in danger?" he asked. The policeman smiled and said, "No, Joe, you're safe here. I'm here to make sure you're patient. So there's no need to worry." Joe was relieved and thanked the policeman. He was glad to know that he was safe and that the policeman was looking out for him. Joe smiled and said, "Thank you, officer." Then he went on with living his life, confident in the knowledge that he was safe and protected.
