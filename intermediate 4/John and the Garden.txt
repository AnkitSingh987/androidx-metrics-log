Category: Intermediate
Title: John and the Garden

John had left his recorder in the garden. He wanted to record the birds singing. John ran outside to get it. When he got to the garden, the recorder was wrapped up in a big brown paper. He tried to open it, but it was very hard to take off. John was getting frustrated. He looked for something to help him open the wrap, but he couldn't find anything. John decided to try one last time to take off the wrap with his hands. He pulled at it and pulled until it finally opened. John was so relieved and excited to get his recorder back. He quickly pressed the record button and started to record the beautiful songs of the birds.

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim was an honest boy who always told the truth. One day, Tim found a suit in his closet. It was a very nice suit. Tim wore the suit to a party. The suit made him feel very excite. All his friends liked his suit too. They played games and ate cake. The party was so much fun. At the end of the party, Tim's mom asked him if he had fun. Tim said, "Yes, I had a lot of fun in my new suit." His mom was happy to hear that. Tim went to bed feeling happy and proud of his honest heart.

Once upon a time, in a big green forest, there lived a panda named Pandy. Pandy was a good panda who loved to cook. Every day, Pandy would pick fresh bamboo from the forest to cook for his friends. One sunny day, Pandy's friend, Birdy, was feeling sad. Pandy wanted to help Birdy feel better. He thought, "I will cook Birdy's favorite food!" So, Pandy cooked a big bowl of tasty bamboo for Birdy. When Birdy saw the bowl, she felt happy and smiled. Birdy shared the yummy bamboo with her friends. All the animals in the forest enjoyed the food Pandy cooked. They all thanked Pandy for being a good friend. The forest was full of happy animals. The moral of the story is: when you do good things for others, it can make everyone happy.

Once upon a time, there was a small town. In the town, there was a big stack of blocks. The blocks were all colors: red, blue, yellow, and green. The people in the town liked to play with the blocks. They built tall towers and long bridges. One day, a big wind came and blew the blocks all around the town. The people were sad because the blocks were not in a stack anymore. They had to find all the blocks and put them back together. They looked under trees, behind houses, and in the grass. When they found all the blocks, they tried to put the stack back together. They put the blocks tight so they would not fall again. Then they all sat down and watched the stack. They were happy to see it settle and not move anymore. The people played with the blocks again and had lots of fun.

Once upon a time, there was a big fish named Fin. Fin loved to dive deep in the water. He had many friends who liked to play with him. They would all dive together and have lots of fun. One day, Fin met a new friend named Tim. Tim was a little crab who had a lot of pretty shells. Fin thought the shells were very nice. Tim said, "I will trade you a shell for a big dive with you." Fin was happy and said, "Yes, let's trade!" Fin and Tim had a great time diving and trading shells. Fin was very generous and gave Tim some of his own shells too. They played all day, and in the end, they were the best of friends. And they lived happily ever after.

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big box near a tree. The box has many letters on it. "What is this?" Anna asks. "Maybe it is a game," Ben says. "Let's go and see." They run to the box. They are eager to find out what is inside. They open the box. They see many envelopes with stamps and names. "These are letters," Anna says. "They are for people." "Who are they from?" Ben asks. "I don't know," Anna says. "Maybe they are from far away. Maybe they are from someone who loves them." They pick up some letters and look at them. They see pictures and words. They try to read them, but they are too hard. "I wish we could read them," Ben says. "Maybe they are stories." "Maybe we can make our own stories," Anna says. "We can use our imagination." They sit down and pretend to read the letters. They make up stories about the people who sent them and the people who received them. They laugh and have fun. They forget about the time. They do not see the mailman who comes to the park. He is looking for his box. He sees Anna and Ben with the letters. He is angry. "Hey, what are you doing?" he shouts. "Those are not your letters. Those are mine. Give them back." Anna and Ben are scared. They drop the letters and run away. They do not want to play with the box anymore. They go back to their homes. They are sad. They liked the letters. They liked the stories. They wonder if they will ever see them again.
