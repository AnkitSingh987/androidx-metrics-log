Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim and the Bowl

One day, a little boy named Tim was playing with his toy car on the floor. His mom was in the kitchen making a spicy soup. She called out to Tim, "Do you want to try some soup?" Tim didn't answer because he was so busy playing. He drove his car around the floor, making vroom-vroom noises. He didn't know that his mom had put a bowl of soup on the table for him to try. Later, Tim's dad came home from work. He saw the bowl of soup on the table and thought it was for him. He took a big spoonful and tasted it. "Wow, this is spicy!" he said. Tim looked up from his car and saw his dad eating the soup. He felt sad because he wanted to try it too. Tim learned that he should always answer when someone asks him a question. That way, he won't miss out on anything fun or yummy. And he also learned that sometimes, surprises can be spicy!

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a girl named Lily. She liked to play with her toy lion. The lion was fierce, but Lily was not scared. She knew it was just a toy. One day, Lily's friend, Tim, came over. He saw the fierce lion and got scared. Lily said, "Do not be scared, Tim. It is just a toy. Let me explain." She showed Tim that the lion was not real, and they both laughed. Now, Lily and Tim play with the fierce lion every day. They like to make it roar and run around the room. They have lots of fun, and they are not scared anymore.

Once upon a time, there were two friends called Harry and Joe. They were best pals and did everything together. One day, Harry wanted to show Joe something special. He took Joe to a secret field. There, he warned Joe that life could be difficult. Joe was curious and wanted to explore the field, but Harry warned him not to. But Joe didn't listen, and ran into the field. Suddenly, Joe's foot got stuck in a big hole, and he couldn't get out. Harry tried his best to help Joe, but he couldn't get him out. They were stuck for hours. Eventually, Harry had to go home and leave Joe behind. Sadly, Joe was never seen again.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there lived a little boy named Tim. Tim was restless. He could not sit still. One day, his mom gave him a job. She said, "Tim, please spread peanut butter on the bread." Tim said, "Okay, Mom!" He took the knife and spread the peanut butter on the bread. But he was not careful. He spread it too fast and too thick. The peanut butter fell on the floor. Tim's dog, Spot, saw the peanut butter on the floor. He ate it all up. But dogs should not eat peanut butter. Spot felt sick. Tim and his mom took Spot to the vet. The vet said, "Spot will be okay, but he needs to rest." The bad ending made Tim very sad. He learned that he should be careful and not be restless when doing things. The moral value of the story is to always be careful and take your time when doing tasks, especially when it involves others.

One day, a sweet little girl named Lily went for a walk with her mom. They were going to the park to play. Lily was very happy and excited. She put on her shoes and her mom helped her buckle her belt. At the park, Lily saw her friend, Tim. They wanted to play on the slide. But there was a big puddle of water at the bottom. Lily said, "Oh no! We can't slide. The water will make us wet!" Tim had an idea. He found a big stick and said, "We can use this to prevent getting wet!" They used the stick to push the water away from the slide. Now they could play and have fun without getting wet.

One day, a bird went soaring high in the sky. He had always been an amazing flyer, but he hadn't seen anything as beautiful as this. He felt like he could go forever! Suddenly, a terrible gust of wind hit him unexpectedly and sent the bird flying out of control. He quickly got caught in a huge, tangled knot of twigs and branches near the ground. He struggled and struggled to get away but it was no use. It was so disgusting being trapped there. The bird began to cry out for help. "Please help me," he pleaded. But no one ever came. So the bird stayed stuck in the knot, day after day, until eventually, he died. The bird never knew the freedom of the sky again.
