Category: Intermediate
Title: Tommy and the Lake

Tommy loved to visit his grandpa, who lived in a big house with many old things. Grandpa had a job that Tommy did not understand. He said he was a historian, and he studied ancient people and places. He had many books and pictures and maps and things that he found in faraway lands. One day, Tommy asked grandpa if he could see his job. Grandpa smiled and took him to his study, where he had a big desk and a computer and a shelf full of boxes. He opened one of the boxes and showed Tommy a shiny metal thing with a pointy end and a handle. "What is this, grandpa?" Tommy asked, curious. "This is a sword, Tommy. It is a very old and special sword. It belonged to a king who lived a long time ago, before you and me and even your daddy were born. He was a brave and good king, and he used this sword to fight bad people who wanted to hurt his people. He was very famous and loved by many. His name was Arthur." Grandpa explained. "Wow, grandpa, that is amazing! How did you find this sword?" Tommy asked, amazed. "Well, Tommy, that is a long and exciting story. I found this sword in a lake, in a country called England. There was a legend that said that only the true king could pull this sword out of a stone. But no one could do it, until Arthur came along. He was just a boy, like you, but he had a big heart and a strong will. He pulled the sword out of the stone, and became the king. He named the sword Excalibur, and it was his most precious thing. He kept it with him until he died, and then he asked his friend to throw it back into the lake, where it waited for me to find it." Grandpa explained. "That is so cool, grandpa! Can I hold the sword?" Tommy asked, eager. "Sure, Tommy, but be careful. It is very sharp and heavy. You have to hold it with both hands, and don't swing it around. It is not a toy, it is a treasure." Grandpa explained. Tommy nodded and took the sword from grandpa. He felt its weight and its shine. He felt like a king himself. He wondered what adventures he could have with the sword. He looked at grandpa and smiled. "Thank you, grandpa, for showing me your job. You are the best grandpa ever. I love you." Tommy said. "I love you too, Tommy. You are my best grandson ever. And maybe, one day, you will be a historian too, and find more ancient things and stories to share with me." Grandpa said.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a gym. In this gym, many kids loved to play and have fun. One day, a boy named Tim went to the gym to play with his friends. They all wanted to complete a big puzzle together. The puzzle had many pieces and was a picture of a happy sun. As they started to put the puzzle together, Tim noticed that he was not very good at it. He felt a little ashamed. He did not want his friends to see that he was having a hard time. So, Tim tried harder and harder to find the right pieces. After a while, Tim got better at the puzzle. With the help of his friends, they were able to complete the puzzle. The happy sun was now all together and shining brightly. Tim was no longer ashamed, and he learned that it was okay to ask for help. They all smiled and had a great day at the gym.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play with their toys in their room. But sometimes they want different toys and they fight. One day, Anna sees a big red car on the shelf. She wants to play with it. She climbs on a chair and reaches for the car. But Ben sees her and he wants the car too. He pulls the car from her hand and runs away. "Give it back!" Anna shouts. "It's my turn!" "No, it's mine!" Ben says. "You have the doll!" Anna is angry. She chases Ben around the room. They knock over the books, the lamp and the vase. They make a big mess. Mom hears the noise and comes to see. She sees the mess and the twins fighting. She is not happy. "What are you doing?" Mom asks. "You are in big trouble!" Anna and Ben stop fighting. They look at Mom. They feel sorry. "We are sorry, Mom," they say. "We wanted the car." Mom sighs. She picks up the car and gives it to them. "You can both play with the car," she says. "But you have to share and be nice. And you have to clean up this mess. Do you understand?" Anna and Ben nod. They say sorry again. They hug Mom. They hug each other. They take the car and play together. They spend the rest of the day having fun. They learn that sharing is easy and fighting is hard.
