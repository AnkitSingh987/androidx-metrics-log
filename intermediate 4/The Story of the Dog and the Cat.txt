Category: Intermediate
Title: The Story of the Dog and the Cat

One day, a big dog and a little cat were walking together. The big dog said, "I think we should go right." The little cat said, "No, I disagree. We should go left." They did not know what to do. Then, they saw a nice bird. The bird said, "Why not try both ways?" The big dog went right, and the little cat went left. They both walked and walked. Suddenly, the big dog found a big, yummy bone. The little cat found a soft, warm bed. They both felt grateful for the bird's help. The big dog and the little cat realized that it was good to try new things. They were happy and became better friends.

Once upon a time, a little dog named Max lived in a small town. Max loved to play outside in the summer. One day, Max saw a big dog named Sam. Max wanted to be friends with Sam. Max said, "Hi Sam, can I follow you and play?" Sam looked at Max and said, "Okay, but it might be difficult because I am big and you are small." Max was happy and followed Sam all day. They played in the park, ran in the grass, and had a lot of fun. At the end of the day, Max was tired but happy. He learned that even if something is difficult, it is important to try. Max and Sam became best friends and played together every summer day. And the moral of the story is: always try, even when it's difficult, and you might make a great friend.

Once upon a time there was a brown bear who lived in a wide forest. Every day he would go for a walk in the woods. One day, the bear heard music coming from the river. He followed the sound and saw a bright light. He stepped closer and saw the most beautiful sight. A tiny fairy was dancing in the light of the sun. Her dress was white like snow and her wings were gold. The bear stepped closer, curiosity in his eyes. "Hello," said the fairy. "What do you want to do?" The bear smiled and replied, "Dance with me." The bear and the fairy danced in a circle around the river. The bear spun and twirled around the fairy. It felt like the sun was dancing around them, sprinkling them with joy. After a few minutes, the fairy and the bear stopped dancing and waved goodbye to each other. And the brown bear walked home, a smile on his face.

One day, a little girl named Lucy saw a small flame in her room. She was scared and called her mom. "Mom, there is a flame in my room!" she cried. Her mom came quickly and put out the flame. She told Lucy, "We need to replace the flame with something safe." So, Lucy and her mom went to the store. They found a nice, organized shelf with many things on it. They saw a toy that looked like a flame, but it was not hot. It was just a toy. They bought the toy and went back home. They put the toy flame in Lucy's room. Lucy was very happy. But then, something unexpected happened. The toy flame started to talk! "Hello, Lucy!" it said. Lucy was surprised, but she liked her new talking toy. Now, she was not scared of the flame anymore.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. He loved to watch his favorite show on TV. One day, the cable was gone. Tim was very sad. He missed his show a lot. Tim's mom saw him looking impatient. She asked, "What's wrong, Tim?" Tim said, "I miss my show. The cable is gone." Tim's mom wanted to help him feel better. Tim's mom had an idea. She said, "Let's play a game while we wait for the cable to come back." Tim liked this idea. They played and had fun. Soon, the cable came back, and Tim could watch his show again. Tim was happy, and he learned that sometimes it's good to wait.

One day, a little boy named Tim found a big book. He wanted to read it. So, he sat down and started to read the book. He liked the book because it had many fun pictures. In the book, he saw a picture of a refrigerator. Tim was hungry, so he went to the kitchen. He opened the refrigerator and found a yummy apple. He ate the apple and felt happy. Then, Tim saw a picture of a dangerous lion in the book. He was scared, but his mom told him it was just a picture. Tim felt better and kept reading the book. He had a fun day reading the big book.
