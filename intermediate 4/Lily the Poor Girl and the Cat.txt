Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily the Poor Girl and the Cat

Once upon a time, there was a poor little girl named Lily. Lily had a big umbrella. She loved her umbrella very much. It was red and blue, and she took it everywhere she went. One day, Lily went to the park with her umbrella. She saw a cat stuck in a tree. The cat was scared. Lily wanted to help the cat. So, she opened her umbrella and held it under the tree. She told the cat, "Jump on the umbrella, and I will catch you." The cat watched Lily and her umbrella. It was still scared, but it knew Lily wanted to help. The cat jumped onto the umbrella. Lily caught the cat in her umbrella, and they both fell down on the soft grass. Lily and the cat were happy. They played in the park all day. From that day on, Lily and the cat were best friends. They always took the umbrella with them, so they could play and have fun together.

One day, a white cat named Snow was very tired. She wanted to sleep. So, she found a row of soft pillows to lie down on. Snow was so happy and said, "This is the best place to sleep!" While Snow was sleeping, a little girl named Lily found her. Lily saw Snow and said, "Oh, what a pretty white cat! I want to play with you!" Lily picked up Snow and started to play with her. Snow woke up and was surprised. She did not expect to be picked up! But Snow saw Lily's happy face and decided to play with her too. They played all day and became best friends. In the end, they both slept on the row of soft pillows, happy and tired.

One day, a poor bird named Tim could not whistle. He was sad because all his friends could whistle. Tim wanted to whistle too. He tried and tried, but he just could not do it. Tim met a cat named Sam. Sam said, "I can help you whistle, but you need a passport." Tim asked, "What is a passport?" Sam told him, "A passport is a special paper that lets you go to new places." Tim and Sam went to find a passport. They found one under a big tree. Tim was happy. Now he could go to the place where he could learn to whistle. They went to the place and met a wise old owl. The wise old owl taught Tim how to whistle. Tim practiced and practiced. Soon, he could whistle just like his friends. Tim was very happy. He thanked Sam and the wise old owl for helping him. Now, Tim could whistle and play with his friends.

One day, a big whale swam in the deep blue sea. The whale saw a pretty flower that was about to bloom. The whale was so happy to see the flower. He knew that when it bloomed, it would be very pretty. "Hello, little flower," said the whale. "I can't wait to see you bloom!" The flower smiled at the whale. It was excited to bloom too. But it needed some help. The flower told the whale that it was very thirsty. The whale wanted to help the flower. He swam to the top of the water and took a big breath. Then, he sprayed water from his blowhole onto the flower. The flower drank the water and started to grow. The flower grew and grew, and soon, it was ready to bloom. The whale watched as the flower bloomed into a beautiful, bright color. But then, the whale saw that the flower was spoiled. It had too much water and its petals were falling off. "Oh no, little flower!" said the whale. "I'm so sorry I gave you too much water." The flower smiled at the whale and said, "It's okay, big whale. Thank you for helping me. I still got to bloom, and I was happy to meet you." The whale smiled and swam away, happy to have met the flower too.

Tom and Mia went to the zoo with their mom. They wanted to see the animals and learn new things. Mom said they could choose one animal to observe closely and draw a picture of it. Tom and Mia looked at the lions, the monkeys, the giraffes, and the penguins. They were all very interesting, but they wanted to find something more original. Then they saw the otters. The otters were swimming, playing, and sliding in the water. They looked very happy and funny. "Can we observe the otters, mom?" Tom asked. "Sure, let's go closer and watch them," mom said. She gave them each a paper and a crayon. Tom and Mia sat on a bench and observed the otters. They noticed how they had brown fur, long tails, and small ears. They saw how they used their paws to hold fish and shells. They heard how they made squeaky noises to communicate. "Look, that one is doing a trick!" Mia said, pointing at an otter that was spinning in the air. "Wow, that's amazing!" Tom said. He tried to draw the otter on his paper, but it was too fast. Mia decided to draw a different otter, one that was resting on a rock. She drew its shape, its fur, and its eyes. She was very proud of her picture. "Mom, look at my picture!" Mia said, showing her mom. "That's very good, Mia. You observed the otter very well," mom said. "Thank you, mom. What about Tom's picture?" Mia asked. Tom looked at his paper. He had only drawn a circle and some lines. He felt sad. "I'm sorry, mom. I couldn't draw the otter. It was too hard," Tom said. "That's okay, Tom. You tried your best. And you know what? Your picture is very original. No one else has drawn an otter like that," mom said. Tom smiled. He realized that his picture was special, just like the otter. He showed it to Mia and the otters. They all squeaked in approval.
