Category: Intermediate
Title: Bob the Smart Bunny and the Forest

Once upon a time, there was a smart bunny named Bob. Bob liked to dance and play with his friends in the forest. One day, Bob found some corn near his home. He thought, "I will save this corn for a special day." The next day, the sun was shining and all the animals were happy. Bob remembered the corn he found. He had an idea. Bob told his friends, "Let's have a big dance party and share the corn!" Bob and his friends worked together to set up the party. They put the corn in the middle of the forest where everyone could see it. When everything was ready, the music started to play. All the animals danced and laughed together. They ate the corn and had a great time. In the end, Bob's smart idea made everyone happy.

One day, a hunter went out into the forest. He was very eager to find something interesting, so he dashed around looking everywhere. Suddenly, the hunter noticed a bunny, and he smiled. The bunny smiled back and hopped away. The hunter kept walking, and soon he found a golden squirrel. When the hunter smiled at it, the squirrel chattered and ran into a tree. The hunter laughed and kept looking around. Soon, the hunter came across some wild foxes. They were all sitting in the sun together when the hunter arrived. He smiled at them, and they all started barking excitedly. The hunter laughed and moved on. When the hunter finished his hunt in the forest, he was very happy. He smiled with pride at all the creatures that he had encountered, and he was excited to go back out again.

Sara and Ben are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big green slide. Sara wants to go on the slide, but Ben is scared. "Come on, Ben, it is fun!" Sara says. "You can go with me." "No, thank you. It is too high. I might fall." Ben says. He shakes his head. Sara thinks of a clever idea. She sees a small green ball on the grass. She picks it up and offers it to Ben. "Look, Ben, I have a green ball. Do you want to play with it?" Sara says. "We can throw it and catch it." Ben likes the green ball. He smiles and nods. "Yes, please. I like the green ball. It is soft and round." Ben says. He takes the ball from Sara. Sara and Ben play with the green ball. They have fun. Sara throws the ball high in the air. Ben catches it with his hands. They laugh and clap. "See, Ben, you are not scared of high things. You can catch the ball." Sara says. "Maybe you can try the slide too." Ben looks at the slide. He sees other children going on it. They look happy. He thinks maybe he can do it too. "OK, Sara, I will try the slide. But you have to go with me." Ben says. He gives the ball back to Sara. Sara and Ben hold hands. They walk to the slide. They climb the steps. They sit on the slide. They slide down together. They feel the wind on their faces. They scream and giggle. They reach the bottom. They jump off the slide. They hug each other. "Wow, that was fun! I did it!" Ben says. He is proud of himself. "You did it, Ben! You are brave!" Sara says. She is happy for him. Sara and Ben are friends. They like to play in the park. They like the green slide and the green ball. They are clever and kind. They have a good day.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there lived a stubborn girl named Lily. She loved to count everything around her. One day, she went to the market with her mom. Lily saw a big pile of peppers. She wanted to count them all. "Mom, can I count the peppers?" Lily asked. Her mom said, "Yes, but be quick. We need to go home soon." Lily started counting the peppers, "One, two, three, four..." She was very happy. A little boy named Tim saw Lily counting the peppers. He was curious and wanted to help. "Hi, I'm Tim. Can I count with you?" he asked. Lily said, "Yes, let's count together!" They continued counting the peppers until they reached the last one. "Twenty!" they shouted together. Lily and Tim became good friends. They loved counting things together. From that day on, they counted everything they saw - from flowers in the park to stars in the sky. Lily was not as stubborn anymore, and she learned that counting was more fun with a friend.
