Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Sock

One day, a little boy named Tim found a smelly sock. He did not like the smell, so he decided to rush and put it in the wash. As he ran, he saw a label on the sock. It had a funny picture on it. Tim laughed at the picture and wanted to show it to his mom. Tim showed his mom the label on the smelly sock. His mom laughed too and said it was a silly picture. She told Tim that they should wash the sock, so it would not be smelly anymore. Tim liked that idea and helped his mom put the sock in the wash. After the sock was clean, Tim and his mom looked at the label again. The funny picture was still there, but the sock was not smelly anymore. Tim was happy that the sock was clean and he could laugh at the funny label without the bad smell.

Once upon a time, there was a curious little cat named Kitty. Kitty loved to explore and play all day. One day, she saw a group of birds playing in the park. Kitty wanted to join them. Kitty walked up to the group and said, "Hi, can I play with you?" The birds looked at Kitty and said, "Okay, let's play!" They all started to play and have fun together. As they were playing, a big dog came running towards them. The birds got scared and yelled, "Stop!" The dog stopped and looked at the group. Kitty, being curious, asked the dog, "Do you want to play too?" The dog smiled and said, "Yes, I do!" So, Kitty, the birds, and the dog all played happily together.

One day, there were two boys. They were best friends. They played together all the time. But then, something happened. They had an argument and they split up. They argued over a toy they both wanted and they yelled at each other. The law says that friends should not fight. It was bad to argue. The boys felt very guilty. They both decided that it was time to make up. They hugged each other and promised to never fight again. They stayed friends forever.

Tom and Lily were playing in the old barn. They liked to pretend they were explorers, finding treasures and secrets. They climbed on the hay bales, crawled under the planks, and peeked through the cracks. They saw many things: a rusty bike, a broken doll, a shiny tin can. But they also saw many cobwebs. Cobwebs were sticky and icky. They made Tom and Lily sneeze and itch. They did not like cobwebs. They tried to avoid them, but sometimes they touched them by mistake. "Yuck!" Tom said, as he wiped a cobweb from his face. "I hate cobwebs. They are everywhere." "Me too," Lily agreed. "They are gross. Let's get rid of them." They decided to unite and work together. They found a broom and a stick. They used them to sweep and poke the cobwebs. They made a big pile of cobwebs in the corner. They felt proud and happy. But then they heard a voice. A small, sad voice. "Please, stop. You are hurting us." Tom and Lily looked around. They saw a spider on the wall. It was looking at them with big, wet eyes. "Who are you?" Tom asked. "I am Sam. And these are my friends. We live in the cobwebs. They are our homes. You are destroying them." Tom and Lily felt ashamed. They did not know that the cobwebs were homes for spiders. They did not mean to hurt them. They felt sorry. "We are sorry, Sam," Lily said. "We did not know. We were just playing." "Can you please stop?" Sam asked. "We have nowhere else to go. We are harmless. We only eat flies and bugs. We do not bother you." Tom and Lily nodded. They put down the broom and the stick. They looked at the pile of cobwebs. They felt bad. "Is there anything we can do to help?" Tom asked. Sam thought for a moment. He had an idea. "Maybe you can help us rebuild our homes. You can use the cobwebs you swept. You can make new shapes and patterns. You can be creative. We will be grateful." Tom and Lily liked the idea. They thought it sounded fun. They agreed to help. They picked up the cobwebs and started to make new homes for the spiders. They made circles and stars and hearts. They made letters and numbers and animals. They made a big, beautiful web. The spiders were happy. They thanked Tom and Lily. They invited them to visit anytime. They became friends. Tom and Lily learned a lesson. They learned that cobwebs were not just sticky and icky. They learned that they were homes for spiders. They learned that they should not judge things by how they look. They learned that they should be kind and respectful to all living things. They smiled and hugged. They felt good. They had united with the spiders. They had made a difference.
