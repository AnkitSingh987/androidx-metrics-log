Category: Intermediate
Title: Amy the Girl and the Squirrel

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. She loved to count. One day, she went to the park with her mom. They saw a big tree and Amy wanted to count the leaves. While Amy was counting, she got lost. She was frightened and didn't know where the exit was. She started to cry. A kind squirrel saw Amy and wanted to help her. The squirrel said, "Don't cry, little girl. I will help you find the exit." The squirrel and Amy walked together. They found the exit and Amy saw her mom waiting for her. Amy was happy and hugged the squirrel. She thanked the squirrel for helping her. The squirrel smiled and said, "Always stay close to your mom, and you won't get lost." Amy learned a lesson that day. She knew it was important to stay close to her mom and not wander away. From then on, Amy always stayed close to her mom when they went to the park.

One day, a boy named Tim went to play outside. He saw a big cross on the ground. He wanted to examine it. So, he went close to it and looked at it very carefully. As he was looking at the cross, a small bird came near him. The bird said, "Hello, Tim! What are you doing?" Tim replied, "I am examining this cross on the ground. I don't know who made it." The bird told Tim, "Oh, a bad dog made it. He was digging here to hide his bone." Tim said, "Thank you, bird. I will be careful not to step on it." Tim and the bird became friends. They played together every day and had lots of fun. They both promised to be good and help each other always.

Paula was a little girl who believed in the power of fun. Every day she ran out of the house and into the garden, excited to experience something new. She loved the sun and the fresh air, and the lively critters that she met in the meadows nearby. One day, a little bee flew in front of Paula. It was the most charming bee she had ever seen. Paula could not believe it. She almost stopped and said something, but the bee flew away too quickly. "That bee had a big smile for me," Paula said to her mom. Her mom smiled and said, "Yes, Paulie, there are mysterious and wonderful creatures in the world. We just have to believe!" Paula felt very happy. She ran around the garden, imagining that everywhere she went, she saw something beautiful. Now Paula knew that she could always believe in the power of fun.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to the park. He wore his red sweater. It was a cold day. Tim saw his friend, Sue. Sue had a toy car. The toy car could speed very fast. "Wow! Your car is fast!" said Tim. "Can I play with it?" "Sure!" said Sue. "But my car is fake. It is not a real car." Tim and Sue played with the fake car. They made it speed around the park. They had lots of fun. They laughed and played all day. Tim was happy he wore his red sweater, because it kept him warm.

Jimmy was driving along the road in his car when he heard a loud noise. He remembered his dad telling him that cars need oil to run, so he looked in his car and saw that it was empty. He stopped at the nearest garage to get some oil. When he arrived, he saw a man selling oil. "Excuse me," said Jimmy, "I need some oil for my car." The man smiled. "I think I have some fast oil that your car will like," he said. Jimmy bought the oil and quickly raced back to his car. He remembered his dad's instructions and filled up the car with the oil. As he started the engine, the car purred like a big cat. He was so happy, he drove home as fast as he could!

Once upon a time, there was a big red post box. It lived on a street where all the people come to send their letters. The post box was careful not to let any letters fall out. One sunny day, a little bird came to the post box. The bird was very careful not to touch the letters. It sat on top of the post box and sang a happy song. The people who came to send their letters liked the bird's song. But one day, something unexpected happened. The bird did not come to the post box. The people were sad. They missed the bird's song. Then, a big cat came to the post box. The cat was careful not to touch the letters too. The cat started to sing a happy song just like the bird. The people were surprised and happy again. They liked the cat's song too. From that day on, the cat and the bird took turns to come and sing at the post box. The people loved to send their letters and listen to the happy songs.
