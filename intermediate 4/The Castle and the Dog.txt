Category: Intermediate
Title: The Castle and the Dog

Once upon a time, there was a big castle. In the castle, there lived a wealthy king. The king had a sharp knife that he loved very much. One day, he said to his friend, "I will show you my sharp knife." The king and his friend went to the room with the sharp knife. But when they got there, the knife was gone! The king was very sad. His friend said, "We will find your sharp knife." They looked all around the castle, but they could not find the sharp knife. The king was very sad. But then, they heard a noise. It was the king's dog! The dog had the sharp knife in its mouth. The dog had taken the knife to play with it. The king was happy to have his sharp knife back. He said to his friend, "Thank you for helping me find my sharp knife." They all played together and had a fun day. And the king learned to put his sharp knife in a safe place, so his dog could not take it again.

Once upon a time, there was a wise grandma who had a special puzzle. Every time grandma wanted to fix it, she found a way to solve it. Every time she finished the puzzle, she would look around and ask, “What other puzzles do I need to fix?” One day, grandma asked her little granddaughter if she wanted to join her. Grandma explained that it was a special puzzle and it would teach her something important. Grandma was sure her granddaughter would enjoy it. The little girl was excited to play. She looked at the puzzle and figured it out quickly. It was clear to her how to solve it. Grandma was very proud of her for understanding the puzzle so quickly. She smiled and said, “You are wise! You solved the puzzle just like I did.” Grandma gave the granddaughter a big hug and said, “You don’t always have to solve a puzzle the same way. Sometimes, different solutions can be found if you think outside the box. Be wise and always keep an open mind.” The moral of the story is that it’s important to be wise and think outside the box when you’re trying to solve a puzzle. A wise person doesn’t always have to use the same solutions. With imagination and creativity, they can find new ways to fix things.

One day, a little boy named Tim saw a big, red jeep. He liked the jeep very much. He wanted to show his friend, so he pointed at the jeep and said, "Look, Sam! A big red jeep!" Sam looked at the jeep and smiled. But he saw that the jeep had a flat tire. He looked troubled. "Oh no, Tim! The jeep has a flat tire. It can't go." Tim and Sam wanted to help the jeep. They found a man who could fix the tire. The man said, "Thank you, boys, for telling me. I will fix the jeep now." The boys were happy that they could help the jeep. They watched the man fix the tire. After the tire was fixed, the jeep could go again. Tim and Sam waved goodbye to the man and the jeep as they drove away.

One day, a girl named Sue was playing in her room. She heard a noise outside. She looked out the window and saw a big truck arrive. The truck had a big box for her. Sue was very happy. Sue's mom said, "The box has a telephone for you." Sue looked at the telephone. It was fine and shiny. Sue wanted to play with the telephone, but her brother Tom wanted to play with it too. They both wanted the telephone. Sue said, "I want to play with the telephone!" Tom said, "No, I want to play with it!" They were both upset. Sue's mom came into the room. She said, "You can both play with the telephone. Take turns and share." Sue and Tom listened to their mom. They took turns playing with the fine telephone. They were both happy and had fun playing together. The conflict was over, and they learned to share.
