Category: Intermediate
Title: Kelli the Girl and the Garden

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Kelli. She had a mom and a dad who loved her very much. One day, Kelli was playing in the garden when a fierce storm came in. Kelli was so scared of the lightning that lit up the sky and the loud thunder that shook the ground. Kelli ran inside and hugged her mom. Her mom smiled and told her, "Don't worry, Kelli. Everything will be ok. We just need to forgive the storm and be strong." Kelli didn't understand very well, but she held her mom tightly. Mom looked outside and said, "See, the rain is calming down now. And the lightning isn't so fierce anymore." Kelli smiled and peered out the window. She saw the gentle breeze and the beautiful raindrops. She knew her mom was right - it was time to forgive. The storm eventually passed and Kelli was happy again. She smiled and laughed with her parents, glad that the fierce storm was now over.

One day, Ben and his mom went to the zoo. Ben loved to see the animals, but his favorite was the tiger. He wanted to carry a toy tiger with him, but his mom said no. She said the toy was too big and heavy, and he might lose it or drop it. Ben was sad, but he listened to his mom. He saw the tiger in a big cage, and he waved at it. The tiger looked at him and growled. Ben was not scared, he thought the tiger was saying hello. He asked his mom if he could talk to the tiger. His mom said yes, but he had to be quiet and respectful. Ben went closer to the cage, and said, "Hi, tiger. You are very big and strong. I like you a lot. Do you like me?" The tiger stared at him, and then nodded. Ben was happy, and said, "Can I be your friend? Can I carry you?" The tiger shook his head, and said, "No, little boy. You are too small and weak. You cannot carry me. I am too heavy and fierce. You would hurt yourself or me." Ben was surprised, and said, "But I want to carry you. You are so soft and furry. You look like my toy tiger, but bigger and better. Please, let me carry you." The tiger sighed, and said, "Fine, little boy. If you really want to carry me, I will let you. But you have to be careful and gentle. And you have to promise to put me back in my cage when you are done." Ben was excited, and said, "Thank you, tiger. I promise. I will be the best carrier ever." He reached his arms through the bars, and tried to lift the tiger. But the tiger was too big and heavy, and Ben could not move him. He strained and grunted, but he only managed to pull the tiger's tail. The tiger roared, and said, "Ow, little boy. That hurts. Stop pulling my tail. You cannot carry me. You are too small and weak. I told you so." Ben let go of the tiger's tail, and felt ashamed. He realized he was wrong, and he had been rude and foolish. He said, "I'm sorry, tiger. You were right. I cannot carry you. You are too big and strong. I was silly and mean. Please, forgive me." The tiger looked at him, and said, "It's okay, little boy. I forgive you. You were curious and brave. But you have to learn to listen and respect. Not everyone wants to be carried. And not everyone can be carried. You have to be happy with what you have, and what you can do." Ben nodded, and said, "Thank you, tiger. You are very wise and kind. I have learned my lesson. I will be happy with my toy tiger, and what I can do. And I will be your friend, but I will not try to carry you again." The tiger smiled, and said, "You are welcome, little boy. I am glad you have learned your lesson. And I will be your friend, too. But you have to remember, I am not a toy. I am a real tiger. And real tigers belong in the wild, not in cages." Ben understood, and said, "I know, tiger. I wish you could be free, and live in the forest. Maybe one day, you will. Until then, I will visit you, and talk to you, and wave at you. But I will not bother you, or pull your tail, or try to carry you. Goodbye, tiger. I hope you have a good day." The tiger said, "Goodbye, little boy. I hope you have a good day, too. And thank you for being my friend." Ben and his mom left the zoo, and Ben hugged his toy tiger. He was happy, and he was not ashamed anymore. He had made a new friend, and he had learned a new thing. He had learned to carry his own weight.
