Title: Learning to Spend Carefully

Category: Intermediate

Maya was a vibrant twenty-three-year-old woman who loved the excitement of city life. She worked as an assistant in a busy advertising agency. She earned a reasonable salary, but money always seemed to slip through her fingers like water. Maya loved the thrill of buying new things. She followed many fashion influencers on social media, and she constantly ordered clothes from fast-fashion websites. Her small bedroom was overflowing with colorful dresses, high heels, and accessories, many of which still had the price tags attached.

In contrast, her roommate, Chloe, was completely different. Chloe was a teacher. She earned less money than Maya, but she always seemed calm and financially secure. Chloe was a minimalist. Her bedroom was neat, with only a few high-quality items of clothing in her closet. She cooked her meals at home, rarely bought things online, and always carefully compared prices before making a purchase.

Maya often teased Chloe about her lifestyle. "You are too strict with yourself, Chloe!" Maya would say, opening another package that had arrived in the mail. "You work hard. You should treat yourself sometimes. Look at these boots! They were on sale for only sixty dollars."

Chloe would just smile gently and reply, "I do treat myself, Maya. But I treat myself to things that give me lasting joy, not just a quick thrill. And being debt-free is the best treat of all."

Maya did not understand. For her, spending money was a way to relax after a stressful day at the office. If she felt sad, she bought a new lipstick. If she felt happy, she celebrated by ordering expensive sushi. She lived entirely in the present moment, completely ignoring the future.

This careless behavior continued until the end of a particularly expensive month. It was a Tuesday evening, and Chloe was sitting at the kitchen table with a calculator and a pile of bills.
"Hey, Maya," Chloe called out. "The utility bills arrived today. The electricity and water bills are higher than usual because it is winter. Your half is one hundred and ten dollars. Can you transfer the money to me by tomorrow?"

Maya was lying on the sofa, scrolling through a clothing app on her phone. "Sure, no problem," she said casually. She opened her banking app to make the transfer.
When the screen loaded, Maya stopped breathing. Her account balance was seventeen dollars.
She stared at the screen, hoping it was a mistake. She refreshed the page, but the number remained the same. Seventeen dollars. She had spent all her money, and she still had five days until her next paycheck.

A feeling of cold panic washed over her. She quickly checked her recent transactions. There were countless small charges: five dollars for coffee, fifteen dollars for a quick lunch, forty dollars for a sweater she bought on a whim, thirty dollars for a taxi because she was too lazy to take the bus. It was a continuous stream of thoughtless spending.

Her heart began to beat fast. She felt incredibly embarrassed. She was an adult with a good job, yet she could not even pay her share of the basic household bills. She slowly walked to the kitchen table.

"Chloe," Maya began, her voice trembling slightly. "I... I have a problem."
Chloe looked up, noticing Maya’s pale face. "What is wrong? Are you okay?"
Maya looked at the floor. "I can't pay the bill. I looked at my account, and I only have seventeen dollars left. I spent everything. I am so sorry."

Chloe did not look angry. Instead, she looked sympathetic. She put down her pen. "It is okay, Maya. I can cover your half of the bill this month, and you can pay me back when you get your salary. But we need to talk about this. This is not the first time you have run out of money before payday."

Maya sat down, feeling tears in her eyes. "I know. I am terrible with money. I don't even know where it goes. I just tap my card, and it disappears. I feel like I am always stressed about money, but I can't stop shopping."

"You are not terrible with money," Chloe said kindly. "You just haven't learned how to control your habits yet. Shopping gives you a quick rush of dopamine, like a sugar high. But the happiness doesn't last. I can help you, if you want."
Maya nodded enthusiastically. "Please. I am tired of living like this. I want to change."

"Alright," Chloe said. "We will start with a simple rule. It is called the 'twenty-four-hour rule.' From now on, whenever you want to buy something that is not absolutely necessary—like clothes, gadgets, or decorations—you are not allowed to buy it immediately. You must wait exactly twenty-four hours."

Maya frowned. "Wait? But what if it is on sale and the sale ends?"
"If it is meant to be, it will still be there," Chloe insisted. "The purpose of the rule is to stop impulse buying. Usually, after twenty-four hours, the emotional urge to buy the item disappears, and you realize you don't actually need it. Will you try it?"
"I will try it," Maya promised.

The true test came the following weekend. Maya and Chloe went to a large shopping mall to buy a birthday gift for a mutual friend. While walking past a famous cosmetics store, Maya saw a beautiful, limited-edition eyeshadow palette displayed in the window. It cost forty-five dollars.
Her automatic reaction was to walk inside and buy it. She even took her wallet out of her bag. But Chloe gently touched her arm. "Remember the rule, Maya. Twenty-four hours."

Maya sighed, put her wallet away, and walked past the store. That evening, she thought about the eyeshadow palette. She imagined how nice the colors would look. But the next morning, when she woke up, the desire had faded. She looked in her bathroom drawer and realized she already owned three similar palettes that she barely used. She didn't buy the new one. She had just saved forty-five dollars by doing absolutely nothing.

Encouraged by this small victory, Maya agreed to try Chloe's next challenge: the "no-spend weekend." The goal was to go an entire weekend without spending a single dollar.

On Saturday, instead of going to a cafe for breakfast, Maya and Chloe made pancakes at home using ingredients they already had. In the afternoon, instead of going shopping, they went for a long walk in the city park and visited a free art gallery. In the evening, instead of ordering expensive takeout, they cooked a vegetable stir-fry and watched a movie on television.

By Sunday night, Maya felt a strange sense of peace. She had not bought anything, but she had enjoyed a wonderful weekend. She realized that she did not need to spend money to have fun or feel relaxed. The time spent with her friend and the simple activities were just as satisfying, and they didn't leave her feeling guilty.

Over the next few months, Maya’s financial situation changed completely. She deleted the shopping apps from her phone. She started tracking her expenses and bringing her lunch to the office. When she received her salary, she immediately transferred a portion of it to her savings account before doing anything else.

One evening, Chloe was sitting at the kitchen table, looking at the bills again. "The internet bill is due, Maya. Your half is thirty dollars."
Maya smiled. She opened her banking app, confidently tapped a few buttons, and transferred the money. Her balance was healthy, and her savings were growing.
"Sent," Maya said proudly.
Chloe smiled back. "You have learned to spend carefully, Maya. I am really proud of you."
Maya looked around her room. It was much cleaner now, as she had sold many of her unworn clothes. She felt lighter, happier, and finally in control of her life. She realized that true financial freedom was not about buying whatever she wanted; it was about wanting only what she truly needed.
