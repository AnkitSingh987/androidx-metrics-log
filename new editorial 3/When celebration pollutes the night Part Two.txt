Title: When celebration pollutes the night Part Two

When celebration pollutes the night

This should be regarded as a question for serious research rather than an established cause-and-effect conclusion. Perhaps this is why I feel we need to speak about aesthetic pollution  -  an emerging dimension of environmental degradation in which excessive artificial light, sound and visual clutter diminish the beauty, harmony and sensory character of a place. Electricity itself is not the problem; indeed, it is one of the greatest achievements of modern civilisation and an essential foundation for rural development.

Reliable electricity has transformed education, healthcare, communication, agriculture, small industries and countless aspects of everyday life. The question is not whether villages should be illuminated, but how much illumination is necessary and where it becomes excessive. Progress should provide light for learning, safety and productive activity not turn every social celebration into a competition of brightness and noise.

What we need is not less progress, but more responsible progress that respects both human aspirations and the natural environment. I sometimes feel an irresistible urge to stand in the middle of my village and speak aloud  -  not against electricity, prosperity or celebration, but against our failure to recognise what we are losing. The answer is not to return to a life without electricity, nor to reject progress.

It is to learn restraint. A truly beautiful celebration need not conquer the night with dazzling lights and deafening sound. Perhaps the real measure of progress is whether we can celebrate joyfully while leaving the stars visible, the night peaceful, the environment healthy and the minds of our children illuminated by something more enduring than artificial light: knowledge, imagination and wisdom.

By DR DEBAPRIYA MUKHERJEE (The writer is former Senior Scientist, Central Pollution Contral Board)
