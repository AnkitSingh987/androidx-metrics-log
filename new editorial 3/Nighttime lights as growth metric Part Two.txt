Title: Nighttime lights as growth metric Part Two

Nighttime lights as growth metric

Researchers often adjust for these factors using complementary data, such as electricity consumption or industrial surveys. Policy Recommendations To harness the full potential of NTL data, India should integrate it more systematically into official statistics. The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) could use NTL as a complementary indicator in quarterly State GDP assessments, reducing reliance on delayed surveys.

State governments can employ NTL to identify infrastructure gaps (such as poorly lit, low-activity districts) and target investments in roads, electricity, or industrial parks. Development programmes, whether on urban planning, rural electrification, or disaster relief, can benefit from NTL-based monitoring to ensure resources reach lagging regions. For a country as vast and diverse as India, no single statistic can capture the full picture of growth.

But nighttime lights provide a new lens as it is independent, dynamic, and spatially detailed. They do not replace official data, but they enrich it, adding real-time insights into how the economy is evolving on the ground. From space, India's growth story is literally written in lights: revealing not just how much the economy is expanding, but where prosperity is spreading, and where it still needs to reach.
