Title: Indias deeptech democracy for inclusive AI Part One

India's deep-tech democracy for inclusive AI

The country is democratising deep tech through shared compute, open data, and decentralised talent Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as the defining force in shaping economies, transforming societies, and redrawing the boundaries of human potential in the world. Yet for much of the Global South, access to this transformative power has remained elusive. The AI revolution, concentrated in a handful of advanced economies, risks widening the global inequality of opportunity, creating a new digital divide between those who build and those who merely consume technology.

India is changing that story. Through the IndiaAI Mission launched in 2024, the country is charting a bold new course, one that treats AI not as a luxury or proprietary tool but as a public good. India is now pioneering a public model of innovation which can be called "deep-tech democracy" anchored in state stewardship, shared infrastructure, and social inclusion.

By democratising access to compute, data, and talent, India is transforming AI from an exclusive enterprise to a public utility for all, proving that technology leadership can be both sovereign and inclusive. The world today faces a striking imbalance in AI capacity. A handful of nations control nearly all the advanced computing resources and proprietary datasets, giving them disproportionate power over how algorithms are trained and whose realities they represent.

For the Global South, this imbalance has meant dependence on technology, infrastructure, skills and often, governance models shaped elsewhere. India's response is to democratise deep tech itself. It rests on three interlocking pillars: shared compute, open data, and decentralised talent. Through this model, India has created a digital public infrastructure that allows every student, start-up, and researcher to participate in shaping the next wave of technological progress.

Public model of innovation The IndiaAI Mission, backed by an allocation of over ₹10,000 crore, translates India's vision of deep-tech democracy into action through an interconnected network of national initiatives designed to make AI accessible, inclusive, and locally relevant. Over 38,000 GPUs are being deployed to build India's first national AI compute grid, giving start-ups and researchers affordable access to the computing power hitherto limited to global tech giants. The AI Kosh platform is unlocking over 360 curated, non-personal datasets across sectors such as agriculture, health, climate, and governance ensuring that Indian innovators work with data grounded in local realities rather than imported assumptions.

Complementing this is Digital India Bhashini, a multilingual AI framework that offers speech and translation tools in 22 Indian languages, supported by Project Vaani's 150,000-hour speech dataset, making digital access truly language-neutral. To ensure that innovation is not confined to India's metros, the FutureSkills and AI Labs initiative is establishing 570 labs in Tier 2 and 3 cities and extending 13,500 AI fellowships to students across disciplines launching a grassroots movement that democratises deep-tech learning and turns India's diversity into its greatest strength. Underlying these initiatives is the Samaj, Sarkar, Bazaar philosophy, a uniquely Indian approach that unites society, government, and market to ensure technology serves people first.

This collaborative model ensures that AI solutions are not only powerful, but also ethical, transparent, and locally relevant. The India-AI Impact Summit 2026 to be held in February 2026 in New Delhi embodies this transformative vision on a global stage. The summit brings together governments, industry leaders, and innovators from across the Global South to deliberate on one fundamental question: how can AI drive inclusive growth with equity, equality and accessibility?
