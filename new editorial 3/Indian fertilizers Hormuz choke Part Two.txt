Title: Indian fertilizers Hormuz choke Part Two

Indian fertilizers' Hormuz choke

Logistics disruptions further aggravate the problem. Rising war-risk insurance premiums, vessel shortages, and rerouting of ships in Gulf waters increase freight costs and delay shipments. For bulk commodities like fertilizers, even modest increases in shipping costs can significantly raise the landed price of imports, thereby expanding India's fertilizer import bill during prolonged geopolitical turbulence.

Although India currently maintains fertilizer stocks exceeding two months of national consumption, providing a short-term buffer against immediate shortages, the principal risk lies in price escalation rather than physical scarcity. Higher international prices can increase the government's subsidy burden and strain fiscal balances while raising agricultural input costs and triggering broader cost-push inflation in food prices. Way Forward To address these vulnerabilities, India must adopt a multi-layered strategy to strengthen fertilizer supply chain resilience.

First, diversification of import sources is essential by diversifying procurement beyond the Gulf region, particularly from Russia, Canada, and South America. This can reduce excessive dependence on a single geopolitical corridor. Second, India should intensify efforts to secure overseas mineral resources, especially phosphate rock and potash reserves.

Correspondingly, strategic investments and joint ventures with resource-rich countries can ensure long-term supply stability while reducing exposure to volatile global markets. Third, strengthening domestic production capacity remains crucial and continued investment in gas-based urea plants, modernization of fertilizer manufacturing infrastructure, and improved energy efficiency can enhance supply stability. Simultaneously, promoting nano-fertilizers and balanced nutrient use can reduce dependence on imported DAP and potash.

Moreover, encouraging pulses cultivation such as moong and urad after the rabi harvest can serve dual purposes, reducing pulses imports while enriching soils through natural nitrogen fixation and green manure, thereby lowering chemical fertilizer use. Fourth, maintaining strategic fertilizer reserves will cushion the domestic market from global disruptions. Higher inventories during geopolitical uncertainty can stabilize prices and ensure timely supply during key agricultural seasons.

Finally, improving maritime logistics resilience is vital. Diversified shipping routes, stronger coordination between fertilizer importers and shipping companies, and mobilizing efforts of its P&I club for unrestricted insurance services can help sustain fertilizer flows during disruptions. While current stocks provide short-term relief, the Hormuz crisis highlights deeper structural vulnerabilities.

Strengthening diversification, domestic capacity, resource partnerships, and logistics resilience will be essential for safeguarding India's agricultural productivity and long-term food security. By Ram Singh The writer is Professor & Head, IIFT New Delhi
