Title: Digital governance is transformative Part Two

Digital governance is transformative

No more waiting endlessly to find out what is actually going on after filing the request; the dataset is already here. Yet, enhancing trust in the system is not a cakewalk; so much remains to be done. Every one of these systems runs on personal data  -  loads of it. And this is a double-edged sword. Personal data in irresponsible hands turns into a surveillance system.

Services delivered at speed is what people crave for, but they also want corruption eliminated speedily, alongside freedom and real privacy from the state's prying eyes. Ensured by independent regulatory supervision, the DPDP (Digital Personal Data Protection) framework is not a cosmetic initiative. It is the line between a state that is merely better at watching and the one that is accountable.

Get this wrong, and transparency starts being something citizens fear instead of something they trust. The age of omnipotent AI The Prime Minister has called AI 'All Inclusive', which now seems to be the one-stop solution for all ills, capable of detecting fraudulent welfare claims in real time, flagging anomalies in procurement before they become scandals and forecasting where corruption is likely to surface next. In a country where land disputes choke the judicial ecosystem, blockchain can make land records genuinely tamper-proof.

Satellite imagery now verifies whether a public project is still stuck in file or has seen the light of the day. The very crux of digital governance is to ensure, humans no longer override the system quietly. Nothing works, however, if the algorithms making these calls are black boxes  -  becoming the new opaque discretion; unaccountable and unexplainable.

The recent incident of Claude getting unauthorised access to systems of three organisations during internal cybersecurity testing, unfolds a more capable AI posing serious threat dimensions. The 20th century's most transformative governance innovation was the Right to Information. The defining governance idea of the 21st century is DPI, a framework designed so intelligently that citizens need not ask for information because transparency is woven into the system itself.

Technology got India this far. But only institutional discipline will ensure the dream of Viksit Bharat becomes a reality. By Amitabh Ranjan & Sarvjeet Kaur Chatrath Ranjan is Registrar, Indian Institute of Public Administration, New Delhi, and Chatrath is Management Lead at the Canberra Business School.
