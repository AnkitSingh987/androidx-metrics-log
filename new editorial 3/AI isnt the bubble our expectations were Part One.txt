Title: AI isnt the bubble our expectations were Part One

AI isn't the bubble, our expectations were

AI's profound values lies in making humans more capable, not replacing them The mood around AI has flipped with startling speed. Is it the next great bubble? The recent sharp Nasdaq sell-off, fuelled by growing unease over inflated AI expectations, has turned private doubts into public panic.

Add to that a blunt MIT study declaring enterprise generative AI largely underwhelming, and the verdict seems damning: AI promised transformation and delivered mere PPT slides. But that conclusion mistakes market jitters and poor implementation for technological failure. It risks discarding one of humanity's most powerful tools just as we are learning how not to misuse it.

What failed was not AI, but the way we framed it. We treated AI as a magic replacement for human labour rather than as a system designed to elevate human capability. We expected machines to think for us, instead of helping us think better. The backlash, then, is not a signal to abandon AI but an invitation to reimagine its role.

AI's most profound value does not lie in automation alone but in augmentation. To make humans more capable, more self-aware, and paradoxically, more human. Humanising AI Social psychologist Heidi Grant elegantly explains how AI can make us more human: "AI can help us become who we already want to be, by exposing blind spots, we are notoriously bad at seeing ourselves." In many enterprises, AI systems now analyse 100% of calls, emails, and interactions… something no manager could ever do.

They identify behavioural patterns, communication gaps, and moments of friction. Employees can rehearse difficult conversations in low-stakes simulations, receive personalised feedback, and iterate without reputational risk. This reframes AI as a mirror and a coach, not as a decision-maker. The data supports this direction.

Organisational AI adoption rose from roughly 20% in 2017 to 75% now in some form. Gartner projects that by 2030, all IT work will involve AI, but only 25% will be fully automated. The remaining 75% will be performed by humans working with AI. The future, in other words, is a hybrid workforce with humans and AI agents working together.

Being "more human" doesn't mean resisting technology but embracing uniquely human disciplines such as empathy, judgement, ethics, meaning, and social connection. To a certain extent, AI is playing the support role in these domains when deployed right. A study among 5000 customer service agents showed that GenAI increased productivity by 15%, with the biggest gains among less-experienced employees.

Quality of service also improved along with speed. Ai didn't replace empathy here; it helped humans deliver it better. Aiding businesses A survey of small businesses this year revealed that over three-quarters of marketers believed AI improved the quality of their work and helped them compete with much larger firms.

AI acted as an equaliser here, amplifying human creativity rather than erasing it. AI can handle the cognitive heavy lifting  -  retrieval, synthesis, pattern recognition  -  so humans can focus on what actually matters: relationships and judgement. AI has the potential to restore work that is more humane.

The broader societal implications of human-centred AI are even more compelling. In education, adaptive AI tutors personalise learning paths, identify conceptual gaps, and allow students to learn at their own pace. The same "safe simulation" logic that helps professionals practise tough conversations can help students build confidence without fear of failure.
