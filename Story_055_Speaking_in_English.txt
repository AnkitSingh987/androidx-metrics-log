Title: Speaking in English
Category: Beginner

My name is Carlos. I am eleven years old. I live in Spain, but I am learning English at school. I like the English language. I can read English books if they are simple. I can write English sentences in my notebook. But I have one big problem. I am very nervous when I have to speak English. 

When my teacher, Mr. Davies, asks me a question, my mind goes blank. I know the words in my head, but they do not come out of my mouth. My tongue feels heavy. I worry about making a mistake with the grammar. I worry that my pronunciation is bad. So, I usually just say 'yes' or 'no' very quietly. 

Today is Thursday. Mr. Davies has a surprise for our class. 
"Listen carefully, everyone," Mr. Davies says. "Today, we have a special visitor. Her name is Mrs. Smith. She is a teacher from London. She does not speak Spanish. She only speaks English."
A tall woman with blonde hair walks into the classroom. She smiles warmly at us. 
"Good morning, class," she says in a bright voice. 
"Good morning," the class replies together. 

Mr. Davies continues, "Mrs. Smith wants to talk to you. I want each of you to stand up, say your name, and tell her one thing you like to do. You must do this in English."

My heart drops into my stomach. Speak English? In front of the whole class? To a real person from London? I feel very hot. My hands start to sweat. I look at my desk. I want to become invisible. 

Mr. Davies starts at the front row. 
A girl named Maria stands up first. She is very confident. 
"Hello. My name is Maria," she says clearly. "I like to play basketball."
"Very nice to meet you, Maria," Mrs. Smith says with a smile. "Basketball is a great sport."

The next boy stands up. "My name is Pablo. I like to read books."
"Excellent, Pablo," the visitor replies. 

The line gets closer and closer to my desk. I am in the third row. My breathing is fast. I repeat my sentence in my head again and again. 'My name is Carlos. I like to play football. My name is Carlos. I like to play football.'
It is a very simple sentence, but I am terrified I will forget it. 

Finally, the boy next to me sits down. It is my turn. 
Mr. Davies looks at me and nods. "Go ahead, Carlos."
I push my chair back slowly. My legs feel like jelly, but I stand up. I look at Mrs. Smith. She has kind blue eyes. She is looking at me, waiting. 

I open my mouth. My throat is dry. 
"H-hello," I stammer. The word comes out very quietly. 
I clear my throat and try again. I speak a little louder. 
"Hello. My name is Carlos," I say. 
I stop. My mind goes completely blank. What do I like to do? I cannot remember the English word for football. Is it soccer? Is it football? Panic rises in my chest. 

Mrs. Smith smiles gently. She leans forward a little bit. 
"Hello, Carlos," she says softly. "It is very nice to meet you. Do you like sports?"
She gives me a helpful question. Her slow, clear English calms me down. I take a deep breath. The words come back to my mind. 

"Yes," I answer. My voice is stronger now. "I like sports. I like to play football with my friends."
I did it! I spoke a whole sentence. 
"Football is fantastic," Mrs. Smith says. "What is your favorite team?"
This is an easy question. I know the answer immediately. 
"My favorite team is Real Madrid," I say proudly. I do not feel nervous anymore. I am just having a conversation. 

"Wonderful," Mrs. Smith says. "Thank you, Carlos."
I sit down. I let out a huge breath. I feel a massive sense of relief. 

The rest of the students finish their turns. Then Mrs. Smith tells us a funny story about her dog in London. I listen carefully, and I understand most of what she says. 

When the bell rings, the class is over. I pack my bag. 
Mr. Davies walks past my desk. 
"Well done today, Carlos," he says. "You spoke very well. You just needed a little confidence."
"Thank you, Mr. Davies," I say. 

I walk out of the school feeling very happy. Speaking in a new language is scary, but it is also very exciting. When Mrs. Smith understood me, I felt like I climbed a high mountain. I know my English is not perfect. I know I will make mistakes in the future. But I am not afraid anymore. Next time someone speaks to me in English, I will not look at the floor. I will stand tall, look them in the eye, and speak.
