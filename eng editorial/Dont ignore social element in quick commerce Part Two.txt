Title: Dont ignore social element in quick commerce Part Two

Don't ignore social element in quick commerce

Additionally, one in four accidents involves riders, resulting in ₹6,000 crore in annual claims attributed to algorithmic pressure. Furthermore, PM 2.5 exposure, 2.2 times the urban average, equals a lifetime healthcare burden of ₹12,000 crore for one lakh riders. Third, the quick commerce industry is a high-speed engine where the digital platform is the sleek exterior, but the human riders are the internal pistons.

While the engine's speed (market growth) is doubling every year, the oil (wages and safety protections) required to keep those pistons from burning out has yet to be topped up. If the engine continues to accelerate without lubricating the moving parts, it risks a total mechanical failure  -  not of the technology, but of the social contract. Fourth, regulations alone cannot change a cultural mindset that treats speed as an unquestionable virtue.

As quick commerce expands and ultrafast delivery becomes routine, responsibility cannot rest only with platforms or the state. Consumers, too, must adjust their expectations, which they reinforce with every 10-minute promise. The fundamental transformation India needs is not in cutting delivery times but acknowledging the people behind this convenience -  the riders whose safety, health, and livelihoods absorb the hidden costs of speed.

If the growing political attention to gig work triggers even a slight shift in how such labour is perceived and valued, it could help build an urban economy where efficiency is no longer achieved by pushing risk and pressure onto the most vulnerable.
