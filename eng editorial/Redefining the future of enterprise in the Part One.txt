Redefining the future of enterprise in the age of Agentic AI

In the age of Agentic AI GCC comeback will not be about returning to old strengths; it will be about building new ones If AI agents can run operations end-to-end from anywhere, are GCCs still essential? It's a question top of mind for many enterprise leaders as Agentic AI moves from concept to capability. For decades, India's Global Capability Centres (GCCs) have been the quiet powerhouses that have fuelled global enterprise success.

Once insourcing hubs built for efficiency, they evolved into multi-functional centres delivering expertise in IT, finance, customer service and R&D. Now, with Agentic AI poised to reshape what is possible, GCCs stand at the edge of their most significant reinvention yet. This new wave of AI builds on the creativity and insight generation of Generative AI, adding the ability to autonomously execute complex, multi-step tasks, make decisions and adapt in real time.

By 2028, 33 per cent% of enterprise software is expected to embed Agentic AI, and by 2029, it could resolve 80% of customer service issues, cutting operational costs by 30 per cent. The potential is immense, but the real story is not about GCCs becoming obsolete. It is about how they will evolve from process engines into strategic command centres that orchestrate, govern, innovate and amplify AI's impact across the enterprise.

And that evolution starts with one critical foundation: data. Data readiness: The foundation of Agentic AI transformation For Agentic AI to deliver its potential, data must be ready for machines to understand. Historically, enterprise data was organised mainly for human reporting and analysis. In the Agentic era, it will need to be structured, harmonised and enriched so AI systems can process, reason and act autonomously.

GCCs are in a prime position to make this happen. They can build and maintain hybrid data ecosystems that serve both people and AI, embed compliance across regions and keep data pipelines tuned for real-time decision-making. This capability will support AI operations and strengthen oversight, risk control and organisational resilience.

Consider healthcare: Agentic AI systems could handle up to 80 per cent of routine documentation, freeing clinicians to focus on patient care. GCCs will ensure these systems follow regulations, protect data integrity and deliver significant results. GCCs will evolve into strategic leaders, using automation to drive decisions, shape policies, and enable human - AI collaboration for lasting impact.

Cognitive advantage and innovation Once the data foundation is in place, GCCs will deliver true cognitive advantage by bringing together deep domain expertise and contextual intelligence including the organization processes. In these centres, hubs of excellence will take shape in prompt engineering, agent training and synthetic data generation. They will build capabilities that go far beyond the limits of traditional outsourcing, giving enterprises the AI muscle they need while ensuring oversight and human judgment remain central.

Take finance: GCCs could run AI systems that handle up to 90 per cent of transactional processes, giving teams the freedom to focus on forecasting, strategy and innovation. In customer service, intelligent platforms could resolve most queries, while sensitive cases get the human attention they truly need. Equally important will be the role of GCCs as innovation hubs.

Within these centres, teams will bring hybrid human - AI workflows to life, designing, testing and scaling everything. From execution engines to strategic command centres With the foundation built and capabilities in place, GCCs will have the opportunity to move from process delivery to strategic orchestration. In the Agentic era, they will act as command centres for AI-driven enterprises: directing intelligent agent networks, setting priorities, tracking performance and ensuring every action supports business goals.
