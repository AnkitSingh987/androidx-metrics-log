Title: Why attention should be listed as a Part Three

Why attention should be listed as a competency in higher education

Every institution audits what it spends. None audits what it asks people to remember. Third, a weekly reset. What helped, what crept back, one tweak. IQAC measures compliance; it could measure coherence. A "stopped doing log" is as valuable as an action-taken report, and considerably rarer. Fourth, let us model it.

If the Vice Chancellor's office sends WhatsApp messages at 11.00 pm, no policy will save the institution. Culture is what leaders do on ordinary Tuesdays. The Panchatantra's Brahmin killed the mongoose that saved his child because he acted on the first thing he saw. The monkeys watered the garden to death because they measured what was easy to measure.

We do something similar with attention such as counting hours logged, lectures delivered, portals launched, credits accumulated, while losing the capacity that makes any of it mean anything. The good news is that the fix is subtractive and subtraction is cheap. No budget line, no new committee. Let us start with three things to reduce this attention issue: First, turn off 10 notifications.

Pick the ones you never once acted on. Second, cancel one meeting such as the recurring one nobody defends. Watch what fails to collapse. Third, protect one hour. Same time daily, door shut, phone in another room. The garden, it turns out, mostly needs to be left alone to grow. By Ram Kumar Kakani Views expressed are personal The writer is the Vice-Chancellor of RV University, Karnataka.
