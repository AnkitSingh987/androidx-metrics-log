Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Window

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play with his blocks. He would stack them up high and then knock them down. One day, Tim was trying to build a big tower with his blocks, but he was getting frustrated. The blocks kept falling down and he didn't know what to do. As Tim stared at the blocks, something unexpected happened. A little bird flew into his room through the open window. The bird looked at the blocks, then looked at Tim, and then back at the blocks. To Tim's surprise, the bird started to pick up the blocks with its beak and stack them on top of each other. Tim watched as the bird built a tall tower with the blocks. He was so amazed by what the bird could do. When the tower was finished, the bird looked at Tim and gave a happy chirp. Tim clapped and laughed as the bird flew back out the window. From that day on, Tim knew that even when things were hard, he just had to keep trying and maybe, just maybe, something unexpected would happen to help him.

Tom and Anna like to play with their cart. They put their toys in the cart and push it around the yard. Sometimes they pretend the cart is a car or a train or a boat. One day, Tom and Anna find a big box in the garage. They open the box and see many new toys. They are very happy and want to play with them. They take some toys out of the box and put them in their cart. "Look, Anna, this is a robot. It can talk and move. And this is a doll. She has a dress and a hat. And this is a ball. It can bounce and roll. These are the best toys ever!" Tom says. "Yes, Tom, they are very nice. But we have to share them. We can't take all of them. There are other kids who want to play with them too. These toys are not ours. They belong to the church. Mom and Dad put them in the box to give them away. They are for the poor kids who have no toys. That is very important." Anna says. "But I want to keep them. They are so fun. We can play with them all day. We don't have to give them away. We can hide them in our room. No one will know. Please, Anna, let's keep them." Tom says. "No, Tom, that is wrong. That is not nice. That is not fair. The poor kids need the toys more than we do. We have many toys already. We have to be kind and generous. We have to help others. That is what Mom and Dad teach us. That is what God wants us to do. Please, Tom, let's give them away." Anna says. Tom and Anna argue for a while. They both want to play with the new toys, but they have different ideas. Tom wants to keep them and gain more fun. Anna wants to give them away and gain more love. They don't know what to do. They are sad and angry. Then they hear Mom and Dad calling them. They are ready to go to the church. They have to put the toys back in the box and load it in the car. They have to make a choice. What will they do?

Lily and Ben were playing in the backyard. They liked to pretend they were knights and dragons. Lily had a stick that she used as a weapon. Ben had a cardboard box that he used as a shield. "Roar!" Ben said, jumping out from behind a bush. "I am a fierce dragon and I will eat you!" "Oh no!" Lily said, waving her stick. "I am a brave knight and I will stop you!" They ran around the yard, chasing and dodging each other. They had a lot of fun. But then, Lily hit Ben's box too hard and it broke. Ben fell down and scraped his knee. He started to cry. "Ow! Ow! Ow!" Ben said, holding his knee. "You hurt me, Lily! That was not a nice thing to do!" Lily felt bad. She did not mean to hurt Ben. She dropped her stick and ran to him. "I'm sorry, Ben!" Lily said, hugging him. "I did not want to hurt you. I was just playing. Are you okay?" Ben sniffed and looked at Lily. He saw that she was sorry. He hugged her back. "I'm okay, Lily," Ben said. "I know you were just playing. But you have to be careful with your weapon. It can hurt someone even if it looks harmless." Lily nodded. She learned her lesson. She and Ben went inside to get a band-aid and a cookie. They decided to play something else that was safer and nicer. They were still friends.
