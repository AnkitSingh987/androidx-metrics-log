Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Busy Boy and the Sue

Once upon a time, there was a busy little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play outside in the snow. One day, he saw his friend, Sue, making a big snowman. "Can I join you?" Tim asked Sue. "Yes, please!" Sue said with a big smile. They both worked hard to make the snowman together. After they finished the snowman, they decided to have a snowball fight. Tim and Sue laughed and played until they were tired. They went home to warm up and have hot cocoa. They both agreed it was the best snowy day ever.

Sam and Ben love to play with their toy trains. They have a big track that goes around the room. They make the trains go fast and slow, and make noises like choo-choo and beep-beep. One day, Sam's mom says, "Sam, it's time to go to the park. Bring your coat and your hat. You can bring one toy, too." Sam thinks for a moment. He wants to bring his train, but the track is too big. He asks Ben, "Can I bring your train? It's smaller than mine." Ben says, "No, you can't. It's my train. You have your own train." Sam feels sad. He says, "But I like your train. It's red and shiny. My train is blue and filthy. It has dirt and dust on it." Ben says, "That's because you never clean it. You should take care of your toys. Then they will look nice." Sam says, "I'm sorry. I will clean it later. Please, can I borrow your train? Just for today. I will give it back to you when we come home." Ben thinks for a moment. He likes Sam. He is his best friend. He says, "Okay, fine. You can borrow my train. But be careful with it. Don't lose it or break it. And say thank you." Sam says, "Thank you, Ben. You are a good friend. I will be careful with your train. I will have fun at the park." Sam's mom says, "Come on, Sam. Let's go. Say bye to Ben." Sam says, "Bye, Ben. See you later." Sam and Ben wave to each other. Sam puts on his coat and his hat. He holds Ben's train in his hand. He is happy. He hopes Ben will like his train when it is clean.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to play at the park. He loved to run and jump on the grass. Tim saw a big sign with the word "law" on it. The sign said, "Be careful! Do not slip on the grass." Tim did not know what "law" meant, but he did not want to slip. Tim was a careless boy. He did not listen to the sign. He ran fast on the wet grass. Suddenly, Tim's feet slipped from under him. He fell down hard on the ground. Tim felt sad and hurt because he did not follow the law. Tim's mom came to help him. She told him, "Tim, you need to be more careful when you play. You should listen to the sign and follow the law." Tim learned his lesson. From that day on, he played carefully and never slipped on the grass again.

The sun was shining bright in the sky as three-year-old Mary and her mom walked through the park. Mary looked around with wide, curious eyes. "Let's read a book, Mom!" Mary said, looking up at her mom with enthusiasm. Her mother smiled. "Of course, sweetie." She reached down into the trunk beside the bench and pulled out a big storybook. "Oh, it's about a princess!" Mary said, eyes twinkling with excitement as she opened to the first page. She began to read, but after a few minutes Mary became embarrassed. She couldn't read the words very well. Her mom hugged her and said, "Don't be embarrassed, Mary. You're doing so well! You'll be able to read this story all on your own very soon." Mary was encouraged by her mother's words, so she kept on reading. With each page, she became more and more confident and her words became clearer and clearer. Sure enough, only a few pages later, she was reading the story all on her own and with great fluency. The look of pride on her mom's face was priceless!
