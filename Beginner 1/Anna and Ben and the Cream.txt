Category: Beginner
Title: Anna and Ben and the Cream

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play with Ben's dog, Spot. Spot is a loyal dog. He always follows Ben and Anna and does what they say. One day, Ben and Anna go to the park. They see a big truck with a picture of a cone and cream on it. The truck makes a loud sound. "Ding ding ding!" Ben and Anna run to the truck. They want to buy some cream. "Hello, kids. Do you want some cream?" the man in the truck asks. "Yes, please!" Ben and Anna say. The man gives them each a cone with cream on top. Ben and Anna pay him with their coins. They thank him and walk away. Spot sees the cream. He likes cream. He wants to taste it. He runs after Ben and Anna. He jumps and tries to catch the cream. "Spot, no!" Ben shouts. "This is my cream!" "Spot, stop!" Anna says. "This is my cream!" But Spot does not listen. He is too hungry. He catches Ben's cream with his mouth. He licks it and makes a happy sound. "Woof woof!" Ben is sad. He has no cream. He looks at Spot. He is angry. He says, "Bad dog! You are not loyal. You are greedy. Go away!" Spot feels sorry. He drops the cream. He whines and lowers his head. He loves Ben. He does not want to make him sad. Anna sees Ben and Spot. She feels sorry for them. She has an idea. She says, "Ben, don't be mad at Spot. He is still loyal. He just likes cream. Look, I have some cream. We can share it. You can have half and I can have half. And we can give Spot a little bit too. OK?" Ben thinks about it. He says, "OK. That is nice of you, Anna. Thank you. And I'm sorry, Spot. You are still my friend. Come here, boy." Anna breaks her cone in half. She gives one half to Ben and keeps one half for herself. They each have some cream. They also give Spot a little bit of cream. He licks it and wags his tail. He is happy. Ben, Anna and Spot hug each other. They are friends. They share their cream. They are loyal. They are happy. The end.

Once upon a time, there was a happy musician who played the guitar. He loved to make music for everyone in the town. All the people would stop and admire his songs. They would dance and clap their hands. One day, a mean man came to the town. He did not like the happy musician's music. He said, "Stop playing! I don't want to hear your music!" The musician felt sad and stopped playing. A little girl saw the musician was sad. She said, "Don't be sad. We all love your music. Don't let the mean man stop you." The musician smiled and started to play again. The mean man saw everyone enjoying the music and decided to join them. He learned that being happy and kind was better than being mean. And they all lived happily ever after. The moral of the story is: Don't let others bring you down. Keep doing what makes you and others happy.

Once upon a time, there was a kind king. The king had many friends, but he wanted more friends to join him in his big castle. So, he sent out a message to all the people in his land. "Come to my castle and join me," said the king. The people were happy to join the king. They walked, ran, and skipped to the castle. They wanted to be friends with the king too. When they got to the castle, they saw that the king was very fit. He liked to run and jump and play with his friends. The king and his new friends had a big party. They ate yummy food, played fun games, and danced all day. The king was so happy to have more friends join him. And, the people were happy to have a kind and fit king. They all lived happily ever after.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to the kitchen. He saw a thick, shiny faucet. Tim wanted to play with the faucet. He turned it on and water came out. "Wow!" said Tim, "This is fun!" Tim's mom heard the water and came into the kitchen. "Tim, what are you doing?" she asked. Tim looked at his mom and said, "I'm playing with the faucet!" His mom said, "No, Tim, we don't play with faucets. Turn it off, please." Tim felt sad, but he turned off the faucet. Later, Tim was playing with his toys when he started to cry. His mom came in to see what was wrong. "Why are you crying, Tim?" she asked. Tim said, "I miss playing with the faucet." His mom hugged him and said, "It's okay to be sad, but we have to be careful with water. Let's find another game to play together." Tim stopped crying and played with his mom, feeling happy again.
