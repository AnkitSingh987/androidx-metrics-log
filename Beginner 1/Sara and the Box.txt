Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and the Box

Sara liked to play with her mom's jewelry. She liked the shiny rings and necklaces and bracelets. She liked to put them on and pretend she was a princess. But she did not like to put them back in the box. She left them on the floor or on the table or on the bed. One day, her mom came home and saw the mess. She was not happy. She said to Sara, "You have to be more organized. You have to put the jewelry back in the box. If you don't, you will lose them or break them or someone will take them. Do you understand?" Sara did not understand. She did not want to put the jewelry back in the box. She wanted to keep playing with them. She said to her mom, "No, I don't want to. I like the jewelry. They are pretty and fun. You have too many. You don't need them all. Let me have some." Her mom did not like that. She said to Sara, "You are being rude and selfish. You cannot have the jewelry. They are mine. They are special to me. Some of them are from your grandma and your dad and your aunt. They are not toys. You have to respect them and take care of them. And you have to listen to me. Now, put the jewelry back in the box. Or you will be in trouble." Sara did not like that. She started to cry and complain. She said to her mom, "You are mean and unfair. You don't love me. You love the jewelry more than me. You don't let me have any fun. You are always telling me what to do. I hate you." Her mom was sad and angry. She said to Sara, "That is not true. I love you very much. But you are being naughty and ungrateful. You have to learn to share and follow the rules. You have to apologize and say sorry. And you have to put the jewelry back in the box. Now." Sara did not want to say sorry. She did not want to put the jewelry back in the box. But she saw her mom's face and she felt bad. She knew she had hurt her mom's feelings. She knew she had done something wrong. She did not want to be in trouble. She did not want to lose her mom's love. So she said to her mom, "I'm sorry, mom. I love you. I will put the jewelry back in the box. Please don't be mad at me." Her mom smiled and hugged her. She said to Sara, "I'm sorry, too. I love you. I'm not mad at you. I'm glad you said sorry. I'm proud of you. You are a good girl. Thank you for putting the jewelry back in the box. You can play with them again another day. But only with my permission. And only if you promise to be organized. OK?" Sara nodded and smiled. She said to her mom, "OK, mom. I promise. Thank you for letting me play with the jewelry. They are very pretty and special. Just like you."

Tom and Lily were hungry. They wanted to eat some soup. Mom gave them each a bowl of soup. The soup was hot and yummy. "Can I have more soup, please?" Tom asked Mom. "Sure, you can have more soup. But be careful, it is still hot." Mom said. She used a big spoon to increase the soup in Tom's bowl. "Thank you, Mom!" Tom said. He blew on his soup to cool it down. He ate his soup with a small spoon. Lily watched Tom. She wanted more soup too. She liked soup. She opened her mouth and said, "More soup, please!" Mom smiled. She used the big spoon to increase the soup in Lily's bowl. "Thank you, Mom!" Lily said. She blew on her soup to cool it down. She ate her soup with a small spoon. Tom and Lily ate all their soup. They were happy and full. They gave Mom a hug and a kiss. "Good job, Tom and Lily! You ate all your soup. Now you can have some fruit for dessert." Mom said. Tom and Lily were excited. They loved fruit. They ran to the table and picked their favorite fruit. They ate their fruit with their fingers. They made funny faces and laughed. Mom laughed too. She was proud of Tom and Lily. They were good children.
