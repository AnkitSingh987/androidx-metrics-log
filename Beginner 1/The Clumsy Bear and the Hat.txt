Category: Beginner
Title: The Clumsy Bear and the Hat

Once upon a time, there was a clumsy bear. He was always tripping over his own feet and bumping into things. One day, he found a pile of clothes. Lots of shirts and pants were folded up in a neat pile. The bear was so excited. He grabbed one of the shirts and quickly tried to put it on. He was so clumsy, though. He had trouble getting the shirt over his head. After some trying he finally managed to close it up, happy to finally be wearing something. The bear did the same with the pants, but he still felt something was missing. He rummaged through the pile again and grabbed a hat. He put it on his head and closed it tight. Now he was ready. He said to himself, "I finally look good! Now I can go show my friends."

Once upon a time, there was a little dog named Spot. Spot lived in a small house with a big, heavy door. One day, the door was open, and Spot saw his chance to escape and play outside. Spot ran outside and saw his friend, the cat. The cat said, "Spot, where are you going?" Spot said, "I escape from my house! Let's play!" The cat and Spot played in the sun all day long. As the day ended, Spot and the cat were tired. Spot said, "I need to go home now." They went back to the house with the heavy door. The door was still open, so Spot went inside. He was happy to be home and had a lot of fun playing with his friend.

Lila and Ben are young twins who like to swim. They have a big pool in their backyard. They put on their swimsuits and run to the pool. They jump in and splash each other. "Look, Lila, I can swim like a fish!" Ben says. He moves his arms and legs fast in the water. "I can swim like a mermaid!" Lila says. She flips her hair and makes a tail with her legs. They swim and play for a long time. They are happy and tired. "Let's go inside and have a snack," Ben says. He climbs out of the pool and grabs a towel. "OK, but first I want to switch the pool light on," Lila says. She sees a switch on the wall. She likes to see the pool glow at night. She reaches for the switch, but it is too high for her. She jumps and stretches, but she can't touch it. "Ben, help me!" she says. Ben comes back and sees Lila. He laughs. "Lila, you are silly. The switch is not for the pool light. It is for the sprinklers. If you switch it on, you will get wet again!" He pulls Lila away from the switch and wraps her in a towel. "Come on, let's go. We can see the pool light later. Mom has cookies and milk for us." They run inside, holding hands. They are still wet and giggly. They love to swim and switch. They are young and fun.

Tom and Mia were playing with a big ball in the park. They liked to kick the ball and run after it. Sometimes they would try to catch the ball with their hands, but they were clumsy and often missed. One time, the ball rolled near a big tree. Tom ran to get the ball, but he saw something shiny on the tree. It was a knot in the wood, and it looked like a star. Tom wanted to touch the knot, because he liked shiny things. "Tom, come back!" Mia called. "We have to share the ball!" But Tom did not listen. He reached out his hand to touch the knot. But the knot was not smooth. It was rough and sharp. Tom felt a pain in his finger. He had a splinter! "Ow, ow, ow!" Tom cried. He dropped the ball and held his finger. He ran to Mia and showed her his finger. "Look, Mia, it hurts!" Mia looked at Tom's finger. She saw the splinter. She felt sorry for Tom, but she also felt angry. "Tom, you were silly. You should not touch things you do not know. You could get hurt. And you did not share the ball. That was not nice." Tom felt ashamed. He knew Mia was right. He had been selfish and curious. He had not been a good friend. He said sorry to Mia. "I'm sorry, Mia. I was silly. I should not touch the knot. I should share the ball. Can you help me with the splinter?" Mia nodded. She was a kind friend. She took Tom's hand and gently pulled out the splinter. She blew on his finger and gave him a hug. "It's okay, Tom. I forgive you. But next time, be careful and be fair. Then we can have more fun." Tom smiled. He thanked Mia. He learned his lesson. He did not touch the knot again. He shared the ball with Mia. They played happily in the park. They were good friends.
