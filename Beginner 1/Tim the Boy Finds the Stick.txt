Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy Finds the Stick

One day, a boy named Tim went to the park. He saw a big mud puddle. Tim wanted to play in it. He saw a stick and picked it up. He started to poke the mud with the stick. A girl named Sue saw Tim playing. She asked, "Can I play too?" Tim said, "Yes, pick a stick from the available ones." Sue picked a stick and joined Tim. They played and laughed together. As they poked the mud, they found a shiny rock. They picked it up and saw it was a gold coin. Tim and Sue were very happy. They ran home to show their mom and dad their unexpected find.

Once upon a time, there was a little bird named Bobo. Bobo had a goal. He wanted to learn how to fly high in the sky. But the wind was harsh and strong. It made it hard for Bobo to fly. One day, Bobo's mom told him a story. She said, "Bobo, do you remember the story of the strong tree?" This story reminded Bobo of how the tree grew big and strong, even in the harsh wind. Bobo knew that he could be like the tree and learn to fly high, even with the strong wind. Bobo practiced every day. He flapped his wings and jumped from branch to branch. Then, one day, the wind was not so harsh. Bobo felt strong, just like the tree in the story. He flapped his wings, jumped off the branch, and flew high in the sky. Bobo reached his goal, and he was so happy.

Tom and Mia are friends. They like to play together. One day, they go to Mia's house. Mia has a big piano in her living room. Tom wants to try it. "Can I play the piano?" Tom asks Mia. "Sure, you can play the piano. But be careful. Don't hit it too hard. It will make a loud noise and hurt your ears." Mia says. Tom nods. He sits on the piano bench. He puts his hands on the keys. He presses one key. It makes a nice sound. He presses another key. It makes another nice sound. He likes the piano. He presses more keys. He makes a song. Mia listens to Tom's song. She thinks it is interesting. She claps her hands. She says, "You are good at the piano, Tom. You make an interesting song." Tom smiles. He is happy. He wants to make more songs. He presses more keys. He presses them faster and harder. He makes a loud noise. Mia covers her ears. She does not like the loud noise. She says, "Tom, stop! You are hitting the piano too hard. You are making a bad noise. You are hurting my ears." Tom does not stop. He thinks the loud noise is fun. He laughs. He says, "No, I like the loud noise. It is funny. I want to make more noise." Mia gets angry. She does not like Tom's noise. She does not think it is funny. She wants him to stop. She gets up from the couch. She walks to the piano. She slaps Tom's hand. She says, "Stop it, Tom! You are being rude. You are not playing the piano. You are breaking the piano. You are not my friend." Tom feels a pain in his hand. He stops pressing the keys. He looks at Mia. He sees her angry face. He feels sad. He says, "I'm sorry, Mia. I did not mean to be rude. I did not mean to break the piano. I did not mean to hurt your ears. I was just having fun. Please don't be mad at me. Please be my friend." Mia looks at Tom. She sees his sad face. She feels sorry. She says, "I'm sorry, too, Tom. I did not mean to slap your hand. I was just scared. I thought you would break the piano. I thought you did not care about my ears. I was just angry. Please don't be sad. Please be my friend." Tom and Mia hug. They say, "We are sorry. We are friends." They go back to the couch. They watch TV. They are happy. They do not play the piano anymore. They think it is too loud. They think it is not interesting.
