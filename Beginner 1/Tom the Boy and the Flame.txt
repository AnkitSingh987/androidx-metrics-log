Category: Beginner
Title: Tom the Boy and the Flame

Once upon a time, in a small village, there was a boy named Tom. Tom loved to read. He read all day long. One day, he found a big book with a picture of a flame on the cover. The flame was big and impressive. Tom showed the book to his mom. "Look, Mom! I found a book with a big flame. Can I read it?" Tom asked. "Yes, Tom. You can read the book," Mom said with a smile. Tom read the book about the impressive flame. He learned that the flame could talk and help people. Tom wished he could meet the flame one day. He told his friends about the flame and they all wanted to meet it too. At night, Tom and his friends went to the woods to find the impressive flame. They found it near a big tree. The flame said, "Hello, kids! I am here to help you. Do you need anything?" Tom and his friends were happy to meet the flame. They thanked the flame for being so helpful and went back to the village. From that day on, Tom and his friends always remembered the impressive flame and the fun adventure they had.

Tim and Jen are friends. They like to play with blocks. They have many blocks of different colors. Tim likes blue blocks. Jen likes red blocks. One day, they want to build a big tower with their blocks. They start to put blocks on top of each other. But they have a problem. They do not have enough blocks of their favorite colors. Tim wants more blue blocks. Jen wants more red blocks. They see a big box of blocks in the corner. They run to the box. They hope to find more blocks of their favorite colors. But they do not ask the teacher if they can use the box. They do not know that the box belongs to another class. They open the box. They are happy. They see many blue and red blocks. They take the blocks and run back to their tower. They do not share the blocks with the other kids. They do not say thank you to the teacher. They finish their tower. It is very tall and colorful. They are proud of their tower. They want to show it to the teacher. They call the teacher. "Look at our tower!" they say. The teacher comes to see their tower. She is not happy. She is angry. She knows that they took the blocks from the other class. She asks them, "Did you ask me if you can use the box of blocks?" Tim and Jen do not answer. They look down. They feel bad. They know that they did something wrong. The teacher says, "You should not take things that are not yours. You should ask before you use something. You should share with the other kids. You should say thank you when someone gives you something. That is how you show respect and kindness. Do you understand?" Tim and Jen nod. They understand. They are sorry. They say, "We are sorry, teacher. We did not ask. We did not share. We did not say thank you. We will not do it again. Can we give the blocks back to the other class?" The teacher smiles. She says, "Yes, you can give the blocks back. And you can say sorry to the other class. And you can help them build their own tower. That is how you show that you are sorry and that you want to be friends. Do you want to do that?" Tim and Jen nod. They want to do that. They take the blocks and go to the other class. They say sorry and give the blocks back. They help the other kids build their own tower. They share and say thank you. They make new friends. They are happy. The moral of the story is: You should not take things that are not yours. You should ask before you use something. You should share with the other kids. You should say thank you when someone gives you something. That is how you show respect and kindness.
