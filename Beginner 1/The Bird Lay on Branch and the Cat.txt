Category: Beginner
Title: The Bird Lay on Branch and the Cat

One day, a bird lay on a branch in a big tree. The bird saw a cat on the ground. The cat looked up and saw the bird. The cat wanted to play with the bird. The cat said, "Bird, can I come up and play with you?" The bird was sorry, but it was scared. The bird said, "No, cat, you cannot come up. You are too big." The cat felt sad. It wanted to be friends with the bird. The cat found a small toy and put it on the ground. The cat said, "Bird, I have a toy for you. We can play together." The bird saw the toy and was happy. It flew down from the branch and played with the cat. They became good friends and played together every day.

Tom and Sam are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they find a bottle in the grass. The bottle is shiny and has a paper inside. "Look, a bottle!" Tom says. "Maybe it has a secret message." "Let's open it and see," Sam says. They pull the cork and take out the paper. The paper has words and pictures on it. "What does it say?" Tom asks. Sam tries to read the paper. He knows some words, but not all. He says, "It says... mail this bottle to... a big number. And you will get... a good surprise." "Wow, a good surprise!" Tom says. "Maybe it is a toy or a candy. Let's mail the bottle." They run to the mailbox and put the bottle inside. They are happy and excited. They wait for the good surprise. But the good surprise never comes. The bottle was a trick. The paper was from a bad person who wanted to make trouble. The bottle had a bomb inside. The bomb goes off in the mailbox and makes a loud noise. The mailbox breaks and catches fire. The fire spreads to the park and the trees. The park is ruined. Tom and Sam are scared and sad. They run away from the fire and cry. They wish they never found the bottle. They wish they never mailed the bottle. They wish they knew the words on the paper. They wish they had a good surprise. But they don't. They have a bad ending.

Once upon a time, there was a cool dog named Spot. Spot liked to play with his ball and run. One day, Spot saw a big red truck. The truck was loud and fast. Spot wanted to know what the truck was for. Spot asked his friend, Cat, to explain the big red truck. Cat said, "That is an emergency truck. It helps when there is a problem." Spot thought that was cool. He wanted to help, too. One day, Spot saw a little girl crying. She lost her toy. Spot knew this was a problem, so he acted like the emergency truck. He ran fast and found the toy. Spot gave the toy back to the girl, and she was happy. Spot felt good because he helped, just like the big red truck.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue liked to play with her toys. One day, she found a telephone in her toy box. Sue did not recognize the telephone. She thought it was a new toy. Sue showed the telephone to her mom. "Look, Mom! I found a new toy!" she said. Her mom smiled and said, "That's not a toy, Sue. That's a telephone. We can use it to talk to people who are far away." Later that day, Sue was playing with her toys again. She saw that her doll's dress was too small. "Oh no, this dress doesn't fit!" she said. Sue remembered the telephone her mom had shown her. She got the telephone and called her grandma. "Hi, Grandma! Can you help me? My doll's dress doesn't fit," Sue said. Grandma was happy to help. She came over and made a new dress for Sue's doll. Sue was very happy, and now she knew what a telephone was for.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to design things. She made different drawings of houses, animals, and trees. One day, Sue's mom took her to the store. Sue saw many things with different prices. Some things were low in price and others were high in price. Sue found a big box of crayons. The crayons had many different colors. Sue wanted the crayons to design more things. But the price was high. Sue was sad. She did not have enough money for the big box of crayons. Sue's mom saw her sad face and said, "Sue, you can still design with the small box of crayons. The price is low, and you can still make pretty drawings." Sue thought about it and picked the small box of crayons. She was happy. Sue learned that you don't need the most expensive things to design and have fun. The important thing is to use what you have and be creative. And that was the moral value of the story.
