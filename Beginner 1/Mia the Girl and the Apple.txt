Category: Beginner
Title: Mia the Girl and the Apple

One day, a little girl named Mia went outside to play. She saw the wind blowing the leaves on the trees. Mia felt happy and started to dance with the wind. She heard the wind talk to her. It said, "Hello, Mia! Let's play together!" Mia and the wind played all day long. They made the leaves dance and the flowers sway. The wind even helped Mia fly her kite high in the sky. After a while, Mia's tummy started to growl. She knew it was time for a snack. Mia ran inside and asked her mom for something delicious to eat. Her mom gave her a big, red apple. Mia went back outside and sat under a tree. She took a big bite of her delicious apple and said, "Thank you, wind, for playing with me today. This apple is for both of us!" The wind blew gently, making the leaves dance again. Mia knew that the wind was happy too. They both enjoyed the delicious apple and continued to play together for the rest of the day.

"Hop, hop, hop," shouted Henry as he jumped around the room. His mom was smiling and clapping her hands. In the middle of the living room floor was a white box. He ran over to it, and asked, "What is it, Mommy?" His Mom explained, "It's a surprise! Inside you'll find some scissors." Henry was excited! He hopped on one foot as he tried to open the box. With a few tries, he finally got it open. Inside he found a pair of white scissors. Henry asked, "What can I do with these?" His Mom smiled and said, "You can use them to cut paper and help me with crafts!" Henry couldn't wait. He grabbed his scissors and hopped around the room, cutting and snipping things like paper and fabric. He was having so much fun! "Thank you Mommy," said Henry. "I love my scissors!"

Tom was a boy who liked to run. He ran every day with his dog, Spot. He ran in the park, in the street, and in the yard. He ran so fast that he could beat anyone in a race. One day, Tom saw a poster for a big race. The poster said that the winner would get a prize: a shiny vest. Tom wanted the vest very much. He thought it would make him look cool and strong. He decided to join the race. The day of the race came. Tom put on his shoes and his hat. He took Spot with him. He went to the park where the race was. He saw many other boys and girls who wanted to run. Some of them looked bigger and faster than him. Tom felt a little scared. But he did not give up. He said to himself, "I can do it. I can run fast. I can win the vest." He said to Spot, "You can help me. You can run with me. You can cheer me on." Spot barked and wagged his tail. The race started. Tom ran as fast as he could. He ran past some of the other runners. He ran with Spot by his side. He ran and ran and ran. He saw the finish line. He saw the vest. He ran even faster. He crossed the line. He looked around. He was the first one. He had won the race. He had won the vest. He was so happy. He hugged Spot. He put on the vest. It fit him well. It was shiny and warm. He felt cool and strong. He had gained a vest. He had gained a prize. He had gained a smile. He had been successful. He was proud of himself. He was proud of Spot. He was a boy who liked to run.

Once upon a time, in a small village, there was a little girl named Ann. Ann loved to eat pears. One day, she saw a big pear tree in her yard. She wanted to pick the pears, but they were too high. Ann saw her friend, Tom, walking by. She asked him, "Can you help me get the pears?" Tom was tired, but he wanted to help his friend. So, he climbed the tree and picked the pears for Ann. Ann was so happy and said, "Thank you, Tom!" Ann shared the pears with Tom, and they both enjoyed the sweet fruit. They learned that when you help others, even when you are tired, good things happen. And it is always nice to say "thank you" and share with your friends.
