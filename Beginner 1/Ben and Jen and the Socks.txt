Category: Beginner
Title: Ben and Jen and the Socks

Ben and Jen are twins. They like to play with their toys and books. But they do not like to wear socks. They think socks are boring and itchy. One day, Mom says, "We need to go to the store. You need new socks for winter. Put on your shoes and coats." Ben and Jen groan. They do not want to go to the store. They do not want new socks. They want to stay home and play. At the store, Mom shows them many socks. Some are red, some are blue, some have stripes, some have dots. "Look, these are nice socks. They are soft and warm. Which ones do you like?" Mom asks. Ben and Jen shake their heads. They do not like any of the socks. They all look the same to them. They are all regular socks. Then, they see something different. On a shelf, there are socks that look like animals. There are socks with ears, tails, eyes, and noses. There are socks that look like cats, dogs, bears, and rabbits. "Wow, look at these socks!" Ben says. "They are so cute and funny!" Jen says. They pick up the socks and try them on. They make noises and pretend to be animals. They laugh and have fun. Mom smiles and says, "You like these socks, don't you? They are not regular socks. They are special socks. Do you want to buy them?" Ben and Jen nod. They hug Mom and say, "Thank you, Mom. We love these socks. They are the best socks ever."

Tom and Lily were playing with blocks. They wanted to build a big wall with their blocks. They had many blocks of different colors and shapes. "Look, I have a red block. It is square. It can fit here," Tom said. He put his block on the wall. "I have a blue block. It is round. It can fit here," Lily said. She put her block on the wall. They kept adding blocks to the wall. They were happy. But then, they ran out of blocks. The wall was not finished. There was a big gap in the middle. "Oh no, we need more blocks. How can we finish the wall?" Tom asked. Lily looked around. She saw Sam, their friend, playing with his blocks. He had many blocks of different colors and shapes. "Hey, Sam, can we use some of your blocks? We want to finish our wall," Lily asked. Sam looked at Tom and Lily. He looked at their wall. He looked at his blocks. "No, you can't. These are my blocks. I don't want to share. Go away," Sam said. He hugged his blocks. Tom and Lily felt sad. They wanted to finish their wall. They wanted to play with Sam. "Why is Sam so mean? He has so many blocks. He can share some with us," Tom said. "Maybe he is afraid. Maybe he thinks we will take all his blocks. Maybe he doesn't know how to play with others," Lily said. They thought for a while. They had an idea. "Sam, we are sorry. We don't want to take your blocks. We want to include you. We want to play with you. You can help us finish our wall. You can choose where to put your blocks. You can make the wall more beautiful. You can be our friend," Lily said. Tom and Lily smiled at Sam. They showed him their wall. They showed him the gap. Sam looked at Tom and Lily. He looked at their wall. He looked at his blocks. He felt sorry. He felt lonely. He felt curious. He put down his blocks. He walked to Tom and Lily. He smiled at them. "Okay, I will share my blocks. I will help you finish your wall. I will play with you. You can be my friends," Sam said. They hugged each other. They picked up some blocks. They finished the wall. They made the wall more beautiful. They played together. They were happy. The moral of the story is: Sharing is caring. It is better to include others than to exclude them. Friendship is more fun than selfishness.

Once upon a time, there was a gifted boy named Tim. He could do many things very well. One day, Tim found a video. He wanted to watch the video on the TV. So, he put the video into the TV and pressed play. The video was not nice. It showed people being mean to each other. Tim felt sad when he watched the video. He did not like what he saw. The video made Tim want to be mean to his friends. The next day, Tim was mean to his friends at school. They did not like it and did not want to play with him anymore. Tim was sad and alone. He learned that watching bad things can make you do bad things too. The moral of the story is to be careful about what you watch, because it can change how you act.
