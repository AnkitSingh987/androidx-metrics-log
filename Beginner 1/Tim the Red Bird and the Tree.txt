Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Red Bird and the Tree

Once upon a time, there was a little red bird named Tim. Tim was very selfish. He did not like to share his food with other birds. One day, Tim found a big pile of seeds. He thought, "I will keep all these seeds for myself." Soon, it was very hot outside. The heat made all the birds thirsty. They could not find any water to drink. Tim began to wonder if there was any water nearby. He saw a big tree in the distance. Tim thought, "Maybe there is water by that tree." Tim flew to the tree and found a big puddle of water. He was so happy! But then, he saw the other thirsty birds. Tim thought about the seeds he did not share. He did not want to be selfish anymore. So, Tim told the other birds about the water. They all drank and were happy. Tim learned that sharing was good.

One day, a little girl named Mia went to the park with her mom. Mia loved to play on the slide. She would climb up the steps and slide down, laughing all the time. Mia saw her friend, a boy named Tom, playing on the swings. Tom looked at Mia and felt envious because he wanted to play on the slide too. Mia and Tom decided to play together. They took turns on the slide, and then they went to discover new things in the park. They found a big tree with a hole in it. They looked inside and found a small, cute bird. The bird was making a nest for her babies. Mia and Tom were happy to discover the bird's nest. They played in the park all day, and when it was time to go home, they said goodbye to the bird and the slide. Mia and Tom couldn't wait to come back to the park and discover more fun things together.

Ben and Lily were playing in the garden. They liked to dig in the dirt and look for bugs. Sometimes they found worms, snails, or ants. They put them in a jar and watched them. "Look, Lily, I found a beetle!" Ben said. He held up a shiny black bug with long legs and horns. "It is rough and hard. Do you want to touch it?" Lily shook her head. She did not like beetles. They were scary and ugly. She liked butterflies and ladybugs. They were pretty and soft. "No, thank you, Ben. I don't want to touch it. You can keep it." Ben smiled. He liked beetles. They were cool and strong. He put the beetle in the jar with the other bugs. He closed the lid and shook the jar gently. He wanted to see the beetle move. But the beetle did not move. It was still and quiet. Ben frowned. He opened the lid and poked the beetle with his finger. It did not move. It was not a beetle. It was a toy. "Surprise!" Lily said. She giggled and clapped her hands. She had tricked Ben. She had put the toy beetle in the dirt when he was not looking. She thought it was funny. Ben was not happy. He felt silly and angry. He did not like to be tricked. He threw the toy beetle on the ground and stomped on it. He broke it into pieces. He said, "That was not nice, Lily. That was mean. You are a bad sister." Lily stopped giggling. She felt sorry and sad. She did not mean to make Ben angry. She wanted to make him laugh. She said, "I'm sorry, Ben. I was just joking. I didn't know you would be mad. Please don't be mad. You are a good brother." Ben looked at Lily. He saw her tears and heard her voice. He felt sorry and sad too. He did not mean to hurt her feelings. He wanted to make her happy. He said, "I'm sorry, Lily. I was just mad. I didn't know you were joking. Please don't cry. You are a good sister." Ben and Lily hugged each other. They said they were sorry and they loved each other. They picked up the pieces of the toy beetle and threw them in the trash. They decided to play with the real bugs instead. They were more fun and less trouble.
