Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Shop

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was very impatient. One day, he saw a pretty rug in a shop. He wanted it so much for his room. He asked his mom, "Mom, can I have the rug, please?" His mom said, "Not today, Tim. We don't have enough money." But Tim did not listen. He was very impatient. He took the rug and ran out of the shop. The shop owner saw Tim and was very mad. He called Tim's mom. Tim's mom was sad and said, "I am sorry. I will bring Tim back." Tim's mom took him back to the shop. She said, "You must say sorry and give back the rug." Tim did not want to. He was still impatient. But he said sorry and gave back the rug. The shop owner was still mad. He told Tim and his mom to leave the shop and not come back. Tim was sad. He did not get the rug, and now he could not go to the shop anymore.

Once upon a time, there was a little orange goat. The goat lived on a big farm with many friends. The goat liked to play and jump all day. One day, the goat saw a big orange ball in the grass. The goat went near the ball and said, "Wow! What a big ball! I want to play with it!" The goat tried to pick up the ball with its mouth, but it was too big. So, the goat tried to push the ball with its head. But the ball was very strong. It did not move. The goat pushed harder and harder, but the ball did not move at all. The goat felt sad and started to cry. Then, a big bird came down from the sky and said, "Don't cry, little goat. I can help you." The bird used its beak to tear the ball a little. The air came out of the ball, and it became smaller. The goat was happy and said, "Thank you, big bird!" Now, the goat could play with the ball. The goat and the bird played with the ball all day, and they became best friends.

Once upon a time, there were two twins named Tom and Tim. They liked to play near a big, rough tree. One day, Tom had an idea. He said, "Let's lean on the tree and see who can push it harder!" Tim agreed, and they both leaned on the tree. They pushed with all their might, but the tree did not move. They were tired, so they sat down to rest. As they sat, they heard a noise from behind the rough tree. It was a little bird who was stuck in the tree. The bird said, "Help me, please! I can't get out!" Tom and Tim looked at each other and knew they had to help. They leaned on the tree again, but this time, they pushed together. With one big push, the tree moved just enough for the little bird to fly free. The bird thanked the twins and flew away. Tom and Tim learned that working together made them stronger, and they became the best of friends. From that day on, they always helped each other, and they all lived happily ever after.

Tom and Sam were brothers who liked to play with snow. They made snowmen, snowballs and snow forts. They had fun in the winter. But one day, the sun came out and the snow started to melt. Tom and Sam were sad. They wanted to keep their snow things. They started to fight over the last snowball. They threw it at each other and yelled. "Stop it! Stop it!" their mom said. "You are making a war over nothing. The snow will melt anyway. You should share and be kind to each other." Tom and Sam looked at their mom. They looked at the snow. They looked at each other. They felt bad. They were being mean and silly. They were making themselves and their mom miserable. They said sorry to each other and to their mom. They hugged and smiled. They decided to play something else. They found some crayons and paper and drew pictures of snowmen, snowballs and snow forts. They had fun in the spring. The moral of the story is: Fighting over things that don't last is foolish and unhappy. Sharing and being kind is wise and happy.
