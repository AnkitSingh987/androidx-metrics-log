Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and the Song

Sara liked to play the flute. She had a small flute that made nice sounds when she blew into it. She liked to play songs for her friends and family. One day, she went to the park with her mom and dad. She saw a big duck pond with many ducks. She wanted to play the flute for the ducks. She asked her mom and dad, "Can I play the flute for the ducks?" Her mom and dad said, "Yes, you can, but be careful. Don't go too close to the water or the ducks. They might bite you or take your flute." Sara nodded and went to the edge of the pond. She held her flute and played a song. The ducks heard the song and came closer. They looked at Sara and her flute. They quacked and wiggled their tails. Sara smiled and played another song. She liked the ducks. They seemed to like her flute. She wondered if they could sing too. She stopped playing and asked the ducks, "Can you sing with me?" The ducks did not answer. They opened their beaks and tried to grab Sara's flute. Sara was scared and pulled her flute away. She ran back to her mom and dad. She said, "The ducks are not nice. They want to eat my flute." Her mom and dad hugged her and said, "Don't worry, Sara. The ducks are not mean. They are just hungry. They think your flute is food. They don't know what a flute is." Sara said, "Oh, I see. But my flute is not food. It is a toy. It is not yummy." Her mom and dad said, "That's right. Your flute is not yummy. But you know what is yummy? Ice cream. Do you want some ice cream?" Sara said, "Yes, please. I love ice cream. Can I have chocolate?" Her mom and dad said, "Sure. Let's go get some ice cream. And maybe later, you can play the flute for us. We like your songs." Sara said, "Okay. Thank you, mom and dad. You are nice. I love you." They walked to the ice cream shop. Sara held her flute and her mom and dad's hands. She was happy. She liked to play the flute. She liked ice cream. She liked her mom and dad. And she liked to question. She always had many questions. She wanted to learn new things. She was a curious girl.

One day, a little fish named Fin swam near the shore. The tide was moving in and out. Fin saw a big crab named Carl. Carl was sad because he could not find his way back to his home in the sea. Fin said, "I can help you, Carl. Swim with me and we can find your home." They swam together. The tide was strong, but Fin and Carl kept swimming. The waves were harsh, but they did not give up. As they swam, a big wave came and made Carl fall onto a big rock. Fin was scared, but Carl just laughed. "This is my home," he said. "I live on this rock! Thank you for helping me find it, Fin." They were happy and became best friends.

Sara did not want to wake up. She heard her mom say, "Sara, it is time to get up. You have school today." Sara hid under her blanket. She liked school, but she was tired. She wanted to sleep more. She heard her brother Tom say, "I don't have school today. I have a cold. I can stay in bed and watch TV." Sara peeked out of her blanket. She saw Tom smile. He looked happy. Sara felt envious. She wanted to stay in bed and watch TV too. She said, "Mom, I have a cold too. I can't go to school." She coughed a little. She hoped her mom would believe her. Her mom came to her room. She touched Sara's forehead. She said, "You don't have a fever. You are not sick. You have to go to school. Tom is sick. He has to stay home. He can't play with his friends. He can't go outside. He is bored." Sara thought about her friends. She thought about the playground. She thought about the stories and the songs and the games at school. She realized that school was fun. She said, "Sorry, mom. I was lying. I don't have a cold. I want to go to school." Her mom smiled. She said, "That's good, Sara. I'm proud of you for telling the truth. Now, get dressed and have some breakfast. You don't want to be late for school." Sara got out of bed. She gave Tom a hug. She said, "I hope you feel better soon, Tom. Maybe you can watch some TV with me when I come back from school." Tom said, "Thanks, Sara. Have a good day at school. Maybe I can play with you tomorrow." Sara and Tom were happy. They loved each other. They learned a lesson. They knew that lying was bad and that telling the truth was good. They knew that sometimes they had to do things they didn't want to do, but that they could also have fun and enjoy what they had. They knew that being envious was not nice and that being grateful was better. They were good kids.
