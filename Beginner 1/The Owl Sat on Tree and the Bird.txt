Category: Beginner
Title: The Owl Sat on Tree and the Bird

One day, a big owl sat on a tree. The owl was very dirty. He had mud on his wings. He wanted to clean up, but he didn't know how. He saw a little bird fly by very fast. The little bird had a lot of speed. "Help me, little bird," said the dirty owl. "I want to be clean like you. How do I clean my wings?" The little bird looked at the owl and said, "You need to fly fast like me. The speed will make the mud fly off your wings." So, the dirty owl tried to fly fast. He used all his speed. But the mud did not come off. The owl felt sad. He thought he would be dirty forever. Just then, a big rain cloud came over the tree. It started to rain. The rain washed the mud off the owl's wings. He was clean! The owl was happy and thanked the little bird. The little bird smiled and said, "Sometimes, help comes when you least expect it." They both flew off into the sky, happy and clean.

Anna loved to play with her dolls. She had a big dollhouse, with many rooms and furniture. She liked to pretend that her dolls were her friends, and that they had tea parties and adventures together. She always used her favorite cup, a pink one with flowers, to pour tea for her dolls. One day, her brother Tom came into her room. He was older and bigger than Anna, and he liked to tease her. He saw her cup on the table, and he had an idea. He said, "Hey, Anna, can I have some tea?" Anna was happy that Tom wanted to play with her. She said, "Sure, Tom, sit down and I'll pour you some." She picked up her cup and filled it with water from a pitcher. Tom took the cup and pretended to drink. He made a face and said, "Yuck, this tea is awful! It tastes like dirt!" He spat out the water and threw the cup on the floor. The cup broke into pieces, and Anna gasped. She loved her cup, and now it was gone. She felt angry and sad. She said, "Tom, you're mean! You broke my cup! Go away!" She started to cry. Tom felt bad. He didn't mean to break the cup, he just wanted to make a joke. He said, "I'm sorry, Anna, I didn't know you liked the cup so much. I was just playing. Please don't cry." He tried to hug her, but she pushed him away. She said, "No, you're not sorry. You don't care about me or my dolls. You don't believe in tea parties. You're a bad brother!" She ran to her bed and buried her face in her pillow. Tom felt worse. He wanted to make Anna happy, not sad. He looked at the broken cup, and he had an idea. He ran to his room and got some glue and tape. He came back to Anna's room and said, "Anna, look, I fixed your cup. See, it's good as new. I'm really sorry, Anna, I do care about you and your dolls. I do believe in tea parties. Can we be friends again?" He showed her the cup, which was still cracked and taped, but looked like a cup again. Anna looked at the cup, and then at Tom. She saw that he was sorry, and that he tried to fix his mistake. She felt a little better. She said, "Thank you, Tom, for fixing my cup. It's not the same, but it's still my cup. I'm sorry, too, for being mean to you. You're not a bad brother. You're a good brother. Can we have a tea party together?" She smiled and wiped her tears. Tom smiled, too. He said, "Yes, Anna, we can have a tea party together. I'll be your guest, and you'll be the host. You can pour me some tea, and I'll drink it. I won't make a face, I promise. I'll say it's the best tea ever." He sat down and took the cup. He said, "Cheers, Anna, to tea parties and friends." He clinked the cup with Anna's, and they both laughed. They had a tea party, and they had fun. They forgot about the broken cup, and they remembered that they loved each other.
