Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Bird

One day, a boy named Tim saw a big wall. He wanted to go to the other side. But the wall was too tall. Tim felt sad. Tim asked his friend, Sam, "Can you help me get over the wall?" Sam said, "We can work together." They tried to climb the wall, but it was still too hard. Tim and Sam felt tired. A kind bird saw them and said, "I can help you fly over the wall." Tim and Sam were grateful. They held onto the bird, and it took them to the other side. They were very happy and thanked the bird.

Lily and Ben were playing in the kitchen. They liked to pretend they were making tea and cake for their dolls. Lily had a pink cup and Ben had a blue cup. They filled their cups with water from the tap and poured it into a big pot. "Let's make some tea!" Lily said. "We need to boil the water first." She turned on the stove and put the pot on it. She did not know that the stove was hot and dangerous. She just wanted to play. Ben saw what Lily did and got scared. He knew that the stove was not for playing. He tried to stop Lily. "No, Lily, no!" he shouted. "The stove is hot! It can burn you! Turn it off!" But Lily did not listen. She thought Ben was being silly. She wanted to make tea. "Go away, Ben!" she said. "I know what I'm doing. This is my game. You can't tell me what to do!" She reached for the pot handle. But the pot was too hot. It burned her hand. She screamed and dropped the pot. The water spilled everywhere. It made a big mess. Lily started to cry. She was hurt and scared. Ben ran to her and hugged her. He was sorry for her. "I'm sorry, Lily, I'm sorry!" he said. "Are you okay? Let me see your hand." He looked at her hand. It was red and sore. He got a towel and wrapped it around her hand. He tried to dry it and make it feel better. "Let's go to Mommy," he said. "She can help you. She can put some cream on your hand. It will heal soon." He took Lily by the hand and led her to the living room. They left their cups on the floor. They forgot about their game. Mommy was sitting on the couch. She was reading a book. It was an expensive book. She liked it very much. She did not hear Lily and Ben in the kitchen. She did not know what they did. She saw them coming. She saw Lily's hand. She saw the towel. She got worried. "What happened?" she asked. "What did you do? Are you hurt?" Lily and Ben told her what happened. They told her they were playing in the kitchen. They told her they were making tea. They told her they turned on the stove. They told her they spilled the water. They told her they were sorry. Mommy was angry and sad. She was angry that they played with the stove. She was sad that they hurt themselves. She was also angry that they made a mess. She was also sad that they ruined her book. She took Lily to the bathroom. She washed her hand. She put some cream on it. She put a bandage on it. She kissed it and hugged her. She took Ben to the kitchen. She cleaned the water. She picked up their cups. She saw her book. It was wet and torn. She sighed and threw it away. She took them to the couch. She sat them down. She looked at them. "I love you, Lily and Ben," she said. "But you have to be careful. You have to listen. You have to obey. The stove is not for playing. It is for cooking. It is hot and dangerous. You could have burned yourself badly. You could have started a fire. You could have hurt Mommy too." She paused and took a deep breath. "And you have to respect Mommy's things. You have to be gentle. You have to be careful. This was my book. It was expensive. It was special. It was a gift from Daddy. You ruined it. You made me sad." She paused and wiped a tear. "I'm sorry, Mommy," Lily and Ben said. "We're sorry. We didn't mean to. We were just playing. We won't do it again. We love you, Mommy." They hugged her and kissed her. They said they were sorry again. Mommy smiled and hugged them back. She said she loved them too. She said she forgave them. She said they were good kids. She said they could play again. But not in the kitchen. And not with the stove. And not with her books. She said they could play with their dolls. And their cups. And their water. But only in their room. And only with a plastic pot. And only with cold water. She said they could make tea and cake. But only pretend. And only for fun. And only with love.
