Category: Beginner
Title: The Tree and the Cat

Once upon a time, there was a big tree. Under the big tree, there was a little house. In the little house, there was a cat. The cat was very happy. One day, the cat went for a walk. The cat saw a big ball. The big ball was red. The cat played with the big ball. The cat was very happy. The cat went back to the little house under the big tree. The cat took a nap. The cat had a good day. The cat was very happy.

One day, a dog named Spot went for a jog. Spot liked to jog in his own style. He would run fast, then slow, then fast again. Spot was a happy dog. As Spot jogged, he saw a big hill. The hill was very high. Spot wanted to jog up the hill. He knew it would be hard, but he wanted to try. Spot jogged up the hill. He went slow at first, then fast. He was tired, but he did not stop. When Spot reached the top of the high hill, he looked down and saw his home. Spot was proud of himself for jogging up the high hill in his own style.

Once upon a time, there was a gifted boy named Tim. Tim loved to collect things. He had many toys, but his favorite thing to collect was stars. Tim would look at the night sky and wish he could touch them. One night, Tim had a plan. He took a big ladder and put it against his house. He climbed up and up, trying to reach the stars. He thought he could bring them home and show his friends. But as Tim climbed higher, he could not reach the stars. Then, something unexpected happened. The stars came down to him! They danced around him, making him happy. Tim learned that he didn't need to collect stars. They were his friends, and they would always be there at night to make him smile.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to eat. One day, Sue's mom gave her a big plate of food. The plate had a lot of dry bread on it. Sue did not like dry bread. She wanted to quit eating it. Sue's mom told her a story. The story was about a little bird who did not like to eat dry bread too. But the bird's mom said, "If you eat the dry bread, you will grow big and strong." The little bird ate the dry bread and became big and strong. Sue thought about the story. She did not want to quit eating the dry bread now. She ate all the dry bread on her plate. Sue felt proud. She knew that eating the dry bread would make her big and strong, just like the little bird. And that day, Sue learned that sometimes, we have to do things we don't like to become better.

Once upon a time, in a big field, there was a lot of green grass. Many animals lived there and played all day. They were very happy. One day, a little rabbit was playing in the grass. He was having fun, but he was also a bit foolish. He liked to play tricks on his friends. This time, he wanted to scare them. The little rabbit hid in the grass and shouted, "Help! Help!" All his friends started to panic. They ran to help the little rabbit, but when they found him, he was just laughing. "It was just a joke!" he said. His friends were not happy. The next day, the little rabbit was playing again. This time, he really needed help. But his friends didn't come. They thought he was being foolish again. The little rabbit learned that it's not good to scare your friends.

Once upon a time, there was a bald man named Tom. Tom had no hair on his head. He lived in a small house near the woods. Tom liked to trade toys with his friends. One day, Tom went to his friend's house to trade a red ball for a blue car. On the way, he met a big bird. The bird saw Tom's bald head and started to tease him. The bird said, "Ha! You have no hair on your head!" Tom felt sad and walked away. He went to his friend's house and traded his red ball for the blue car. Tom's friend saw that he was sad and asked, "Why are you sad, Tom?" Tom told his friend about the bird that teased him. Tom's friend said, "Don't be sad, Tom. We love you just the way you are." Tom felt happy again. He played with his new blue car and forgot about the big bird. And from that day on, Tom knew that true friends don't care if you are bald or have hair. They love you for who you are.
