Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Story

One day, a boy named Tim found a mop. The mop was rough. Tim wanted to use the mop to clean his room. He said, "I will clean my room with this mop." Tim started to clean, but the mop did not work well. It was too rough and made a mess. Tim's mom came in and saw the mess. She said, "Tim, you need to quit using that mop. It is too rough." Tim felt sad. He wanted to clean his room, but the mop was not good. Then, a girl named Sue came in. She had a soft mop. Sue said, "Let's trade mops. My mop is soft and will clean better." Tim and Sue traded mops. Tim's room got clean and he was happy. The moral of the story is to ask for help when you need it and to not give up when things get hard.

Lily and Sam are friends. They like to watch dancers on TV. They see how they jump and spin and wear pretty clothes. They want to be dancers too. One day, they find a big box in the garage. It has old clothes and shoes and hats. They decide to play dress up and pretend to be dancers. They put on the clothes and shoes and hats. They look funny and laugh. They go to the living room and turn on the TV. They see a dancer show. They try to copy what the dancers do. They jump and spin and wave their arms. They have fun. But then, Sam jumps too high and lands on his foot. He cries out. He feels hurt. He can't stand on his foot. Lily stops and runs to him. She sees his foot is red and swollen. "Are you OK, Sam?" she asks. She is worried. "No, Lily. It hurts a lot. I can't dance anymore." he says. He is sad. Lily hugs him. She says, "Don't worry, Sam. I will help you. We can call Mom. She will make it better. You will dance again soon." She helps him sit on the couch. She gets the phone and calls Mom. She tells her what happened. Mom says she will come home soon and take Sam to the doctor. Lily and Sam wait for Mom. They watch the dancer show on TV. They still like to watch dancers. But they are more careful now. They don't want to get hurt again.

Once upon a time, there was a big bookcase in a little girl's room. The bookcase had many books. The little girl loved to read. She had a dog named Spot. Spot was a deaf dog, but he was very happy. One day, the little girl's mom said she would allow her to pick one book from the bookcase to read before bed. The girl was so excited. She looked at the bookcase and saw a book about a cat and a dog. She thought Spot would like the story too. That night, the little girl read the book to Spot. In the story, the cat and the dog became best friends. The little girl and Spot smiled. They knew they were best friends too. The book from the bookcase made them both very happy.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play together. They have many toys and books. But sometimes they fight over them. They do not like to share. One day, their mom says, "I have to go to university. I have a big test. You have to stay with grandma. She will take care of you." Anna and Ben are sad. They do not want their mom to go. They hug her and say, "We will miss you, mom. Please come back soon." Mom smiles and says, "I will miss you too, my loves. But I have to go. University is important. It helps me learn new things. I will be back in the afternoon. Be good and listen to grandma." Mom leaves. Anna and Ben wave goodbye. Then they go to their room. They see their toys and books. They want to play. Anna picks up a doll. She says, "This is my doll. I want to play with her." Ben sees a car. He says, "This is my car. I want to play with it." They play happily for a while. But then they get bored. They look at each other's toys. They want to try them. Anna says, "Can I play with your car, Ben? It looks fun." Ben says, "No, you cannot. This is my car. You have your doll." Anna says, "But I want to play with your car. You are selfish. You do not share." Ben says, "You are selfish too. You do not share your doll." They start to argue. They raise their voices. They pull each other's toys. Grandma hears them. She comes to their room. She sees them fighting. She is not happy. She says, "Stop it, Anna and Ben. You are being naughty. Why are you fighting over your toys? You have enough toys for both of you. You should share and take turns. That is what good children do." Anna and Ben look at grandma. They feel ashamed. They know she is right. They say, "We are sorry, grandma. We were wrong. We will share and take turns." Grandma says, "That is good. I am glad you understand. Sharing and taking turns make you happy and kind. Now, why don't you hug and say sorry to each other?" Anna and Ben hug and say sorry. They smile and say, "We love you, grandma. Thank you for teaching us." Grandma says, "I love you too, my dears. You are welcome. Now, let's go to the kitchen. I have made some cookies for you. You can have one each. But you have to part them with me."
