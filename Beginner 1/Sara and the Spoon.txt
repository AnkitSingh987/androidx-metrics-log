Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and the Spoon

Sara likes to learn new things. She likes to learn how to draw, how to sing, and how to count. But she does not like to learn how to eat yogurt. She thinks yogurt is yucky and slimy. One day, her mom gives her a bowl of yogurt for breakfast. Sara makes a face and says, "No, thank you. I do not want yogurt. I want toast." Her mom says, "You have to try it, Sara. Yogurt is good for you. It has milk and fruit and helps you grow." Sara does not want to grow. She wants to stay small and play with her toys. She says, "No, thank you. I do not want to grow. I want to have fun." Her mom says, "You can have fun and grow, Sara. Eating yogurt is fun. You can make funny shapes with your spoon, like a star or a heart." Sara looks at the bowl of yogurt. She sees some red bits and some blue bits. She thinks they are berries. She likes berries. She picks up her spoon and dips it in the yogurt. She makes a star shape with the yogurt and the berries. She thinks it looks pretty. She brings the spoon to her mouth and takes a small bite. She is surprised. The yogurt is not yucky or slimy. It is sweet and creamy. She likes the yogurt. She takes another bite and makes a heart shape. She smiles and says, "Yogurt is yummy. I like yogurt. Thank you, mom." Her mom smiles and says, "You're welcome, Sara. I'm glad you learned to like yogurt. You are a good girl." Sara feels happy and proud. She eats the rest of her yogurt and asks for more. She learns that trying new things can be fun and tasty.

Tom and Anna are going to visit their grandma. They are very happy. They want to pack their toys and books in their bags. Mom says they can only take a few things. She helps them choose. "Can I take this?" Tom asks, holding a long train. "No, Tom, that is too big. It will not fit in your bag. Pick something smaller," Mom says. "What about this?" Anna asks, showing a doll. "Yes, Anna, you can take that. It is not too big. Put it in your bag," Mom says. Tom and Anna finish packing. They put their bags by the door. They go to the kitchen to have a snack. Mom goes to the cabinet to get some cookies. She opens the cabinet. CRASH! The cabinet falls down. It is broken. It hits Mom on the head. She falls down too. She is hurt. She does not move. Tom and Anna hear the noise. They run to the kitchen. They see Mom on the floor. They see the cabinet on top of her. They are scared. They cry. "Mom! Mom! Wake up!" Tom shouts. "Mom! Mom! Please be okay!" Anna cries. But Mom does not wake up. She does not say anything. She does not move. Tom and Anna are very sad. They do not go to visit their grandma. They do not play with their toys and books. They wait for Dad to come home. They hope he can help Mom. They hope Mom will be okay. But they do not know. They are afraid.

Once upon a time, there was a kind boy named Tim. He liked to help his mom in the kitchen. One day, his mom asked him to serve food to their friends. Tim was excited and said, "Yes, Mom! I will serve the food." His mom gave him a big spoon. It was a tool to help him serve the food. Tim took the spoon and started to serve the food to their friends. But, when he scooped the food, the spoon broke. It was a fake tool! Tim was sad and said, "Mom, the spoon broke." His mom smiled and said, "That's okay, Tim. We can use another spoon." She gave him a real spoon and they served the food together. Their friends were happy and enjoyed the meal. In the end, Tim learned that it's important to use the right tools for the job. He also learned that even when things go wrong, it's okay because we can always try again.
