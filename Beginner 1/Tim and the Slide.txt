Category: Beginner
Title: Tim and the Slide

One day, a little boy named Tim went to play at the park. He saw a big slide and wanted to try it. Tim felt excited and ran to the slide. He climbed up the steps and sat at the top. He looked down and felt a bit scared, but he was ready to slide. As Tim started to slide, he saw a sharp point on the slide. He got confused and didn't know what to do. He tried to stop, but he slid too fast. The point on the slide was getting closer and closer. Tim reached the point and it hurt him. He cried out loud and felt sad. His fun day at the park was now a bad day. Tim wished the slide didn't have that point, so he could play and be happy.

Once upon a time, there was a hairy dog named Spot. Spot loved to run and play in the park. One day, Spot saw a big red ball. Spot wanted to play with the ball, so his owner said, "Release!" Spot ran fast to the ball and started to play. While Spot was playing, a big shock came. It was a loud sound from the sky. Spot got scared and ran away from the park. Spot's owner tried to find him, but Spot was too fast. Spot ran and ran, trying to get away from the shock. Spot ran far away and got lost. He was very sad, and his owner was sad too. They could not find each other. Spot was alone, and his owner missed him very much. The bad ending made them both sad, and they wished they could be together again.

One day, a thin cat named Tom went to the farm. Tom was hungry and wanted to eat. He saw hay in the farm and thought it was food. He took a big bite, but it was not good. Tom was sad. Tom saw a cow named Daisy. He asked, "Daisy, do you have some food?" Daisy said, "I eat hay, but I can release some milk for you." Tom was happy and drank the milk. It was yummy. Just then, a big wind came and blew the hay away. Tom and Daisy were scared. But, the wind also brought some fish from the river. Tom and Daisy ate the fish and were happy again. The wind had brought them a surprise treat.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a boy named Tim. Tim loved to fix things. He had a big box of tools. Tim was very flexible. He could bend and twist to reach anything. One day, Tim found a broken toy car. He said, "I will fix this car and make it like new." Tim worked hard to fix the toy car. He used his flexible arms to reach all the small parts. He put the wheels back on, and painted the car red. When he was done, the car looked new. Tim was happy. He said, "Now, I will find the owner of this car." Tim went to the park where he found the car. He saw a little girl named Lily. Lily was sad because she lost her toy car. Tim went up to her and said, "Is this your car? I found it and fixed it for you." Lily looked at the car and smiled. She said, "Yes, it is my car! Thank you, Tim!" Lily and Tim played with the car in the park. They had a race to see who could push the car the fastest. Tim was fast, but Lily was faster. She won the race. Tim said, "Good job, Lily! You win!" They both laughed and played with the car until it was time to go home. Tim and Lily became best friends, and they always helped each other fix things.

Lily and Ben were playing in the garden. They saw a big jar of powder on the table. It was white and soft. "What is this?" Lily asked. "I don't know. Maybe it is snow," Ben said. They opened the jar and took some powder in their hands. They threw it in the air and watched it fall. It looked like snow, but it did not feel cold. "Let's spread it on the grass," Lily said. "It will be fun." They took more powder and ran around the garden. They spread it on the grass, on the flowers, on the trees, and on each other. They laughed and shouted. But then, they heard a voice. "Hey, what are you doing?" It was Mom. She looked angry. She came to the garden and saw the mess. The powder was everywhere. It was not snow. It was flour. "Flour? Why did you take the flour?" Mom asked. "We thought it was snow," Lily said. "We wanted to play," Ben said. Mom shook her head. "Flour is not for playing. Flour is for baking. You wasted a lot of flour. And you made a big mess. You have to clean it up." Lily and Ben felt sorry. They did not know they did something bad. They thought they were having fun. "Sorry, Mom," they said. Mom sighed. "It's okay. I know you did not mean to. But next time, ask me before you take something. And don't play with boring things like flour. There are many other things you can play with. Come on, I'll show you." She took them inside and gave them some toys. They played with the toys and forgot about the flour. They learned their lesson. Flour is not snow. Flour is boring.
