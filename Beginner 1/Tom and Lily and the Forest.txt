Category: Beginner
Title: Tom and Lily and the Forest

Tom and Lily were twins who liked to play outside. One day, they decided to explore the forest behind their house. They packed some snacks and water in their backpacks and told their mom where they were going. "Be careful and don't go too far," their mom said. "And come back before it gets dark." "OK, mom," Tom and Lily said. They put on their hats and shoes and ran to the forest. The forest was full of trees and flowers and animals. Tom and Lily saw a squirrel, a bird, a rabbit and a butterfly. They followed a path that led them deeper into the forest. They were having so much fun that they did not notice how far they had gone. "Look, Lily, a pond!" Tom said, pointing to a shiny spot of water. "Let's go see if there are any fish." "OK, Tom," Lily said. They ran towards the pond, but they did not see a big root sticking out of the ground. Tom stumbled over the root and fell down. He scraped his knee and it started to bleed. "Ow, ow, ow!" Tom cried. "It hurts!" Lily ran to him and helped him up. She saw his knee and felt sorry for him. "Don't cry, Tom," she said. "It's OK. I have a band-aid in my backpack. We can put it on your knee and it will feel better." She opened her backpack and looked for a band-aid. But she could not find one. She had forgotten to pack any. "Oh, no," she said. "I don't have any band-aids. What can we do?" Tom looked around and saw some big leaves on a nearby plant. He had an idea. "Maybe we can use these leaves," he said. "They are soft and green and available. We can wrap them around your knee and make a leaf band-aid." Lily thought that was a good idea. She picked some leaves and gave them to Tom. He wrapped them around his knee and tied them with a twig. "There," he said. "How does that feel?" "It feels better," Tom said. "Thank you, Lily. You are a good sister." "You're welcome, Tom. You are a good brother," Lily said. They hugged each other and smiled. They decided to go back home and show their mom their leaf band-aid. They followed the path and found their way out of the forest. They ran to their house and told their mom what had happened. Their mom was glad that they were safe and that they had helped each other. She praised them for being brave and creative. She also gave them a real band-aid and a kiss. Tom and Lily were happy. They had a fun adventure in the forest and learned a new way to make a band-aid. They decided to go back to the forest another day and see what else they could find.

Once upon a time, there was a small boy named Tim. He lived in a tiny house with his mom and dad. Tim loved to eat pastry. His mom would make the best pastry in their little town. One day, Tim saw a big bird flying in the distant sky. The bird was so big and pretty. Tim wanted to be friends with the big bird. So, he decided to pray. He closed his eyes and said, "Please, big bird, come be my friend." The next day, the big bird came to Tim's house. Tim was so happy! He gave the bird some pastry to eat. The bird loved the pastry too. From that day on, Tim and the big bird were the best of friends. They played together every day and shared many pastries.

Tom and Anna are friends. They like to play in the park. They like to swing on the swings. They swing high and low. They laugh and have fun. One day, Tom has a birthday. He gets a present from his mom. It is a big red ball. He is very happy. He wants to show Anna his present. He runs to the park with his ball. He sees Anna on the swing. He calls her name. "Anna, Anna, look at my present! It is a big red ball!" Anna stops swinging. She looks at Tom's present. She thinks it is very interesting. She wants to play with it. She jumps off the swing and runs to Tom. "Can I play with your ball, Tom? Please, please, please!" Tom is not sure. He likes his ball. He does not want to share it. He says, "No, Anna, this is my ball. It is my present. You cannot play with it. Go away!" Anna is sad. She feels hurt. She says, "But Tom, we are friends. Friends share. Why are you so mean?" Tom does not listen. He turns away. He plays with his ball by himself. He bounces it and kicks it. He thinks he is having fun. But soon, he feels lonely. He misses Anna. He looks around. He does not see her. He wonders where she is. He feels sorry. He thinks he was mean. He wants to say sorry. He wants to share his ball. He runs to the swings. He hopes to find Anna there. But he does not. He sees another boy on the swing. He has a present too. It is a yellow truck. It is very interesting. Tom wants to play with the truck. He goes to the boy. He says, "Hi, can I play with your truck? Please, please, please!" The boy looks at Tom. He says, "No, this is my truck. It is my present. You cannot play with it. Go away!" Tom feels sad. He feels hurt. He knows how Anna felt. He wishes he was nicer to her. He learns a lesson. He learns to share. He learns to be a good friend. He runs to find Anna. He hopes she is still in the park. He hopes she will forgive him. He hopes they can play together. He hopes they can swing on the swings. He hopes they can laugh and have fun.
