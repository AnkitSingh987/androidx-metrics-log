Category: Beginner
Title: Bow the Dog and the Cat

Once upon a time, there was a little dog named Bow. Bow liked to play and run. One day, Bow met a big cat. The cat was very difficult to be friends with. But Bow wanted to try. Bow said to the cat, "Do you want to play with me?" The cat looked at Bow and said, "I don't know. What do you like to play?" Bow said, "I like to play catch and run." The cat thought about it and said, "Okay, let's play." While they were playing, Bow started to sneeze. "Achoo! Achoo!" Bow sneezed again and again. The cat asked, "Why do you sneeze?" Bow said, "I don't know. Maybe I am sick." The cat said, "You should go home and rest." Bow went home and took a nap. When Bow woke up, he felt better. The next day, Bow and the cat played together again. They became good friends and played every day. And Bow did not sneeze anymore.

Tim and Sam were best friends. They liked to play with toys and books and cars. One day, they found a big box in the garage. Inside the box, there were many things that looked old and strange. They saw a skull. It was white and hard and had no eyes or nose. "Wow, look at this!" Tim said. "It is a skull. It is from a wild animal. Maybe a lion or a bear." "Can I see it?" Sam asked. He wanted to touch the skull and feel its teeth. "OK, but be careful. It is mine. I found it first." Tim said. He gave the skull to Sam. Sam held the skull in his hands. He liked how it felt. He put it on his head and pretended to be a wild animal. He roared and growled and ran around the garage. "Give it back to me!" Tim said. He was angry. He wanted his skull back. He tried to grab it from Sam. "No, it is mine now. I like it. I want to keep it." Sam said. He ran away from Tim. He did not want to share. They started to fight. They pushed and pulled and shouted. They did not act like friends. They acted like wild animals. "Stop it, both of you!" A voice said. It was Mom. She heard the noise and came to the garage. She saw the skull and the box and the boys. "What are you doing? Where did you get this?" Mom asked. She was not happy. She took the skull from Sam and put it back in the box. "This is not a toy. This is a skull. It is from a museum. Dad borrowed it for his work. He is a scientist. He studies old things. You are not supposed to touch it. You could break it or hurt yourself." Mom said. "Sorry, Mom." Tim and Sam said. They felt bad. They did not know the skull was important. They did not mean to make Mom angry. "Next time, ask before you borrow something. And share with each other. You are friends, not wild animals." Mom said. She hugged them and gave them a kiss. "OK, Mom." Tim and Sam said. They hugged each other and said sorry. They decided to play with something else. They learned their lesson. They did not want to fight over a skull again.

Once upon a time, there was a large frog named Fred. Fred lived in a pond with many fish. Every day, Fred would jump and play with his friends. One day, Fred saw a big hole in the ground. It was a drain. The water from the pond was going into the drain. Fred was scared that the pond would become empty. Fred had an idea. He asked all his friends to help him fill the drain with rocks and mud. They worked together and filled the drain. The pond stayed full and everyone was happy. Fred and his friends played and splashed in the water all day long.

Jack loved playing with his trucks. He was three years old and liked to race them around the house. But his wife didn't like it because it made too much noise. She asked him to play with something else, but he refused. "No," he shouted and continued playing. His wife was getting frustrated. She asked him to stop, but Jack still refused. "Please," she said again. "Why won't you listen to me?" But Jack wouldn't stop. He kept racing his trucks around. Finally, his wife was so frustrated she yelled at him. "Stop it right now!" Jack was so scared he dropped his trucks and ran to his room. He didn't want to speak to anyone. His wife felt bad, but she knew she had done the right thing. She left him alone until he was ready to talk.
