Category: Beginner
Title: Jill and the Hill

Once upon a time, there were two best friends. Their names were Jill and Bill. They lived at the bottom of a big hill. One day, Jill said to Bill, "I'm so happy we're friends!" Bill smiled and said, "Me too!" They decided to climb up the hill together. As they reached the top, they looked around and saw a beautiful view. They hugged each other and shared an honest smile. Then they ran back down the hill, laughing and holding hands. When they got back to the bottom, Jill said, "That was so fun. We should do it again!" Bill nodded and said, "You're right. Let's hug to show we're still best friends!" Jill and Bill hugged each other and stayed that way for the longest time. Jill said, "I'm so happy we're friends. I'll always be honest and be a good friend to you." Bill agreed and said, "Me too!"

Sara and Anna are sisters. They like to play with their dolls. Sara has a doll with long, yellow hair. Anna has a doll with short, brown hair. Sara likes to comb her doll's hair and make it shiny. Anna wants to comb Sara's doll's hair too. "Can I comb your doll's hair?" Anna asks Sara. "No, you can't. It's my doll. You have your own doll," Sara says. "But your doll's hair is so pretty. I want to make it pretty too," Anna says. Sara is envious of Anna's doll. She thinks her doll's hair is boring. She wants to have a doll with short, brown hair. She has an idea. "OK, you can comb my doll's hair. But only if I can comb your doll's hair," Sara says. Anna is happy. She agrees. She takes Sara's doll and starts to comb her hair. Sara takes Anna's doll and starts to comb her hair. But Sara does not comb her hair gently. She combs her hair hard and fast. She wants to make her hair look bad. "Ow, ow, ow!" Anna's doll says. "You are hurting me. Stop it!" Anna hears her doll's cry. She looks at Sara. She sees that Sara is pulling her doll's hair. She sees that her doll's hair is messy and tangled. "Hey, what are you doing?" Anna says. "You are ruining my doll's hair. Give her back to me!" Sara does not want to give her back. She wants to keep her doll. She turns away from Anna. She holds her doll tight. "No, she is my doll now. You can have my doll. She has ugly hair," Sara says. Anna is angry. She tries to grab her doll from Sara. Sara pushes her away. They start to fight. They pull and tug and shout. "Stop, stop, stop!" their mom says. She hears the noise. She comes to their room. She sees the dolls and the sisters. She is not happy. "What is going on here?" she says. "Why are you fighting over the dolls? Why are you hurting the dolls?" Sara and Anna stop fighting. They look at their mom. They feel ashamed. They tell her what happened. They say they are sorry. Mom sighs. She picks up the dolls. She combs their hair gently. She makes them look nice again. She gives them back to the sisters. "Look, the dolls are beautiful. They have different hair, but they are both lovely. Just like you two. You are sisters. You should love each other and share your toys. Not be envious and mean. Do you understand?" she says. Sara and Anna nod. They understand. They hug their mom. They hug their dolls. They hug each other. "I'm sorry, Sara. I should have asked you nicely. Can I comb your doll's hair sometimes?" Anna says. "I'm sorry, Anna. I should have let you comb my doll's hair. Can I comb your doll's hair sometimes?" Sara says. They smile. They agree. They play with their dolls together. They are happy.

Ben and Tom are friends. They like to play football in the park. One day, they find a big box near the trees. They open the box and see many things inside. There are books, toys, clothes, and a shiny football. "Wow, look at this football!" Ben says. "It is new and red. Can we play with it?" Tom nods. He likes the football too. He takes it out of the box and kicks it to Ben. Ben kicks it back to Tom. They have fun with the football. But then, a man comes to the park. He looks angry. He sees the box and the football. "Hey, you kids!" he shouts. "That is my box and my football! Give them back to me!" Ben and Tom are scared. They do not know the man. They do not want to give back the football. "But we found them here," Ben says. "They are not yours." "They are mine!" the man says. "I left them here for a while. They are very useful to me. The football is a gift for my son. The books are for my work. The toys and clothes are for charity. Now, give them back or I will call the police!" Ben and Tom do not want to make the man more angry. They do not want to go to the police. They decide to give back the football and the box. They are sad. They liked the football. "Sorry, mister," Tom says. "We did not know. Here is your football and your box." The man takes the football and the box. He does not say thank you. He walks away. Ben and Tom look at each other. They are still friends. They do not need the football to play. They can play with other things. They go to the swings and have fun.
