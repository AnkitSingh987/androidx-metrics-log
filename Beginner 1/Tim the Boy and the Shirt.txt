Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Shirt

One day, a boy named Tim had a new shirt. It was a fine shirt with a big red car on it. Tim wanted to show his friend, Sam, his new shirt. He put on the shirt and went to Sam's house. "Look, Sam!" said Tim. "I have a new shirt. It has a big red car on it." Sam looked at the shirt and said, "Wow, that is a fine shirt. I wish I had one like it." Just then, a big wind came. It blew very hard. The wind blew Tim's shirt right off his back! The shirt went up, up, up into the sky. Tim and Sam were very sad. They did not know what to do. But then, a big bird flew down from the sky. The bird had Tim's shirt in its beak! The bird gave the shirt back to Tim. Tim and Sam were so happy. They thanked the bird, and Tim put his fine shirt back on.

One day, a little boy named Tim went for a walk in the woods. He saw a trail and decided to follow it. The trail was not smooth, but a little harsh with rocks and sticks. Tim was very excited to see where the trail would lead him. As he walked, he met a talking rabbit. The rabbit said, "Hello, Tim! Where are you going?" Tim replied, "I am following this trail. I want to see where it goes!" The rabbit decided to join Tim on his walk. They talked and laughed together as they walked on the trail. After walking for a while, they reached the end of the trail. They found a big, beautiful rainbow. Tim and the rabbit were so happy to see it. They both knew that they had found something very special. Tim replied, "This was the best walk ever!" The rabbit agreed, and they both went back home, excited to tell their friends about their adventure.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there lived a boy named Tim. Tim had a plan to grow a pumpkin. He wanted to grow the biggest pumpkin ever. So, he went to his garden and planted a tiny seed in the ground. He watered it every day and waited for the pumpkin to grow. One day, Tim saw a tiny green thing coming out of the ground. It was his pumpkin! He was very happy. But as the pumpkin grew bigger and bigger, it started to look weird. It was not round and orange like other pumpkins. It was bumpy and had many colors. Tim's friends laughed at his weird pumpkin, but he did not care. He loved his pumpkin and knew it was special. When Halloween came, Tim made a funny face on his pumpkin and put a light inside. Everyone in the town loved Tim's weird pumpkin and said it was the best one they had ever seen.

Tim likes to play outside, but he does not like to bathe. He thinks water is cold and soap is icky. He runs away from his mom when she calls him to the bathroom. He hides under the bed and hopes she will not find him. But his mom is smart and she knows where he is. She looks under the bed and sees his shadow. She says, "Tim, I can see you. Come out and bathe, or you will be dirty and smelly. You don't want that, do you?" Tim shakes his head, but he is still scared. He says, "Mom, please don't make me bathe. I don't like it. It hurts my eyes and my nose. Can I just wipe with a towel?" His mom shakes her head, too. She says, "No, Tim, that is not enough. You need to bathe with water and soap to wash away the dirt and germs. It will not hurt you if you close your eyes and hold your nose. I will help you and make it fun. We can play with bubbles and toys and sing songs. How about that?" Tim thinks for a moment. He likes bubbles and toys and songs. He does not want to be dirty and smelly. He decides to give it a try. He crawls out from under the bed and takes his mom's hand. She smiles and leads him to the bathroom. She fills the tub with warm water and adds some soap. She gives him a big rubber duck and a small boat. She says, "Look, Tim, you have a big duck and a small boat. You can make them swim and splash and race. Isn't that fun?" Tim nods and smiles. He puts the duck and the boat in the water and makes them move. He sees the bubbles and pops them with his fingers. He hears his mom sing a silly song and joins her. He forgets his fear and enjoys his bath. He says, "Mom, you were right. Bathing is not so bad. It is fun. Thank you for helping me." His mom hugs him and says, "You're welcome, Tim. I'm proud of you for being brave and trying something new. You are a good boy. And now you are clean and fresh and ready for bed. How do you feel?" Tim feels happy and cozy. He says, "I feel good, mom. I love you." His mom says, "I love you, too, Tim. Good night." She wraps him in a towel and dries him off. She puts on his pajamas and tucks him in his bed. She kisses him on the forehead and turns off the light. She says, "Sleep tight, Tim. Don't let the bedbugs bite." Tim giggles and says, "Don't worry, mom. They won't. They don't like soap." He closes his eyes and falls asleep. He dreams of ducks and boats and bubbles and songs. He does not see his shadow. He is not afraid. He is happy.
