Category: Beginner
Title: Max the Brown Dog and the Cat

Once upon a time, there was a brown dog named Max. Max was hungry, so he went to the kitchen. He saw a tray on the table with a big sandwich on it. Max wanted the sandwich, but the table was too high. He knew he had to struggle to get it. Max tried to jump up to the table, but he could not reach it. He tried again and again. Then, Max saw his friend, the cat. The cat was good at jumping. Max said, "Cat, can you help me get the sandwich?" The cat said, "Yes, I can help you, Max." The cat jumped up on the table and pushed the tray down. The sandwich fell off the tray and onto the floor. Max was very happy. He ate the sandwich and thanked the cat. From that day on, Max and the cat were best friends. They knew they could always help each other.

One day, a waffle lived in a small house. The waffle was fearful because it did not want to rot. It tried to stay away from wet places and be safe. The waffle had a friend who was a cupcake. They played together every day. One day, the waffle and cupcake went to the park. They played on the swings and the slide. They were very happy. But then, it started to rain. The waffle was scared because it did not want to get wet and rot. The waffle and the cupcake tried to run home, but it was raining too much. The waffle got wet and started to feel bad. The cupcake tried to help, but it was too late. The waffle got too wet and began to rot. The waffle was not happy anymore.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a clever cat named Sam. Sam liked to play on the street with his friends. They would run, jump, and chase each other all day long. One day, Sam found a big sack near a tree. He had a fun idea. He called his friends and said, "Let's play a new game with this sack!" They all agreed and were very excited. Sam and his friends took turns getting in the sack and rolling down the street. They laughed and had so much fun. The clever cat and his friends played with the sack until the sun went down. They all went home tired but happy, and they couldn't wait to play with the sack again the next day.

Ben was a bald boy who liked to play with his toys. He had many toys, but he did not value them. He would throw them, break them, and lose them. He did not care if they got dirty or hurt. One day, he went to visit his grandma. She had a big box of toys that she kept for him. She said, "These are your daddy's toys when he was a little boy. He valued them very much. He took good care of them and played with them gently. He loved to touch them and feel their shapes and colors. He gave them to me to keep for you. You can play with them, but you have to value them too." Ben was curious. He opened the box and saw many toys. He saw cars, trains, dolls, blocks, and books. He picked one toy and threw it on the floor. He heard a loud crack. He picked another toy and bit it. He tasted something bitter. He picked another toy and ripped it. He saw some stuffing come out. He did not like these toys. He did not value them. His grandma saw what he did. She was sad and angry. She said, "Ben, what are you doing? You are ruining your daddy's toys. He loved these toys. He would be very upset if he saw what you did. You do not value these toys. You do not value anything. You are a bad boy." Ben felt ashamed. He saw his grandma's tears and his daddy's toys. He realized he was wrong. He said, "I'm sorry, grandma. I'm sorry, daddy. I did not know these toys were so special. I did not mean to hurt them. I want to value them. I want to value you. Please forgive me." His grandma hugged him and said, "I forgive you, Ben. But you have to learn to value things. Things are not just for fun. They have stories and memories. They have feelings and meanings. They are gifts and treasures. You have to touch them with love and respect. You have to value them with your heart and mind. Then you will be a good boy." Ben nodded and said, "I understand, grandma. I will value things. I will value you. I love you, grandma. I love you, daddy. Thank you for the toys. I will play with them nicely. I will touch them gently. I will value them always."
