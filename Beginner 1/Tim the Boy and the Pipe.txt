Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Pipe

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim liked to play outside in the sun. He had a fine red ball that he would take with him everywhere he went. One day, Tim found a pipe in his yard. It was a big, blue pipe. Tim thought it was fun to put his ball in the pipe and watch it roll out the other side. He would laugh and clap his hands. Tim and his fine red ball played with the pipe all day long. His mom and dad watched him and smiled. They were happy to see Tim having fun. And that night, Tim went to bed with a big smile on his face, dreaming of his new pipe and the fun he would have tomorrow.

Ben and Lily were twins. They liked to play with their toys and books. But sometimes they fought over the same thing. One day, they found a big envelope in the mail. It had many stamps on it. The stamps had pictures of animals and places. Ben and Lily wanted the stamps. They both grabbed the envelope and pulled hard. The envelope ripped and the stamps fell out. Ben and Lily each picked up some stamps and ran to their rooms. They did not want to share. Mom saw what happened. She was not happy. She went to Ben's room and said, "Ben, why did you take the stamps? They are not yours. You have to share with Lily. Sharing is nice." Ben said, "But I like the stamps. They are pretty. Lily has enough toys. She does not need the stamps." Mom said, "That is not fair, Ben. Lily likes the stamps too. They are not toys. They are for sending letters. Maybe you can learn something from the stamps. They show you different things from the world. You can make a game with them. But you have to share with Lily. Or you can give them back to me." Ben thought for a moment. He did not want to give the stamps back to Mom. He wanted to keep them. But he did not want to be mean to Lily. He remembered that Lily sometimes shared her books and crayons with him. He felt sorry for making her sad. He said, "OK, Mom. I will share with Lily. Maybe we can play with the stamps together. Maybe we can make a collage or a puzzle. Maybe we can pretend we are traveling to the places on the stamps. That sounds fun." Mom smiled and said, "That is a good idea, Ben. I am proud of you. Sharing is fun. Come on, let's go to Lily's room and see if she wants to play with you." Ben and Lily hugged and said sorry to each other. They decided to share the stamps and play with them. They learned new words and facts from the stamps. They had a lot of fun. They did not think the stamps were boring anymore. They thought they were amazing.

One day, a cute bunny named Bob hopped near a stream. He saw a frog sitting on a rock. The frog said, "Hi, Bunny! Want to play with me?" Bob said, "Yes, but first, I must pay my friend Bird for the yummy berries he gave me." So, Bob hopped away to find Bird. He gave Bird some shiny rocks as a thank you for the berries. Just then, the stream started to make a big splash! A big fish jumped out and landed on the grass. The fish said, "Help! I can't swim on land!" Bob and Bird helped the fish back into the stream. The fish was happy and said, "Thank you, friends!" And they all played together by the stream.

One day, a little dog was lost in the snow. He was cold and hungry and scared. He shivered and whimpered and looked for a way home. He saw a big house with lights and smoke. He thought maybe there was a human inside who could help him. He ran to the door and scratched and barked. He hoped the human would hear him and open the door. He waited and waited, but no one came. He felt more and more cold and sad. He wondered if he would ever see his family again. Then he heard a loud noise and saw a big car. He thought maybe the human was in the car and would see him and take him in. He wagged his tail and ran to the car. He tried to get the human's attention and show him he was friendly. But the human did not see him. The human was angry and in a hurry. He did not care about the little dog. He drove fast and hit the little dog with his car. The little dog yelped and fell to the ground. He felt a lot of pain and could not move. The human did not stop or look back. He drove away and left the little dog alone in the snow. The little dog cried and wished he had a human who was kind and thoughtful. He closed his eyes and stopped breathing. He was gone.
