Category: Beginner
Title: Mia the Girl and the Orange

One day, a little girl named Mia went to the park with her mom. Mia saw her friend Tom playing with a big orange ball. She ran to him and said, "Hi, Tom! Can I play too?" Tom said, "Sure, Mia! Let's play catch!" They played catch with the big orange ball. They laughed and had lots of fun. After playing, Mia's mom came and said, "Mia, I have a snack for you." She gave Mia a piece of melon. Mia took a bite and said, "Yummy! I love melon. Do you want some, Tom?" Tom said, "Yes, please!" Mia's mom gave Tom a piece of melon too. They ate their melon and felt happy. Mia and Tom played in the park until it was time to go home. Mia's mom said, "Mia, it's time to go." Mia hugged Tom and said, "Bye, Tom! Let's play again soon!" Tom said, "Bye, Mia! I had fun today!"

One day, a girl named Sue had a tea part with her friends. They all sat down and got ready to drink tea. Sue poured tea into cups for her friends. They were all very happy to be at the tea part. As they started to drink the tea, they made a face. The tea was sour! Sue did not know why the tea was sour. They did not want to drink sour tea. They all looked at each other and wondered what to do. Then, a big twist! A small mouse came out from under the table. The mouse had put a lemon in the tea pot! Sue and her friends laughed. They took the lemon out and the tea was not sour anymore. They all enjoyed the tea part and even shared some tea with the mouse.

Once, there were two friends. One was named Ann and one was named Bill. Ann and Bill both wanted to do their best in their exam. But they disagreed about what to do. Ann wanted to practice the exam by herself. She wanted to do it alone. But Bill wanted to practice with Ann. He thought it would be more fun if they studied together. Ann and Bill agreed to disagree about the exam. They got a little jealous of how each other wanted to practice. They argued and said mean things to each other. But when the exam was over, Ann and Bill were friends again. The exam was now behind them and they were glad they could still be friends.

Once upon a time, there was a prince. The prince lived in a big, pretty house. He had many friends who liked to play with him. One day, the prince was playing with his friends. They were playing a game with a ball. The prince was very happy. But then, one of his friends threw the ball too hard. The ball hit the prince's face. It made him very angry. The angry prince wanted to slap his friend. But he knew that slapping was not nice. So, he took a deep breath and calmed down. The prince said sorry to his friend for being angry. His friend said sorry for throwing the ball too hard. They hugged and played happily again.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a big hurricane. The wind blew hard, and the rain fell fast. People in the town were scared, but they stayed inside their homes to be safe. One day, a little boy named Tim went outside to play after the hurricane. He saw a small hurt bird on the ground. It could not fly because its wing was hurt. Tim wanted to help the bird, so he picked it up and took it home. In his home, Tim tried to help the bird. He put a soft cloth around the hurt wing. While he was doing this, he stumbled but did not fall. The bird got better, and Tim was happy. After some days, the bird could fly again. Tim let the bird go, and it flew away into the sky. Tim felt good because he helped the hurt bird.

Once upon a time, there was a lazy cat named Tom. Tom loved to sleep all day. One hot day, Tom was very hot and he wanted to find a way to cool off. He saw a fan in the corner of the room. Tom walked up to the fan and said, "Hello, Fan. Can you help me stay cool?" The fan said, "Yes, Tom. I can help you. Just sit in front of me and I will blow cool air on you." So Tom sat in front of the fan and felt the cool air. He was so happy that he started to repeat, "Thank you, Fan. Thank you, Fan." Over and over again, Tom thanked the fan for helping him stay cool on the hot day. And the fan was happy to help Tom, the lazy cat.
