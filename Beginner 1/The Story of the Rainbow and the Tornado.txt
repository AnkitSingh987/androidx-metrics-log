Category: Beginner
Title: The Story of the Rainbow and the Tornado

One day, a colorful tornado came to town. Everyone was scared and ran away. But the three-year-old boy bravely stayed where he was. He watched the tornado move around the town, making a lot of noise. After a while, the tornado went away and everything was quiet again. The boy was very tired so he went home and rested. He lay down in his bed and thought about the tornado. He was happy that it was gone and everyone was safe. As he drifted off to sleep, he thought of the tornado being replaced by a rainbow of colorful birds.

Once upon a time, there was a big tree with many branches. On one branch, there lived a little bird named Tim. Tim was a serious bird who liked to keep his nest very clean. One sunny day, Tim decided to bathe in a small pond near the tree. As he splashed in the water, he saw a frog named Fred. Fred was sitting on a rock near the pond. "Hi, Fred!" Tim said. "Do you want to bathe with me?" Fred smiled and said, "Yes, Tim! I love to bathe too!" So, Tim and Fred played and bathed in the pond together. They had a lot of fun and became good friends. From that day on, they always bathed and played together in the pond.

Anna was very happy. She had an invitation to go to Lily's birthday party. She put on her pink dress and her shiny shoes. She wanted to look pretty for the party. She ran to the door and opened it. She saw her mom waiting for her in the car. She waved to her mom and ran down the steps. But she did not see the ice on the ground. She slipped and fell. Her dress got dirty and her shoes got broken. She felt a big pain in her knee. She started to cry. Her mom heard her cry and ran to her. She picked her up and hugged her. She said, "Oh, Anna, I'm so sorry. Are you okay? Let me see your knee." She looked at Anna's knee and saw a small cut. She said, "It's not too bad. I have a band-aid in the car. We can clean it and put it on. Then you can still go to the party. Do you want to go?" Anna nodded. She said, "Yes, I want to go. But my dress is dirty and my shoes are broken. I don't look pretty anymore." Her mom smiled and said, "You are always pretty, Anna. And I have a surprise for you. I have a new dress and a new pair of shoes in the car. They are for you. I wanted to give them to you at the party, but I think you need them now. Do you want to see them?" Anna smiled and said, "Yes, I want to see them. Thank you, mom. You are the best mom ever." Her mom kissed her and said, "You are welcome, Anna. You are the best daughter ever. Now let's go to the party and have some fun."

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big tree with many peaches. The peaches are red and yellow and look yummy. "Let us pick some peaches," Ben says. "We can eat them for snack." Anna nods. She likes peaches. They run to the tree and reach for the peaches. But the tree is very high. They cannot get the peaches. "We need a ladder," Ben says. "Or a stick. Or a chair." Anna looks around. She sees a big rock. She has an idea. "Ben, come here," she says. "I have a plan. We can work together." She tells Ben her plan. Ben likes it. They push the rock to the tree. They climb on the rock. Now they are closer to the peaches. "Good job, Anna," Ben says. "You are smart." "Thank you, Ben," Anna says. "You are strong." They smile at each other. They pick some peaches. They are happy. But then they hear a loud roar. They turn around. They see a big bear. The bear is hungry. He wants the peaches. He is angry. Anna and Ben are fearful. They drop the peaches. They jump off the rock. They run away. They scream for help. "Help, help, a bear, a bear!" they shout. The bear chases them. He is fast. He is scary. But then they see their moms. Their moms are in the park. They hear their screams. They see the bear. They are brave. They grab some sticks. They wave them at the bear. They yell at the bear. "Go away, go away, bad bear, bad bear!" they shout. The bear is afraid. He does not like the sticks. He does not like the moms. He runs away. He leaves the peaches. Anna and Ben are safe. They hug their moms. They thank their moms. "Thank you, mom, thank you, mom," they say. Their moms hug them back. They are proud of them. "You are brave, Anna, you are brave, Ben," they say. They take them to the tree. They pick some peaches. They share them with their moms. They are delicious. They are happy. They are friends. They are a team.
