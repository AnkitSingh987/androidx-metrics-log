Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Succeed

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim had a big head. He wanted to do something great. He wanted to succeed. One day, Tim saw a big tree. He wanted to climb it. Tim's mom said, "Be careful!" But Tim did not listen. He wanted to show he could succeed. He started to climb the tree. Tim went up and up. He felt sorry that he did not listen to his mom. Then, Tim fell. His head hit the ground. Tim did not succeed. He was hurt and sad. The end.

One day, a little girl named Sue found a weird card on the ground. It had a picture of a cat on it. She showed it to her friend, Tom. Tom said, "I think this card can change things!" The next day, they went to the park to try the card. Sue held the card and said, "Card, change the tree into a big ice cream!" Suddenly, the tree turned into a big ice cream cone. Sue and Tom were very happy and ate the ice cream. Later, they saw a little bird with a hurt wing. Sue said, "Let's use the card to help the bird!" Tom agreed. Sue held the card and said, "Card, change the bird's wing to be all better!" The bird's wing was all better and it flew away. Sue and Tom knew the weird card was magic and they used it to help their friends.

Tom liked to perform tricks for his friends. He had a red hat, a long stick and a shiny knife. He would wave the stick and say magic words, then pull out the knife and cut a rope or a fruit. His friends would clap and cheer for him. One day, Tom wanted to perform a new trick. He saw a big pumpkin in the garden and thought it would be fun to cut it with his knife. He took the pumpkin and put it on a table. He waved his stick and said his magic words. Then he grabbed his knife and tried to cut the pumpkin. But the pumpkin was too hard and the knife was too weak. Tom pushed and pushed, but the knife did not go in. He pushed harder and harder, but the knife only bent and broke. Tom felt sad and angry. He threw the knife and the stick on the ground and started to cry. His friends saw him and ran to him. They hugged him and said it was okay. They said they still liked his tricks and he was a good performer. They said they could help him find a new knife and a new stick. They said they could also help him carve the pumpkin and make a funny face on it. Tom smiled and thanked his friends. He felt better and happy. He said he was sorry for being mad and he wanted to share the pumpkin with them. They all agreed and went to the kitchen. They carved the pumpkin and made a funny face on it. They also made pumpkin pie and pumpkin seeds. They ate and laughed and had fun. Tom was glad he had such good friends. He decided to perform a new trick for them next time, but with a softer fruit.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. He had a box that he loved very much. The box was fragile, so he took good care of it. Tim liked to play with the box and include it in all his games. One day, Tim found a roll of tape. He thought it would be fun to use the tape to make the box stronger. So, he put tape all over the box. Tim was happy because now the box was not fragile anymore. Tim played with his strong box all day. But then, something bad happened. The tape was too strong and it made the box stick together. Tim could not open the box anymore. He was very sad because he could not play with his box again. The end.

Once upon a time, there was a playful dog named Spot. Spot loved to run and jump all day long. He had a best friend named Sam. Sam was a little boy who liked to play with Spot. One sunny day, Sam and Spot played pretend. Sam said, "Let's be chefs and make yummy food!" Spot barked and wagged his tail. They went to the kitchen and found a big plate. Sam put pretend food on the plate. He said, "Here, Spot! Try our tasty food!" Spot barked and pretended to eat the food. Sam and Spot played pretend all day. They made more pretend food and put it on the plate. They had so much fun being chefs. At the end of the day, Sam and Spot were very tired. They went to bed and had sweet dreams about their playful day.
