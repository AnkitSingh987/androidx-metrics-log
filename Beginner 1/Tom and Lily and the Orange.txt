Category: Beginner
Title: Tom and Lily and the Orange

Tom and Lily were friends who liked to play outside. One day, they saw a big orange on a tree. They wanted to eat the orange, but it was too high for them to reach. They had an idea. They found a big rock and put it under the tree. Then, they took turns to jump on the rock and try to grab the orange. But the rock was not stable, and they kept falling down. They did not give up. They jumped and jumped, but the orange was still too far. They started to get tired and angry. They began to push and shout at each other. "Stop it!" said a voice. It was Mom, who came to see what they were doing. She saw the rock, the orange, and the two sad and dirty children. She was not happy. She took them inside and gave them some water and cookies. She told them that jumping on a rock was dangerous and rude. She said that they should have asked for help or looked for another orange. She said that they should share and be kind to each other. Tom and Lily felt sorry for what they did. They hugged and said sorry to Mom and to each other. They learned that jumping is not always the best way to get what you want, and that friendship is more important than an orange.

Tim is a boy who likes to drink milk. He has a glass that he always uses for his milk. It is a blue glass with a picture of a cow on it. He thinks it is a reliable glass, because it never breaks or spills. One day, Tim wants to drink some milk, but he cannot find his glass. He looks in the kitchen, in the living room, in his bedroom, but he cannot see it anywhere. He feels sad and hungry. He asks his mom, "Where is my glass?" His mom says, "I don't know, Tim. Maybe you left it somewhere. Why don't you use another glass?" Tim shakes his head. He does not want another glass. He only wants his glass. He says, "No, thank you, mom. I will find my glass." He keeps looking for his glass. He goes to the bathroom, to the garage, to the garden, but he still cannot find it. He feels more sad and more hungry. He wonders, "Where is my glass?" Then he remembers something. He remembers that yesterday he played with his friend Sam. He remembers that he brought his glass with him, because he wanted to drink some milk at Sam's house. He remembers that he put his glass on a table in Sam's backyard. He thinks, "Maybe my glass is still at Sam's house." He runs to his mom and says, "Mom, I know where my glass is. It is at Sam's house. Can we go there and get it?" His mom smiles and says, "Sure, Tim. Let's go to Sam's house and get your glass. But first, you have to eat something. You must be very hungry." Tim nods and says, "OK, mom. But can I eat something quick? I really want my glass." His mom says, "Of course, Tim. You can eat something quick. But you have to be careful with your glass. It is a reliable glass, but it can still break or get lost." Tim says, "I know, mom. I will be careful with my glass. It is my favorite glass. I love my glass." He eats something quick and then he and his mom go to Sam's house. Sam is happy to see them. He says, "Hi, Tim. Hi, Tim's mom. Do you want to play?" Tim says, "Hi, Sam. Maybe later. Right now, I want my glass. Do you know where it is?" Sam says, "Oh, yes. I know where it is. It is on the table in the backyard. I saw it this morning. I was going to bring it to you, but I forgot. I'm sorry, Tim." Tim says, "That's OK, Sam. I'm glad you found it. Can I have it, please?" Sam says, "Sure, Tim. Here it is. Here is your glass." He gives Tim his glass. Tim takes it and hugs it. He says, "Thank you, Sam. Thank you for giving me my glass. It is a reliable glass. It never breaks or spills. It is a blue glass with a picture of a cow on it. It is my glass. I love my glass." He drinks some milk from his glass. He feels happy and full. He says, "Do you want some milk, Sam?" Sam says, "Yes, please, Tim. I like milk, too." Tim says, "OK, Sam. Let's go to the kitchen and get some milk. Then we can play." They go to the kitchen and get some milk. They drink their milk and then they play. They have fun. They are friends. They are happy. And Tim has his glass. His reliable glass. His blue glass with a picture of a cow on it. His glass. He loves his glass. The end.
