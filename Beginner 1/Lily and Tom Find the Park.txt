Category: Beginner
Title: Lily and Tom Find the Park

Lily and Tom were going to the park with Mom. They got in the car and Mom drove on the road. But there were many cars on the road. They were stuck in traffic. Lily and Tom were bored and hungry. "Mom, when will we get to the park?" Lily asked. "Soon, honey, soon. There is a lot of traffic today. Maybe there was an accident or something." Mom said. "Can we have a snack, Mom?" Tom asked. "Sure, sweetie, sure. I have some crackers and juice in the bag. Here you go." Mom gave them some food and drinks. Lily and Tom ate and drank. They liked the crackers and juice. They were not cheap. Mom bought them from the good store. They were tasty and fresh. But the traffic did not move. The sun was hot and bright. The car was warm and dry. Lily and Tom felt sleepy. They yawned and closed their eyes. "Mom, can we take a nap?" Lily asked. "Of course, honey, of course. You can rest your head on the pillow. I will wake you up when we get to the park." Mom said. Lily and Tom snuggled up on the back seat. They fell asleep. Mom smiled and turned on the radio. She hoped the traffic would clear soon. She wanted to play with Lily and Tom at the park. She loved them very much.

Once upon a time, there was an obedient dog named Spot. Spot liked to help his owner, Tim. Tim was a little boy with a big smile. They lived in a small, happy house. One day, Tim saw a big box with a ribbon on top. The box had a tag that said "Open me!" Tim tried to open the box, but it was too tight. He asked Spot to help him. Spot wagged his tail and was ready to help. Spot used his strong teeth to pull the ribbon. The ribbon came off, and the box popped open! Inside the box was a big, bouncy ball. Tim and Spot played with the ball all day long. They were very happy, and Tim was proud of his obedient friend, Spot.

Once upon a time, in a small forest, there was a little leaf. The leaf lived on a big tree with many other leaves. They all liked to play together, and they would dance when the wind blew. One day, the wind came and blew all the leaves off the tree, but the little leaf did not want to go. The little leaf was selfish and wanted to remain on the tree all by itself. The other leaves went far away, but the little leaf stayed. As the days went by, the little leaf was lonely. It missed its friends and wished it had not been so selfish. The little leaf learned that it was more fun to play with others than to be alone.

Once there was a silly bear, who was very furry. One day, he went out and saw a big fountain. He was so excited to try and drink from it. "Hi fountain!" he said. "Can I eat you?" He reached out his paw, but the fountain stayed still. The bear didn't understand and asked again. "Fountain, can I eat you?" The fountain still didn't answer. Then a little bird flew down. "No, silly bear," said the bird. "You can't eat a fountain. But you can drink the water. Go ahead, try it!" The bear was very happy and took a long drink. He drank and drank and felt cool and refreshed. "Thank you, fountain!" said the bear. "This was much better than trying to eat you!"

One day, a little girl named Lily went to the forest with her mom. They wanted to pick some berries for a pie. Lily liked berries very much. She had a basket to put them in. As they walked, they saw a big wolf lying on the grass. He looked very sleepy. His eyes were closed and he was snoring. Lily was curious. She wanted to see the wolf up close. She left her basket and tiptoed to the wolf. "Hello, Mr. Wolf," she said softly. "Are you having a nice nap?" The wolf did not answer. He was still snoring. Lily touched his fur. It was soft and warm. She smiled. She liked the wolf. She saw a stream nearby. She had an idea. She ran to the stream and filled a cup with water. She came back to the wolf and poured the water on his head. She wanted to wake him up and play with him. The wolf opened his eyes. He was wet and angry. He growled and showed his teeth. He did not like the water. He did not like the girl. He wanted to eat her. Lily was scared. She screamed and ran away. She dropped the cup and ran to her mom. Her mom heard her scream and saw the wolf. She grabbed a stick and waved it at the wolf. She shouted at him to go away. The wolf was afraid of the stick and the mom. He ran away too. He did not want to be hit. He did not want to be wet. He wanted to be sleepy. Lily and her mom hugged each other. They were safe and happy. They picked up their basket and the cup and went home. They made a pie with the berries. They did not pour water on anyone. They learned a lesson. They did not bother sleepy wolves.
