Category: Beginner
Title: Amy the Girl and the Park

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. She loved to play in the park. One sunny day, she saw a pretty rock on the ground. It was shiny and bright. "Wow!" said Amy. "What a pretty gem!" Amy's friend, Tim, came to play too. He saw the gem in Amy's hand. "Can I see?" he asked. Amy was eager to show her new friend the gem. "Look, Tim!" she said. "Isn't it pretty?" "Yes, it is!" Tim said. They played with the gem all day. They pretended it could make them become anything they wanted. Amy became a big, strong lion. Tim became a tall, fast giraffe. They had so much fun playing together. At the end of the day, Amy and Tim went home. They were happy they found the gem. They knew they would have many more fun days playing with it. And they did.

Tim and Sam are friends. They like to play with cars and trucks. They have many cars and trucks in their room. Some cars and trucks have motors. Motors make them go fast and make noises. One day, Tim and Sam find a big truck with a motor in the garage. They want to play with it. They take it to the yard. They turn on the motor. The truck goes vroom vroom. "Wow, this truck is cool!" Tim says. "Yes, it is!" Sam says. They take turns to drive the truck. They have fun. But then, Tim wants to drive the truck more. He does not want to share. He is bossy. "Give me the truck, Sam. It is my turn!" Tim says. "No, it is not. You had a long turn. It is my turn!" Sam says. They pull the truck. They fight. They are angry. Mom hears them. She comes to the yard. She sees them fighting. "What are you doing, boys?" Mom asks. "We are playing with the truck, Mom. But Tim is bossy. He does not want to share!" Sam says. "Is that true, Tim?" Mom asks. Tim is quiet. He knows he is wrong. Mom says, "Boys, you have to share. Sharing is nice. Sharing is fun. Sharing is how you learn to be friends. Do you want to learn to be friends?" Tim and Sam nod. They are sorry. "OK, then. Say sorry to each other. And take turns to play with the truck. And be careful with the motor. It is not a toy. It is for grown-ups." Mom says. Tim and Sam say sorry. They hug. They smile. They share the truck. They play nicely. They learn to be friends.

There was a lovely girl named Sue. She had a big sack. Sue wanted to fill the sack with things she liked. Sue walked around and put a ball in the sack. Then she put a toy car in the sack. The sack was heavy on her waist. But Sue was happy. Sue took the sack home. She showed her mom and dad. They smiled and said, "Good job, Sue!" Sue felt proud and played with her toys all day.

One day, a gray cat named Tom put on a suit. He looked very nice. Tom wanted to show his friends his new suit. So, he went outside to find his friends. Tom saw his friend, a bird named Sally. Sally said, "Wow, Tom! You look nice in your suit!" But as Tom walked, he heard a sound. His suit had split! Tom was sad. Sally wanted to help Tom feel better. Sally said, "Don't be sad, Tom. We can fix your suit." So, Sally and Tom went to find some string. They found some and fixed the suit. Tom was happy again. He said, "Thank you, Sally!" They played together all day, and Tom's suit stayed nice.

Once upon a time, there was a dependable dog named Spot. Spot loved to play outside with his friends. One day, while they were playing, Spot found some dust. He thought it would be fun to roll in the dust. Spot rolled and rolled in the dust. He got very dirty. His friends laughed at him, and Spot felt embarrass. He did not like being laughed at. Spot ran home to his mom. Spot's mom saw how dirty he was and gave him a bath. She told him that it was okay to feel embarrass, but that he was still a dependable dog. Spot felt better and went back to play with his friends. They all had fun together, and Spot learned that it was okay to be embarrass sometimes.
