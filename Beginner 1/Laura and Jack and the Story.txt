Category: Beginner
Title: Laura and Jack and the Story

Once upon a time there were two friends, Laura and Jack. They liked to dance every day. One day, Jack got a staff and was so excited. He told Laura to come and dance with him. She replied, "but I don't know how to use a staff!" Jack said, "Don't worry, it's easy. Just grab it and let's have a dance!" They were both really happy, so they started their dance with the staff. As they were spinning it around together, the staff hit Laura in the face and she started to cry. She said, "It's so bitter to get hit with your staff, Jack!" Jack promised to be more careful and gave Laura a hug. Then they both laughed and held hands as they danced around in circles. The moral of the story is to be careful when you use a staff and have fun when you dance.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play with his blocks. One day, he found a box of new blocks. They were very sharp. Tim's mom said, "Be patient, Tim. I will help you play with the sharp blocks." Tim did not want to wait. He wanted to play with the blocks right away. He started to build a big tower with the sharp blocks. Tim's mom saw him and said, "Tim, please wait. The blocks are sharp and can hurt you." But Tim didn't listen. He kept playing with the sharp blocks. Suddenly, he felt a big pain in his hand. He had cut himself on the sharp blocks. Tim cried and his mom came to help him. She said, "I told you to be patient, Tim. Now you are hurt." The moral of the story is to always listen to your parents and be patient.

Once there was a frog named Spring. Spring wore a cap on his head. He lived in a pond near a wealthy man's big house. Spring liked to hop and play with his friends all day. One day, Spring saw the wealthy man drop a shiny coin into the pond. Spring thought it was a toy and wanted to play with it. He put the coin in his cap and showed his friends. His friends told him it was not a toy but a special thing that could buy food and toys. Spring wanted to give the coin back to the wealthy man, but he could not find him. So, Spring and his friends decided to use the coin to help others. They bought food for hungry animals and toys for little kids. In the end, the wealthy man saw what Spring did and was very happy. He let Spring keep the cap and the shiny coin. The moral of the story is to always help others and share what you have.

Tim likes to play golf with his dad. They go to a big field with holes and flags. Tim has a small stick and a white ball. His dad has a big stick and a white ball. Tim watches his dad hit the ball with the big stick. The ball flies far away and lands near a flag. Tim claps his hands and says, "Wow, dad, you are good!" Then it is Tim's turn. He tries to hit the ball with the small stick, but he misses. He tries again, but he misses. He feels sad and says, "I can't do it, dad, it is too hard!" His dad smiles and says, "Don't worry, Tim, you can do it. You just need to push the ball with the stick. Look, I will show you." His dad puts his hands over Tim's hands and helps him push the ball with the stick. The ball rolls on the ground and goes into the hole. Tim cheers and says, "Yay, dad, we did it!" His dad hugs him and says, "Yes, we did, Tim, you are great! You see, pushing is useful. It can help you play golf and do many other things. Can you think of something else you can push?" Tim thinks for a moment and says, "I can push my toy car. I can push the swing. I can push the door. I can push you, dad!" He giggles and pushes his dad gently. His dad laughs and says, "Yes, you can, Tim, but be careful. Pushing can also be rude or dangerous. You should only push when it is nice or fun. Do you understand?" Tim nods and says, "I understand, dad. Pushing is useful, but not always. I will be a good boy and only push when I should. Can we play golf again?" His dad says, "Of course, Tim, we can play golf anytime. I love playing golf with you. You are my best friend." They hug and walk back to the car, holding their sticks and balls. They are happy and proud. They like golf. They like pushing. They like each other.
