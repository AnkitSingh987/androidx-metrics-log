Category: Beginner
Title: Sue the Thoughtful Girl and the Door

Once upon a time, there was a thoughtful girl named Sue. She loved to stir her hot chocolate on cold days. One day, the weather was very cold and windy. Sue wanted to share her hot chocolate with her friend, Tim. Sue went to Tim's house and said, "Tim, let's have hot chocolate. The weather is cold." Tim was happy and said, "Yes, Sue, I love hot chocolate!" So, they went to Sue's house to make hot chocolate. Sue and Tim were stirring their hot chocolate when they heard a loud noise. The wind was blowing very hard. It blew the door open and made a big mess in the house. Sue and Tim were sad. Their hot chocolate was cold, and they had to clean up the mess. The day did not end well for Sue and Tim.

Tom and Sue are playing with their dolls. They like to dress them up and make them talk. Tom has a big box of clothes for his dolls. Sue has a small bag of clothes for hers. "Can I have some of your clothes?" Sue asks Tom. "My dolls are cold and bored." "No, you can't," Tom says. "They are mine. You have your own clothes." "But your clothes are nicer and more colorful," Sue says. "My clothes are dull and old. Please, Tom, share with me." Tom shakes his head. He does not want to share. He likes his clothes. He thinks Sue's clothes are ugly and boring. Sue feels sad and angry. She wants to have fun with Tom and his dolls. She looks around the room. She sees a pair of scissors on the table. She has an idea. She sneaks to the table and grabs the scissors. She runs back to Tom's box of clothes. She starts to cut them with the scissors. She makes holes and tears in them. She laughs. "Hey, what are you doing?" Tom shouts. He sees Sue with the scissors and his clothes. He is shocked and mad. He tries to stop her. "Stop it, Sue! You are ruining my clothes! Give me back the scissors!" "No, I won't!" Sue says. "You are mean and selfish! You don't share with me! Now your clothes are dull and old too!" Tom and Sue fight over the scissors. They pull and tug at them. They are both loud and angry. Their mom hears them. She comes to the room. She sees the mess. She sees the scissors and the clothes. She is upset and worried. "What is going on here?" she asks. "Why are you fighting? Why are you cutting the clothes?" Tom and Sue stop fighting. They look at their mom. They are scared and sorry. They tell their mom what happened. They tell her why they are mad at each other. They tell her they are sorry. Their mom is not happy. She tells them they are both wrong. She tells them they should not cut the clothes. She tells them they should share and play nicely. She tells them they have to fix the clothes. She gives them a needle and some thread. She tells them to deliver the clothes to each other. She tells them to say sorry and thank you. She tells them to hug and make up. Tom and Sue do what their mom says. They deliver the clothes to each other. They say sorry and thank you. They hug and make up. They feel better. They are friends again. They decide to mix and match their clothes. They make new outfits for their dolls. They have fun. Their mom smiles. She is glad they are happy. She is proud they learned their lesson. She hopes they will not fight again.

Anna and Ben are playing in the park. They see a squirrel on a tree. The squirrel has a wide tail and big eyes. It is holding a nut in its mouth. "Look, a squirrel!" Anna says. "It is so cute!" "Can we pet it?" Ben asks. Anna shakes her head. "No, we can't. Squirrels are wild animals. They might bite or run away." "Then how can we be friends with it?" Ben wonders. Anna thinks for a moment. "Maybe we can learn from it. We can watch what it does and how it lives." Ben nods. "That sounds fun. Let's learn from the squirrel." They sit on the grass and observe the squirrel. They see how it climbs the tree, how it jumps from branch to branch, how it buries its nut in the ground, and how it makes noises with its mouth. They try to copy the squirrel. They pretend to climb the tree, to jump like the squirrel, to hide their snacks in the grass, and to chatter like the squirrel. The squirrel notices them and looks curious. It comes closer and sniffs them. It sees that they are not squirrels, but children. It decides that they are not a threat, but a game. It tosses its nut to them and waits for them to toss it back. Anna and Ben are surprised and happy. They play catch with the squirrel. They laugh and have fun. They learn from the squirrel, and the squirrel learns from them. They become friends in the park.
