Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and the Mom

Sara was sick. She had a fever and a cough. She felt hot and wet. Her mom took her to the doctor. The doctor was nice. He gave her some medicine and a sticker. "Take this medicine three times a day," the doctor said. "And drink lots of water. You will feel better soon." Sara and her mom went home. Sara lay on the couch. She drank some water. She took her medicine. She watched some cartoons. She felt a little better. The next day, Sara's mom had to go to work. She called a nurse to come and take care of Sara. The nurse was not nice. She was ignorant. She did not know how to make Sara feel better. She did not give Sara her medicine. She did not give Sara water. She did not let Sara watch cartoons. She made Sara eat dry toast. She said it was good for her cough. Sara did not like the dry toast. It hurt her throat. She wanted water. She wanted her mom. She wanted the nice doctor. She cried. "Why are you crying?" the nurse asked. "Stop being a baby. You are not that sick. Eat your toast and be quiet." Sara felt angry. She felt sad. She felt sick. She threw the dry toast at the nurse. The nurse got mad. She yelled at Sara. She grabbed Sara's arm. She hurt Sara. Sara screamed. She screamed loud. She screamed for her mom. She screamed for the nice doctor. She screamed for help. Her mom heard her scream. She came back from work. She saw the nurse. She saw the dry toast. She saw Sara's arm. She saw Sara's tears. She fired the nurse. She hugged Sara. She gave Sara water. She gave Sara medicine. She gave Sara a kiss. "I'm sorry, Sara," her mom said. "I did not know the nurse was ignorant. I will never leave you with her again. You are my brave girl. I love you." Sara smiled. She felt loved. She felt better. She said, "I love you too, mom."

Tom and Mia are friends. They like to play with blocks. They have many blocks of different colors and shapes. They can make towers, houses, cars and animals with the blocks. One day, Tom and Mia are playing with blocks in the park. They are making a big tower with red, blue, yellow and green blocks. They are happy and proud of their tower. "Look at our tower, Mia! It is so tall and pretty!" Tom says. "Yes, Tom! It is the best tower ever! We are very good at making blocks!" Mia says. But then, a big wind comes. It blows hard and fast. It makes the tower fall down. The blocks scatter everywhere. Tom and Mia are sad and angry. They feel bad. "Bad wind! Go away! You broke our tower!" Tom shouts. "No, no, no! Our tower is gone! We worked so hard!" Mia cries. They look at the broken tower and the blocks. They see a pale block that they have never seen before. It is white and shiny. It looks different from the other blocks. "What is this, Tom? It is a new block. It is pale and bright. Where did it come from?" Mia asks. "I don't know, Mia. Maybe the wind brought it. Maybe it is a special block. Maybe it can make us feel better." Tom says. They pick up the pale block and hold it in their hands. It feels warm and soft. It makes a gentle sound like a bell. It makes them feel calm and happy. They smile and hug each other. "Maybe this is a magic block, Mia. Maybe it is a gift from the wind. Maybe it is saying sorry for breaking our tower." Tom says. "Maybe you are right, Tom. Maybe we can make a new tower with this block. Maybe it will be even better than the old one." Mia says. They start to gather the other blocks and build a new tower. They put the pale block on the top. It shines like a star. They feel good. They are friends. They like to play with blocks.

Once upon a time, there was a pretty bird named Blue. Blue lived in a small cage in a little girl's room. Every day, Blue would look out the window and sigh. He wanted to fly free and escape his cage. One sunny day, the little girl left the cage open. Blue saw his chance to escape. He flew out the window and up into the sky. Blue was so happy to be free. Blue flew around and met new friends. He played with other pretty birds in the trees. At the end of the day, Blue decided to go back to the little girl. She was sad without him. Blue knew he could still have fun and be free, even if he lived in a cage.
