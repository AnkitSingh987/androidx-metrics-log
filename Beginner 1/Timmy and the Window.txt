Category: Beginner
Title: Timmy and the Window

Timmy was walking home from school when he stumbled upon a bakery. The bakery smelled like sweet pastries and Timmy's stomach started to rumble. He wanted to try one of the treats in the window, so he moved to the door and peeked inside. Timmy saw a tall man behind the counter, who welcomed him with a friendly smile. "What can I get for you today, young man?" the man asked. Timmy liked the look of the heavy pastry in the window, so he pointed and asked for it. The man gave him the pastry and Timmy thanked him. "Have a great day!" the man said. Timmy said goodbye and moved to the park. He enjoyed the heavy pastry while sitting on the swing in the park. He was so happy that he had found this special treat!

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tom. Tom loved to stir things. One day, he found a magic pot. The pot was very old and original. Tom was very happy. Tom showed the magic pot to his friend, Sally. Sally said, "Tom, let's stir something in the pot!" Tom agreed. They stirred and stirred. They hoped for something nice to come out of the pot. But, oh no! A big, mean monster came out of the pot. The monster was not happy. It roared, "Why did you stir me?" Tom and Sally were very scared. The monster chased them away. The moral of the story is: be careful what you stir, because you might not like what comes out.

Once upon a time, there was a humble tutor named Mr. Fox. He loved to teach little animals how to read and write. One sunny day, Mr. Fox was walking to his class when he saw a little bird named Tweetie. Tweetie said, "Hi, Mr. Fox! I want to learn how to write my name. Can you help me?" Mr. Fox smiled and said, "Of course, Tweetie! Let's go to my class and I will teach you." On their way to the class, they saw a big puddle. Tweetie jumped in and began to soak. Mr. Fox laughed and said, "Tweetie, you are so funny! But we need to go to class now." Tweetie got out of the puddle and they continued walking. In the class, Mr. Fox taught Tweetie how to write her name. Tweetie was so happy and said, "Thank you, Mr. Fox! You are the best tutor ever!" Mr. Fox smiled and felt very proud of his little student. From that day on, Tweetie and Mr. Fox became best friends. They always had fun learning together and playing in the puddles after class.

Tom and Mia are friends. They like to play outside in the park. One day, they see a lot of fog in the air. The fog is white and soft. It makes everything look blurry. "Wow, look at the fog!" Tom says. "It is like a big cloud on the ground." "Can we play in the fog?" Mia asks. "It looks fun." Tom nods. "Yes, let's play hide and seek in the fog. I will count and you will hide. Then I will find you." "OK, but be careful. Don't go too far in the fog. We have to stay safe." Mia says. Tom agrees. He closes his eyes and starts to count. "One, two, three, four, five..." Mia runs away and looks for a good hiding spot. She sees a big tree and hides behind it. She hopes Tom will not see her. Tom finishes counting. "Ten! Ready or not, here I come!" He opens his eyes and looks around. He sees the fog everywhere. He can't see Mia or the park. He feels scared. "Mia, where are you?" He calls out. "I can't see you in the fog." Mia hears Tom's voice. She feels sorry for him. She comes out from behind the tree and waves at him. "I'm here, Tom. I'm sorry. The fog is too thick. Let's go back to the park." Tom sees Mia and runs to her. He hugs her. "I'm glad you are here, Mia. I was afraid I lost you in the fog. Let's go back to the park. The fog is not fun. It is scary." Mia nods. "Yes, let's go back to the park. The park is fun. It is safe." They hold hands and walk back to the park. They see the sun shining through the fog. They smile and play together. They are happy. They are safe.
