Category: Beginner
Title: Sam the Lovely Squirrel and the Park

One day, a lovely little squirrel named Sam was in the park. He was looking for peanuts to eat. Sam loved peanuts very much. He looked under the trees and near the flowers. Sam saw a big peanut on the ground. He was very happy. "Yum, a big peanut for me!" Sam said. But when he tried to pick it up, it was stuck. Sam pulled and pulled, but the peanut did not move. Just then, a kind bird named Bella arrived. She saw Sam trying to get the peanut. "Let me help you," said Bella. Together, they pulled and pulled. Suddenly, the peanut popped out of the ground! But it was not a peanut. It was a shiny key! Sam and Bella were surprised. They looked at each other and laughed. They did not find a peanut, but they found a new friend in each other.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim liked to play outside in the sun. One day, it rained and everything was wet. Tim put on his rain boots and went outside to play. As Tim splashed in the puddles, he saw a funny frog. The frog jumped high and made Tim laugh. Tim said, "Hi frog, you make me laugh! Let's be friends!" The frog said, "Ribbit! I like that word, friends!" Tim and the frog played all day in the wet rain. They jumped and laughed together. When the rain stopped, they said goodbye. Tim went home and told his mom about his new friend. And from that day on, Tim and the frog played together, rain or shine.

Once upon a time, there was a big cat. The cat was very stupid. It liked to fold things. One day, the cat saw a bird. The bird was asleep in a tree. The cat wanted to play with the bird. So, it climbed the tree. The cat was not good at climbing. It fell down. The cat was sad. The bird woke up. It saw the cat on the ground. The bird laughed at the cat. The cat felt bad. The cat did not like the bird. But the cat could not do anything. The cat stayed sad. The end.

Sara and Ben were playing with their toy cars in the backyard. They liked to make vroom-vroom noises and pretend they were racing. Sara had a red car and Ben had a blue car. "Let's see who is faster," Sara said. "We can race to the fence and back." "OK," Ben said. "But first we need to fill our cars with fuel." They ran to the shed and found a can of water. They poured some water into their cars and said it was fuel. "Ready, set, go!" Sara shouted. They pushed their cars along the grass and tried to reach the fence first. But something was wrong. Sara's car stopped halfway and made a terrible noise. It sounded like it was broken. "What happened?" Ben asked. He stopped his car and ran to Sara. "I don't know," Sara said. She looked at her car and saw water dripping from it. "Maybe the fuel was bad." She picked up her car and shook it. More water came out. "That's not fuel, that's water!" Ben said. He looked at the can and saw a label that said "Water. Do not drink." "Oh no," Sara said. "We made a mistake. We used the wrong can." She felt sad and angry. She liked her car and did not want it to be broken. "Why did you use this can?" she questioned Ben. "Did you not read the label?" Ben felt sorry and scared. He did not read the label. He did not know how to read. "I'm sorry, Sara," he said. "I did not know. I just saw a can and thought it was fuel." He hugged Sara and said he was sorry again. Sara hugged him back and said it was OK. She knew he did not mean to hurt her car. "Maybe we can fix it," she said. "Let's go and ask Dad. He knows about cars." They took their cars and the can and ran to the house. They hoped Dad could help them. They learned a lesson. They should always check what they use before they play.
