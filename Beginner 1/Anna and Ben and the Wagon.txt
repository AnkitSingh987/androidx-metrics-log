Category: Beginner
Title: Anna and Ben and the Wagon

Anna and Ben are playing with blocks. They like to build towers and bridges and houses. They have many blocks of different colors and shapes. Some blocks have symbols on them, like stars and hearts and circles. Anna wants to load all the blocks with symbols in her wagon. She thinks they are pretty and special. She says to Ben, "These are my blocks. I want to take them to my room." Ben does not like that. He wants to play with some of the blocks with symbols too. He says to Anna, "No, they are not your blocks. They are our blocks. We have to share them." Anna and Ben start to argue. They pull and tug at the blocks. They make loud noises. They are not happy. Mom hears them and comes to see what is wrong. She sees the blocks and the wagon and the children. She says to them, "What are you doing? Why are you fighting?" Anna and Ben tell Mom their problem. Mom listens and nods. She says to them, "I understand. You both like the blocks with symbols. But you have to learn to play together. You have to take turns and be kind. How about you load some of the blocks in the wagon and some in a basket? Then you can both take some to your rooms and some to the living room. And you can trade them later if you want." Anna and Ben think about Mom's idea. They decide to try it. They say sorry to each other and hug. They load the blocks in the wagon and the basket. They smile and say thank you to Mom. Mom is proud of them. She says to them, "Good job, Anna and Ben. You are good siblings. Now let's go inside. It is cold outside. How about some hot chocolate and cookies?"

One day, a little gray bunny named Ben hopped to his friend Tom's house. Ben wanted to borrow something from Tom. Tom was a big red bird who lived in a tall tree. "Hi Tom, can I borrow your power to fly?" asked Ben. Tom looked at Ben and said, "I can't give you my power to fly, but I can help you get up high in my tree." Ben was happy and said, "Thank you, Tom!" Tom picked Ben up in his strong bird feet and flew him up to the tree. They sat on a branch and looked at the pretty sky. They saw the sun and the clouds. They laughed and talked for a long time. Ben was happy he could be up high with his friend Tom, even if he could not fly.

Lily and Ben were playing near the cliff. They liked to look at the birds and the sea. But they knew they had to be careful and not go too close to the edge. One day, they saw a shiny thing on the cliff. It looked like a star. They wanted to see it better. Ben said, "Let's go get it. Maybe it's a treasure." Lily said, "No, Ben. It's too dangerous. Mommy said we can't go near the cliff. We might fall and get hurt." Ben said, "But I want it. It's so pretty. You're just scared. I'm not scared. I'm brave." He ran towards the shiny thing. Lily shouted, "Ben, stop! Come back!" But Ben didn't listen. He reached the shiny thing. It was a delicate necklace. He picked it up and smiled. He said, "Look, Lily. I got it. It's mine." But then he heard a loud crack. The cliff was breaking. He felt the ground move. He was scared. He dropped the necklace and tried to run back. But it was too late. He slipped and fell. Lily screamed. She ran to the edge and looked down. She saw Ben hanging on a branch. He was crying. He said, "Lily, help me! I'm sorry. I was wrong. Please help me!" Lily was scared too. But she wanted to help Ben. She saw a rope nearby. It was from their tent. She grabbed it and threw it to Ben. She said, "Ben, catch this. I'll pull you up. Hold on tight." Ben caught the rope. Lily pulled with all her strength. She wiped the sweat from her face. She said, "Ben, you're almost there. Don't let go." Ben climbed up the rope. Lily reached out and grabbed his hand. She pulled him up. They hugged each other. They were safe. They said, "Thank you. I love you. I'm sorry." They looked at the necklace. It was broken. They didn't care. They threw it away. They said, "It's not a treasure. It's a trap. We don't need it. We have each other." They walked away from the cliff. They held hands. They said, "Let's go home. Mommy will be worried. We'll never play near the cliff again."
