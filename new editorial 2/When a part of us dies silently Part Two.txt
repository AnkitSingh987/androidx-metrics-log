Title: When a part of us dies silently Part Two

When a part of us dies silently

Communities themselves play the most vital role. A language survives only when it is spoken at home, sung in festivals, used in daily conversations, and passed naturally from one generation to the next. Pride in one's mother tongue is the strongest safeguard against extinction. Protecting endangered languages is not an exercise in nostalgia.

It is an investment in humanity's intellectual and cultural diversity. Just as biodiversity strengthens ecosystems, linguistic diversity enriches human civilization by offering multiple ways of understanding life and solving problems. Each language is a unique voice in the great conversation of humanity.

The last speakers of endangered languages remind us that every language matters, regardless of how many people speak it. Their voices are not echoes from a fading past but urgent calls to preserve the richness of human expression. If we allow these voices to disappear, we lose not only words but also wisdom, identity, and history.

Saving endangered languages is, ultimately, an act of preserving the collective heritage of humankind. The writer is a Thrissur-based accountant and freelance contributor By NARAYANAN KIZHUMUNDAYUR
