Title: The war on Iran and the Gulfs shattered Part Three

The war on Iran and the Gulf's shattered illusions

The Gulf is no longer the untouchable safe haven it once claimed to be. Iran is no longer a formidable regional power but a wounded state struggling to survive. The region as a whole faces a crossroads: it can either descend into a cycle of instability and retaliation, or it can seize the opportunity to build a new narrative of cooperation and resilience.

India can play a constructive role, leveraging its good relations with the Arab states and its historic ties to Iran, to serve as a bridge for reconstruction rather than watching as the region spirals into dysfunction and despair. One lesson is undeniable: prosperity built on fragile foundations cannot endure. The Gulf must now confront the reality that its future depends not only on wealth and ambition but also on stability and cooperation.

Whether it can rise to that challenge, once the Americans have flown away, depends on all of us in the region who have an interest in maintaining the stability and prosperity that was once the envy of the world. By Shashi Tharoor Fourth-term Member of Parliament, Lok Sabha  for Thiruvananthapuram; Chairman of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on External Affairs; Sahitya Akademi Award-winning author of 29 books, including Pax Indica (2012) and The New World Disorder (2020)
