Title: India must own its energy security Part Two

India must own its energy security

Onion prices at least lead to action. Oil evokes nothing. Rhetoric and lament won't solve a problem that has persisted for 50 years. India needs a delivery system. Every energy security initiative should have a public deadline, a designated owner, and penalties for delay. An annual Energy Security Report to Parliament should monitor each project.

The plan, in three buckets Here is a plan with numbers we should hold ourselves to. Now  -  the next 12 months: Caverns and oil company tanks hold about 74 days of net oil imports, and there is no gas reserve. Raise oil cover to 90 days and build the first gas reserve. Install 4 gigawatts of two-hour battery capacity at renewable pooling stations to capture most of the clean power now lost.

Rupee crude and gas contracts on Indian exchanges hedge both the fuel and the dollar. Listed importers, airlines and power producers must publish a board-approved hedging policy. Require agricultural pump sets and industrial motors to carry star labels. Nudge offices to adopt remote work on days when supply is at risk.

Electric vehicles made up 12.3 per cent of August 2026 registrations. This should rise to 15 per cent in a year, and make every new government vehicle and city bus electric. Next  -  one to five years: Bypass both straits. Oman's Ras Markaz terminal near Duqm lies outside Hormuz and the Red Sea. India should co-invest, book capacity and store crude there, and set a feasibility deadline for the Oman-Gujarat deep-sea gas pipeline.

The Mahanadi find off Konark and the Andaman find at Sri Vijayapuram should reach first gas within five years. Switch fuels. Retrofit our 25 gigawatts of gas plants to run on imported or domestic gas, ethanol or compressed biogas. Meet 100 per cent of this year's transmission target of 25,146 circuit kilometres.

Set up special benches be set up to settle grid expansion-related disputes within a year. Meet the goal of 30 per cent electric vehicle sales by 2030. Put a solar-powered, battery-equipped charging station every 25 kilometres on national highways. Later  -  five years and beyond: Reduce oil import dependence from 88.7 per cent to 67 per cent by 2035.

Go nuclear. Reach 100 gigawatts by 2047, with small modular reactors beside steel mills and data centres, and private capital. Build cities that need less fuel. Sixty per cent of city trips are under five kilometres. Add footpaths, cycle lanes and bus feeders, and zone homes and offices near metro stations.

Make all new two-wheelers, three-wheelers and city buses electric by 2035, and most new cars by 2047. Finally, these three numbers will tell us whether the plan works: the days of cover we hold, the share of energy we make at home, and the share of energy projects completed on time. The first two are too low.

We have never measured the third. The bell has rung from Hormuz and the Red Sea. It will ring again. We must be ready before it does. By CKG Nair & V Shunmugam Nair is former Director, NISM, and Shunmugam is Partner, MCQube
