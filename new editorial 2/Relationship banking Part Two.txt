Title: Relationship banking Part Two

Relationship banking

Delegated authority The solution lies in institutionalising delegated authority. Banks should consider introducing a Digital Bearer Management System or Registered Representative System through their Internet Banking and Mobile Banking platforms. Customers could register authorised bearers using OTP-based authentication, specify the categories of documents that may be collected, prescribe the validity period of the authorisation and revoke it instantly whenever required.

Every authorisation should generate a complete digital audit trail, giving confidence to customers, branch employees and auditors alike. The framework should extend to non-individual customers as well. Proprietorships, partnership firms, LLPs and companies should be able to nominate employees, accountants, finance executives or chartered accountants as authorised document recipients.

Such authorisations may be role-based, document-specific or time-bound, reducing dependence on paper authorisation letters while improving operational efficiency and auditability. A structured digital framework would offer benefits far beyond regulatory compliance. It would protect customer privacy, provide legal certainty to branch employees, reduce disputes at branch counters and improve customer convenience.

More importantly, it would recognise a practical reality: the objective of the DPDP framework is not to prevent customers from entrusting family members or employees with routine banking responsibilities. It is to ensure that such delegation is explicit, verifiable and under the customer's control. As banks prepare for the full enforcement of the DPDP framework on May 13, 2027, they have an opportunity to address an operational blind spot before it becomes a compliance challenge.

India has successfully digitised payments, mandates, nomination and customer consent. The next logical step is to digitise delegated authority. The DPDP era does not call for the end of bearer transactions; it calls for transforming informal trust into formally authorised trust. By N Gnanabharathi The writer is a senior banker
