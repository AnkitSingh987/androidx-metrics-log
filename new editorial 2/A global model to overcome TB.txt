Title: A global model to overcome TB

A global model to overcome TB

India's campaign driven by inspiring TB survivors to lead the fight against the disease within their communities India's TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyan stands as a beacon of hope and innovation in the global fight against tuberculosis, achieving a remarkable 21 per cent decline in TB incidence - from 237 to 187 cases per lakh population between 2015 and 2024 - nearly double the global average. This remarkable progress is a result of increased healthcare financing, evidence-based public health action and policy commitment at the highest levels of government. This determined and collective effort has driven a comprehensive, technology-led, and community-powered campaign that has fundamentally changed how TB is detected, treated, and prevented in India.

At the heart of this transformation is a unique and evidence-driven community-screening approach, designed to find asymptomatic TB patients among vulnerable populations. Research has shown that subclinical, asymptomatic TB is a major contributor to disease transmission, and TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyan has embraced this insight with unprecedented scale. Over 19 crore people have been screened, leading to the detection of over 8.61 lakh asymptomatic TB cases among the 24.5 lakh patients diagnosed since December 7, 2024.

This proactive, technology-enabled strategy is breaking transmission chains and reducing incidence by identifying and treating TB in its earliest stages, even before classical symptoms appear. India's pioneering use of AI-enabled handheld X-ray devices has allowed for rapid, large-scale screening, especially in urban slums, congregate settings, and remote areas. This is backed by the world's largest TB laboratory network, with rapid molecular testing and expanded drug susceptibility coverage ensuring that all patients, including those with drug-resistant TB, receive timely and accurate diagnosis.

The campaign's emphasis on early detection is transforming TB elimination from a reactive to a proactive effort, making India a global leader in innovative case-finding. Jan Bhagidari approach At the heart of this transformation is the Jan Bhagidari approach that has mobilised over 2 lakh youth volunteers and more than 6.77 lakh Ni-kshay Mitras, who provide psychosocial support and encouragement to TB patients across the country. Over 15 lakh Ni-kshay Shivirs have been conducted, rallying communities and fostering a sense of ownership in TB screening and care This public movement has not only reduced stigma but has also empowered communities to actively participate in TB elimination.

Community awareness campaigns, school-based education, and involvement of local leaders, district administration and TB survivors have played a critical role in normalising TB and encouraging early treatment-seeking behaviour, especially among women and marginalised groups. As the world searches for feasible models to eliminate TB, India's TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyan stands out as an ambitious blueprint rooted in scale, innovation and citizen participation. India's campaign is powered by the inspiring TB survivors who have become champions, known as TB Vijetas, leading the fight against the disease within their own communities.

These individuals, having experienced TB first-hand, play a critical role in reducing stigma by sharing their stories, encouraging treatment adherence, and providing peer support to newly diagnosed patients. Their involvement breaks down barriers of fear and discrimination that often prevent people from seeking timely care. Nutrition powers prevention India's leadership in providing nutritional support is another critical pillar of its success.

The Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana has disbursed over ₹4,406 crore directly to 1.37 crore beneficiaries since its launch in 2018, with the monthly support increased in 2024 from ₹500 to ₹1,000 per patient per month during the course of TB treatment. This direct benefit transfer ensures that TB patients can afford supplementary nutrition they need for recovery, while the Ni-kshay Mitra initiative has distributed over 45 lakh nutritious food baskets, rallying community support and providing additional nutritional aid. These efforts have improved treatment adherence and treatment outcomes, empowering communities to actively participate in TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyan.

Research has shown that under-nutrition is a key risk factor for TB, contributing to nearly 35 per cent of India's TB burden, and that nutritional interventions can reduce TB incidence by up to 48 per cent among household contacts. By seamlessly integrating nutrition into routine TB care, TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyan is supporting patient resilience, enhancing treatment success, and empowering communities to accelerate our progress towards a TB-free India. From villages to megacities, the Abhiyan is beginning to translate hope into health, bringing lifesaving support to grassroots long burdened by TB disease.
