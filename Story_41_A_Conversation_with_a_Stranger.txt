Title: A Conversation with a Stranger

Category: Intermediate

It was a cold, rainy Tuesday afternoon in London. The sky was dark gray, and the rain fell steadily against the windows of the small café. Inside, it was warm and smelled like freshly ground coffee and sweet pastries. The café was crowded because people were trying to escape the terrible weather.
At a small wooden table near the window sat Arthur. He was a young man from Canada, visiting England for the first time. He had a large, complicated map spread out in front of him. He was trying to plan his travel for the next day, but the map was very confusing. There were too many lines, colors, and small names. Arthur sighed and took a sip of his hot coffee. He felt a little lonely and frustrated. He wanted to visit a famous castle in the countryside, but he didn't know which train to take.
Suddenly, a woman approached his table. She was holding a cup of tea and looking around the crowded room. She had dark hair, kind brown eyes, and she was wearing a thick red scarf.
"Excuse me," she said politely. "I'm sorry to bother you, but there are no empty tables left. Do you mind if I sit here?"
Arthur looked up, surprised. "Oh, no, not at all. Please, sit down," he replied. He quickly folded his large map to make space for her tea.
"Thank you very much," she said with a warm smile. She sat down and took off her wet coat. "The weather is completely miserable today, isn't it? I thought autumn in London was usually a bit drier than this."
"It really is terrible," Arthur agreed. "I am visiting from Toronto, and everyone told me it rains a lot here, but I didn't expect this much rain. My shoes are completely soaked."
The woman laughed. It was a nice, bright sound. "I should introduce myself. My name is Clara. I live a few streets away from here. I always come to this café when I want to read a good book."
"It's nice to meet you, Clara. I'm Arthur," he said. "Are you a student?"
"No, I finished university three years ago," Clara explained. "I work as a graphic designer for a small advertising company. It's an interesting job, but I spend all day looking at a computer screen. So, on my days off, I try to get away from technology. What about you, Arthur? What brings you to London?"
"I am an architect," Arthur said proudly. "I design residential buildings. I have always wanted to see the historical architecture in Europe. I saved money for two years to take this trip. London is amazing, but it is also very big and slightly overwhelming."
Clara nodded understandingly. "Yes, it can be very busy and confusing for visitors. I noticed you were looking at a map earlier. Are you trying to find a specific place?"
"Actually, yes," Arthur said, feeling relieved. "I really want to visit Warwick Castle tomorrow. I read that it is one of the most well-preserved castles in the country. But I cannot figure out the train system. The schedule seems very complicated, and I don't want to get lost."
Clara leaned forward. "Oh, Warwick Castle is beautiful! I went there last year with my sister. You definitely must go. It is not as difficult to reach as it looks on that map. If you go to Marylebone Station, there is a direct train that takes about an hour and a half."
"A direct train?" Arthur asked. "The guidebook said I had to change trains in Birmingham."
"The guidebook is probably old," Clara told him. "They added a new direct route a few months ago. If you take the nine o'clock train in the morning, you will arrive before the big crowds of tourists. But you should buy your ticket online tonight. If you wait until tomorrow morning, it will be much more expensive."
Arthur smiled widely. "That is incredibly helpful. Thank you, Clara. I was getting a headache trying to understand this map. You have saved my trip."
"I am happy to help," she replied. She took a sip of her tea and looked out the window at the heavy rain. "It's always nice to meet people from other countries. Traveling is wonderful, but it can be lonely sometimes. Have you been to any other cities in the UK?"
"Not yet," Arthur said. "I am staying in London for five days, and then I am taking a train to Edinburgh, Scotland. I have always wanted to see the Scottish Highlands."
"Scotland is magical," Clara said softly. Her eyes looked dreamy. "I went to university in Edinburgh. The city is full of history, and the mountains in the north are absolutely stunning. However, you must bring very warm clothes. If you think London is cold, you will be shocked by the wind in Scotland."
They talked for more than an hour. They discussed their favorite books, their childhood dreams, and the differences between life in Canada and England. Arthur learned that Clara loved photography and wanted to open her own studio one day. Clara learned that Arthur was nervous about returning home because he wanted to start his own architecture firm, but he was afraid of failing.
"You shouldn't let fear stop you," Clara told him seriously. "If you don't try, you will always wonder what might have happened. Starting a business is risky, but it is better than working for someone else and feeling unhappy. You clearly have passion for your work. You should trust yourself."
Arthur listened carefully to her words. It was strange how sometimes a stranger could give the best advice. Because Clara didn't know his family or his boss, she could look at his situation clearly.
"You are right," Arthur said thoughtfully. "I think I just needed someone to say it out loud. Thank you, Clara. You have given me a lot to think about."
Clara smiled and checked her watch. "Oh, look at the time! It's already five o'clock. I need to go to the supermarket before it closes, and then I promised to make dinner for my roommate."
She stood up and put on her red scarf and wet coat. The rain outside had stopped, and the evening sun was trying to shine through the clouds.
"It was wonderful to meet you, Arthur," Clara said sincerely. "I hope you have a fantastic time at Warwick Castle tomorrow, and a safe journey to Scotland."
"It was wonderful to meet you too, Clara," Arthur replied, standing up to shake her hand. "Thank you for the travel advice, and for the life advice. I really appreciate it."
Clara waved goodbye and walked out of the café. Arthur sat back down and looked at his folded map. He didn't feel lonely or frustrated anymore. He felt inspired and excited for the future. He took out his phone and booked his train ticket for the next morning. Then, he opened his notebook and started sketching a design for his new architecture firm. Sometimes, a simple conversation with a stranger could change the direction of your day, or even the direction of your life.
