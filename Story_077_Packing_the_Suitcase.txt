Title: Packing the Suitcase
Category: Beginner

Friday evening finally arrived. Tomorrow was the big day. The Miller family was leaving for their weekend trip to the countryside to visit their grandparents. Everyone in the house was busy preparing. 

In her bedroom, ten-year-old Sophie pulled her small pink suitcase out from under her bed. She opened it on the floor. It was completely empty. She walked to her wooden wardrobe and opened the doors. She had to choose the right clothes for three days. 

Her mother walked into the room holding a pile of clean laundry. 
"Remember what I told you, Sophie," Mrs. Miller said. "It is going to be cold in the village. You need to pack warm clothes. Don't pack your summer dresses or thin t-shirts."
"I know, Mom," Sophie replied. "I am packing my thick red sweater and my blue jeans."
"Good girl," her mother smiled. "And don't forget extra socks. Your feet will get cold if you walk in the wet grass. Make sure you pack at least four pairs."

Sophie nodded and went to her drawer. She took out four pairs of warm, fluffy socks and put them neatly in the corner of the pink suitcase. Then, she folded two pairs of jeans, three long-sleeved shirts, and her favorite thick red sweater. She placed them carefully inside. 

"What else do I need?" Sophie asked herself quietly. She thought about her morning routine. 
She went into the bathroom and grabbed her pink toothbrush from the cup. She took her toothpaste and her hairbrush. She put them all into a small plastic bag so they would not get her clothes wet, and she packed the bag in the suitcase. 

In the room next door, Thomas was packing his own bag. He had a dark blue duffel bag. He was not as neat as his sister. He just rolled his clothes into balls and pushed them into the bag. 
He packed his jeans, his sweaters, and his thick winter coat. Then, he went to his bookshelf. 
"I will need something to do on the train," he thought. He picked out two comic books and a small handheld video game. He shoved them into the side pocket of the bag. 

Downstairs in the living room, Mr. Miller was packing the large family suitcase. He was in charge of the important things. He packed the camera, the train tickets, and the presents they bought for the grandparents. They bought a beautiful new scarf for Grandma and a book about gardening for Grandpa. 

"Did you pack the phone charger?" Mrs. Miller called from the kitchen. She was preparing some sandwiches and snacks for the train journey. 
"Yes, I put it in the front pocket," Mr. Miller answered. He zipped up the large suitcase with a loud noise. "That is the main bag finished. How are the children doing?"

He walked upstairs and checked on Thomas and Sophie. 
"Are you both ready?" he asked, looking into their bedrooms. 
"My bag is full," Thomas said proudly, pointing to his messy blue bag. 
"Mine is perfect," Sophie said, snapping her pink suitcase shut. 

"Excellent," their father said. "Bring your bags downstairs and put them by the front door. We have to wake up very early tomorrow. The train leaves at eight o'clock, and we cannot be late."

Thomas and Sophie carried their bags down the stairs. The bags were surprisingly heavy for a short weekend trip. They lined them up neatly next to the front door, right beside Mr. Miller's large suitcase and Mrs. Miller's bag of food. 

Looking at the row of luggage made the trip feel very real. The house was quiet again, but it was an excited kind of quiet. Everything was packed, the tickets were ready, and the plan was set. All they had to do now was sleep. 
Sophie climbed into bed, but she found it very hard to close her eyes. She kept thinking about the train ride and the new house in the countryside. She wondered if she packed everything she needed. She remembered the warm socks, the toothbrush, and the red sweater. Yes, she was ready. She finally drifted off to sleep, dreaming of the adventures waiting for them in the morning.
