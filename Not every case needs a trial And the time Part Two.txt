Title: Not every case needs a trial And the time Part Two

Not every case needs a trial. And the time for a mediation push is now

Last, there should be clear guidelines that set a mandate for mediation. It is a tough task to balance informality and an action-based approach with structure and predictability. Nonetheless, there is precedent for this. For instance, drafting clear questions of what each party wants, the cost incurred in case litigation is pursued, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, timelines and most importantly, execution of the agreement and its oversight.

This way, medication becomes the first step with a clear mandate. In many ways, mediation is different from much of the legal system. It is not bound by rules or procedure. It is certainly not adversarial. Having said this, lawyers are trained to assess cases objectively  -  to weigh the strengths of their propositions as against those of the adversary.

A lawyer's primary job, in terms of representation, is to understand the clients' needs and persuade the court to see things from their perspective. In that sense, mediation requires these very skills, albeit channelled differently. Mediation is certainly not a new tool or topic. However, with recent developments and a push for it, it may be a golden period to implement it large scale, to see it reach its full potential as a tool to minimise litigation.

It certainly lends itself to most civil disputes and to many family disputes. It is a question of structuring in ways to make it an attractive and feasible alternative to litigation. With the history that it has and a firm commitment, mediation can certainly stand up to this challenge. Written by Vishavjeet Chaudhary The writer is a Delhi-based Advocate and a Door Tenant in London
