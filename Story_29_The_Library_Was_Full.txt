Title: The Library Was Full

Category: Intermediate

It was the second week of December, the most stressful time of the year at the university. Final exams were approaching, and every student was desperate for a quiet place to study.

Maya, a third-year engineering student, had a very difficult exam on Monday morning. She needed to study fluid dynamics. It was a subject that required intense concentration, a clear mind, and a lot of desk space for her large textbooks, notebooks, and calculator. 

On Saturday morning, Maya woke up at seven o'clock. She packed her heavy backpack and marched toward the main university library. She usually liked studying in her apartment, but her two roommates were hosting a holiday party that weekend. The apartment was already filled with loud music, decorations, and people. It was impossible to focus. 

Maya arrived at the library at eight o'clock, right when the doors opened. However, she was not the only student with this idea. A massive crowd of tired students was already waiting outside. When the doors unlocked, everyone rushed inside like a wave. 

Maya walked quickly to the third floor, which was the designated silent study area. She always sat at a specific table in the corner, near a large window. But when she reached the third floor, she stopped in shock. Every single desk, table, and comfortable chair was occupied. Some students had even brought blankets and were sitting on the floor between the bookshelves. 

The library was completely full. 

Maya sighed heavily. She walked down to the second floor, which allowed quiet talking. It was also completely full. Students had covered the tables with books, laptops, and coffee cups. She walked down to the first floor, the group study area. It was loud and chaotic, but even there, she could not find an empty seat. 

"This is impossible," Maya muttered to herself. She walked out of the library, feeling frustrated. She had planned to study for eight hours today. Now, her plan was ruined. 

She stood on the sidewalk, holding her heavy backpack, and thought about her options. She could not go home because of the party. The small coffee shops near the university would be too noisy, and the tables were too small for all her engineering books. 

"Think, Maya, think," she told herself. "Where else is quiet?"

She decided to walk towards the center of the city. Maybe she could find a public library. The air was cold, and a light snow began to fall. After walking for twenty minutes, her shoulders began to ache from the weight of her books. She needed to sit down soon. 

She turned a corner and saw a large, old building made of red brick. A wooden sign hung above the door: "The City Botanical Garden - Winter Conservatory." Maya had passed this building many times, but she had never gone inside. She looked through the large glass windows. She saw huge green plants and tall trees. It looked warm and peaceful. 

She walked closer and read a small paper sign on the door: "Cafe open inside. Free Wi-Fi. Quiet reading encouraged."

"A cafe inside a greenhouse?" Maya thought. "It is worth a try."

She opened the heavy wooden door and stepped inside. Immediately, the cold winter air was replaced by warm, humid air. It smelled like wet earth and sweet flowers. The contrast with the freezing street outside was incredible. 

Maya walked through a short hallway and entered the main conservatory. It was breathtaking. The roof was made of glass, letting in the gray winter light. Tall palm trees reached toward the ceiling. Bright pink and purple orchids grew on the walls. In the center of the room, there was a small pond with a little waterfall. The sound of the falling water was soft and relaxing. 

On the left side of the room, hidden behind some large fern plants, was a small cafe area. There were about ten round wooden tables. To Maya's amazement, the place was almost empty. Only two older women were sitting at a table, drinking tea and reading books. 

Maya walked to the counter. A friendly man with a green apron smiled at her. 

"Welcome," he said quietly. "Can I get you something?"

"Just a large black coffee, please," Maya said. She looked around. "Is it always this quiet here?"

"Usually, yes," the man replied, handing her the coffee. "People forget about the botanical garden in the winter. They think there is nothing to see. But the indoor conservatory is always warm. It is our little secret."

Maya paid for her coffee and chose a table near a large, beautiful banana tree. The table was big enough for her laptop and her massive fluid dynamics textbook. She sat down, took off her heavy winter coat, and took a deep breath. The warm air filled her lungs. She felt her stress melting away. 

She opened her book and began to study. The environment was perfect. It was warm, but not stuffy. The gentle sound of the waterfall blocked out any background noise, acting like natural white noise. The bright green color of the plants made her feel awake and energized. Whenever she solved a difficult math problem, she looked up and rested her eyes on the beautiful flowers. 

For the first time in weeks, Maya felt truly focused. She studied for three hours without stopping. She did not check her phone once. The complex formulas suddenly seemed easier to understand. The peaceful atmosphere of the greenhouse helped her mind concentrate deeply.

Around noon, she got up to stretch her legs and buy a sandwich from the cafe. She walked around the small pond, admiring the colorful fish swimming in the water. She realized how lucky she was. If the university library had not been full, she would have spent the day in a dark, crowded room, feeling stressed and anxious. Instead, she was studying in a beautiful, tropical paradise in the middle of a snowy city. 

She returned to her table and continued studying. The hours passed quickly. In the late afternoon, a few more people arrived, but they all spoke in whispers, respecting the quiet atmosphere. 

By six o'clock, it was dark outside. The glass roof reflected the warm yellow lights of the cafe. Maya closed her textbook. She had finished all the chapters she needed to review. She had completed all her practice problems. She felt completely prepared for her Monday exam. 

She packed her backpack slowly, not wanting to leave. She went to the counter to say goodbye to the barista. 

"Thank you," she said. "This is the best study spot in the city. I cannot believe I never knew about it."

"I am glad you liked it," he smiled. "Good luck with whatever you are studying. You look very serious."

"Engineering," Maya laughed. "And I think I will need more luck next week. I have another exam on Thursday."

"Well, we are open every day," the barista said. "And we always have coffee."

Maya walked back out into the cold, snowy night. Her backpack was still heavy, but she felt incredibly light. Her mind was clear and organized. She walked back to her apartment. The party was still going on, but Maya did not care. She went straight to her bedroom, fell onto her bed, and slept peacefully. 

On Monday morning, Maya took her fluid dynamics exam. The questions were difficult, but she remembered everything she had studied in the warm greenhouse. She passed the exam with an excellent grade. 

For the rest of her university years, Maya never went back to the main library during exam week. Let the other students fight for seats in the crowded, stressful rooms, she thought. She had her own secret paradise. Whenever the pressure became too much, she packed her bag and escaped to the quiet, green world of the winter conservatory.
