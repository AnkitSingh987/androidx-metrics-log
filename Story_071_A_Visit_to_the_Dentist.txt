Title: A Visit to the Dentist
Category: Beginner

It is Wednesday afternoon. School is finished for the day. Eight-year-old Lily is sitting in the back of her mother's car. They are not driving home. They are driving to the center of town. 

"I do not want to go," Lily says. She crosses her arms. 
"I know, Lily," her mother says from the front seat. "Nobody really likes going to the dentist. But it is very important. Dr. Martin just wants to look at your teeth."
"But what if he finds a problem?" Lily asks. She is worried. She does not want a filling. 
"If you brush your teeth every day, you will not have any problems," her mother explains. "It is just a regular checkup."

They park the car and walk into a tall brick building. They take the elevator to the third floor. The door has a sign that says 'Dr. Martin, Family Dentist'. 
They walk into the waiting room. It is a very clean room. There are comfortable chairs and a small table with magazines. Soft music is playing. 
Lily's mother speaks to the receptionist. Then, they sit down to wait. 

Lily feels nervous. Her stomach feels like it is jumping. She does not like the smell of the dentist's office. It smells like strong soap and medicine. 
After ten minutes, a woman in blue clothes comes to the door. She is a dental nurse. 
"Lily?" the nurse says with a friendly smile. "It is your turn. Please come with me."

Lily and her mother follow the nurse down a short hallway. They go into a bright room. In the middle of the room, there is a very big chair. It looks like a spaceship chair. 
Dr. Martin is standing next to the chair. He is wearing a white coat and a mask over his mouth. But he pulls the mask down so Lily can see his face. He has a kind smile. 

"Hello, Lily," Dr. Martin says. "How are you today?"
"I am okay, thank you," Lily says quietly. 
"Please climb up into the big chair," he says. 

Lily climbs into the chair. It is soft and comfortable. 
"Now, let's make you comfortable," Dr. Martin says. He presses a button on the side of the chair. The chair slowly moves backward. It goes down until Lily is lying almost flat. It feels a little strange, but it is not scary. 
The nurse puts a paper bib around Lily's neck. 
"This is to keep your clothes clean," the nurse explains. 

"Okay, Lily. I am going to turn on a bright light," Dr. Martin says. "It will shine on your mouth so I can see your teeth. Please open your mouth very wide. Like a big lion!"
Lily opens her mouth as wide as she can. "Ahhh," she says. 
Dr. Martin turns on the light above the chair. It is very bright, so Lily closes her eyes a little bit. 

The dentist takes a small mirror with a long handle. He takes a small metal tool. 
"I am not going to hurt you," he says. "I am just going to count your teeth and check if they are strong."
He puts the small mirror inside Lily's mouth. He taps her teeth gently with the metal tool. Tap, tap, tap. 
"One, two, three, four," he counts quietly. "These teeth look very good. Very white and clean."

Lily relaxes. The tapping does not hurt at all. It just feels a little funny. 
Dr. Martin looks at all the teeth on the bottom. Then he looks at all the teeth on the top. He checks the front, the back, and the sides. 
"Open a little wider, please," he says. 
Lily opens her mouth wider. 

"Well, Mother," Dr. Martin says after a few minutes. "I have good news. Lily has beautiful teeth. There are no holes. There are no problems. She is doing a fantastic job."
Lily is so happy to hear this. Her stomach stops jumping. 
Dr. Martin turns off the bright light. He presses the button on the chair, and it moves up again. Lily sits up. 
The nurse takes off the paper bib. 

"You can close your mouth now, Lily," Dr. Martin laughs. 
Lily closes her mouth. She gives him a big smile. 
"You see?" her mother says. "I told you there was nothing to worry about."
"Yes, you were right," Lily admits. 

A visit to the dentist is not scary if you take care of your teeth. The big chair is fun to sit in, and it is nice to hear that your teeth are strong and healthy. Lily is glad the checkup is finished, and she is very proud of her clean, white smile.
