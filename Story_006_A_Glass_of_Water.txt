Title: A Glass of Water
Category: Beginner

It is night time. It is very dark. The moon is in the sky. It is a white moon. The stars are shining. It is a quiet night.
Everyone in the house is sleeping. The mother is sleeping. The father is sleeping. The children are sleeping. The house is very quiet.

Sarah is in her bed. She is seven years old. She is sleeping under a warm blanket. 
Suddenly, Sarah opens her eyes. She is awake. She looks at the window. It is dark outside. She looks at her clock. The clock is red. It says two o'clock. 
Sarah does not want to sleep. She feels something. Her mouth is dry. Her throat is dry. She is very thirsty. She wants water. She needs a glass of water.

Sarah sits up in her bed. She rubs her eyes. She is a little tired, but she is very thirsty. She must go to the kitchen. 
She gets out of bed. The floor is cold. She does not put on her slippers. She walks with bare feet. She walks very slowly. She does not want to make a noise. She does not want to wake up her mother and father.

She opens her bedroom door. The door makes a small sound. 'Squeak.' Sarah stops. She listens. Nothing happens. Her parents are still sleeping. 
She walks into the hall. The hall is very dark. She cannot see well. She puts her hand on the wall. The wall is smooth. She walks along the wall. She walks to the kitchen.

She arrives at the kitchen. It is dark in the kitchen too. She knows where the light switch is. It is on the wall near the door. She reaches up her hand. She touches the switch. She turns on the light. Click!
The light is very bright. Sarah closes her eyes for a second. Then she opens them. She can see everything now. She sees the table. She sees the chairs. She sees the fridge. She sees the sink.

Sarah walks to the cupboard. The cupboard is high. She is short. She stands on her toes. She opens the cupboard door. She looks inside. She sees plates. She sees bowls. She sees glasses. 
She wants a glass. She reaches up. She takes a blue plastic glass. She likes the blue glass. It is her favorite glass. It will not break if it falls.

Now she needs water. She walks to the kitchen sink. She puts the glass in the sink. She turns on the cold water tap. The water comes out. 'Splash, splash.' It is cold and clear. 
She puts the blue glass under the water. The glass fills up. It is full of cold water. She turns off the tap. 
She holds the glass with two hands. It is heavy. She brings the glass to her mouth. She drinks the water. 
"Glug, glug, glug."
The water is cold. The water is fresh. The water is very good. Her mouth is not dry anymore. Her throat is not dry anymore. She feels much better. 

But then, an accident happens. Sarah's hand slips. The glass tilts. Some water spills. The water falls on her pajama top. The water falls on the floor. 
"Oh no," Sarah whispers. 
She looks at her pajama top. It is wet. It feels cold. She looks at the floor. There is a small puddle of water on the floor. 

Sarah knows she must clean it up. She cannot leave water on the floor. Someone can slip and fall. 
She puts the blue glass on the kitchen counter. She looks for a towel. There is a small towel hanging near the sink. It is a yellow towel. 
She takes the yellow towel. She bends down. She wipes the floor with the towel. She rubs the floor until it is dry. The puddle is gone. The floor is clean and dry. 

She takes the wet yellow towel. She puts it on the counter. She will put it in the washing machine tomorrow. 
She looks at her wet pajama top. It is only a little wet. It will dry soon. She does not need to change it. 

Sarah is not thirsty anymore. She is happy. Now she wants to go back to sleep. 
She takes the blue glass. She puts it in the sink. She washes it with a little water. She leaves it in the sink. 
She walks back to the kitchen door. She turns off the light. Click! The kitchen is dark again. 

She walks back down the hall. She puts her hand on the wall again. The hall is still dark. The house is still quiet. Her bare feet make no sound on the floor. 
She goes back into her bedroom. She closes the door quietly. She walks to her bed. She climbs into her bed. She pulls the warm blanket over her body. 
She is comfortable. She is not thirsty. She closes her eyes. The house is quiet. The night is dark. Sarah falls asleep very fast. She will sleep until the morning.
