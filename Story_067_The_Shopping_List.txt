Title: The Shopping List
Category: Beginner

It is Thursday evening. The kitchen is quiet. Jack's father is opening the cupboards and looking inside. He opens the fridge and looks inside. He sighs. 
"Jack," his father calls out. "We need to go to the shop. We do not have anything for dinner, and we do not have anything for breakfast tomorrow."
Jack is nine years old. He walks into the kitchen. "Are we going to the big supermarket?" he asks. 
"No, the big supermarket is too far," his father says. "We will just go to the small grocery store on the corner. But we need to make a list first, so we do not forget anything."

His father takes a piece of paper and a blue pen from a drawer. 
"Okay, Jack. You can be the list manager," he says. He hands the pen and paper to Jack. "I will look in the cupboards, and you write down what we need."
"I am ready," Jack says. He sits at the kitchen table. He holds the pen carefully. 

His father looks in the fridge again. "We need milk," he says. 
Jack writes the word 'milk' at the top of the paper. 
"We need eggs," his father continues. "And we need some butter."
Jack writes 'eggs' and 'butter' under 'milk'. 

"Now, what should we eat for dinner tonight?" his father asks. "Do you want chicken or pasta?"
"I want pasta, please!" Jack says. He loves pasta with tomato sauce. 
"Okay, pasta it is," his father says. He looks in the cupboard. "We have pasta, but we do not have any sauce. Write down 'tomato sauce'."
Jack writes 'tomato sauce' on the list. 
"And we should have a salad with our pasta," his father says. "So, write down 'lettuce' and 'tomatoes'."
Jack writes 'lettuce' and 'tomatoes'. 
"Is that everything?" Jack asks. 
"Let me think," his father says. He scratches his chin. "Ah, yes. We need bread for breakfast tomorrow. And maybe some orange juice."

Jack adds 'bread' and 'orange juice' to the bottom of the list. He looks at the piece of paper. There are eight items on the list. 
"The list is finished," Jack says. 
"Great job, list manager," his father smiles. "Put on your jacket. Let's go."

They walk down the street to the small grocery store. It is not as big as the supermarket, but it has everything they need. 
Jack's father takes a red plastic shopping basket. 
"Okay, Jack. Read the first item on the list," his father says. 
Jack looks at the paper. "Milk," he reads. 
"Right. Let's go to the fridge at the back," his father says. 

They walk to the fridge. His father takes a bottle of milk and puts it in the red basket. 
"Now," his father says, "take your pen and cross 'milk' off the list. Draw a line through the word."
Jack takes his blue pen from his pocket. He draws a line through the word 'milk'. He likes doing this. It makes him feel very organized. 

"What is next?" his father asks. 
"Eggs and butter," Jack reads. 
They find the eggs and the butter. His father puts them in the basket. Jack crosses them off the list. 
Next, they find the tomato sauce in the middle aisle. Jack crosses it off. 
They go to the vegetable section for the lettuce and tomatoes. Jack crosses them off. 

"The basket is getting heavy," his father says. "What is left on our list?"
Jack looks at the paper. "We only have two things left. Bread and orange juice."
They walk to the front of the store. They find a fresh loaf of bread and a carton of orange juice. 
Jack crosses the last two words off the list. Every word has a blue line through it now. 

"We are finished!" Jack says happily. "We found everything on the list."
"That is because we had a very good list manager," his father says. "If we do not have a list, I always forget something. Last week, I forgot the milk, and we had to eat dry cereal for breakfast."
Jack laughs. "I remember that. It was terrible."

They walk to the cash register to pay for their food. Jack folds the piece of paper and puts it in his pocket. He knows that a shopping list is a very simple thing, but it is also a very useful thing. It helps you remember, and it helps you organize your thoughts. He is happy he could help his father today. Now, they can go home and cook a delicious pasta dinner.
