Title: Making Tea
Category: Beginner

It is four o'clock in the afternoon. It is a cool autumn day. The leaves on the trees outside are yellow and red. 
Mrs. Clark is in her living room. She is sitting in a comfortable armchair. She is reading a magazine. 
Her friend, Mrs. Davies, is visiting her. Mrs. Davies is sitting on the sofa. They are talking about their gardens and their grandchildren. 

"Would you like a cup of tea, Mary?" Mrs. Clark asks her friend. 
"Oh, yes, please, Helen," Mrs. Davies answers. "A cup of tea would be lovely. It is a little chilly today."
"I will make some right now," Mrs. Clark says. 

She stands up and walks to the kitchen. Her kitchen is small and very neat. 
First, she needs water. She takes the electric kettle. The kettle is white. She takes the lid off the kettle. She puts the kettle under the tap in the sink. She turns on the cold water. The water fills the kettle. She puts the lid back on. 

She places the kettle on its base. She plugs it into the wall. She presses the button on the kettle. A small red light turns on. The kettle starts to make a quiet noise. It is heating the water. 

While the water heats, Mrs. Clark prepares the cups. She opens the glass cupboard. She takes out two beautiful cups. The cups are white with a pattern of blue flowers. They match the saucers. She puts the cups and saucers on a wooden tray. 
Next, she needs the tea. She opens a small tin box. Inside the box, there are many tea bags. She takes out two round tea bags. She puts one tea bag into each cup. 

Now, she needs sugar and milk. Mrs. Davies likes sugar and milk in her tea. 
Mrs. Clark takes a small white sugar bowl. It is full of white sugar. She puts a small silver spoon next to the sugar bowl. She puts the sugar bowl on the wooden tray. 
She goes to the fridge. She takes out a small jug of cold milk. She puts the milk jug on the tray too. 

The noise from the kettle becomes louder. It sounds like a rushing wind. Then, the water starts to bubble. It is boiling. 
Click! 
The button on the kettle pops up. The red light turns off. The water is ready. 

Mrs. Clark takes the handle of the kettle. The kettle is very hot, so she must be careful. She carries the kettle to the wooden tray. 
She slowly pours the boiling water into the first cup. The water hits the tea bag. Instantly, the clear water turns a light brown color. A wonderful smell of tea rises into the air. 
She pours the boiling water into the second cup. She fills both cups near to the top. She puts the kettle down safely on the counter. 

Now, the tea must steep. It needs a few minutes to become strong and dark. Mrs. Clark waits for three minutes. She watches the water become a deep, rich brown. 
After three minutes, she takes the silver spoon. She carefully lifts the tea bag out of the first cup. She presses it gently against the side of the cup to get all the water out. She puts the wet tea bag on a small dish. She does the same thing with the second tea bag. 

The tea is ready to serve. 
Mrs. Clark picks up the wooden tray. She carries it carefully from the kitchen to the living room. She walks slowly so she does not spill the hot tea. 
"Here we are," she says cheerfully. 

She places the tray on the low coffee table in front of the sofa. 
"Thank you, Helen," Mrs. Davies says. "That smells wonderful."
"Do you want me to pour the milk?" Mrs. Clark asks. 
"Yes, please. Just a little bit of milk," Mrs. Davies says. 

Mrs. Clark takes the small jug. She pours a splash of cold milk into her friend's cup. The dark brown tea turns a lighter, creamy color. 
"And sugar?" Mrs. Clark asks. 
"One spoon, please," Mrs. Davies answers. 
Mrs. Clark uses the silver spoon to put one spoonful of sugar into the cup. She stirs the tea gently. The spoon makes a soft 'clink, clink' sound against the cup. 

She hands the cup and saucer to Mrs. Davies. 
"Be careful, it is very hot," she warns. 
"Thank you," Mrs. Davies says. She holds the warm cup in her hands. 

Mrs. Clark prepares her own tea. She likes her tea black. No milk, no sugar. She just takes her cup and sits back in her comfortable armchair. 
They both take a sip of their tea. 
"Ah, that is perfect," Mrs. Davies says with a smile. "You make a very good cup of tea, Helen."
"Thank you, Mary," Mrs. Clark says. 

They sit together in the warm living room. Outside, the autumn wind blows the yellow leaves. Inside, they drink their hot tea. They talk and laugh. Making tea is a simple thing, but sharing tea with a good friend makes it a very special part of the day.
