Title: A Busy Day at the Office

Category: Intermediate

Tom worked as the office administrator for 'BuildWell Architecture', a medium-sized firm located on the top floor of a glass building. His job was to keep the office running smoothly. He managed the supplies, answered the main phone line, greeted visitors, and helped the architects with administrative tasks. Most days, Tom enjoyed a peaceful and predictable routine. He liked knowing exactly what was going to happen.

However, a peaceful routine is never guaranteed in a busy office. 

It was a Tuesday morning in late spring. Tom arrived at the office at eight o'clock, humming a cheerful tune. He turned on the lights, started the large coffee machine in the kitchen, and sat down at his clean, organized desk near the front entrance. 

At exactly eight-fifteen, the office phone rang. It was Brenda, the company’s cheerful receptionist. 
"Hi Tom, I am so sorry," Brenda said, coughing loudly into the phone. "I have a terrible fever. I cannot come to work today. You will have to handle the front desk by yourself."
"Oh, don't worry about it, Brenda," Tom replied kindly, though he felt a small stab of anxiety. "Get some rest and drink lots of water. I can handle things here."

Tom hung up the phone and took a deep breath. Covering the reception desk meant he had to do two jobs at the same time. It would be challenging, but he thought he could manage it if the day was quiet. 

Unfortunately, the day was not going to be quiet. 

At nine o'clock, the main architectural team arrived. They were preparing for a massive presentation for a new city hospital project. The lead architect, Mr. Vance, walked quickly past Tom's desk looking stressed. 
"Tom, we need fifty copies of the hospital blueprints printed and bound by eleven o'clock," Mr. Vance said rapidly. "The clients are coming at eleven-thirty."
"I will start right now, Mr. Vance," Tom said, writing the task on his notepad. 

Tom went to the large copy room and started the heavy industrial printer. Just as the machine began to print the first blueprint, the office suddenly became completely silent. The soft humming of the air conditioning stopped. The computer screens turned black. 

The power had gone out. 

A collective groan echoed through the office. Architects stepped out of their offices, looking frustrated. 
"What happened to the electricity?" someone shouted. 

Tom immediately grabbed his mobile phone and called the building manager. "Hello, this is Tom from BuildWell on the top floor. We just lost all power."
"I know, I know," the building manager sighed heavily. "A construction crew down the street accidentally cut the main power line. The electric company is working on it, but it might take an hour or two to fix."

Tom ended the call and stood in the middle of the office. He had to make an announcement. "Attention everyone! A main power line was cut outside. It might take up to two hours to fix. Please try to save your laptop batteries."

Mr. Vance walked over to Tom, looking panicked. "Tom, this is a disaster! The clients are arriving in two hours. We cannot show them a dark office, and I cannot print the blueprints without electricity. What are we going to do?"

Tom knew he had to stay calm. If he panicked, everyone else would panic. He took a quick moment to think and prioritize the problems. 
"Okay, Mr. Vance, let's solve this," Tom said confidently. "First, the building has large glass windows. We have plenty of natural sunlight in the main conference room, so the meeting will not be completely dark. Second, I will take the files on a USB drive and run to the printing shop across the street. They have their own power generator. I can get the blueprints printed there."

Mr. Vance looked relieved. "That is a brilliant idea, Tom. Please hurry."

Tom grabbed his jacket, copied the files to a USB drive, and ran out the door. He ran down six flights of stairs because the elevators were not working. He ran across the busy street to the printing shop, ordered the fifty copies, and waited impatiently while the machines worked. 

While he was waiting, his mobile phone rang. It was Brenda's emergency line, which was forwarded to his phone. 
"BuildWell Architecture, Tom speaking," he answered, trying to catch his breath. 
"Hello, this is Mrs. Gable from the hospital board," a strict voice said. "My team and I have arrived early. We are sitting in our car in your parking lot. Are we allowed to come upstairs now?"

Tom looked at his watch. It was only ten-fifteen. The VIP clients were over an hour early, and the office was still dark and chaotic. 
"Mrs. Gable, we would be delighted to welcome you early," Tom lied smoothly, forcing a cheerful tone. "However, the building is currently experiencing a temporary power issue. It would be much more comfortable for you to wait. Let me buy your team coffee at the luxury cafe next to our building while we prepare the meeting room."

"Oh, a coffee sounds lovely," Mrs. Gable replied, her tone softening. "We will meet you there."

Tom paid for the blueprints, grabbed the heavy boxes, and ran back across the street. He dropped the boxes in the office lobby and quickly explained the situation to Mr. Vance. 
"The blueprints are here. The clients are early. I am going to buy them coffee and keep them entertained until the power comes back," Tom said rapidly. 

Tom spent the next hour sitting in the cafe with four serious hospital directors. He used all of his conversational skills to keep them smiling and talking about local sports and travel. He constantly checked his phone, waiting for a message from the office. 

Finally, at eleven-twenty, his phone buzzed. It was a text from Mr. Vance: *Power is back! Office is ready. Bring them up.*

Tom smiled warmly at the clients. "Excellent news, everyone. The office is fully prepared for you. Shall we go upstairs?"

When they walked into the BuildWell office, everything was perfect. The lights were shining, the air conditioning was humming, and the beautifully bound blueprints were placed neatly on the large conference table. Mr. Vance shook hands with the clients, and the meeting began successfully. 

Tom collapsed into his chair at the front desk. He was sweating, his legs ached from running, and he was mentally exhausted. He spent the rest of the afternoon doing his normal tasks quietly, answering emails and organizing files. 

At the end of the day, after the clients had left with big smiles, Mr. Vance walked out of the conference room and came straight to Tom's desk. 
"Tom, I want to thank you," Mr. Vance said seriously. "Today was a complete disaster, but you kept your cool. You solved the printing problem, you handled the clients perfectly, and you saved our presentation. You are the glue that keeps this office together."

Tom smiled weakly. "Thank you, Mr. Vance. I am just glad I could help."

As Tom locked the office doors that evening, he reflected on the crazy day. He usually preferred his quiet, predictable routine. But as he walked to the elevator, he realized something new about himself. When everything went wrong, he didn't freeze. He could prioritize problems, make quick decisions, and handle intense pressure. He was stronger and more capable than he had thought. He smiled to himself, looking forward to a quiet Wednesday, but knowing he was ready for whatever happened next.
