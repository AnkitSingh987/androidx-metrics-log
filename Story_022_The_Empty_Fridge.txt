Title: The Empty Fridge
Category: Beginner

It is five o'clock in the afternoon. Mark comes home from work. He is a teacher. He is tired. He is also very hungry. 
He walks into his apartment. He takes off his coat. He takes off his shoes. 
"I want to eat something," Mark says to himself. "I want a big sandwich. Or maybe some eggs."

He walks to the kitchen. His kitchen is small and clean. The biggest thing in the kitchen is the white fridge. 
Mark goes to the fridge. He holds the handle. He pulls the door open. 
He looks inside. 
He expects to see food. He expects to see cheese, meat, eggs, and vegetables. 
But he does not see those things. 

The fridge is almost empty. 
There is a bottle of cold water on the top shelf. There is an old, yellow lemon on the middle shelf. There is a small jar of mustard on the door. 
That is all. There is no cheese. There is no meat. There are no eggs. There are no vegetables. 
The fridge is white and clean, but it is empty. 

Mark is surprised. "Where is all my food?" he asks. 
Then, he remembers. He did not go to the supermarket this week. He was very busy at work. He ate lunch at school. He ate dinner at a restaurant yesterday. He forgot to buy food for his house. 
He closes the fridge door. He feels sad. His stomach makes a noise. It says 'Grumble, grumble'. His stomach wants food. 

Mark opens the kitchen cupboards. Maybe there is something in the cupboards. 
He opens the first cupboard. He sees a box of tea. He sees a bag of sugar. He sees some salt. 
He opens the second cupboard. He sees a clean white plate. He sees a blue bowl. He sees two glasses. 
There is no food in the cupboards. There is no bread. There is no pasta. There is no rice. 

Mark sits on a chair in the kitchen. He is very hungry. He cannot eat an old lemon. He cannot eat mustard. He cannot eat sugar. 
"I have a big problem," he says. "I have no food."
He looks at his watch. It is five-fifteen. The supermarket is still open. The supermarket is not very far. It is at the end of the street. 
"I must go shopping," he decides. "I must go to the supermarket right now."

He stands up. He walks back to the hall. He puts on his shoes. He puts on his coat. He takes his wallet. He takes his keys. He takes a large, reusable shopping bag. He does not want to use plastic bags. 
He opens his apartment door. He walks outside. 

It is a nice afternoon. The sun is going down. The street is busy. Many people are walking home from work. Many people are going to the shops. 
Mark walks quickly. His stomach makes another noise. 'Grumble.' 
"Be quiet, stomach," Mark says. "We are going to get food."

He arrives at the supermarket. The supermarket is big and bright. There are many aisles. There are many shelves. There is a lot of food. 
Mark takes a shopping cart. He pushes the cart into the supermarket. 
First, he goes to the fruit and vegetable section. He buys apples, bananas, and oranges. He buys tomatoes, onions, and potatoes. 
Then, he goes to the dairy section. He buys a big bottle of milk. He buys some yellow cheese. He buys a box of six eggs. He buys some butter. 

Next, he goes to the meat section. He buys some chicken. He buys some fish. 
Finally, he goes to the bakery section. The bread smells delicious. He buys a fresh, brown loaf of bread. 
His shopping cart is full now. It is heavy. He has a lot of food. 

Mark pushes the heavy cart to the cashier. He puts all the food on the belt. The cashier scans the food. Beep, beep, beep. 
"That will be forty-five dollars," the cashier says. 
Mark pays with his card. He puts all the food into his large shopping bag. The bag is very heavy. 

He walks out of the supermarket. He walks back to his apartment. He carries the heavy bag. He is tired, but he is happy. He has food. 
He arrives at his apartment. He goes to the kitchen. 
He opens the white fridge. The fridge is not empty anymore. He puts the milk, the cheese, the eggs, and the butter inside. He puts the chicken and the fish inside. He puts the vegetables in the bottom drawer. 
The fridge looks beautiful. It is full of delicious food. 

Mark takes two slices of fresh bread. He puts butter on the bread. He puts a slice of cheese on the bread. He makes a simple cheese sandwich. 
He takes a bite. It is the best sandwich in the world. 
He is not hungry anymore. His stomach is happy. The empty fridge is full, and Mark is very happy.
