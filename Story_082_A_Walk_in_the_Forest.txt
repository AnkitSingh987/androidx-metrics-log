Title: A Walk in the Forest
Category: Beginner

On Sunday morning, Thomas woke up early. He looked out of the window in his blue bedroom. The sky was pale grey, and a thick, white mist covered the ground. The back garden looked like a mysterious island in the clouds. 
He put on his warm clothes, his thick socks, and his strong boots. He walked quietly down the creaky wooden stairs. 

Grandpa was already in the kitchen, drinking a cup of strong black coffee. He was wearing a heavy green jacket and muddy brown boots. 
"Good morning, Thomas," Grandpa said quietly. "You are up early. Your sister and your parents are still sleeping."
"I want to explore," Thomas said. "Can we go outside?"
"I was just about to go for my morning walk," Grandpa smiled. "Would you like to come with me? There is a beautiful forest behind the house."
"Yes, please!" Thomas answered eagerly. 

They walked out of the back door into the cold morning air. Thomas zipped his coat all the way up. The mist was cold and wet on his face. 
Grandpa led the way across the large garden. They walked past the old apple trees, which had no leaves because it was November. They walked past a wooden shed where Grandpa kept his tools. At the end of the garden, there was a small wooden gate. 

Grandpa opened the gate. It squeaked loudly. 
"This is the entrance to the forest," he said. 
They stepped onto a narrow dirt path covered in brown and yellow autumn leaves. The forest was very dense. The trees were tall and dark, and their branches reached up high into the misty sky. 

It was incredibly quiet. In the city, there was always noise—cars driving, people talking, sirens wailing. But here in the forest, the only sound was the crunching of the dry leaves under their boots. Crunch, crunch, crunch. 
"Listen," Grandpa whispered, stopping suddenly. He held up a finger. 

Thomas stopped and listened hard. At first, he heard nothing. Then, he heard a small, sharp sound high above his head. 'Tap, tap, tap-tap-tap.'
"What is that?" Thomas whispered back. 
"That is a woodpecker," Grandpa explained softly. "It is a special bird that uses its strong beak to hit the trees. It is looking for small insects hidden under the bark."
Thomas looked up, trying to see the bird, but the branches were too thick and the mist was too heavy. 

They continued walking down the narrow path. The trees smelled like damp earth and pine needles. 
Suddenly, Thomas saw a quick movement on the ground near a large oak tree. A small animal with a long, bushy red tail ran quickly up the trunk of the tree. 
"Grandpa, look! A squirrel!" Thomas pointed excitedly. 

"Ah, yes," Grandpa smiled. "That is a red squirrel. They are very busy at this time of year. They collect nuts and hide them in the ground. They need to save food for the cold winter months when there is nothing to eat."
The squirrel stopped on a high branch. It looked down at them with bright, dark eyes. Then, it jumped quickly to another tree and disappeared into the leaves. 

They walked deeper into the forest. The mist began to lift as the sun slowly rose higher in the sky. Pale yellow sunlight broke through the branches, making the wet leaves shine like gold. 
They reached a small, clear stream. The water flowed gently over smooth grey rocks. 
"I like this forest," Thomas said, looking at the water. "It is very peaceful."

"It is," Grandpa agreed, standing beside him. "Nature is very good for the mind. When you live in the city, everything moves very fast. But here, everything has its own slow time. The trees grow slowly, the river flows steadily, and the animals prepare for winter without rushing."

They stood by the stream for a few more minutes, enjoying the quiet morning. Thomas took a deep breath of the fresh, cold air. He felt very happy. He didn't miss his video games or his television at all. 

"Well, my boy," Grandpa said, patting Thomas on the shoulder. "We should head back to the house. I am sure Grandma is cooking breakfast, and I am very hungry."
"Me too," Thomas agreed, realizing his stomach was empty. 

They turned around and followed the crunchy leaf path back through the forest. When they reached the wooden gate, the mist was completely gone. The back garden looked bright and welcoming in the morning sun. Walking in the forest was a perfect start to the day, and Thomas knew he would remember the quiet trees, the tapping woodpecker, and the busy red squirrel for a long time.
