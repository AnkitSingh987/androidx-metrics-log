Title: A Clean Smile
Category: Beginner

Lily is still sitting in the big dentist chair. Dr. Martin just told her that she has no problems with her teeth. She is very happy. But the visit is not completely finished yet. 

"Your teeth look great, Lily," Dr. Martin says. "But I want to make sure you know exactly how to brush them. We want them to stay perfect."
"I brush them every morning and every night," Lily says. 
"That is the perfect routine," Dr. Martin agrees. "But how you brush is also very important."

Dr. Martin turns to a small table. He picks up a large set of plastic teeth. It looks like a giant, funny mouth. Then, he picks up a very large toothbrush. 
Lily laughs. The plastic teeth and the giant toothbrush look very silly. 

"Okay, watch closely," Dr. Martin says. He holds the plastic teeth in one hand. "Many people only brush the front of their teeth. They move the brush from side to side like a saw."
He moves the giant toothbrush quickly across the front of the plastic teeth. 
"But this is not the best way," he explains. "If you do this, you miss the dirt between the teeth."

"What is the best way?" Lily asks. She is very interested. 
"The best way is to brush in small circles," Dr. Martin says. 
He puts the giant toothbrush on the plastic teeth again. This time, he moves it in slow, small circles. 
"Circle, circle, circle," he says. "You must brush the front of the teeth. Then, you must open your mouth and brush the top of the teeth where you chew your food."
He brushes the top of the plastic teeth. 

"And do not forget the most important part," he adds. "You must brush the back of your teeth. The part next to your tongue."
He moves the brush to the inside of the plastic mouth. 
"Small circles everywhere," he repeats. "It should take you two full minutes to brush your teeth correctly. Do you think you can do that?"
"Yes, Dr. Martin," Lily says. "Small circles for two minutes. Front, top, and back."
"Excellent," the dentist smiles. 

"And what about toothpaste?" Lily's mother asks from her chair in the corner. 
"You do not need a lot of toothpaste," Dr. Martin answers. "You only need a small amount, about the size of a green pea. Too much toothpaste is not necessary."

Dr. Martin puts down the giant teeth and the giant toothbrush. He walks over to a small cabinet. He opens the drawer and takes out a small, colorful bag. 
"Because you are such a good patient today, Lily," he says, "I have a small gift for you."
He hands the colorful bag to Lily. 

Lily opens the bag. Inside, there is a brand new toothbrush. It is bright pink, which is her favorite color. There is also a small tube of special children's toothpaste. It has a picture of a smiling crocodile on it. 
"Thank you very much, Dr. Martin!" Lily says excitedly. She loves the pink toothbrush. 
"You are very welcome," he says. "Remember, you should get a new toothbrush every three months. When the bristles on the brush look old and bent, it is time for a new one."

"I will remember," Lily promises. 
"Well, you are all finished here," Dr. Martin says. "Keep up the good work. I will see you again in six months for your next checkup."
"Goodbye, Dr. Martin," Lily says. 

Lily and her mother walk out of the bright room. They say goodbye to the receptionist and go down the elevator. 
When they are back in the car, Lily looks at her new pink toothbrush. 
"I think I want to brush my teeth right now," she says. 
Her mother laughs loudly. "You just had them checked! You can wait until tonight before you go to bed."
"Okay," Lily agrees. "But tonight, I am going to use my new pink brush. And I am going to do small circles for exactly two minutes."

Her mother starts the car. "That sounds like a great plan. A healthy smile is the most beautiful thing you can wear."
Lily smiles in the mirror of the car. Her teeth are white and clean. Going to the dentist is not scary at all. It is just a way to keep your smile looking perfect.
