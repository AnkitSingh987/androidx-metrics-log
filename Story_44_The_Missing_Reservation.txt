Title: The Missing Reservation

Category: Intermediate

Emma checked her reflection in the glass door of the restaurant and took a deep breath. She was wearing her favorite dark blue dress, and she felt fantastic. Tonight was a very special occasion. After three years of hard work, long hours, and difficult projects, she had finally been promoted to Senior Marketing Director at her company. To celebrate, she had invited her three best friends—Chloe, Mark, and Sam—to dinner at 'La Luna', the most popular and expensive Italian restaurant in the city.
She pushed open the heavy glass doors and walked into the elegant lobby. The restaurant was beautiful. Soft yellow lights hung from the ceiling, and there were large green plants in every corner. The smell of garlic, fresh basil, and roasting meat made Emma’s stomach rumble with hunger. The dining room was completely full, filled with the loud, happy sounds of people talking and laughing.
Her friends were already waiting for her near the entrance. Chloe hugged her immediately. "Congratulations, Madam Director! We are so proud of you!"
"Thank you!" Emma laughed, hugging Mark and Sam as well. "I am so excited. I have been wanting to eat here for months. I hear their truffle pasta is incredible."
They walked together to the front desk, where a tall man in a dark suit was looking at a computer screen.
"Good evening," Emma said cheerfully. "I have a reservation for a table of four under the name Emma Carter. For 7:30 PM."
The host smiled politely and typed on his keyboard. He looked at the screen, then scrolled down a bit further. His smile slowly faded, and a small line of worry appeared between his eyebrows.
"I'm sorry, Miss Carter. Did you say it was for tonight? Friday, the 14th?" he asked.
"Yes, that's right," Emma replied. She felt a sudden, cold feeling in her stomach. "A table for four."
The host typed something else, then shook his head. "I apologize, but I cannot find a reservation under the name Carter in our system for tonight."
Emma stared at him in shock. "That's impossible. I made this reservation myself. I called three weeks ago to make sure I could get a table for a Friday night."
Mark stepped forward gently. "Could it be under a different name? Maybe my name, Mark Evans? Or perhaps it was spelled wrong?"
The host searched again but shook his head. "No, sir. I have checked all the names for parties of four this evening. We are completely fully booked. We don't have any empty tables."
Emma felt embarrassed and panicked. This was supposed to be a perfect evening. She had promised her friends an amazing dinner, and now they had nowhere to eat. "Are you absolutely sure? Please, this is a very important celebration. I know I spoke to someone named Julian when I called. I even requested a table near the window."
"Julian is our daytime manager," the host explained calmly. "Let me go to the back office and check the physical reservation book. Sometimes, the daytime staff writes special notes there before entering them into the computer system. Please give me one moment."
The host walked quickly toward the back of the restaurant. Emma turned to her friends, her face red. "I am so sorry, guys. I don't know what happened. I definitely made the reservation."
"Don't worry about it, Emma," Sam said kindly, patting her shoulder. "Mistakes happen. It's just a restaurant. If we can't eat here, we will go somewhere else. The important thing is that we are celebrating your promotion together."
"Sam is right," Chloe agreed. "We could go to that nice Mexican place across the street, or get pizza. It really doesn't matter."
Despite their kind words, Emma still felt terrible. Ten minutes passed, and they stood awkwardly near the entrance as other happy guests walked past them to their tables.
Finally, the host returned. Beside him was an older man wearing a manager's badge. He looked very apologetic.
"Miss Carter?" the manager said. "My name is Mr. Rossi, I am the general manager. I am deeply sorry for this confusion. We found your reservation."
"You did?" Emma asked, feeling a rush of relief. "What happened?"
"It seems my daytime manager, Julian, made a serious administrative error," Mr. Rossi explained, looking slightly embarrassed. "When you called three weeks ago, he wrote your reservation down correctly in the physical book. However, when he transferred the information to our digital system, he accidentally put your reservation in for next month, on the 14th of November, instead of October."
"Oh, I see," Emma said. She was glad she wasn't crazy, but she was still worried. "Does that mean we don't have a table tonight?"
Mr. Rossi smiled warmly. "Absolutely not. This was entirely our mistake, and we will fix it. We want you to celebrate your promotion properly. Unfortunately, the main dining room is currently full. However, if you are willing to wait for about twenty minutes, we have a private dining room in the back that is usually reserved for large parties. We can prepare it just for the four of you."
Emma looked at her friends, who were all nodding enthusiastically. "That would be wonderful," she said. "Thank you."
"While you wait," Mr. Rossi continued, gesturing toward the bar area, "please sit at the bar. Your drinks and any appetizers you would like to order while waiting will be completely free of charge. It is the least we can do to apologize for the stress."
The group happily moved to the bar. The bartender was friendly and made them delicious cocktails. They ordered some olives and fresh bread with olive oil. The stressful situation suddenly felt like a fun adventure. They laughed, toasted to Emma's success, and enjoyed the lively atmosphere.
Exactly twenty minutes later, the host came to the bar. "Miss Carter? Your table is ready. Please follow me."
He led them through the busy restaurant, past the kitchen, and opened a set of heavy wooden doors. Inside was a beautiful, quiet room with a single round table set with white linen, shining glasses, and candles. It was perfect, much better than a normal table in the crowded dining room.
The waiter pulled out Emma's chair for her. "We have also instructed the kitchen to send out a complimentary dessert for you at the end of your meal, to celebrate your promotion," he told her.
Emma sat down, looking at her three best friends smiling across the table. The evening had started with a terrible problem, but it had turned into something incredibly special. It reminded her that in life, just like in business, things don't always go according to plan. But if you stay calm and communicate clearly, the solution can sometimes be better than the original plan.
