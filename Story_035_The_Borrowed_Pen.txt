Title: The Borrowed Pen
Category: Beginner

It is afternoon. The school day is almost over. The children in Room 8 are very tired. But they have one more lesson. It is an English lesson. 
Miss Taylor is their teacher. She is writing sentences on the white board. 
"Please write these sentences in your notebooks," Miss Taylor says. "You must practice your handwriting. Write neatly."

Sam opens his notebook. He is nine years old. He wants to write neatly. He takes his pen from his pencil case. It is a blue pen. 
He starts to write the first sentence. 
'The cat sits on the mat.'
He writes 'The cat s...'. Then, the blue ink stops. 
The pen makes a scratchy sound on the paper, but there is no blue line. 

Sam looks at his pen. He shakes it. He tries to write again. 
Nothing happens. 
He draws circles on a scrap of paper. He presses hard. No ink comes out. The pen is empty. The pen is dead. 

Sam looks in his pencil case. He has pencils. He has a red pen for marking mistakes. But he does not have another blue pen or a black pen. 
He cannot write his sentences with a pencil. Miss Taylor said they must use a pen. He cannot write his sentences with a red pen. Red is for teachers. 
He has a problem. He needs a pen right now. 

He looks at the girl sitting next to him. Her name is Lily. Lily is very organized. Her desk is always tidy. She is writing beautifully in her notebook. 
Next to her notebook, there is a large, clear pencil case. Sam can see many pens inside. There are blue pens, black pens, green pens, and pink pens. 
Sam feels a little shy, but he must ask. 

"Excuse me, Lily," Sam whispers. 
Lily stops writing. She looks at him. "Yes, Sam?" 
"My blue pen is empty. It stopped working," Sam says. He shows her his empty pen. "Can I please borrow a pen from you? Just for this lesson."
Lily looks at her pencil case. She smiles. 
"Of course," she says. "You can borrow one."

She opens her clear pencil case. 
"What color do you want?" she asks. "I have blue or black."
"Blue, please," Sam says. 
Lily takes out a nice blue pen. It has a soft grip. She hands it to Sam. 
"Here you go," she says. 
"Thank you very much, Lily," Sam says. "I will give it back at the end of the lesson."
"You are welcome," she says. She goes back to her writing. 

Sam takes the borrowed pen. He holds it in his hand. It feels different from his old pen, but it is very nice. 
He puts the pen on the paper. He finishes the word 'sits'. The blue ink is bright and clear. It flows smoothly. 
He writes the rest of the sentence. 'The cat sits on the mat.'
His handwriting looks very good with this pen. 

He copies all the sentences from the white board. He works quietly and carefully. He finishes his work before the bell rings. 
He puts his notebook in his bag. 
Then, the bell rings loudly. Brrrrrring! 
The school day is over. The children are excited. They talk loudly. They pack their bags quickly. 

Sam remembers the pen. He does not put the borrowed pen in his bag. That would be stealing. He must return it. 
He turns to Lily. She is putting her clear pencil case into her bag. 
"Lily," Sam says. 
She looks up. 
Sam holds out the blue pen. "Here is your pen. Thank you for lending it to me. It is a very good pen."
Lily takes the pen from his hand. She puts it back in her pencil case. 
"I am glad it helped," she says. "You are welcome, Sam."

"I will buy a new pen on my way home today," Sam says. "I will buy two new pens, so I don't run out of ink again."
"That is a good idea," Lily says with a smile. 

They both stand up. They put their chairs under their desks. They walk out of the classroom together. 
Sam is happy. He finished his work. His handwriting was neat. And he learned a good lesson. It is okay to ask for help when you have a problem. And it is very important to always return what you borrow.
