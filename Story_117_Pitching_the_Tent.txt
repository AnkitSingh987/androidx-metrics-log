Title: Pitching the Tent
Category: Beginner

The drive to Pine Mountain took three hours. The road went higher and higher, winding through dense, green forests. Ben opened the car window and took a deep breath. His father was right; the air up here was fresh, cool, and smelled wonderfully of pine needles and damp earth. 

They arrived at the Pine Mountain campsite at ten o'clock in the morning. It was a beautiful place. The campsite was located in a large clearing surrounded by incredibly tall trees. A small, clear river flowed quickly along the edge of the campsite. 
There were a few other tents set up in the distance, but it was generally very quiet and peaceful. 

"Okay, first things first," his father said, stopping the car near a flat patch of green grass. "We need to set up our camp before we do anything else. Building a house is our priority."
Ben opened the trunk and pulled out the heavy green tent bag. 

They carried everything over to the flat grass. 
"This looks like a good spot," his father assessed, looking at the ground. "It is flat, and there are no large rocks or tree roots to sleep on. Always check the ground before you pitch a tent."

They took the tent out of the bag and spread the green fabric flat on the grass. 
"Now, we need the poles," his father instructed. Ben took the long, thin metal poles from the bag. They were connected by an elastic string, so they snapped together easily to form two very long, flexible sticks. 

"We have to slide these poles through the fabric sleeves on the outside of the tent," his father explained. "You take that end, and I will take this end. We have to push at the same time."
They worked together as a team. It was a little difficult because the poles were very long and kept bending in the wrong direction. 
"Push gently, Ben," his father said. "If you push too hard, you might rip the fabric."

Finally, both poles were in place, forming a large 'X' shape across the green fabric. 
"Okay, this is the hard part," his father said, standing at one corner. "When I say 'go', we both push our ends of the poles inward, and the tent will pop up. Ready? One, two, three, go!"

Ben pushed his metal pole hard. Suddenly, the flat green fabric rose up into the air, forming a perfect dome shape. 
"We did it!" Ben cheered, holding his side of the tent up. 
"Not quite yet," his father corrected him, wiping sweat from his forehead. "If the wind blows now, our tent will fly away like a giant green balloon. We need to secure it to the ground with the pegs."

His father handed Ben a small rubber hammer and a handful of metal pegs. 
"Hit the pegs through the loops at the corners of the tent," his father directed. "Hit them deep into the soil at a forty-five-degree angle. That makes them much stronger."

Ben knelt on the grass. He placed a metal peg through a fabric loop and hit it hard with the rubber hammer. 'Thwack, thwack, thwack.' The metal went deep into the dark earth. He moved around the tent, hammering in all eight pegs until the tent was tight and secure. 

"Excellent work," his father praised him, shaking the tent to test it. It did not move. "A very solid construction."
They unzipped the front door and threw their sleeping bags and foam mats inside. 
"The house is built," Ben smiled, feeling a sense of accomplishment. "What is next on the schedule?"
"Next," his father said, pointing towards the tall, rocky peaks in the distance. "We put on our strong boots and we go for a long hike. I want to see the top of that mountain."
