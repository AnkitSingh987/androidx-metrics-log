Title: Meeting an Old Friend
Category: Beginner

It is a busy Saturday afternoon in the city center. Many people are walking on the wide sidewalks. They are carrying shopping bags, drinking coffee, and talking to their friends. 
Emily is walking down the street. She is thirty years old. She works in an office, and Saturday is her day to relax. She is wearing a yellow summer dress and sunglasses. She is looking at the clothes in the shop windows. 

Suddenly, she sees a woman walking towards her. The woman has short, curly brown hair and is wearing a blue jacket. 
Emily stops walking. She knows that face. She knows that curly brown hair. 
"Is that Rachel?" Emily whispers to herself. 

She has not seen Rachel for five years. They were very good friends at university. They studied together, lived in the same apartment, and shared many secrets. But after university, Rachel moved to another country, and they lost contact. 

Emily walks a little faster. As the woman gets closer, Emily is sure. It is her old friend. 
"Rachel!" Emily calls out loudly. 

The woman in the blue jacket stops. She looks around, confused. Then, she sees Emily. Her eyes open wide in surprise. A massive smile spreads across her face. 
"Emily? Oh my goodness! Emily!" Rachel shouts. 

They run towards each other. They hug tightly right in the middle of the busy sidewalk. People walk around them, but they do not care. They are so happy to see each other. 
"I cannot believe it is you!" Emily says, stepping back to look at her friend. "You look fantastic! I love your short hair."
"Thank you!" Rachel laughs. "And you look exactly the same. You still love wearing yellow."

"What are you doing here?" Emily asks quickly. "I thought you lived in Australia!"
"I did live in Australia for four years," Rachel explains. "But I moved back here three months ago. I got a new job in this city. I have been meaning to call you, but I lost my old phone contacts."
"That is wonderful news!" Emily says. "I am so glad you are back."

"Me too," Rachel says. "It is great to be home. How are you? What are you doing these days?"
"I am good," Emily answers. "I am still working at the same marketing company. It is busy, but I enjoy it."
"Are you still living in the small apartment near the river?" Rachel asks. She remembers many details from their past. 
"Yes, I am still there," Emily smiles. "I love that apartment too much to leave."

They stand on the sidewalk for a few minutes, asking questions rapidly. It feels like no time has passed. They are talking easily and comfortably, just like old times. 
"Listen," Rachel says. "We cannot talk on the street all afternoon. People are trying to walk past us. Do you have time for a coffee? I really want to catch up properly."
"I have plenty of time," Emily says happily. "I was just doing some window shopping. I would love a coffee."

"Great," Rachel says. "There is a nice, quiet café just around the corner. Let's go there."
They walk together to the café. They sit at a small table near the window. A waiter comes, and they both order a cappuccino and a slice of chocolate cake. 

For the next two hours, they sit in the café and talk. They talk about their jobs, their families, and their plans for the future. Rachel tells Emily stories about her life in Australia. She talks about the beaches, the strange animals, and the hot weather. Emily tells Rachel all the news about their old university friends. 

They laugh a lot. They remember funny things they did when they were younger. 
"Do you remember when we burned the dinner and had to eat cereal instead?" Emily asks, laughing. 
"Yes!" Rachel laughs loudly. "We were terrible cooks then."

When the sun starts to go down, they finally stop talking. Their coffee cups are empty, and their cake is gone. 
"This was wonderful, Rachel," Emily says. "I am so happy I saw you today."
"It was the best surprise," Rachel agrees. 

They stand up and pay the bill. Before they leave the café, Emily takes out her phone. 
"We must not lose contact again," she says firmly. "Give me your new phone number."
Rachel takes Emily's phone and types her number into it. She calls her own phone so she has Emily's number too. 
"There," Rachel says. "Now we are connected again."

"Are you free next Friday evening?" Emily asks. "You can come to my apartment for dinner. I promise I will not burn the food this time."
Rachel smiles warmly. "I would love to. Next Friday is perfect."
They walk out of the café and hug one more time. They walk in different directions, but they are both smiling. Meeting an old friend on a busy street is a wonderful piece of luck. It brings back good memories and starts a new chapter of friendship.
