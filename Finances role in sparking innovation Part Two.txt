Title: Finances role in sparking innovation Part Two

Finance's role in sparking innovation

There is no attention paid to the total time and effort in mass-scale switching to a product that is not better than what people are already using. Anyhow, China did not grow spectacularly from such bans. The growth came from massive low-cost exports. India has no such exports. Now, China is leading or competing with the world in several emerging technologies.

India is not. So, India has to find its own way. Funding risk One of the main reasons for India not having breakthrough innovations from its start-ups is that India's financial markets are not willing or able to finance ideas that are seen as high risk. Even somewhat established start-ups use or need foreign funds, though some start-ups do manage with Indian funds.

However, India's savings generally flow into less risky ventures, such as projects undertaken by flourishing conglomerates. A key part of the problem is that India does not have the financial instruments needed to channel non-risk-averse funds into risky ventures. The average Indian saver will never finance risky investments.

However, now India has enough people who can risk some money in hopes of high rewards. But they are not well-connected to risky start-ups. We need to create new workable channels to channel money from willing Indian savers to risky start-ups. It's not something that can be done easily or quickly. This is what the RBI, the Union Finance Ministry, financially savvy private groups, and thinkers need to put together.

It's this financial sector innovation that will spur breakthrough innovations from bright young Indians. Let's get moving. By Subodh Mathur The writer is an economist with extensive practical public policy experience
