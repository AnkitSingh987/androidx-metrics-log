Title: The New Plate
Category: Beginner

Alice is in a shop. It is a shop that sells things for the home. It sells glasses, cups, pots, and pans. It is a very big shop with many shiny things. 
Alice is looking for something special. She wants to buy a new plate. 

Yesterday, Alice had an accident in her kitchen. She was washing the dishes after dinner. The soap made her hands slippery. A plate slipped from her hands. It fell on the hard floor. 'Crash!' The plate broke into many small pieces. 
Alice was sad. It was one of her favorite plates. It was part of a set of four. Now she only has three plates. She needs four plates because she often invites her three friends for dinner. 

So, today she is in the shop to buy a new plate. She wants to find a plate that matches her other three plates. Her other plates are white with a thin gold line around the edge. 
Alice walks down the aisles. She looks at the shelves. There are hundreds of plates. 
She sees blue plates. She sees red plates. She sees plates with pictures of flowers. She sees square plates. 

"Where are the simple white plates?" she asks herself. 
She turns a corner. Ah, there they are. 
There is a whole shelf of white plates. She looks at them closely. Some are completely white. Some have a silver line. 
Then, she sees it. It is a white plate with a thin gold line around the edge. It looks exactly like her broken plate. 

Alice reaches out and picks up the plate. It is heavy and smooth. The white part is very bright. The gold line is very shiny. 
She turns the plate over. She looks at the price on the back. It is ten dollars. Ten dollars is a good price for a nice plate. 
"I will buy this one," Alice decides. 

She walks to the front of the shop. She goes to the cashier. 
The cashier is a young woman. She smiles at Alice. 
"Did you find everything you need?" the cashier asks. 
"Yes, thank you. I just need this plate," Alice says. She puts the plate gently on the counter. 

The cashier scans the barcode on the bottom of the plate. Beep. 
"That is ten dollars, please," she says. 
Alice pays with a ten-dollar bill. 
"Would you like me to wrap it in paper?" the cashier asks. "It is fragile. It can break easily."
"Yes, please," Alice says. "Wrap it very well."

The cashier takes some thick brown paper. She wraps the plate carefully. She puts tape on the paper. Then she puts the wrapped plate into a strong plastic bag. 
"Thank you," Alice says. She takes the bag. 
"Have a nice day," the cashier says. 

Alice walks out of the shop. She holds the bag carefully. She does not swing the bag. She does not want to drop it. She walks slowly to the bus stop. She rides the bus home. She sits with the bag on her lap. She holds it with two hands. 

She arrives at her apartment. She unlocks the door. She goes straight to the kitchen. 
She takes the wrapped plate out of the bag. She takes off the tape. She opens the brown paper. 
The new plate is perfect. It is very clean and shiny. 

But Alice must wash it before she uses it. It came from a shop. Many people touched it in the shop. It might be dusty. 
She turns on the warm water in the sink. She puts a little liquid soap on a yellow sponge. 
She holds the plate carefully with one hand. She washes the front of the plate with the sponge. She washes the gold line. She washes the back of the plate. There are lots of soap bubbles. 
Then, she rinses the plate under the warm water. The soap bubbles wash away. The plate is squeaky clean. 

She takes a dry, clean towel. She dries the plate completely. She rubs it until it shines. 
Now, she wants to compare it. 
She opens her kitchen cupboard. She takes out one of her old plates. 
She puts the old plate on the counter. She puts the new plate next to it. 

She looks at them. They are exactly the same size. They are exactly the same shape. The white color is the same. The gold line is the same. 
Alice smiles. She is very pleased. 
"They are a perfect match," she says happily. "Nobody will know this is a new plate."

She puts the old plate back into the cupboard. She puts the new plate on top of it. Now she has four plates again. 
She is ready for her dinner party this weekend. She will cook a big meal for her friends. She will serve the food on her four matching plates. 
She closes the cupboard door. She is very happy she went shopping today. The broken plate is forgotten. The new plate is safe in its new home.
