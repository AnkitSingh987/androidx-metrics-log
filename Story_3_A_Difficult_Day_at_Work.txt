Title: A Difficult Day at Work

Category: Intermediate

Tom loved his job working at the front desk of the Grand Plaza Hotel. It was usually a quiet, predictable job. He enjoyed greeting guests, giving them recommendations for local restaurants, and ensuring their stay was comfortable. He wore a crisp, white shirt and a dark blue suit, and he always kept a friendly smile on his face. However, Tuesday morning proved to be anything but predictable. 

It all started when Tom’s alarm clock failed to go off. He woke up in a panic, realizing he was already thirty minutes late. He jumped out of bed, put on his uniform as fast as he could, and ran out the door without eating breakfast or drinking coffee. 

As if waking up late wasn't bad enough, the traffic on the highway was terrible. A delivery truck had broken down, blocking two lanes, and cars were moving incredibly slowly. By the time Tom finally arrived at the hotel, parked his car, and ran into the beautiful marble lobby, he was forty-five minutes late.

He rushed behind the front desk, breathing heavily. His manager, Sarah, was already there. She looked unusually stressed and pale. 

“I am so sorry I’m late, Sarah,” Tom said quickly, trying to fix his tie. “My alarm didn't ring, and then there was a huge traffic jam—”

“Don't worry about that right now,” Sarah interrupted, rubbing her forehead. “We have a much bigger problem. The hotel's central computer reservation system crashed fifteen minutes ago. The entire network is completely down. We can't see who is checking in, who is checking out, or which rooms are clean. The IT department is trying to fix it, but they said it might take a few hours.”

Tom felt his stomach drop. A modern hotel relies entirely on its computer system. Without it, they were blind. 

“What do we do?” Tom asked, trying to remain calm.

“We do it the old-fashioned way,” Sarah said, handing him a thick stack of printed papers and a pen. “Luckily, the night shift printed a backup report at 4:00 AM. We have to manually match arriving guests with empty rooms and write everything down. If a guest wants to check out, we have to write down their room number and manually process their credit cards later. I need you to handle the front desk while I go upstairs to coordinate with the housekeeping staff using a radio.”

“I can do that,” Tom nodded, though he felt incredibly nervous. 

Just as Sarah walked away, the automatic doors opened, and a large group of people entered the lobby. Among them was a tall man in an expensive grey suit who looked extremely impatient. He walked straight to Tom’s desk and placed his leather bag heavily on the counter.

“Good morning, sir. Welcome to the Grand Plaza,” Tom said, forcing his usual polite smile. “How can I help you today?”

“My name is Richard Henderson,” the man said loudly. “I need to check into my room immediately. I have a very important business meeting in one hour, and I need to change my clothes and take a shower.”

Tom quickly looked at his printed backup report. He found the name 'Henderson', but according to the paper, the guest's assigned room on the tenth floor had not been cleaned yet by 4:00 AM. Since the computer was down, Tom had no idea if the housekeeping staff had cleaned it recently. 

“I apologize, Mr. Henderson,” Tom said politely. “Unfortunately, our computer systems are currently down, and official check-in time is not until 3:00 PM. I am not certain if your room is ready at this moment.”

Mr. Henderson’s face turned red with anger. “What do you mean you don't know? This is supposed to be a luxury hotel! I requested an early check-in when I booked the room a month ago. I pay a lot of money to stay here, and I expect my room to be ready!”

Several other guests in the lobby turned their heads to look at the angry man. Tom felt a drop of sweat run down his neck. His instinct was to argue back, to explain that it wasn't his fault the system crashed, but he knew that would only make the situation worse. He took a deep, slow breath to calm himself. 

“You are absolutely right to be frustrated, Mr. Henderson, and I sincerely apologize for the inconvenience,” Tom said in a calm, steady voice. “I understand how important your meeting is. Please give me just one moment to personally check the status of your room with our housekeeping manager.”

Tom picked up the two-way radio and called Sarah. “Sarah, this is Tom. Do we know if room 1014 is clean and ready?”

There was static on the radio before Sarah answered. “Let me ask the cleaners on the tenth floor... Stand by.”

A minute passed. Mr. Henderson tapped his fingers impatiently on the desk. 

“Tom,” Sarah's voice came back on the radio. “Room 1014 is currently being cleaned. It will take at least twenty more minutes.”

Tom put down the radio and looked at the angry guest. “Mr. Henderson, the cleaning staff is currently finishing your room. It will be completely ready in twenty minutes.”

“Twenty minutes? I don't have twenty minutes to stand here waiting!” the man complained loudly. 

“I completely understand,” Tom replied smoothly, thinking quickly. “While you wait, I would like to offer you complimentary access to our Executive VIP Lounge on the top floor. It is very quiet there. You can have a fresh cup of premium coffee, eat some breakfast, and relax. I will personally come and find you in the lounge the exact moment your room is ready and bring you your keys.”

Mr. Henderson stopped tapping his fingers. He looked slightly surprised by the offer. The VIP Lounge usually cost fifty dollars extra per day. 

“The VIP lounge?” he repeated, his voice softening slightly. “Fine. I suppose I can wait there and have a coffee.”

“Thank you for your patience, sir,” Tom smiled, calling a hotel porter to guide the man to the elevator. 

For the next three hours, the lobby was incredibly busy. Tom had to manually check in over twenty guests using only his printed papers. He had to call the housekeeping department constantly on the radio to verify room statuses. It was slow and frustrating work, but Tom remained professional, polite, and calm with every single guest. He apologized for the delays and offered free drinks to people who had to wait.

Finally, at 12:30 PM, the computer screens at the front desk suddenly lit up. The reservation software loaded successfully. The IT department had fixed the problem.

Tom let out a huge sigh of relief. He quickly typed in the manual information he had collected all morning to update the system. 

Later that afternoon, as Tom was organizing some documents, Mr. Henderson approached the front desk again. He was wearing a fresh suit and looked much more relaxed. 

Tom immediately stood up straight. “Good afternoon, Mr. Henderson. I hope your business meeting went well.”

“It went very well, thank you,” the man said. He paused for a moment and looked at Tom. “I wanted to come back and apologize for my behavior this morning. I was very stressed about my meeting, and I was rude to you. You handled the situation perfectly. You remained calm, you solved my problem, and the coffee in the VIP lounge was excellent. You are very good at your job.”

“Thank you very much, sir. I appreciate your kind words, and I'm glad we could help,” Tom smiled genuinely. 

After the man left, Sarah walked up to the desk and patted Tom on the shoulder. “I heard everything. You did a fantastic job today, Tom. Keeping your cool under pressure is the most important skill in this industry.”

Tom smiled, feeling a deep sense of pride. The morning had started as a complete disaster, but he had managed to turn it around. He had learned that he could handle difficult situations and angry people without losing his temper. As he looked around the peaceful hotel lobby, he realized that even on the hardest days, he truly loved his job.
