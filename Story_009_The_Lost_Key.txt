Title: The Lost Key
Category: Beginner

David is a young man. He is twenty years old. He lives in a small apartment. Today is Saturday. It is a sunny afternoon. David wants to go outside. He wants to go to the park. He wants to meet his friends. 

He is ready to go. He is wearing his jeans. He is wearing a white T-shirt. He is wearing his red sneakers. 
He takes his phone from the table. He puts it in his pocket. He takes his wallet from the desk. He puts it in his other pocket. 
Now, he needs his house key. He cannot leave his apartment without the key. He must lock the door. And he needs the key to come back inside later. 

David walks to the small table near the front door. He always puts his key on this table. He looks at the table. 
There is a letter on the table. There is a pen on the table. There is an old newspaper on the table. But there is no key. The key is missing. 

"Where is my key?" David says to himself. 
He moves the letter. The key is not under it. He moves the newspaper. The key is not under it. He is confused. He always leaves it there. 

David starts to look around the living room. The living room is not very big. There is a sofa, a television, and a coffee table. 
He walks to the sofa. He looks on the sofa cushions. There is a remote control. There is a magazine. But no key. 
He lifts the cushions. Sometimes things fall under the cushions. He sees a coin. He sees some dust. But he does not see a key. 

He walks to the coffee table. There is a cup of coffee from the morning. There is a book. But there is no key. 
David is starting to feel annoyed. He wants to go to the park. His friends are waiting for him. 
He looks at his watch. It is two o'clock. He is going to be late. 

"Think, David, think," he says. "When did I come home yesterday?"
He remembers yesterday evening. He came home from work. He was very tired. He unlocked the door. He came inside. 
"Did I put the key in my bag?" he thinks. 
He goes to his bedroom. His black work bag is on the chair. He opens the bag. He looks inside. He sees his notebook. He sees his pens. He searches with his hands. He reaches the bottom of the bag. Nothing. The key is not in the bag. 

David stands in his bedroom. He looks at his bed. He looks at his desk. Where can it be? 
Then, his phone rings. It is in his pocket. He takes it out. The screen says 'Paul'. Paul is his friend. 
David answers the phone. "Hello, Paul."
"Hi, David," Paul says. "Are you coming to the park? We are here."
"I am trying to come," David says. "But I have a problem."
"What is the problem?" Paul asks. 
"I cannot find my house key," David says. "I cannot leave the apartment. If I leave, I cannot lock the door. And I cannot get back in."
Paul laughs a little. "You always lose things, David. Did you check your pockets?"

David stops. "My pockets?" he says. 
"Yes," Paul says. "The pockets of the clothes you wore yesterday."
David thinks. What did he wear yesterday? He wore his black jacket. It was cold yesterday evening. 
"My black jacket!" David says loudly. 
"Go check," Paul says. "I will wait on the phone."

David runs to the hallway. There is a hook on the wall. His black jacket is hanging on the hook. 
He goes to the jacket. He touches the left pocket. It is flat. Nothing is inside. 
He touches the right pocket. He feels something hard. He feels something small. 
He puts his hand inside the right pocket. His fingers touch cold metal. 
He pulls it out. It is his house key! It has a small blue keychain. 

"I found it!" David shouts into the phone. 
"Where was it?" Paul asks. 
"It was in the pocket of my black jacket," David says. "I put it there yesterday evening and forgot."
"See? I am a genius," Paul says. "Now hurry up. Come to the park."
"I am coming right now," David says. "See you soon."
"See you," Paul says. David hangs up the phone. 

David smiles. He is very happy. He holds the key in his hand tightly. He will not lose it again today. 
He walks to the front door. He opens the door. He steps outside into the hall. He closes the door behind him. He puts the key into the lock. He turns the key. 'Click.' The door is locked. 
His apartment is safe. 
He puts the key safely into the front pocket of his jeans. He pats his pocket to make sure it is there. 
Now he is ready. He runs down the stairs. He walks out of the building. The sun is warm. He is going to the park. He is going to see his friends. And he has his key.
