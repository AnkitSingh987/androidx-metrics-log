Category: Beginner
Title: Lily the Beautiful Cat and the Dog

One day, a beautiful cat named Lily went for a jog. She liked to run in the sun. As she jogged, she saw a big dog. The dog was not nice. Lily was scared. Lily said, "Please don't hurt me, big dog." The big dog barked and chased Lily. Lily ran fast. She knew a place to hide. She thought, "I will go there to be safe." Lily ran to her hiding place. The big dog could not find her. Lily was happy. She said, "I am glad I knew where to hide." In the end, Lily was safe and went home.

One day, a little girl named Mia found a seed in her backyard. She thought, "I want to grow this seed into a great big plant!" So, Mia dug a hole and put the seed in it. She covered the seed with dirt and gave it water. The next day, Mia saw a tiny green shape coming out of the ground. She was very happy and said, "Look, the seed is growing!" Every day, Mia gave the plant water and watched it grow bigger and bigger. One day, Mia's friend Tom came to play. He saw the plant and said, "Wow, Mia! That's a great big plant!" Mia smiled and said, "Yes, I grew it from a tiny seed. Now it's a big, strong plant!" Tom and Mia played near the plant all day, proud of how great it had grown.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue had a friend named Tim. They liked to play together. One day, Sue and Tim went to the park. They saw a big slide and felt the urge to play on it. Sue said, "Tim, do you remember the last time we played on the slide?" Tim nodded and smiled. They both climbed up the slide and slid down, laughing and having fun. After a while, Sue saw a boy named Sam. Sam was sitting alone and looked sad. Sue felt ashamed that they did not invite Sam to play with them. She said to Tim, "Let's ask Sam to play with us!" Tim agreed, and they all played together on the slide, becoming good friends.

Nona and her mom were washing hands every day of the week. Sometimes it was when coming in from outside, and other times it was when Nona was finished with playing in the mud. "Why is it so important to wash hands, Mommy?" asked Nona, squinting her eyes as the stream of warm water ran over her fingers. "To stay healthy and strong," her mom said while rubbing the soap between her fingers. Nona smiled as she could tell her mom was being compassionate, caring for her by helping her wash. The simple ritual became as reassuring to Nona as a big hug. Nona enjoyed the extra time her mom was giving her and treasured learning an important lesson of health and hygiene. At the end of the week, Nona knew exactly what to do: grab a bar of soap and wash her hands!

Once upon a time, there was a silly cat named Tom. He loved to play and make funny faces. One day, Tom met a new friend named Tina. Tina was a little bird who liked to sing. "Hi, I'm Tom," said the silly cat. "Do you want to play with me?" Tina smiled and said, "Yes, I would love to play. But first, I want to show you a special trick." Tina told Tom to close his eyes. She started to sing a little song. As she sang, a magic spell arrived. When Tom opened his eyes, he saw that they were now in a beautiful garden full of flowers and butterflies. "Wow!" said Tom. "That was amazing! How did you do that?" Tina giggled and said, "It's a secret. But now we can play all day in this pretty place." So, Tom and Tina played in the garden until the sun went down. They had lots of fun and became the best of friends. And from that day on, they always had a magical time when they were together.

Lia and Sam were playing in the park. They liked to pretend they were hunters in the jungle. They had sticks that they used as spears. They threw them at the trees and the bushes. "Look, Sam, I found a big bird!" Lia said. She pointed at a gray and white creature with a long neck and legs. It was eating some grass near the fence. "That's not a bird, Lia, that's a giraffe!" Sam said. He had seen one in a book. "It's very unusual. It has spots and horns and a long tongue." "Can we catch it?" Lia asked. She wanted to touch it and see how it felt. "I don't know. It's very tall and fast. Maybe it will run away or kick us." Sam said. He was a little scared of the giraffe. They decided to try anyway. They crept closer to the fence, holding their spears. They waited for the right moment to throw them. But the giraffe saw them. It lifted its head and looked at them with big brown eyes. It made a funny sound, like a whistle. "Hello, children," it said. "What are you doing with those sticks?" Lia and Sam dropped their spears. They were very surprised. They did not know that giraffes could talk. "Uh, we were playing," Lia said. "We're sorry, we didn't mean to hurt you." "That's all right," the giraffe said. "I'm not hurt. I'm just curious. Why do you want to catch me?" "We wanted to see you up close," Sam said. "You're very unusual. We've never seen a giraffe before." "Well, you can see me now," the giraffe said. "But you don't need to throw sticks at me. You can just ask me nicely. I'm very friendly." "Can we touch you?" Lia asked. "Sure, you can touch me," the giraffe said. "But be gentle. And don't pull my tail." Lia and Sam smiled. They walked up to the fence and reached out their hands. The giraffe bent down and let them stroke its fur and horns. It felt soft and warm. They liked the giraffe. It was unusual, but nice. They decided to be friends with it. They asked it many questions and learned many things. They had a lot of fun. The end.
