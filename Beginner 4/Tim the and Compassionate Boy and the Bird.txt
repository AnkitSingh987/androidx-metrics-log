Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the and Compassionate Boy and the Bird

Once upon a time, there was a kind and compassionate boy named Tim. Tim loved to count things. One day, Tim found a shiny trumpet in his room. He was very happy and wanted to play it all the time. One sunny day, Tim took his trumpet to the park. He played the trumpet and counted how many times he could blow it. He counted one, two, three, four, five times. Suddenly, a big wind came and took his trumpet away. Tim was very sad and didn't know what to do. Then, a nice bird saw Tim's sad face and wanted to help. The bird flew high and got the trumpet back for Tim. Tim was so happy and thanked the bird. The bird told Tim that being compassionate and kind to others is important. Tim learned that being nice and helping others makes the world a better place.

---

One day, a little girl named Lily went to the park with her mom. They had a big bag with them. It was a very important day for Lily because it was her birthday. Her mom planned a fun party at the park with all of Lily's friends. Lily's mom put a big table under a tree. She put a pretty cake, plates, and cups on the table. Lily's friends came to the park and they all played games. They laughed and had so much fun. Lily's mom said, "Don't forget, we will eat cake soon!" After playing, Lily and her friends sat at the table. But her mom looked worried. She had forgotten something important. She said, "Oh no, I forgot the forks!" Lily's friends looked at each other, not knowing what to do. Lily's friend, Tim, had an idea. He said, "We can use our hands!" All the kids agreed and they ate the cake with their hands. It was messy but so much fun. Lily's mom smiled and said, "I won't forget the forks next time!" Everyone laughed and they all had a great birthday party.

One day, a small rabbit named Tim hopped into the park. He saw a big, green celery. He was very hungry, so he went near it. But, a big, scary lion entered the park too. The lion looked dangerous and hungry. Tim was scared and said, "Please, Mr. Lion, don't eat me. I am very small. You can eat the big, green celery instead." The lion looked at the celery and then looked at Tim. To Tim's surprise, the lion said, "Thank you, little rabbit. I like celery a lot! I don't want to eat you. Let's be friends and eat the celery together." They both ate the celery and became good friends. From that day on, they played together and shared their food. The dangerous lion was not so dangerous after all.

Tom was a lazy child. He did not like to play, to learn, or to help. He only liked to watch TV and eat snacks. His mom and dad were sad and worried. One day, his mom said, "Tom, you need to improve. You are too lazy. You need to play, to learn, and to help. Or you will not grow strong and smart and kind." Tom said, "No, mom. I do not want to improve. I like to be lazy. It is easy and fun. Playing, learning, and helping are hard and boring." His mom said, "OK, Tom. You can be lazy today. But tomorrow, you will have a surprise." The next day, Tom woke up and saw a big box in his room. He was curious and excited. He opened the box and found a new bike. It was shiny and red and had a bell. He wanted to ride it right away. But his mom said, "Tom, this is your surprise. This is your new bike. But you cannot ride it yet. You need to improve first. You need to play, to learn, and to help for one week. Then you can ride your bike." Tom was angry and sad. He said, "That is not fair, mom. You gave me a bike, but you do not let me ride it. That is mean." His mom said, "No, Tom. I am not mean. I love you. I want you to improve. I want you to be happy and healthy and good. If you are lazy, you will not enjoy your bike. You will be tired and sick and lonely. But if you are active, you will have more energy, more skills, and more friends. You will love your bike more. Trust me, Tom. Improve and you will see." Tom thought about it. He looked at his bike. He looked at his mom. He said, "OK, mom. I will try to improve. I will play, I will learn, I will help. But only for one week. Then I will ride my bike." His mom smiled and hugged him. She said, "Good, Tom. I am proud of you. You will see. You will have fun and learn and help. And you will ride your bike. You will be happy." And so, Tom started to improve. He played with his toys and his friends. He learned new words and numbers and colors. He helped his mom and dad with chores and errands. He felt better and better. He was not lazy anymore. And after one week, he rode his bike. He was so happy. He rang his bell and shouted, "Look at me, mom. Look at me, dad. I am riding my bike. I improved. I am not lazy anymore. Thank you, mom. Thank you, dad. I love you. And I love my bike." The end.
