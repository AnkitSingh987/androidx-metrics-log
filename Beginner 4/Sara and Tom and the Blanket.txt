Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and Tom and the Blanket

Sara and Tom are playing hide and seek in the house. Sara is the seeker and Tom is the hider. Sara closes her eyes and counts to ten. "One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten! Ready or not, here I come!" Sara says. She opens her eyes and looks around. She sees a big wall with many pictures on it. She thinks Tom might be hiding behind the wall. She walks to the wall and peeks behind it. But she does not see Tom. She only sees a lamp and a chair. "Where are you, Tom?" Sara calls out. Tom hears Sara's voice. He is hiding under the bed in the bedroom. He giggles quietly. He thinks Sara will not find him. He likes hiding under the bed. It is dark and cozy. Sara hears Tom's giggle. She follows the sound. She goes to the bedroom and sees the bed. She thinks Tom might be hiding under the bed. She crawls to the bed and lifts the blanket. She sees Tom's feet and legs. "Found you, Tom!" Sara says. Tom laughs and comes out from under the bed. He gives Sara a hug. "You are good at seeking, Sara!" Tom says. "Thank you, Tom! You are good at hiding!" Sara says. They smile and decide to play again. They take turns being the seeker and the hider. They have fun counting and hiding and seeking. They love playing hide and seek.

Lily was a little girl who liked to chew on things. She chewed on her toys, her clothes, and sometimes even her hair. Her mom and dad told her to stop chewing on things, but she did not listen. She thought it was fun and tasty. One day, Lily found a new thing to chew on. It was a shiny red ball that was on the stair. She did not know who it belonged to, but she wanted to try it. She picked it up and put it in her mouth. She bit down hard and felt a pop. The ball was not a ball, it was a balloon! The balloon made a loud noise and scared Lily. She spit out the pieces and started to cry. She was frightened and wanted her mom and dad. She ran down the stair and looked for them. She saw them in the living room and ran to them. "Mama, Papa, help me!" she sobbed. "I chewed on a balloon and it popped!" Her mom and dad hugged her and comforted her. They told her it was okay and that she was not hurt. They also told her that the balloon was not hers and that she should not chew on things that are not food. "I'm sorry, Mama, Papa," Lily said. "I won't chew on things anymore. I only want to chew on food." Her mom and dad smiled and kissed her. They gave her a cookie and a glass of milk. Lily chewed on the cookie and drank the milk. She felt better and learned her lesson. She decided to chew on food and not on things.

Lily liked to draw with her crayons. She had many colors and made pictures of flowers, animals and people. She was very proud of her drawings and showed them to her mom and dad. One day, her uncle came to visit. He was not nice. He was rude. He did not say hello to Lily or play with her. He only talked to mom and dad about boring things. Lily wanted to show him her drawings, but he did not look at them. He said they were messy and silly. Lily was sad and angry. She decided to teach her uncle a lesson. She took her red crayon and sneaked behind him. She marked his shirt with a big X. Then she ran away and hid under the table. "Hey, what is this?" her uncle shouted. He saw the red mark on his shirt and was very mad. He looked around and saw Lily under the table. He pointed at her and said, "You did this, you naughty girl! How dare you ruin my shirt!" Lily did not say sorry. She stuck out her tongue and said, "You are rude, uncle! You did not like my drawings, so I did not like your shirt!" Mom and dad heard the noise and came to see what happened. They saw the red mark on uncle's shirt and Lily under the table. They were not happy. They scolded Lily for being rude and told her to apologize to her uncle. They also scolded uncle for being rude and told him to be nice to Lily. They made them hug and say sorry to each other. Lily and uncle hugged and said sorry, but they did not mean it. They still did not like each other. Lily hoped uncle would go away soon. Uncle hoped Lily would not mark his shirt again.
