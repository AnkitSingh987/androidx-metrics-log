Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Baby and the Park

Once upon a time, there was a baby named Tim. He was a patient baby who always waited for his turn. Tim had a big sister named Sue. They liked to play together. One sunny day, they went to the park. Tim and Sue played on the swings and the slide. They got very thirsty from playing. Sue found a water fountain to get a drink. Sue drank first, and then it was Tim's turn. But Tim was too small to reach the water. Sue helped Tim by lifting him up. Tim drank and felt better. The moral of the story is to always help others when they need it, just like Sue helped Tim. Being patient and kind to one another makes everyone happy.

Once upon a time, there was a big bookcase in a small room. It was a very sad bookcase. It felt miserable because it had no books to hold. One day, a happy girl named Lily came into the room. She saw the sad bookcase and said, "Why are you so sad, bookcase?" The bookcase replied, "I am sad because I have no books to hold." Lily wanted to help the bookcase. She thought and thought about what she could do. Then, she had an idea! She could mail letters to her friends and ask them to send their extra books. Soon, the mail came and it had many books for the bookcase. The bookcase was so happy! It was no longer sad and miserable. It was now a very happy bookcase, filled with books. Lily and the bookcase became best friends. They spent their days reading and playing together, and they lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a big, red ball. The ball loved to rush and play with its friends. One day, the ball met a small, green apple. The apple was sour, but it wanted to play too. So, the ball and the apple played together all day. The next day, the ball said, "I have to rush to play with my friends. Do you want to come?" The apple said, "I want to play, but I must stay here. I can't move like you." So, the ball said, "Okay, I will stay and play with you." Just then, a big wind came and blew the apple up into the air. The apple could move now! The ball and the apple were so happy. They rushed and played together all day long. And they became the best of friends.

One day, a dog named Max went for a jog. The sun was up, and the sky was blue. Max liked to jog in the park. He saw his friends and they played together. Then, it started to rain. Max got wet from the rain. He looked for a place to hide. He found a big tree and went under it. Max was happy to be under the tree. Max waited for the rain to stop. His friends came to find him under the tree. They all played together again. The sun came back out, and they had a fun day.

Once upon a time, there was a messy room. In the room, toys were everywhere. A little boy named Tim lived there. Tim did not like to clean up. One day, his mom said, "Tim, you have to clean this room. It is too messy!" That night, Tim had a nightmare. In his dream, the toys came to life. They started to fight each other. The cars fought the dolls, and the teddy bears fought the blocks. It was very loud and scary. Tim woke up and knew what he had to do. He started to clean up his room. He put the cars away, the dolls in their place, and the teddy bears on the bed. When he was done, he felt very good. His room was clean, and he knew he would not have any more nightmares.

Once upon a time, there was a nice little girl named Sue. She loved to match her socks with her dress every day. One day, she went for a walk in the woods with her mom. They saw a big log on the ground. Sue wanted to sit on the log and take a break. As they sat on the log, Sue and her mom heard a small voice. "Hello, can you help me?" said the voice. Sue looked around and saw a tiny bug stuck under a small rock. "Oh no!" said Sue, "Let's help the bug." Sue and her mom moved the rock and the bug was free. The bug thanked them and said, "I am a magic bug! I will give you a wish for helping me." Sue wished for her socks to always match her dress. The magic bug made Sue's wish come true. From that day on, Sue's socks always matched her dress, and she was very happy.
