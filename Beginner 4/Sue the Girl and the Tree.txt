Category: Beginner
Title: Sue the Girl and the Tree

Once upon a time, there was a girl named Sue. Sue had a sack and a journal. She loved to write in her journal every day. Sue's sack was filthy, but she did not care. She took her sack and journal everywhere she went. One day, Sue went to the park. She sat down under a big tree and began to write in her journal. Suddenly, a little bird flew down and took Sue's journal! Sue was sad and did not know what to do. She wanted her journal back. Sue followed the little bird, and it led her to a nest high up in the tree. The bird dropped the journal into the nest. Sue was scared, but she knew she had to climb the tree to get her journal back. She put her filthy sack down and started to climb. When Sue reached the nest, she found not only her journal but also many other journals! The little bird had been taking journals from many kids. Sue took all the journals and climbed back down. She gave the journals back to their owners and they were all happy. From that day on, Sue and the little bird became friends. Sue taught the bird not to take things that did not belong to it. The bird helped Sue keep her sack and journal safe. And they all lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim had a big imagination. He could imagine anything he wanted. One day, Tim was playing in the kitchen. He saw a faucet. He wanted to have some fun with it. Tim imagined that the faucet was a successful rocket. He turned the faucet on and water came out. The water was like the fire from the rocket. The rocket was going to the moon! Tim was so happy. He imagined that he was on the rocket, flying high in the sky. When Tim's mom saw the water, she laughed. She knew that Tim was using his imagination. She told Tim that he was a successful astronaut. Tim was very proud. He knew that his imagination could take him anywhere he wanted to go.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to step and dance to music. One day, she found a big radio in her room. She was very happy. Sue turned on the radio and started to dance. She stepped and jumped all around the room. But then, she smelled something disgusting. Sue looked around to find where the bad smell was coming from. She saw a dirty sock under her bed. Sue picked up the sock and threw it in the laundry. The disgusting smell was gone. Sue went back to her radio and continued to step and dance to the music. She had a great day.

Tom and Anna like to play in the park. They run, jump, and swing on the swings. One day, they see a big tree with many branches. They want to climb the tree and see the birds and the sky. "Look, Anna, a tree!" Tom says. "Let's go and climb it!" "OK, Tom, but be careful!" Anna says. "The tree is very high and some branches are weak. It can be dangerous." They run to the tree and start to climb. Tom goes first and Anna follows him. They climb higher and higher, until they reach a big branch. They sit on the branch and look around. They see the park, the flowers, the people, and the clouds. "Wow, this is fun!" Tom says. "We are so high! We can see everything!" "Yes, it is fun!" Anna says. "But we should go down soon. It is getting late and Mom will be worried." Tom nods and gets ready to climb down. But then he notices something. He sees a big, black bird on another branch. The bird has a long, sharp beak and yellow eyes. It looks angry and hungry. It sees Tom and Anna and makes a loud noise. "Anna, look!" Tom says. "A bird! A big, bad bird! It wants to hurt us!" Anna looks and sees the bird. She feels scared and screams. The bird flies towards them and tries to peck them. Tom and Anna dodge and duck, but the bird is fast and strong. It hits the branch they are sitting on and makes it shake. "Help! Help!" Tom and Anna shout. "The bird is dangerous! The branch is breaking!" They hear a crack and feel the branch snap. They fall down with the branch and the bird. They scream and close their eyes. They think they are going to hit the ground and get hurt. But then they feel something soft and warm. They open their eyes and see that they are not on the ground. They are on a big, fluffy blanket. They see Mom and Dad standing next to them. They are holding the blanket and smiling. "Are you OK, kids?" Mom asks. "We saw you fall and we caught you with the blanket. You are safe now." "We are OK, Mom," Tom says. "But the bird! The bird was dangerous! It tried to eat us!" Dad points to the bird. It is lying on the ground, next to the broken branch. It is not moving. It looks sad and hurt. "That was not a bird, kids," Dad says. "That was a kite. A big, black kite. Someone was flying it in the park and it got loose. It got tangled with the branch and fell with you. It did not mean to scare you. It was just a toy." Tom and Anna look at the kite. They see that it is not a bird. It has no feathers, no eyes, no beak. It has strings, paper, and sticks. It is just a kite. "Oh," Tom says. "It was just a kite." "Oh," Anna says. "It was not dangerous." They feel silly and relieved. They hug Mom and Dad and thank them for saving them. They say sorry for climbing the tree and making them worry. They promise to be more careful next time. Mom and Dad hug them back and say they love them. They say they are glad they are OK. They say they can play in the park again, but not on the tree. They say they can fly a kite instead, but not a big, black one. Tom and Anna agree. They say they like kites, but not big, black ones. They say they like trees, but not high, weak ones. They say they like birds, but not angry, hungry ones. They laugh and smile. They are happy and safe. They go home with Mom and Dad. They have learned a lesson. They have had an adventure. They have told a story.
