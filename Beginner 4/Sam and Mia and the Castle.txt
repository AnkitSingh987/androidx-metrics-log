Category: Beginner
Title: Sam and Mia and the Castle

Sam and Mia liked to play in the sand by the sea. They made castles and bridges and tunnels with their buckets and shovels. Sometimes they found shells and stones and seaweed to decorate their sand creations. One day, they saw something shiny and pink in the sand. It was a shrimp. It had tiny eyes and legs and a long tail. Sam wanted to pick it up and look at it. He reached out his hand. "No, Sam, don't touch it!" Mia said. "It might bite you or pinch you. It is not a toy. It is a living thing. We should leave it alone." But Sam did not listen. He insisted that he wanted to see the shrimp. He grabbed it and lifted it up. The shrimp wriggled and squirmed in his hand. It snapped its tail and hit Sam's finger. "Ow!" Sam cried. He dropped the shrimp and shook his hand. It hurt a lot. He had a red mark on his finger. Mia ran to him and hugged him. She was sorry that he was hurt. She said, "I told you not to touch the shrimp, Sam. It was scared of you. It was trying to protect itself. You should be more careful and gentle with animals." Sam nodded. He was sorry too. He said, "You were right, Mia. I was wrong. I should have listened to you. I will not touch shrimps again. I will only look at them from far away." They went back to their sand play. They made a wide moat around their castle. They filled it with water and shells. They saw some shrimps swimming in the water. They watched them with curiosity and respect. They did not bother them. They learned their lesson.

One day, a little bird was in a birdcage. The birdcage was high up in a tree. The bird wanted to fly and spread its wings. It was sad in the birdcage. A big bear came to the tree. The bear saw the bird in the birdcage. The bear said, "Little bird, why are you sad?" The bird said, "I want to fly and spread my wings, but I am in this birdcage." The big bear wanted to help the bird. The bear climbed the tree and opened the birdcage. The bird said, "Thank you, bear!" The bird was happy. It spread its wings and flew high in the sky.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved her bookshelf. On the bookshelf, she had many books. She liked to read them every day. One day, Sue wanted to pour water into a cup. But she was not careful. The water spilled all over the floor. Sue was sad. She wanted to be successful in pouring the water. Sue's mom saw her and helped her. She showed Sue how to pour the water slowly. Sue tried again and this time she was successful! The water went into the cup and not on the floor. Sue was very happy. She went back to her bookshelf and read a book about pouring water. Now, Sue knew how to pour water and she was happy.

Rosy had a very creative idea. She wanted to go on a picnic, so she decided to make a stove! She got an old box and put rocks inside it before finding a long stick. She placed the stick on top of the box and lit a fire. Rosy was so happy when she saw the wood catch fire and release lots of heat. She started cooking her food and enjoyed her delicious picnic. She ate all her food and when she was done she released the fire from the stove. She watched the smoke rise up into the sky, feeling proud of her creative stove. Rosy thanked the stove for its help. She gave it a big hug and promised to make it again soon.

One day, a little girl named Lily went for a walk. She wore her favorite red shoes. She saw a big tree and wanted to see what was behind it. Lily walked around the tree and found a small, cold house. She felt very curious and wanted to enter the house. Lily opened the door and entered the cold house. Inside, she saw a small table with a big shoe on it. She thought it was a funny place for a shoe. Lily picked up the shoe and it was very cold. She decided to put the shoe back on the table. As she was leaving the cold house, a small mouse came out from a hole. The mouse looked at Lily and said, "Thank you for not taking my cold shoe. It is my favorite thing to sleep in." Lily smiled and felt happy that she did not take the mouse's shoe. She waved goodbye to the mouse and went back home.
