Category: Beginner
Title: Tom the Fireman and the Car

Once upon a time, there was a fireman named Tom. Tom was a nice man, but sometimes he was a bit spoiled. He liked to have the best toys and always wanted more. One day, Tom saw a big, red fire truck that he wanted to have. Tom knew he needed to save his money to get the big, red fire truck. But, he saw a shiny toy car and he wanted it too. So, he spent his money on the toy car instead of saving it for the fire truck. This was a bad choice. Soon, there was a big fire in the town. Tom needed the big, red fire truck to help put out the fire. But, because he had spent his money on the shiny toy car, he didn't have the fire truck. The town had to suffer because of Tom's bad choice. The fire burned many houses, and people were very sad. Tom learned that being spoiled can lead to bad things happening.

Ben and Lily went to the grocery with Mom. They liked to help Mom put things in the cart. Ben saw a big bag of cookies. He wanted some cookies. He asked Mom, "Can we have cookies, please?" Mom looked at the bag. She said, "No, Ben. We have enough cookies at home. We need to buy healthy food, like fruits and vegetables." Ben was sad. He did not like fruits and vegetables. He liked cookies. He tried to whistle, but he could not. He only made a funny sound. Lily laughed. She could whistle. She whistled a happy tune. Mom smiled. She said, "Lily, you are very good at whistling. Can you teach Ben how to whistle?" Lily nodded. She said, "Sure, Mom. I can teach Ben. It is easy. You just make a small hole with your lips and blow air." She showed Ben how to whistle. Ben tried to copy her. He made a small hole with his lips and blew air. But he blew too hard. He spat on the cookies. The cookies got wet and sticky. Mom gasped. She said, "Oh, Ben. You are so clumsy. You ruined the cookies. Now we have to pay for them." Ben felt bad. He said, "I'm sorry, Mom. I did not mean to spit on the cookies. I just wanted to whistle like Lily." Mom sighed. She said, "It's okay, Ben. I know you did not mean it. But you have to be more careful. Whistling is not easy. It takes practice. Maybe you can try again later, when we are not near the food." Ben nodded. He said, "Okay, Mom. I will try again later. Maybe I can whistle like Lily someday." Mom hugged Ben. She said, "I'm sure you can, Ben. You are a smart boy. You can learn anything. But for now, let's finish our shopping. And no more spitting, okay?" Ben and Lily said, "Okay, Mom." They helped Mom put the wet cookies in the cart. They also helped Mom pick some fruits and vegetables. They did not ask for more cookies. They were good children.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play outside with his best friend, a dog named Max. They would run and rush through the grass, laughing and barking. One day, Tim and Max found a spicy red pepper on the ground. Tim said to Max, "I dare you to eat it!" Max barked and took a tiny bite. He felt a hot feeling in his mouth and his lung felt funny. Max drank lots of water to make the spicy go away. Tim hugged Max and said, "I'm sorry, Max. I didn't know it would be that spicy." Max licked Tim's face, and they went back to playing. They never ate spicy peppers again, but they still loved to rush and play together in the sun.

Once there was a strong boy named Tony. One day, Tony and his mom went to the park to have a picnic. Tony brought a sandwich with him. At the park, Tony saw a big tree and he ran to it. He wanted to climb it! But it was too high. He was sad. Suddenly, Tony felt the tree shake. He was surprised! He looked up at the tree and saw a big, green parrot. The parrot flew down and landed on Tony's shoulder. Then it said, "Hi, Tony! I will help you climb this tree!" Tony was so happy he began to laugh. He and the parrot had a lot of fun together. Finally, with the parrot's help, Tony reached the top of the tree. Tony planted his arms to the sky and shouted, "I'm strong!" The parrot smiled at Tony and flew away. Tony smiled back, waved goodbye, and enjoyed his picnic sandwich.
