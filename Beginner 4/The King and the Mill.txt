Category: Beginner
Title: The King and the Mill

Once upon a time there was a king who was very powerful. He had a huge mill that stood at the edge of the kingdom. He wanted to make sure his mill kept running so he ordered the people of the kingdom to help. One day, the king was walking near the mill when he saw a little girl. He asked her if she wanted to help with the mill. The little girl smiled and said "Yes, I'd love to help!" The king showed her how to use the mill, and soon the little girl was very good at it. She worked very hard and made the mill run faster than ever before. The king was so pleased that he gave the little girl a big reward. He said to her, "Thank you for your hard work, little girl. I'm sure this mill will be huge one day!" The girl smiled and said "Thank you, King!" with a big happy smile.

Once upon a time, there was a naughty boy. He was very selfish and only wanted to do things his way. One day, he saw a muscle in the park and wanted to remove it from its place. For a naughty little boy, it seemed like a fun thing to do. He wiggled and pulled, but it was too hard for him. He asked his friends for help, but they said it was wrong. They said it was too selfish to remove things that don't belong to you. The naughty boy felt sad and started to cry. But as he was crying, he felt something pushing out of his arms and legs. The muscle in his arm had grown bigger. He realised that to remove the big muscle, he had to use his own strength. He pushed and pulled until the big muscle came off its spot. The naughty boy felt proud and happy that he had used his own muscle. He had learnt an important lesson - it's not selfish to use your own strength to do a good deed.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play in their room. They have many toys and books. They also have a lamp. The lamp is high on a table. The lamp makes light when it is dark. One night, Anna and Ben are not sleepy. They want to play more. They turn on the lamp and look at their books. They see pictures of animals and colors. They say the names of the things they see. "Look, Ben, this is a dog. It is brown and white. It can bark and run," Anna says. "I see, Anna, this is a cat. It is black and yellow. It can meow and jump," Ben says. They are happy and have fun. But they do not hear their mom. She comes to their room and sees the lamp. She is not happy. She says to them: "Anna and Ben, it is late. You should be in bed. Turn off the lamp and go to sleep. You can play more tomorrow." Anna and Ben are sad. They do not want to stop playing. But they know their mom is right. They say sorry to their mom. They turn off the lamp and go to bed. They hug their mom and say good night. They close their eyes and dream of their books.

Once upon a time there were two best friends, Daisy and Bill. Daisy was deaf, so she couldn't always understand what Bill was saying. But she understood the love in his eyes, and he soon learned different ways to talk to her without words. Whenever Daisy felt small, Bill put his arms around her like a stitch and let her know she wasn't alone. One day Bill asked Daisy why she was so quiet. Daisy couldn't answer with words, so she held up her hands and showed him her stitches. They were different from his stitches and they made her look different. Daisy was so scared he would no longer love her when he saw them. But Bill didn't flinch. "That's okay," he said, putting his arms around her. "I still love you, Daisy." The moral of the story is that true love is deaf to differences. No matter how different we are from one another, true friendship and love means being there for one another unconditionally. We can find joy in each other's differences and love each other in spite of them.
