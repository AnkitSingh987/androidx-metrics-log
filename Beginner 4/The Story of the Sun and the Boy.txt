Title: The Story of the Sun and the Boy

Once upon a time, there was a little boy who valued his friends. He decided to throw a party in his backyard for them. It was a good day for the party. The sun was shining and the birds were singing. To cook for everyone, the little boy prepared a big BBQ. He spread the charcoal in the grill and lit the fire. He put the food on the grill and waited for it to get hot. The boy was excited to smell the food cooking. He could tell it was going to be really good. When the food was fully cooked, the boy and his friends enjoyed a tasty barbeque meal. They had fun eating together, and they valued the time they spent together. Even though it was a simple party, the little boy knew that it was a good time with his friends.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to ride his red bike. One sunny day, Tim saw a big shadow on the ground. He was scared and ran to his mom. "Mom, there is a big shadow outside!" Tim said. Mom smiled and said, "Don't worry, Tim. It's just a cloud passing by the sun. It will go away soon." Tim felt better and went back outside to ride his bike. He rode and rode until he felt very sleepy. He went back inside and told his mom, "I am so sleepy, Mom." Mom hugged him and said, "It's time for a nap, Tim. You can ride your bike again later." Tim went to bed, and as he fell asleep, he dreamed of riding his bike with the big shadow as his friend.

The fog was rolling in from the sea. It was thick and dangerous. Little Sandy was wrapped up warmly against the cold, standing at the edge of the beach and looking out over the grey mass, bravely. "Wow, it's really foggy out there," he said. He could just make out the boats out at sea, their sails barely visible in the darkness. His dad joined him, placing a strong arm around his shoulders. "It looks dangerous out there," he said, pointing to the fog, "That's why you should always stay close to the shoreline and never venture too far out at sea. This fog can be deceiving." Little Sandy nodded and sighed and the two of them went back home, where his Dad storied him a story and tucked him in bed. The next day, the fog was even thicker, and the air seemed darker. Little Sandy's dad was pacing up and down the beach, wringing his hands with worry. "Oh Sandy, I was afraid of this!" he said, "Some of the fishermen went too far out yesterday and the fog has been so thick, they can't find their way back!" Little Sandy felt his heart beat faster. Those were the same words his father had spoken the night before. The same warning. He had been right. The fog was dangerous indeed.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim had a toy called a magnet. The magnet could pick up things made of metal. Tim loved to play with his magnet all day. One day, Tim found a tight jar that he could not open. He tried and tried, but it was too tight. Then, Tim had an idea. He thought his magnet might help him open the tight jar. Tim put the magnet near the jar and it stuck to the metal lid. He pulled and pulled, and the lid came off! Tim was so happy. He said "thank you" to his magnet and gave it a hug. Then he played with his magnet and the jar, and he would repeat this fun game every day.

Sam and Ben are brothers. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big truck with ice cream. They run to the truck and ask for ice cream. "Can we have ice cream, please?" Sam says. "Me too, me too!" Ben says. The man in the truck smiles and gives them each a cone with ice cream. Sam gets chocolate and Ben gets vanilla. They say thank you and walk away. But then, a big dog comes out of nowhere. It barks and jumps at Sam and Ben. They get scared and drop their cones. The dog eats their ice cream and runs away. "Hey, that's our ice cream!" Sam cries. "Bad dog, bad dog!" Ben shouts. They feel sad and angry. They look at their empty cones and their sticky hands. They have no ice cream left. "Come on, let's go home," Sam says. They rush to their mom, who is waiting for them. She sees their faces and their cones. "What happened, boys?" she asks. "A dog took our ice cream, mom," Ben says. "Poor you, that's not fair," she says. She hugs them and wipes their hands. She says she will buy them new ice cream later. They feel better and smile. They love their mom. She is the best.
