Category: Beginner
Title: Lily the Girl and the Toys

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She loved to play outside in her yard. One day, she wanted to create something special with her toys. Lily found a perfect spot under a big tree. She began to dig in the dirt with her toy shovel. As she dug, she found a small pebble. It was smooth and shiny, and Lily thought it was very pretty. Lily decided to make a home for the pebble with her toys. She put the pebble on a soft bed of leaves and surrounded it with her toy animals. They all played together until the sun went down. Lily was happy she had created a new friend with the perfect pebble.

Once upon a time, a boy named Tim had a paint box. He loved to paint pretty pictures. One day, he wanted to paint outside. He went to the yard with his paint box. Tim saw a big car. The car was fragile. It had a hole in the side. Tim thought it looked sad. He wanted to paint the car to make it happy. So, he started to paint the car with many colors. While Tim was painting, he saw a can near the car. The can had the word "gas" on it. Tim did not know what "gas" meant. He thought it was paint. He opened the can and poured it on the car. The car started to make a loud noise. Then, the car broke into many pieces. Tim was sad. He did not know that "gas" was not paint.

Once there was a boy named Tim. Tim loved to ride his bike. One day, he found a helmet. It was a big, strong helmet. Tim was very happy. Tim put on the helmet and went to ride his bike. He saw his friend, Sam. Sam was eating a bitter apple. Sam did not like the bitter taste. He wanted to change the apple for a sweet one. Tim saw Sam's sad face and wanted to help. He told Sam, "I will change your bitter apple for a sweet one." Sam was very happy. They both enjoyed their sweet apples together. The moral of the story is to help your friends when they need it.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there lived a parrot. The parrot was very old, or what we can call ancient. The parrot had many friends who liked to visit him. They would play and talk all day long. One day, the parrot's friends came to play, but they were very loud. They laughed and shouted so much that it was hard to hear anything else. The parrot was tired and wanted some quiet time. So, the parrot asked his friends to please be quiet. He said, "Please shut your mouths and let's play a quiet game." They all agreed and played a quiet game. The parrot was happy, and they all had a good time.

Ben and Lily were twins who liked to play with their toys. But sometimes they did not like to share. One day, they both wanted to play with the same red car. Ben grabbed the car and said, "This is mine!" Lily tried to take it and said, "No, this is mine!" They started to pull and push each other, making a lot of noise. Their mom heard them and came to see what was wrong. She saw them fighting over the car and said, "Stop it, both of you! You need to learn to share and be generous. Why don't you take turns with the car, or play with something else?" But Ben and Lily refused to listen to their mom. They said, "No, we want the car!" They kept on arguing and crying, making more noise. Their mom was sad and angry. She said, "If you can't play nicely, then you can't play at all. Give me the car and go to your rooms. Maybe then you will find some peace and quiet." She took the car and put it on a high shelf. Then she sent Ben and Lily to their rooms. They were not happy. They felt sorry for themselves and for making their mom upset. They wished they had been more generous and shared the car. They realized that playing together was more fun than fighting. They decided to say sorry to their mom and to each other. They went to their mom and hugged her. They said, "We are sorry, mom. We were bad. We want to share and be generous. Can we have the car back?" Their mom smiled and said, "I'm glad you are sorry. You can have the car back, but only if you promise to play nicely and take turns. Can you do that?" Ben and Lily nodded and said, "Yes, mom. We can do that." Their mom gave them the car and said, "That's good. Now go and play and have fun. And remember, sharing is caring." Ben and Lily thanked their mom and ran to play with the car. They were happy and peaceful. They learned their lesson.
