Category: Beginner
Title: The Mop and the Toys

Once upon a time, there was a mop. The mop was very sad. It wanted to be like the toys in the playroom. The mop was envious of the toys because they were always played with by the kids. One day, the mop asked a toy car, "Why do the kids play with you and not me?" The toy car said, "Because I am fun and colorful. You are just for cleaning." The mop thought hard. It wanted to end being sad. It wanted to be a fun toy too. So, the mop found some colorful paint and painted itself. It looked like a rainbow. The next day, the kids saw the rainbow mop. They laughed and clapped. They played with the mop all day. The mop was not envious anymore. It was happy to be a fun toy, and the kids loved it. And that's how the mop found its way into the playroom.

Once upon a time, there was a big, big dog. This dog was so big, he was enormous! His name was Max. Max lived in a small house with a nice lady. The nice lady loved Max a lot. She would always pet him and give him yummy food. One day, Max saw the nice lady talking to a man. The man was a judge. The judge said, "There will be a fun dog show tomorrow. The best dog will get a big prize!" Max wanted to win the prize for the nice lady. But Max was very dirty. He knew he had to be clean to win. That night, the nice lady gave Max a bath. She used soap and water to make him clean. Max loved the bath. It made him feel good. The next day, Max went to the dog show. He did his best tricks for the judge. The judge smiled and said, "Max, you are the winner! You are the best dog!" Max was so happy! He won the big prize for the nice lady. They went home and celebrated. Max knew that being clean helped him win. And he was very proud to be the best dog.

One day, a little girl named Lily went to the park with her mom. She saw a lovely, big tree with a swing. Lily ran to the swing and asked her mom, "Can I play on the swing, please?" Her mom smiled and said, "Yes, you can, Lily." Lily sat on the swing and felt the texture of the rope. It was a little rough, but she liked it. She started to swing back and forth, higher and higher. She laughed and said, "Mom, look at me! I'm flying like a bird!" Her mom watched Lily and smiled. She said, "You are doing great, Lily! Keep swinging and have fun!" Lily felt so happy and free on the swing. She had a lovely day at the park with her mom.

Lily loved to write in her journal. She had a special pen and a book with many pages. She wrote about her day, her friends, her toys and her dreams. She also drew pictures of what she saw and felt. One day, she found an ancient journal in the attic. It was very old and dusty. It had a leather cover and a lock. Lily was curious. She wanted to see what was inside. She looked for a key, but she could not find one. She asked her mom if she could open the ancient journal. Her mom said yes, but she had to be careful. She said the journal belonged to her great-grandma, who was a very brave and kind lady. She said the journal included stories of her great-grandma's adventures and secrets. Lily was excited. She wanted to read the stories and learn more about her great-grandma. She asked her mom to help her open the lock. Her mom found a small key in a box and gave it to Lily. Lily put the key in the lock and turned it. The lock clicked and the journal opened. Lily and her mom sat on the floor and looked at the pages. They saw words and pictures that were faded and hard to read. They saw maps and stamps and flowers and feathers. They saw names and dates and places. They saw a lot of love and wonder. Lily felt a connection with her great-grandma. She felt like she was part of her story. She asked her mom if she could write in the ancient journal too. Her mom smiled and said yes, but she had to leave some space for the next generation. She said the journal was a family treasure that they could share and pass on. Lily was happy. She took her pen and wrote her name and the date on a blank page. She wrote that she found the journal and that she loved her great-grandma. She drew a heart and a star. She closed the journal and hugged it. She thanked her mom and said she would keep the journal safe and write in it often. She said she hoped her great-grandma would be proud of her. Her mom hugged her and said she was sure she would.
