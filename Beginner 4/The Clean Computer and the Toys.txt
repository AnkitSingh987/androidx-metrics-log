Category: Beginner
Title: The Clean Computer and the Toys

Once upon a time, there was a clean computer. It was very good at counting numbers. The computer lived in a small room with a little boy named Tim. One day, Tim wanted to know the number of toys he had. He asked the clean computer to help him. They looked at all the toys and counted them together. The computer said, "You have ten toys, Tim!" Tim was happy and thanked the clean computer. They played with the toys and had lots of fun. The clean computer was always there to help Tim learn and play. And they lived happily ever after.

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play outside. One day, they see a big hill near their house. They want to go up the hill and see what is on the other side. They ask their mom if they can go. Mom says yes, but they have to be careful and come back soon. She gives them a bottle of water and a snack. She says they are helpful children and she loves them. Anna and Ben take the water and the snack and say thank you. They start to climb the hill. It is hard and steep, but they are strong and brave. They help each other when they slip or get tired. They see birds and flowers and rocks on the way. After a long time, they reach the top of the hill. They are happy and proud. They look around and see a beautiful valley on the other side. The valley is green and flat and has a river and a bridge and some houses. They think it looks like a fairy tale. "Wow, look at that!" Anna says. "It's so pretty!" Ben says. "Let's go down and see it!" Anna says. "OK, but we have to be quick. Mom will be worried if we are late." Ben says. They agree and start to go down the hill. They are careful and hold hands. They want to see the valley and maybe make some new friends. They are excited and curious. They are happy children.

Lily and Ben were playing with their toys on the rug in the living room. They liked to pretend they were explorers and find treasures in the ancient books and maps on the shelves. They also liked to number the toys and see who had more. "Look, I have five cars and you have four dolls," Ben said. "I have more toys than you." "No, you don't," Lily said. "Dolls are bigger than cars, so they count as two. I have eight toys and you have five." "That's not fair," Ben said. "You can't make up rules like that. Cars are faster and cooler than dolls, so they count as three. I have fifteen toys and you have four." Lily and Ben started to argue and shout. They did not notice that their mom was watching them from the kitchen. She smiled and shook her head. She knew they loved each other, but sometimes they needed a reminder. "Hey, kids, why don't you come here for a minute?" she called. "I have something to show you." Lily and Ben stopped fighting and ran to the kitchen. They saw a big cake on the table, with candles and frosting. It was their dad's birthday, and mom had baked a cake for him. "Wow, that looks yummy," Lily said. "Can we have some?" Ben asked. "Of course, but first, let's number the candles," mom said. "How many candles are there?" Lily and Ben looked at the cake and counted together. "One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten," they said. "Very good," mom said. "Now, do you know what the candles mean?" Lily and Ben thought for a moment. "They mean dad is old," Ben said. "No, they mean dad is ancient," Lily said. Mom laughed and hugged them. "They mean dad is very loved," she said. "And so are you. Now, let's go and surprise him with the cake. And remember, it doesn't matter how many toys you have, as long as you share them and play nicely."

One day, a big frog saw a huge rock. The frog was sad because the rock was in his way. He wanted to go to the other side to play with his friends. The frog thought, "How can I break this huge rock?" He tried to push it, but it was too big. Then, a tiny ant came by. The ant said, "I can help you break the huge rock!" The frog laughed, "How can you help me? You are so small!" The ant said, "Just watch me!" The ant took a deep breath, filling his tiny lung, and blew on the rock. The huge rock broke into many small pieces! The frog was very surprised. He said, "Wow, thank you, tiny ant!" The frog and the ant became friends. They played together and had lots of fun. The frog learned that even the smallest friends can do big things.
