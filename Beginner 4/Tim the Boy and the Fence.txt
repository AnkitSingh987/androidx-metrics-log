Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Fence

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. He loved to play in his yard near the fence. One day, an elderly man came to visit Tim. The man was Tim's grandpa. Tim was happy to see him. "Hi, Tim!" said Grandpa. "Let's play a game with this ball." Tim and Grandpa started to play with the ball. They threw it back and forth, laughing and having fun. Suddenly, the ball went over the fence. Tim was sad, but Grandpa said, "Don't worry, Tim. I'll get the ball for you." Grandpa went near the fence and did a magic trick. He made the ball vanish from the other side of the fence and reappear in his hand. "Wow, Grandpa! How did you do that?" Tim asked with big eyes. Grandpa just winked and smiled. Tim and Grandpa continued to play and had a great day together. From that day on, Tim knew his grandpa was magic.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Emma. Emma loved to collect things. She would pick up rocks, leaves, and even bugs. Emma had a big, soft armchair in her room where she would sit and look at her collection. One day, Emma found a harmless bug in the garden. It was small and had pretty colors. She thought it would be a great addition to her collection. So, she picked it up and took it to her room. She put the bug on her armchair and went to get her magnifying glass to look at it better. While Emma was gone, the bug started to eat her armchair. It ate and ate until there was nothing left but a big hole. When Emma came back, she was very sad to see her armchair ruined. From that day on, she learned to be more careful with the things she collected.

Anna and Ben are playing in the park. They like to swing, slide and run. They are having fun. But it is a hot day. The sun is very bright and makes them sweat. They need to drink water. "Anna, Ben, come here!" Mom calls. "You need to drink some water. It is good for you." Anna and Ben run to Mom. She has a bottle of water for each of them. She gives them the bottles. "Thank you, Mom!" Anna says. She opens her bottle and drinks some water. It is cold and fresh. She feels better. But Ben does not want to drink water. He thinks water is boring. He wants juice or soda. He refuses to open his bottle. "No, thank you, Mom!" Ben says. "I don't want water. Water is boring. I want juice or soda. They are yummy and fun." Mom shakes her head. She is not happy. She knows juice and soda have too much sugar. They are not good for Ben. "Ben, you need to drink water. Water is not boring. Water is healthy and important. It helps you stay cool and strong. Juice and soda have too much sugar. They can make you sick and tired. Please drink some water." But Ben still refuses to drink water. He puts his bottle down and runs away. He wants to play more. Mom sighs. She hopes Ben will change his mind. She does not want him to get thirsty or sick. She loves him very much.

Lily and Max were playing in their room. They had many toys and books, but they wanted to try something new. They saw a big box in the corner. It was full of old clothes that Mom did not want anymore. "Let's examine the clothes," Lily said. "Maybe we can find something fun to wear." "OK," Max agreed. "But we have to be careful. Mom said some of the clothes are ugly and dirty." They opened the box and took out some clothes. There were shirts, pants, skirts, dresses, hats, and scarves. Some of the clothes were too big, too small, or too torn. Some had funny colors or patterns. Lily found a pink dress with flowers. She put it on over her clothes. "Look, Max, I'm a princess!" she said. Max found a blue shirt with stripes. He put it on over his clothes. "Look, Lily, I'm a pirate!" he said. They laughed and pretended to be a princess and a pirate. They used a scarf as a cape and a hat as a crown. They had a lot of fun. But then they heard Mom coming upstairs. She opened the door and saw them in the old clothes. "What are you doing?" she asked. "Those clothes are ugly and dirty. You can't wear them. They are for the trash." Lily and Max felt sad. They liked the clothes. They thought they were fun and pretty. "But Mom, we were just playing," Lily said. "We were examining the clothes," Max said. Mom smiled. She saw that they were using their imagination. She was not angry. "I'm sorry, kids," she said. "I didn't mean to hurt your feelings. You can keep some of the clothes if you want. But you have to wash them first. And you have to put them away when you are done playing. OK?" "OK," Lily and Max said. They hugged Mom and thanked her. They picked some of the clothes they liked and put the rest back in the box. They went to the bathroom and washed the clothes. Then they put them in a drawer in their room. They decided to play with the clothes again tomorrow. They had many more stories to tell.
