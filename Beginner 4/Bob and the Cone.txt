Category: Beginner
Title: Bob and the Cone

Little Bob pushed the cone across the lawn. He was having so much fun! He huffed and puffed as the cone went faster and faster. Suddenly, he heard a grumpy voice say, "Hey! Stop that!" Little Bob looked up and saw the grumpy old man from the house across the street. He was standing with his arms crossed. He scowled at Little Bob and yelled, "Stop pushing that cone!" Little Bob didn't want to but he was scared. He stopped pushing the cone and stepped back. The grumpy old man still looked mad. He shouted, "Get off my lawn or I'll call the police!" Little Bob ran away as fast as he could. He looked back and saw the grumpy old man still scowling at him. He was glad to be far away from that grumpy old man. Little Bob pushed the cone away from the house across the street and had lots of fun pushing it in his own backyard.

Once upon a time, there was a little bird named Tim. Tim was very impatient. He wanted to learn how to fly, but he could not do it yet. Tim's parent told him, "You need to wait and practice more." One sunny day, Tim saw a big tree. He thought, "I want to gain the top of the tree!" He tried to fly, but he was still not good at it. Tim's friend, a kind squirrel, saw him and said, "Hey Tim, don't be impatient. Let's try together!" Tim and the squirrel practiced every day. They climbed and jumped, and Tim learned how to use his wings. After a while, Tim could fly! He was so happy. He flew to the top of the tree with his friend the squirrel. Tim's parent was very proud of him. They said, "You did it, Tim! You gained the top of the tree, and you learned to be patient." And the moral of the story is: If you are patient and practice, you can learn new things and gain your goals.

Once upon a time, there was a player named Tim. Tim loved to play with his ball. One day, while playing, he saw an enormous tree. Tim thought it would be fun to throw his ball up and try to catch it. Tim threw his ball high into the air. But, oh no! The ball got stuck in the tree. Tim was sad. He wanted his ball back. He tried to grab it, but it was too high. Tim knew he needed help. Tim asked his big sister, Sue, to help him. Sue was tall and strong. She tried to grab the ball, but it was still too high. Then, Sue had an idea. She found a long stick. Sue used the stick to push the ball out of the tree. Tim was so happy to have his ball back. They both played together and had lots of fun.

One day, a little boy named Tim saw a big motorcycle. It was very heavy. He wanted to lift it, but he was too small. Tim was persistent. He asked his dad, "Can I lift the motorcycle?" His dad said, "Not now, Tim. You are too small. But if you keep trying, one day you will be big and strong enough to lift it." Tim was sad, but he did not give up. Every day, he would try to lift things that were a little bit heavy. He lifted his toy box, his dog, and even his baby sister. He was getting stronger. One day, when Tim was older, he saw the motorcycle again. He remembered what his dad had told him. He walked up to the motorcycle and tried to lift it. With all his strength, he lifted the motorcycle off the ground. Tim was so happy, and his dad was proud of him. "You did it, Tim," his dad said. "You were persistent, and now you are strong enough to lift the motorcycle." Tim smiled and knew that he could do anything if he kept trying.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there lived a chubby cat named Fluffy. Fluffy loved to play with her toy mouse and sleep in the sun. She was a very happy cat, and everyone loved her. One day, a sneaky fox came into the house. The fox wanted to steal Fluffy's toy mouse. He waited for the silence when everyone was asleep. In the quiet night, the fox took the toy mouse and ran away. When Fluffy woke up, she saw that her toy mouse was gone. She was very sad. But her friends, the little boy and girl, saw the fox running away. They chased the fox and got the toy mouse back. Fluffy was so happy to have her toy mouse again, and they all played together. The house was filled with laughter and joy, and the sneaky fox never came back.
