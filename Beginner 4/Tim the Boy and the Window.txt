Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Window

Once upon a time, in a small town, there lived a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play outside with his friends. One day, while playing, Tim saw dark clouds in the sky. He felt the cold wind blow on his face. The storm was coming. Tim ran to his house and saw his mom. "Mom, a storm is coming!" he said. "Don't worry, Tim. We'll be safe inside our house," Mom replied. They closed all the windows and doors to keep the cold wind out. As the storm passed, Tim looked out the window. He saw a little girl standing outside, all wet and cold. Tim opened the door and said, "Hi, I'm Tim. Come inside and get warm." The girl smiled and said, "Thank you, Tim. My name is Lily." They became good friends and played together after the storm.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Ann. She had a small box of jewelry. Ann loved her jewelry. She had a necklace, a ring, and a bracelet. She wore them every day. One day, Ann went for a walk with her mom. It was cold outside, so she wore her warm coat. Ann liked to whistle while she walked. She whistled a happy song. While they were walking, Ann's bracelet fell off her wrist. She didn't notice. When they got home, Ann looked for her bracelet. It was gone! She was sad. Ann and her mom went back outside to look for the bracelet. They walked and walked. Ann whistled her happy song again. Suddenly, they saw something shiny on the ground. It was Ann's bracelet! She was so happy. They went home, and Ann put her jewelry away safely.

Lily and Ben were playing in the park with their toy cars. They liked to make noises and pretend they were racing. But then they saw a big machine near the swings. It was loud and dirty and had many arms and teeth. It was scary. "What is that?" Lily asked. "I don't know. Maybe it is a monster," Ben said. They wanted to run away, but the machine saw them and moved towards them. It made a roar and reached out with its arms. "Help! Help!" Lily and Ben cried. They dropped their toy cars and hugged each other. They were very afraid. But then they heard a voice. It was their dad. He was wearing a helmet and a vest. He had a tool in his hand. "Don't worry, kids. It's not a monster. It's a digger. I'm working on the new playground. It's safe. Come here," he said. He waved at them and smiled. He walked to the machine and turned it off. He picked up Lily and Ben and hugged them. "Are you okay?" he asked. "Yes, dad. We're okay. But we were scared," Lily said. "Me too," Ben said. "I'm sorry. I should have told you. The digger is not scary. It's just a machine. It helps me dig holes and move dirt. Do you want to see how it works?" he asked. Lily and Ben nodded. They were curious. "Okay. But first, you have to surrender your toy cars. You can't play with them near the machine. It's dangerous. Do you understand?" he asked. Lily and Ben nodded again. They gave their toy cars to their dad. He put them in his pocket. "Good. Now follow me. I'll show you the digger. It's fun," he said. He took them to the machine and explained how it worked. He let them sit in the cab and touch the buttons. He made the machine move and make noises. Lily and Ben laughed and clapped. They were not scared anymore. They liked the digger. It was cool.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. She had an adorable toy named Mixer. Mixer was a fluffy dog with big eyes and a wagging tail. Sue loved Mixer and took him everywhere she went. One day, Sue and Mixer went to the park to play. At the park, Sue saw a big door. She wanted to see what was behind it. She asked her mommy, "Can we unlock the door?" Her mommy said, "Not now, Sue. Maybe later." Sue felt sad, but she knew she had to listen to her mommy. She played with Mixer and forgot about the door. The next day, Sue and Mixer went back to the park. They saw the same door. This time, Sue's mommy said, "Okay, let's unlock the door." Sue was so happy! They opened the door and found a beautiful garden. Sue and Mixer played in the garden all day. They knew that listening to mommy was a good thing.
