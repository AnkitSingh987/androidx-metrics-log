Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Healthy Boy and the Flowers

Once upon a time, there was a healthy boy named Tim. Tim had a red rose that he loved very much. Every day, he would water the rose and watch it grow. One day, a little bug came to live on the rose. "Hi, I am Zip!" said the bug. "I like your rose. Can I stay here?" "Yes, you can," said Tim. "But you must be nice to my rose." Zip and Tim became good friends. They played and talked every day. Zip would tell Tim stories about other bugs and flowers. Tim would laugh and tell Zip stories about his life. One day, Tim saw that his rose was not looking so good. He asked Zip, "What is wrong with my rose? It looks sad." Zip said, "Don't worry, Tim. I will help you. We just need to give it more water and love." So, Tim and Zip worked together to make the rose healthy again. They watered it, talked to it, and even sang to it. Soon, the rose was happy and healthy, just like before. Tim and Zip were very happy, and they knew that they would be friends forever.

Once upon a time, there was a fierce lion named Leo. He loved to run fast and play with his friends. One day, Leo and his friends decided to have a race to see who was the fastest. They were all very excited. Before the race, Leo's friend, Tina the turtle, said, "I hope I can win, but you are very fast, Leo." Leo replied, "We will see, Tina. Just do your best!" The race began, and all the animals ran as fast as they could. Leo was in the lead, but he saw that Tina was very far behind. So, he stopped and waited for her. When Tina caught up, they ran together to the finish line. In the end, Leo and Tina both won the race. All the animals cheered for them. Leo said, "It was more fun to run with my friend than to win alone." After that day, Leo always returned to help his friends, and they all had fun together.

Once upon a time, there was a farmer named Tom. He had a big red barn and many animals. Tom was a very nice man, but sometimes he felt embarrassed. One day, Tom dared to do something new. He wanted to show his friends that he was not afraid. So, he decided to ride his big cow, Bessie, in front of them. He said, "Look at me, I can ride Bessie!" Tom climbed on Bessie's back, and his friends watched. But Bessie did not like it. She started to run very fast. Tom held on tight, but he was scared. Bessie ran and ran, and Tom could not stop her. They went far away from the farm. Tom was lost and very sad. He wished he never dared to ride Bessie. The bad ending made Tom feel more embarrassed than ever.

Tim and Lily like to play with their toy cars. They have many cars of different colors and sizes. They make a track with books and boxes on the floor. They pretend that their cars are very fast and loud. They say "zoom, zoom, zoom" as they move their cars along the track. One day, Tim has an idea. He says to Lily, "Let's improve our track. Let's make it higher and longer. Then our cars can zoom more and more." Lily agrees. They find more books and boxes and stack them up. They make a big hill and a long bridge. They are very proud of their track. They start to play with their cars again. They take turns to push their cars up the hill and down the bridge. They say "zoom, zoom, zoom" louder and louder. They have a lot of fun. But then, something happens. Tim pushes his car too hard. His car flies off the track and hits a vase on the table. The vase falls and breaks. Tim and Lily hear a loud crash. They are scared. They stop playing and look at the mess. Mom comes into the room. She sees the broken vase and the track. She is not happy. She says to Tim and Lily, "What did you do? You made a big mess. You broke my vase. You were not careful." Tim and Lily feel sorry. They say to Mom, "We are sorry, Mom. We did not mean to break your vase. We were just playing with our cars. We wanted to improve our track. We wanted to zoom more and more. Our cars are harmless. They are just toys." Mom sighs. She says to Tim and Lily, "I know you were just playing. But you have to be more careful. You have to respect the things in the house. You have to clean up your mess. And you have to say sorry to Dad. He gave me that vase as a gift." Tim and Lily nod. They say to Mom, "We understand, Mom. We will be more careful. We will respect the things in the house. We will clean up our mess. And we will say sorry to Dad. We love you, Mom." Mom smiles. She says to Tim and Lily, "I love you too, Tim and Lily. Now, let's clean up together. And then, maybe we can find a better place to play with your cars. A place where you can zoom without breaking anything."
