Category: Beginner
Title: Amy the Girl and the Park

Once upon a time, in a big nation, there lived a funny little girl named Amy. She loved to make people laugh and smile. Every day, she would go to the park and play with her friends. One sunny day, Amy met a new friend named Tim. Tim was shy and didn't talk much. Amy wanted to make Tim laugh, so she put on a funny hat and danced around. Tim started to giggle and said, "You're so funny, Amy! Can you teach me how to be funny too?" Amy was happy that Tim liked her funny dance, so she agreed to help him. Together, they practiced making funny faces and telling silly jokes. Soon, Tim was making everyone in the nation laugh too. One day, Tim had a request for Amy. He asked, "Can we have a big party to make everyone laugh together?" Amy thought that was a great idea. So they invited all their friends and had a big, funny party. Everyone in the nation had a great time, and they all laughed and played together. And from that day on, Amy and Tim were the best of friends.

One day, a little girl named Amy found a rare note in her room. The note was on the floor, under her bed. It was a special note because it was shiny and had a picture of a big, happy sun on it. Amy was very excited and wanted to show her mom the rare note. Amy was so excited that she ran to her mom very fast. But while she was running, she did not see her cat, Max. She tripped on Max and fell down. When she fell, the rare note got a tear in it. Amy was very sad because her special note was not perfect anymore. Amy showed her mom the rare note with the tear in it. Her mom gave her a hug and said that it was still a special note. They put tape on the tear to fix it. Now, Amy and her mom look at the rare note together and remember that it is special, even with the tear.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play with their toys and books. Sometimes they also like to pretend they are grown-ups. They have a small table and two chairs in their room. They also have cups and spoons and a pot. One day, they decide to play coffee shop. Anna is the customer and Ben is the seller. Ben fills the pot with water and puts it on the table. He says, "Welcome to my coffee shop. What do you want to drink?" Anna says, "I want coffee, please." Ben says, "OK, coffee is one dollar. Do you have money?" Anna says, "No, I don't have money. But I have this." She shows Ben a shiny necklace. It is her favorite toy. She likes how it sparkles in the light. Ben looks at the necklace. He thinks it is very pretty. He says, "Wow, that is a nice necklace. Where did you get it?" Anna says, "It was a gift from Grandma. She said it is very special. She said I should value it." Ben says, "What does value mean?" Anna says, "It means you like it a lot and you take good care of it. You don't lose it or break it or give it away." Ben says, "Oh, I see. Well, I value this pot. It is my best toy. I use it to make coffee and tea and soup. I don't let anyone else touch it." Anna says, "Can I touch it?" Ben says, "No, you can't. It is mine." Anna says, "But I want to touch it. It looks fun. Can I trade my necklace for your pot?" Ben thinks for a moment. He likes the necklace, but he likes the pot more. He says, "No, you can't. The pot is more important to me than the necklace. I value the pot more." Anna feels sad. She says, "But I value the necklace more. It is more important to me than the coffee. I want to keep it." Ben says, "Then you can't have coffee. You have to pay for it." Anna says, "But I'm thirsty. And I want to play with you." Ben says, "Then you have to find something else to trade. Something I value more than the pot." Anna looks around the room. She sees their books, their toys, their clothes. She doesn't know what to choose. She feels confused. She says, "I don't know what to do. Can we just share?" Ben says, "Share? What does that mean?" Anna says, "It means we both use something and we both enjoy it. We don't have to trade or pay. We just have fun together." Ben says, "Oh, I see. That sounds nice. How do we do that?" Anna says, "We can take turns. You can use my necklace for a while, and I can use your pot for a while. Then we can switch. And we can both drink coffee." Ben says, "OK, that sounds fair. Let's try it." Anna says, "OK, let's do it." They smile at each other. They share the necklace and the pot. They drink the coffee. They have fun. They value each other.
