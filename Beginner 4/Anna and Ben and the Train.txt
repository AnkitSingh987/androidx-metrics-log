Category: Beginner
Title: Anna and Ben and the Train

Anna and Ben like to play with trains. They have a big train set with tracks and bridges and tunnels. They pretend to be the drivers and the passengers. Sometimes they make train noises and say "choo choo". One day, they find a new toy in the toy box. It is a white train that looks very old. It has a funnel and a whistle and a coal car. Anna and Ben are curious. They put the white train on the tracks and turn it on. The white train starts to move. It goes very fast and makes a loud noise. It also makes steam come out of the funnel. The steam is hot and wet. Anna and Ben are amazed. They say "wow, this train is cool". But then, something bad happens. The white train goes too fast and hits a bridge. The bridge breaks and the train falls off the tracks. The train snaps in half and stops working. The steam stops too. Anna and Ben are sad. They say "oh no, the train is broken". They pick up the pieces of the train and put them back in the toy box. They hope that someone can fix the train. They say "sorry, white train, we did not mean to break you". They go back to playing with their other trains. They are more careful and slow. They say "choo choo".

Once upon a time, there was a busy little boy named Tim. He loved to create things with his toys. One day, he found a shiny coin in his room. He thought it was very special. Tim decided to create a big tower with his blocks. He put the shiny coin on top of the tower. He was so proud of his tower. But then, something unexpected happened. A big wind blew through the window and knocked down his tower. The coin fell off and rolled under his bed. Tim looked under his bed and saw the shiny coin. But there was a twist. The coin had changed! It was now a gold coin with a picture of a happy face on it. Tim was so surprised and happy. He knew that he had found a magic coin that day.

Once upon a time there was a girl called Millie. She was three years old and loved going to the creek by her house. Every day, Millie would walk to the creek with a bucket and fill it with water. One day, when Millie was at the creek, she bumped into her friend Alice. The girls started chatting and Alice asked Millie what she was doing. Millie said she was going to cook for dinner. Alice thought it was a great idea and wanted to help. Millie and Alice went back to Millie’s house and started to cook dinner together. The girls were successful in their cooking. They shared the dinner with Millie’s family and they all enjoyed it. Millie and Alice got to do something fun together and cook dinner, and they were both very proud of their success!

Sara and Tom were playing in the garden. They liked to run and jump and laugh. They saw a hedge with many flowers. The flowers were pink and white and yellow. They smelled sweet and nice. "Look, Tom, pretty flowers!" Sara said. She wanted to pick one for her mom. She reached out her hand to the hedge. "Wait, Sara, don't!" Tom said. He knew the hedge had thorns. Thorns were sharp and hurt. He tried to stop Sara, but it was too late. She touched a thorn and cried out. "Ow, ow, ow!" Sara said. She pulled back her hand. It had a red dot on it. It was bleeding. She felt scared and sad. She ran to her mom. "Mom, mom, help!" Sara said. She showed her mom her hand. Her mom took her inside. She washed her hand and put a band-aid on it. She hugged Sara and kissed her. "It's okay, Sara, it's okay," her mom said. "You have a delicate hand. You have to be careful with thorns. They can hurt you." "I'm sorry, mom, I'm sorry," Sara said. She felt sorry for picking the flower. She wanted to return it to the hedge. She thought the hedge would be sad too. "Can I go back to the hedge, mom?" Sara asked. "I want to say sorry to the hedge and the flower." Her mom smiled. She understood Sara's feelings. She was a kind and gentle girl. "Sure, Sara, you can go back to the hedge," her mom said. "But be careful. Don't touch the thorns again. And say hello to Tom for me." Sara nodded. She thanked her mom and ran back to the garden. She saw Tom waiting for her. He looked worried. "Sara, are you okay?" Tom asked. He was sorry for Sara. He wanted to help her. "I'm okay, Tom, I'm okay," Sara said. She showed him her band-aid. She smiled. "My mom fixed my hand. She said I have a delicate hand. Do you have a delicate hand too?" Tom looked at his hand. It was bigger and stronger than Sara's. He shook his head. "No, I don't have a delicate hand," Tom said. "But I have a delicate heart. I care about you, Sara. You are my friend." Sara hugged Tom. She felt happy and warm. She was glad Tom was her friend. "Thank you, Tom, thank you," Sara said. "You are my friend too. Come, let's go to the hedge. I want to say sorry to the hedge and the flower." Tom nodded. He followed Sara to the hedge. They saw the flower that Sara had picked. It was wilting and sad. "I'm sorry, hedge, I'm sorry, flower," Sara said. She put the flower back to the hedge. She hoped it would feel better. "You are pretty and nice. I won't hurt you again." The hedge seemed to hear Sara. It rustled and shook. It made a soft sound. It sounded like "thank you". Sara and Tom smiled. They felt happy and peaceful. They played in the garden until it was time to go home. They said goodbye to the hedge and the flowers. They promised to return another day.
