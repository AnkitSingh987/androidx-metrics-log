Category: Beginner
Title: Lily the Girl Finds the Box

One day, a little girl named Lily went to the park with her mom. They saw a lady who needed help. The lady was trying to carry a big, heavy box. Lily asked her mom, "Can we help the lady, please?" Her mom said, "Of course, let's go!" Lily and her mom went to the lady and helped her carry the box. The lady said, "Thank you so much! You are very kind." Inside the box, there were lots of yummy cookies. The lady gave Lily and her mom some cookies as a thank you gift. They were very happy and said, "Thank you, lady!" Then, a big wind came and blew the box away. The lady was sad because all the cookies were gone. But Lily had an idea. She said, "Let's all help make more cookies together!" So, they went to the lady's house and made lots of yummy cookies. They shared the cookies with their friends and everyone was happy. The moral of the story is that helping others can bring happiness and yummy treats to everyone.

One day, a little boy named Tim found a thermometer. He did not know what it was for, but he thought it was a magic stick. Tim hoped that the magic stick would make him wealthy. He ran to his friend, Sue, to show her his new find. "Look, Sue! I found a magic stick!" Tim said. "I hope it makes us wealthy!" Sue looked at the thermometer and said, "This is not a magic stick, Tim. It is a thermometer. It tells us if it is hot or cold." Just then, a wealthy man walked by and saw the kids with the thermometer. He was looking for one because he had lost his. He was very happy to see it and said, "Oh, thank you for finding my thermometer! I will give you a reward." The wealthy man gave Tim and Sue a big bag of toys and candy. They were so happy! Even though the thermometer was not a magic stick, it still made them feel like they were wealthy.

Lily and Ben were playing with their toys in the living room. Lily had a furry teddy bear that she loved very much. Ben had a box of stamps that he liked to stick on paper. They were having fun, but they also wanted to see what the other had. "Can I see your stamps, Ben?" Lily asked. "OK, but only if I can see your bear, Lily," Ben said. They swapped their toys and looked at them. Lily liked the stamps. They had different colors and pictures. She wanted to stick one on her hand. Ben liked the bear. It was soft and cuddly. He wanted to hug it. But then, Lily saw that Ben was pinching the bear's ear. She did not like that. She thought Ben was hurting the bear. "Stop it, Ben! You are hurting my bear!" Lily shouted. "No, I'm not. I'm just playing with it," Ben said. "Yes, you are. Give it back to me!" Lily said. She reached for the bear, but Ben pulled it away. Lily got angry. She grabbed a stamp and pinched Ben's arm with it. "Ow! That hurts, Lily!" Ben cried. He dropped the bear and rubbed his arm. Lily picked up the bear and hugged it. "I'm sorry, Ben. I didn't mean to hurt you. But you shouldn't pinch my bear," Lily said. "I'm sorry, too, Lily. I didn't mean to hurt your bear. But you shouldn't pinch me with a stamp," Ben said. They looked at each other and felt sorry. They decided to say sorry and share their toys. "I'm sorry, Lily. Can we be friends again?" Ben asked. "I'm sorry, too, Ben. Yes, we can be friends again," Lily said. They smiled and hugged. Then they played with the stamps and the bear together. They had fun and learned to be nice to each other.

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim had a toy pistol that could spray water. He loved to play with it in the yard. One day, he saw a big red ball in the sky. He thought it was a balloon, but it was really the sun. Tim wanted to spray the big red ball with his water pistol. He waited and waited, being very patient. He knew that the sun would come down soon. When the sun started to set, Tim got ready to spray it with water. As the sun went down, it looked like it was getting closer to the ground. Tim aimed his water pistol and sprayed it. The water went up into the air and made a pretty rainbow. Tim was so happy that he could spray the sun and make a rainbow. He knew that being patient was a good thing.
