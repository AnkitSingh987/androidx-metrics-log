Title: Asking for Help

Category: Intermediate

Tom stood on the busy corner of Market Street, looking frantically around him. The sun was shining brightly, and hundreds of people were rushing past him, holding coffee cups and talking loudly on their phones. Tom, however, was not moving. He was staring at the small, black screen of his laptop, pressing the power button over and over again. Nothing happened. The machine was completely dead.
Tom was a junior financial analyst, and this was his first business trip abroad. His company had sent him to Berlin, Germany, to present a major financial report to a potential client. The presentation was scheduled for 2:00 PM, and it was currently 10:30 AM. All of his data, his slides, and his notes were on that laptop. He hadn't backed them up to the cloud because the internet connection at his hotel had been very weak the night before.
Panic began to rise in his chest. If he couldn't fix this laptop in the next three hours, he would ruin the presentation, and possibly his career.
He took out his smartphone and quickly searched for computer repair shops nearby. The map showed a place called 'TechFix' about a twenty-minute walk away, in a neighborhood filled with narrow, winding streets. Tom grabbed his briefcase, tucked the broken laptop under his arm, and started walking fast.
After fifteen minutes, Tom realized he was completely lost. The streets didn't look like they did on the map. The street signs were all in German, a language he did not speak at all. He had turned down a small alleyway to save time, and now he didn't know which direction was north or south.
He stopped near a small bakery. The smell of fresh bread was wonderful, but Tom felt too sick to his stomach to appreciate it. He knew he had to ask for help, even if he felt embarrassed about not knowing the language.
An older woman walked out of the bakery, carrying a paper bag. She looked friendly, wearing a thick sweater and small glasses. Tom took a deep breath and approached her.
"Excuse me, madam," Tom said politely, speaking slowly and clearly. "Do you speak English?"
The woman stopped and smiled gently. "A little bit," she replied with a thick accent. "How can I help you?"
"I am looking for a computer repair shop called TechFix," Tom explained, showing her the name on his phone screen. "My computer is broken, and I have a very important meeting soon. Can you tell me how to get there?"
The woman looked at the screen and then frowned, looking down the street. "Ah, TechFix. Yes, I know it. But it is difficult to explain. You must go straight, then turn left at the blue pharmacy, then walk across a bridge..." She paused, struggling to find the right English words. "It is very confusing. You might get lost again."
Tom sighed. "Is it far from here?"
"Not very far," she said. She looked at his worried face. "You look very stressed, young man. Wait here one moment."
She turned around and spoke loudly in German to a young man who was sitting at a table outside the bakery, drinking an espresso. The young man looked up, nodded, and walked over to them.
"Hello," the young man said in perfect, fluent English. "My aunt says you are trying to find TechFix. I'm Lukas."
"Hi, I'm Tom," he replied, shaking hands. "Yes, I really need to get there quickly. My laptop died, and my entire presentation is on it."
"TechFix is a great shop, they work very fast," Lukas said. "But the streets around here are like a maze for tourists. I have finished my coffee, and I have some free time. I can walk you there, if you like."
Tom felt a huge wave of relief. "Are you sure? I don't want to bother you."
"It's no problem at all," Lukas smiled. "Come on, follow me."
They walked together through the complicated streets. Lukas was very friendly and asked Tom about his trip. Tom explained his job and how nervous he was about the meeting. Talking to Lukas made him feel calmer. Lukas explained that he was a university student studying engineering, and he loved practicing his English with visitors.
In less than ten minutes, they arrived in front of a small shop with a bright blue sign that read 'TechFix'.
"Here we are," Lukas said.
"Thank you so much, Lukas. You really saved me," Tom said, incredibly grateful.
They walked inside. The shop was small and filled with wires, screens, and computer parts. A tall man with a beard was working behind the counter. He greeted them in German.
Tom suddenly realized another problem. What if the technician didn't speak English? How would he explain that he needed it fixed immediately?
Before Tom could try to explain, Lukas started speaking rapidly in German to the technician. He pointed to Tom, pointed to the laptop, and made a gesture like looking at a watch. The technician listened carefully, nodded, and then looked at Tom.
"Lukas told me you have an emergency," the technician said in heavily accented, but clear English. "Let me look at it."
Tom handed the laptop over. The technician plugged it into a special machine and pressed a few buttons. He stared at a screen for a minute.
"Ah, I see the problem," the technician said. "Your battery connector is completely broken. The machine thinks there is no power. It is a common problem with this model."
"Can you fix it?" Tom asked nervously. "I need it by 1:00 PM."
The technician looked at the clock on the wall. It was 11:15 AM. "Yes, I have the replacement part in the back room. It will take about forty-five minutes to replace it and test the system. You can wait here."
Tom almost cried with happiness. "Thank you. Thank you so much."
Lukas patted Tom on the shoulder. "See? Everything is going to be fine. I have to go to my university class now, but you are in good hands."
Tom turned to Lukas. "I don't know how to thank you properly. You didn't have to help a stranger like this."
"It's nothing," Lukas smiled warmly. "When I traveled to London last year, I got lost on the subway. A stranger spent twenty minutes helping me find the right train. I am just returning the favor. Good luck with your presentation, Tom!"
Lukas waved and left the shop. Forty-five minutes later, the technician handed Tom his laptop, fully repaired and turning on perfectly. Tom paid the bill, walked outside, and found a taxi to take him back to his hotel.
At 2:00 PM, Tom stood in front of the clients in a large boardroom. His laptop worked flawlessly, and his presentation was a huge success. As he finished his speech, he thought about the kind woman at the bakery and her helpful nephew. He realized that no matter where you are in the world, or what language you speak, people are generally willing to help you if you are just brave enough to ask.
