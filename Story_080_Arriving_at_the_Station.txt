Title: Arriving at the Station
Category: Beginner

The heavy silver train slowly squeaked to a complete stop. A voice spoke over the speaker in the carriage. 
"We have arrived at Green Valley Station. Please make sure you have all your bags and belongings before leaving the train."
Mr. Miller stood up quickly. He stretched his arms and his back. Sitting for three hours made him feel a little stiff. 
"Okay, everyone," he said. "Gather your things. Let's go."

Thomas picked up his messy blue bag from the floor. Sophie grabbed her small pink suitcase. Mrs. Miller took the empty food bag and her purse. Mr. Miller reached up to the rack and carefully pulled down the large family suitcase. It was very heavy. 
They walked down the narrow aisle towards the doors. The doors slid open with a soft 'whoosh'. 

Cold, fresh air rushed into the warm train carriage. It smelled completely different from the city air. It smelled like wet grass, pine trees, and wood smoke. 
Thomas stepped down onto the concrete platform first. Sophie followed him. They took a deep breath. 
"It is very cold here!" Sophie said, zipping her jacket all the way up to her chin. 
"I told you to pack warm clothes," her mother smiled as she stepped off the train. 

They stood on the platform and looked around. Green Valley Station was very small. It was not like the huge, noisy central station in the city. There was only one small building made of stone, and a few wooden benches. There were no big digital departure boards, only a paper timetable pinned to a wooden board. It was very quiet. The only sound was the wind and the low humming of the train engine. 

"Where are Grandma and Grandpa?" Thomas asked, looking up and down the empty platform. 
"They said they would meet us near the station exit," Mr. Miller replied. He picked up the heavy suitcase. "Let's walk towards the building."

They walked together along the concrete platform. The silver train blew its horn one last time and slowly started to move forward, continuing its journey to the next town. 

As they walked past the small stone building, Sophie suddenly pointed towards the parking lot. 
"Look! There they are!" she shouted happily. 

Standing next to an old green car was an older man with grey hair and a thick winter coat. Next to him stood an older woman wearing a bright red hat and a long scarf. It was Grandpa and Grandma. 

When the grandparents saw the family, their faces broke into massive, joyful smiles. They started walking quickly towards them. 
Thomas and Sophie dropped their bags on the ground. They ran as fast as they could across the pavement. 

"Grandma! Grandpa!" they yelled. 
Grandpa caught Thomas in a big, strong hug and lifted him off the ground. Grandma wrapped her arms tightly around Sophie. 
"Oh, my wonderful grandchildren!" Grandma said. Her eyes were a little bit wet with happy tears. "Look how much you have grown! You are both so tall now."
"It is so good to see you," Grandpa laughed, patting Thomas on the back. "We missed you very much."

Mr. and Mrs. Miller walked up to them, pulling the suitcases. 
"Hello, Dad. Hello, Mom," Mr. Miller said, hugging his parents. 
"Welcome to Green Valley," Grandpa said warmly. "How was the train ride? Was it comfortable?"
"It was very good," Mrs. Miller answered. "The children loved looking out the window at all the cows and rivers."

"Well, it is very cold standing out here," Grandma said, pulling her red scarf tighter. "Let's put these heavy bags in the trunk of the car. I made a huge pot of hot chicken stew at the new house. It is waiting for you on the stove, and it will warm you right up."
"That sounds perfect," Mr. Miller agreed. 

Grandpa opened the trunk of the green car. He helped Mr. Miller lift the large suitcase inside. Thomas and Sophie threw their smaller bags in next to it. They all climbed into the car. It was warm and smelled slightly of peppermint. 
As Grandpa started the engine and drove away from the small station, Thomas and Sophie looked out the back window. The weekend adventure had officially begun, and they were surrounded by the people they loved.
