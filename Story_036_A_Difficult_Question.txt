Title: A Difficult Question
Category: Beginner

It is Wednesday morning. The students are in Room 5. It is a history class. Mr. Brown is the history teacher. He is a tall man with glasses. He knows a lot about the past. 

Mr. Brown is talking about old cities. He is talking about people who lived a long time ago. He points to a big map on the wall. 
"Look at this map," Mr. Brown says. "This is a very old map. It shows a famous city."

Tom is sitting at his desk. He is listening, but he is also looking out the window. He sees a bird in a tree. He watches the bird. 
Suddenly, Mr. Brown stops talking. He looks at the class. 
"Now, I have a question," Mr. Brown says. 

Tom stops looking at the bird. He looks at Mr. Brown. He hopes Mr. Brown does not ask him. 
"Tom," Mr. Brown says. 
Tom feels cold. His heart beats fast. 
"Yes, Mr. Brown?" Tom says quietly. 
"Can you tell us the name of this famous city on the map?" Mr. Brown asks. "I said the name five minutes ago."

Tom looks at the map. He looks at the teacher. His mind is blank. He does not know the answer. He was looking at the bird outside. He was not listening carefully. 
The question is very difficult for him. 

The classroom is very quiet. All the other students look at Tom. Tom feels hot in his face. He is embarrassed. 
He tries to remember. What did Mr. Brown say? Rome? Athens? 
"Is it... is it London?" Tom guesses. He knows it is a bad guess. 

Some students laugh quietly. Mr. Brown does not laugh. He shakes his head. 
"No, Tom, it is not London," Mr. Brown says. "London is an old city, but it is not the city on this map."
Tom looks down at his desk. "I am sorry, Mr. Brown," he says. "I do not know the answer. It is a difficult question for me."

Mr. Brown walks closer to Tom's desk. He speaks softly. 
"It is a difficult question if you do not listen, Tom," Mr. Brown says. "Were you looking out the window?"
Tom nods his head. "Yes, sir. I was looking at a bird."
"Birds are very interesting," Mr. Brown says. "But history is interesting too. You must listen in class."
"I know. I will listen now," Tom promises. 

Mr. Brown smiles a little. "Okay. Let's find the answer together. Look at the map again, Tom."
Tom looks at the map on the wall. 
"Do you see the big river next to the city?" Mr. Brown asks. 
"Yes, I see a blue line," Tom says. "That is the river."
"Good," Mr. Brown says. "And do you remember the story I told about the big pyramids?"
"Pyramids," Tom thinks. He remembers a picture in his book. "Yes! Pyramids are in Egypt."

"Exactly," Mr. Brown says. "So, the country is Egypt. What is the most famous old city in Egypt?"
Tom thinks hard. He remembers reading a book about it last week. 
"Is it... Cairo?" Tom asks. 
Mr. Brown shakes his head. "Cairo is a big city in Egypt today. But we are talking about a very old city."
Tom is confused again. 

A girl named Sarah raises her hand. 
"Yes, Sarah?" Mr. Brown asks. 
"Is it Alexandria?" she asks. 
"No, not Alexandria," Mr. Brown says. "But that is a good guess."

Mr. Brown looks back at Tom. "Let me give you a hint, Tom. The name starts with the letter 'M'."
Tom thinks. 'M'. A famous old city in Egypt starting with 'M'. 
Then, a word pops into his head. 
"Memphis!" Tom shouts. "Is it Memphis?"

Mr. Brown smiles a big smile. "Yes, Tom! Very good. The city on the map is Memphis. It was an important city a very long time ago."
Tom feels very happy. The difficult question is not difficult anymore. He found the answer with the teacher's help. 
The other students nod their heads. Now they all know the answer too. 

"Thank you, Tom," Mr. Brown says. "Now, please open your books to page thirty. Let's read more about Memphis."
Tom opens his history book to page thirty. He takes his pen. He is ready to work. 
He does not look out the window anymore. He does not look for birds. He looks at Mr. Brown. He listens carefully to every word. 
He learns that it is okay to not know an answer. It is okay to say 'I don't know'. A good teacher will help you find the answer. But a good student must always try to listen. Tom wants to be a good student.
