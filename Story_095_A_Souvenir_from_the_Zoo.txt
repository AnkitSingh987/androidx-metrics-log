Title: A Souvenir from the Zoo
Category: Beginner

After they ate their picnic lunch, the family spent the entire afternoon walking around the zoo. They saw heavy grey elephants spraying water on their backs. They saw slippery penguins swimming incredibly fast in their cold pool. They even visited the dark, warm reptile house to see the dangerous snakes and the lazy crocodiles. 

By four o'clock in the afternoon, everyone was very tired. Their feet ached from walking on the hard paths for so many hours. 
"I think it is time to go home," their father announced, looking at the setting sun. "We have seen almost every animal on Sam's map."
"My legs are very tired," Lily agreed, leaning against her mother. 

They started walking slowly towards the main exit. As they walked past the flamingos one last time, they saw a large wooden building near the heavy metal turnstiles. The sign above the door said 'Zoo Gift Shop'. 

"Can we go inside, please?" Sam asked eagerly. "I have five dollars from my allowance. I want to buy something to remember our trip."
"I have five dollars too!" Lily added. 
Their mother smiled. "Okay, we can spend ten minutes in the gift shop. But you can only spend the money you brought. Nothing more."

They pushed the heavy glass door open and walked inside. The gift shop was brightly lit and air-conditioned. It was full of colorful items. There were shelves packed with soft, cuddly animal toys. There were racks of t-shirts with pictures of tigers and bears. There were mugs, keychains, books, and puzzles. It was a paradise for children. 

"Remember, you only have five dollars each," their father reminded them, standing near the door. "Choose carefully."
Sam and Lily walked down the aisles, looking at everything. 

Lily went straight to the soft toys. She picked up a small, fluffy white polar bear. She looked at the price tag. It was twelve dollars. She sighed and put it back on the shelf. She picked up a cute, brown monkey with a long tail. It was fifteen dollars. 
"Everything is too expensive," Lily said sadly. 

Her mother walked over to help her. "The soft toys are usually expensive, Lily. Why don't you look at the smaller things over here?"
She led Lily to a display near the cash register. There were hundreds of small, plastic animal figures. They were very detailed and looked like real, tiny animals. 
Lily looked through the baskets. She remembered her favorite animals from the day. She found a beautiful, tall giraffe with brown spots. She looked at the price sticker on its belly. It was three dollars. 

"I want this giraffe," Lily decided happily. "And I have two dollars left. What else can I buy?"
"You could buy a postcard," her mother suggested, pointing to a spinning rack of glossy cards. 
Lily spun the rack. She found a beautiful postcard with a picture of a baby giraffe on it. It cost one dollar and fifty cents. 
"Perfect," Lily said, holding her plastic giraffe and her postcard. She still had fifty cents left in her pocket. 

Meanwhile, Sam was having a difficult time deciding. He loved the monkey section, but the monkey keychains were six dollars. He looked at the books about snakes, but they were ten dollars. 
He walked up and down the aisles, holding his five-dollar bill tightly. He wanted something useful, something he could keep in his room. 

He walked to the stationery section. He saw a row of very cool pens. The pens were made of wood, and the top of each pen was carved into the shape of an animal head. There was a lion pen, an elephant pen, and an eagle pen. 
Sam picked up the pen with the lion head. It looked very fierce, much fiercer than the sleepy lions they saw in the enclosure. He checked the price. Four dollars and ninety-nine cents. 
It was exactly enough. 

"Did you find something, Sam?" his father asked. 
"Yes," Sam said proudly, showing his father the wooden lion pen. "I am going to use it at school to do my homework."

Sam and Lily walked to the cash register. They waited in a short line. When it was their turn, Lily put her three dollars and fifty cents on the counter. The cashier smiled and put her plastic giraffe and postcard into a small paper bag. Then, Sam gave the cashier his five-dollar bill. The cashier gave him back one shiny penny in change and handed him his lion pen. 

They walked out of the gift shop and through the main exit of the zoo. They walked to their car in the large parking lot. 
They were exhausted, but it was a good, happy kind of exhaustion. They had spent a wonderful day together. 
As their father drove the car out of the parking lot and towards the highway, Sam and Lily sat quietly in the back seat. Lily held her small plastic giraffe, remembering the gentle giants with purple tongues. Sam held his wooden lion pen, ready to write about his great adventure. The souvenirs were small, but they would always help them remember their fantastic day at the City Zoo.
