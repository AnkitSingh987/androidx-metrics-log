Title: The Question I Asked
Category: Beginner

My name is Leo, and I am twelve years old. I am a quiet boy in school. I like to sit at the back of the classroom. I listen to the teacher, I take notes, and I do my work. But I have one big problem. I am afraid to ask questions. 

When I do not understand something, I stay silent. I feel scared that my question is stupid. I think the other students will laugh at me. I think the teacher will be angry. Because of this fear, I often go home confused. I spend a long time trying to figure things out by myself. 

Today is Thursday. We are in science class. Our teacher is Mrs. Baker. She is talking about the water cycle. She is explaining how rain falls from the clouds, goes into the rivers, and then goes back up into the sky. 
She draws a big diagram on the white board. She uses blue markers for the water and yellow markers for the sun. 
"So, the sun heats the water in the ocean," Mrs. Baker says. "And the water turns into a gas. This is called evaporation."

I am writing the word 'evaporation' in my notebook, but I do not understand it. How can water turn into a gas? I can see water, but I cannot see gas. Where does it go? 
I look at the diagram on the board, but it does not help me. I am very confused. 

Mrs. Baker turns around and looks at the class. 
"Does everyone understand evaporation?" she asks. "Are there any questions?"
The classroom is completely silent. Nobody says a word. Nobody raises a hand. 
I look around. My friend Tom is nodding his head. The girl in front of me is looking at her book. It seems that everyone understands except me. 

I feel a knot in my stomach. I really need to know how water turns into gas, because there is a test next week. But I am too scared to ask. My hand feels heavy. I look down at my desk. 
'Just ask,' a small voice in my head says. 'She will not be angry.'

I take a deep breath. My heart is beating very fast. Thump, thump, thump. 
Slowly, I raise my right hand into the air. 
Mrs. Baker sees my hand. She smiles. 
"Yes, Leo? Do you have a question?" she asks gently. 

Every head in the classroom turns to look at me. I feel my face turning red. I want to put my hand down, but it is too late. 
"Um... yes, Mrs. Baker," I say. My voice is very quiet and a little shaky. "I do not understand evaporation. How can water turn into gas? We cannot see gas. Does the water just disappear?"

I wait for the other students to laugh. I close my eyes a little bit. 
But nobody laughs. The room is still quiet. 
Mrs. Baker puts down her marker. She walks closer to my desk. She does not look angry at all. She looks very pleased. 

"That is a fantastic question, Leo," Mrs. Baker says loudly. "A very smart question. I am glad you asked it."
I open my eyes wide. I am surprised. She thinks my question is smart? 

Mrs. Baker looks at the rest of the class. "How many other people did not fully understand evaporation, but were too shy to ask?"
Slowly, one hand goes up in the front row. Then another hand goes up. Then Tom raises his hand. Soon, almost half of the class has their hands in the air. 
I am shocked. I was not the only confused person. Many other students had the same question, but they were also afraid. 

"You see," Mrs. Baker says. "If you do not ask, I do not know that I need to explain it better. Thank you, Leo, for being brave."
She walks back to the white board. 
"Let me explain it in a different way," she says. "Think about a pot of water on a hot stove. When the water gets very hot, what happens?"

A girl named Sarah answers, "It boils, and steam comes out of the pot."
"Exactly!" Mrs. Baker says. "Steam is water gas. You can see steam for a second, but then it mixes with the air and becomes invisible. The sun does the same thing to the ocean, but slowly. The water does not disappear; it just changes form."

Suddenly, everything is clear in my mind. I understand it perfectly. Steam is water gas. 
"Oh, I see now," I say happily. "Thank you, Mrs. Baker."
"You are welcome, Leo," she says. 

The rest of the science class is very interesting. When Mrs. Baker talks about clouds, I have another question. This time, my heart does not beat fast. I do not feel scared. I just raise my hand and ask. 
I learned a very important lesson today. Asking a question does not mean you are stupid. It means you want to learn. And very often, if you have a question, someone else in the room has the exact same question. From now on, I will not be the silent boy at the back of the class. I will raise my hand and ask.
