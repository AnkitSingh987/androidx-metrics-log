Title: The Receipt Was Missing

Category: Intermediate

Tom was a man who loved a good cup of coffee in the morning. For years, he had visited the small cafe on the corner of his street every day before going to work. He enjoyed the friendly atmosphere and the professional quality of the espresso. However, when the cafe raised its prices for the third time in a single year, Tom decided it was time to make a change. He calculated how much money he was spending on coffee every month and realized he could save a significant amount if he bought his own espresso machine.

On a rainy Saturday afternoon, Tom drove to a large department store on the edge of the city. He walked into the kitchen appliance section and was amazed by the variety of coffee machines. There were basic models that cost fifty dollars and complex, shiny silver machines that cost over a thousand. A helpful salesperson showed him a mid-range model that cost two hundred dollars. It had a milk frother, a built-in grinder, and it looked very impressive. The salesperson assured him it was one of their most reliable brands. Convinced, Tom bought the machine, carried the large box to his car, and drove home feeling very pleased with his purchase.
For the first week, the machine was wonderful. Tom watched several online tutorials to learn how to grind the beans correctly and froth the milk to the perfect temperature. He woke up early every morning, enjoying the rich smell of fresh coffee filling his kitchen. He felt proud that he was saving money and developing a new skill.

But on the eighth day, disaster struck. Tom woke up, went to the kitchen, and turned the machine on. It made a strange, loud clicking noise, and then all the lights on the front panel started flashing rapidly. He pressed the buttons, but nothing happened. He unplugged it, waited a few minutes, and plugged it back in, but the lights just kept flashing. The machine was completely broken.
Tom was frustrated, but he wasn't too worried. He knew he had a one-year warranty. He just needed to take it back to the store and exchange it for a new one.
He went to his home office to find the receipt. He usually kept all his important documents and receipts in a specific green folder in his desk drawer. He opened the drawer, took out the folder, and started searching. He found the receipt for his electricity bill, the receipt for his new winter boots, and the warranty card for his television. But the receipt for the coffee machine was not there.

Tom searched his desk, his wallet, the pockets of the jacket he had worn that day, and even the floor of his car. He couldn't find it anywhere. He remembered the cashier handing it to him, but he couldn't remember what he had done with it after that. He had been so excited to open the box that he must have thrown the receipt away with the shopping bag.
He felt a knot of anxiety in his stomach. How was he going to return a broken machine without proof of purchase? He knew store policies were usually very strict about requiring receipts for returns or exchanges.
Taking a deep breath, Tom packed the broken machine back into its original box, put it in his car, and drove back to the department store. He walked up to the Customer Service desk, carrying the heavy box.

A young woman with a name tag that read 'Jessica' was standing behind the counter. She smiled politely. "Good morning. How can I help you today?"
"Good morning," Tom said, placing the box on the counter. "I bought this coffee machine here just over a week ago, and it has already stopped working. It just makes a clicking noise and flashes. I'd like to exchange it for a new one, please."
"I'm so sorry to hear that," Jessica said sympathetically. "We can certainly help you with an exchange. Do you have your receipt with you?"
Tom sighed. "Unfortunately, I don't. I've looked everywhere in my house, and I think I might have accidentally thrown it away with the shopping bags. But I promise you, I bought it right here in this store exactly eight days ago."
Jessica’s smile faded slightly, and she looked apologetic. "I understand, sir. But our store policy strictly requires a receipt for all returns and exchanges. Without a receipt, I have no way of verifying when you bought it, or even if you bought it from our store. I can't process the return on the computer system without scanning the barcode on the receipt."

Tom tried to stay calm. He knew getting angry wouldn't help the situation. "I completely understand your policy," he said reasonably. "But this is a two-hundred-dollar machine, and it broke after a week. There must be some way to prove I bought it here. You have security cameras, don't you?"
"We do have cameras, sir, but we can't review security footage just to verify a purchase. It's against company policy," Jessica explained.
Tom thought for a moment. He suddenly remembered how he had paid. "Wait!" he said. "I paid with my credit card. Can you look up the transaction on your system using my card?"
Jessica shook her head slowly. "Our current point-of-sale system doesn't allow us to search for transactions using the customer's credit card number. We can only scan the receipt."
Tom felt a rising sense of panic. He was going to lose two hundred dollars. Then, he had another idea. He pulled his smartphone out of his pocket and opened his mobile banking application. He scrolled through his recent transactions until he found the one from eight days ago.
"Look," Tom said, holding his phone across the counter. "Here is my bank statement. It shows a charge for two hundred and fifteen dollars—that's the machine plus tax—made at this exact store, on Saturday the fourteenth, at two-thirty in the afternoon."

Jessica looked at the phone screen. "I see the transaction, sir. But I still can't use a bank statement to process an exchange in the computer system. The system requires the original transaction number."
"Could you please call your manager?" Tom asked politely but firmly. "I'm sure if a manager looks at the exact time and date on my bank statement, they can check the store's sales records for that specific time and find the transaction number."
Jessica hesitated, but then nodded. "Let me call him and see what he can do."
She picked up a phone on the counter and spoke quietly for a minute. A few minutes later, a man in a suit walked out from a back office. He introduced himself as David, the store manager.
Tom calmly explained the situation again and showed David his mobile bank statement.
"I understand the difficulty," David said, looking at the phone. "It's highly unusual, but since you have the exact time, date, and amount, I can go to the back office and search the electronic journal for that specific register. If I find the matching transaction, I can print a duplicate receipt."
"Thank you," Tom said, feeling a huge wave of relief. "I would really appreciate that."

David disappeared into the office. Tom waited nervously at the counter for what felt like an hour, though it was only ten minutes. Finally, David returned, holding a small, white piece of paper.
"You're in luck," David smiled. "I found the transaction. Here is a duplicate receipt."
Jessica quickly took the paper, scanned the barcode, and processed the exchange. Within five minutes, Tom was walking out of the store with a brand new, working coffee machine. As he put it in his car, he made a firm promise to himself: he would never, ever throw away a receipt for an electronic device again.
