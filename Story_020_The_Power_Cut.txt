Title: The Power Cut
Category: Beginner

It is a cold winter evening. It is eight o'clock. Outside, the wind is blowing very hard. 
Inside the house, it is warm and bright. The Smith family is in the living room. 
Mr. Smith is reading a book under a tall lamp. Mrs. Smith is knitting a scarf on the sofa. Their son, Oliver, is doing his homework at the small table. He is writing with a pencil. 

The television is on. A man on the television is talking about the weather. He says it is a very bad storm. 
"The wind is very strong tonight," Mr. Smith says. He turns a page of his book. 
"Yes, it is," Mrs. Smith says. "I hope the roof is okay."
Oliver does not say anything. He is thinking about a difficult math problem. 

Suddenly, everything stops. 
The television goes black. The tall lamp goes out. The ceiling light goes out. 
The room is completely dark. 
The house is silent. Only the wind outside is loud. 

"What happened?" Oliver asks. His voice is a little scared. 
"The lights are off," Mr. Smith says. His voice is calm. 
"It is a power cut," Mrs. Smith explains. "The storm broke the electricity lines outside."
"I cannot see anything," Oliver says. "It is too dark."
"Don't move, Oliver," his father says. "Stay in your chair. I will find a light."

Mr. Smith stands up slowly. He puts his hands out in front of him. He does not want to hit a wall or a chair. He walks carefully to the kitchen. 
In the kitchen, he feels for the drawers. He opens the top drawer. He moves his hand inside. He touches a spoon. He touches a knife. Then, he touches something round and plastic. It is a flashlight. 
He picks it up. He pushes the button. 
Click! 
A strong beam of white light cuts through the dark kitchen. 

"I have the flashlight," Mr. Smith calls out. 
He walks back to the living room. The light from the flashlight bounces on the walls. He shines the light on the floor so he can see where to walk. 
He shines the light on his wife. She is still sitting on the sofa. 
He shines the light on his son. Oliver is sitting at his table. He looks happy to see the light. 

"That is better," Mrs. Smith says. "But one flashlight is not enough for the whole house."
"We have candles in the cupboard," Mr. Smith says. 
"I will get them," Mrs. Smith says. She stands up. Mr. Smith gives her the flashlight. 

Mrs. Smith walks to the kitchen. She opens a high cupboard. She finds a box of long white candles. She also finds a small box of matches. 
She brings them back to the living room. 
She puts three candles on the coffee table. She puts one candle on Oliver's table. 
She opens the box of matches. She takes out a small wooden match. She strikes it against the side of the box. 
Sss-crack! 
A small yellow flame appears. 

Mrs. Smith lights the first candle. The flame dances a little. She lights the second candle. She lights the third candle. She lights Oliver's candle. 
Then, she turns off the flashlight. 
The room is not dark anymore. It is filled with soft, yellow light. The candle light makes shadows on the walls. The shadows move when the flames move. It looks very different from the electric light. 

"It is beautiful," Oliver says. 
"It is very cozy," Mrs. Smith agrees. 
But they cannot do their usual things. 
Oliver cannot see well enough to do his math homework. The light is too soft. He puts his pencil down. 
Mr. Smith cannot read his book. 
Mrs. Smith cannot knit her scarf. 

"What do we do now?" Oliver asks. 
"We wait," his father says. "The men will fix the electricity lines soon."
"We can talk," his mother says. "We can tell stories."
"That is a great idea," Mr. Smith says. 

They sit around the coffee table in the soft candle light. The television is quiet. The house is quiet. 
Mr. Smith tells a story about when he was a little boy. He tells a funny story about a dog. Oliver laughs. 
Then Mrs. Smith tells a story. She tells a story about a trip to the mountains. 
Oliver likes listening to the stories. He watches the candle flames. He feels warm and safe with his parents. 

They talk for one hour. Then, they talk for another hour. 
Suddenly, the television comes on with a loud noise. 
The tall lamp turns on. 
The ceiling light turns on. 
The room is very bright. The power is back. 

Oliver covers his eyes for a second. The light is too strong. 
"The power is back!" Mr. Smith says. 
Mrs. Smith blows out the candles. 'Puff, puff, puff.' Small streams of grey smoke go up in the air. 
"The candles were nice," Oliver says. 
"Yes, they were," his mother says. "But I am glad the electricity is back. Now I can finish my knitting."
"And I must finish my math homework," Oliver says. 

The power cut is over. The house is back to normal. But Oliver will remember the cozy evening with the candles and the stories for a long time.
