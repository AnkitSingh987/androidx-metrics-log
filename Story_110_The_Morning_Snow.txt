Title: The Morning Snow
Category: Beginner

Mark woke up the next morning feeling very warm. He was still wrapped tightly in his heavy duvet on the living room armchair. He opened his eyes slowly. 

The room was filled with an incredibly bright, harsh light. It was not the soft golden light of the candles, and it was not the artificial light of electricity. It was pure, bright sunlight reflecting off something very white outside. 

He sat up and looked at the coffee table. The candles were gone. The board games were packed away. His parents were already awake. He could hear them talking quietly in the kitchen. 
Mark stood up, stretched his stiff back, and walked to the large living room window. 

He gasped in amazement. 
The world outside had completely transformed overnight. The storm was over. The dark, howling wind was gone. The sky above was a brilliant, clear, cloudless blue. But the ground was buried under a massive blanket of pure white snow. 

The snow was incredibly deep. It covered the street, the sidewalks, and the cars. The roofs of the houses across the street were piled high with thick, soft white pillows of snow. The branches of the trees were bending downward under the heavy weight. 
Everything looked clean, fresh, and incredibly peaceful. 

Mark walked into the kitchen. His father was standing at the gas stove, boiling a kettle of water. 
"Good morning, sleepyhead," his father smiled. "Did you sleep well in your chair?"
"I slept great," Mark said. "Is the electricity back on?"
"No, the power is still out," his mother said. She was wearing a thick sweater and a scarf indoors. "But I checked my phone. The school sent a message. All schools in the city are closed today because the roads are too dangerous."

"A snow day!" Mark cheered. Every student loved a snow day. It meant no history class and no math test. 

"Don't get too excited," his father warned him, pouring boiling water into a coffee mug. "It is not a holiday. We have a lot of hard work to do outside. The snow is almost half a meter deep in the driveway. If we don't clear it now, it will turn into hard ice tonight, and we won't be able to move the car for a week."

Mark looked out the kitchen window at the driveway. His father was right. The snow was very deep. 
"Okay," Mark agreed. "I will help you shovel."

After a quick, cold breakfast of cereal and milk, Mark and his father dressed for the extreme cold. They put on thick waterproof pants, heavy winter boots, two pairs of socks, warm winter coats, hats, and waterproof gloves. They looked like astronauts preparing to walk on the moon. 

They opened the front door. The cold air hit their faces sharply. The snow had piled up against the door during the night, so they had to push hard to open it. 
They stepped out onto the porch. It was absolutely silent outside. The thick snow absorbed all the normal city sounds. There were no cars driving, no birds singing, no people talking. 

His father handed Mark a smaller blue snow shovel and took the large red one for himself. 
"Okay, we start at the top of the driveway and work our way down to the street," his father directed. "Remember to bend your knees when you lift the snow, or you will hurt your back."

They started to shovel. The snow was light and fluffy, but there was so much of it. Mark pushed his blue shovel into the white bank, lifted the snow, and threw it onto the grass next to the driveway. 'Scrape, lift, throw.' They worked in a steady rhythm. 

It was very hard physical work. After twenty minutes, Mark was breathing heavily, and he could feel sweat on his back, even though the air temperature was below freezing. 
"This is exhausting," Mark panted, leaning on his shovel for a rest. 
"You are doing a great job," his father encouraged him. "Look behind you."

Mark looked back at the house. They had cleared a large, clean path all the way from the garage door down to the middle of the driveway. The dark grey concrete looked strange against the bright white snow walls on either side. 
"We are halfway there," his father said. 

They worked for another hour until the entire driveway was clear and they had scattered the rock salt to prevent ice. 
They stood on the street, leaning on their shovels, exhausted but proud. The sun shone brightly on the beautiful, silent winter world. The storm had brought cold and darkness, but it had also left behind a sparkling, magical morning.
