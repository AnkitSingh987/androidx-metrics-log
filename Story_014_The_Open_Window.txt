Title: The Open Window
Category: Beginner

It is a quiet afternoon in the house. Mr. Harris is in his study. The study is a small room where he works. 
There is a big desk in the middle of the room. The desk is covered with many papers. Mr. Harris is a writer. He writes stories. He is writing a new story today. 
He sits on his comfortable chair. He holds a pen in his hand. He writes on a piece of white paper. 

The weather is nice today. It is spring. The sun is shining, but it is not too hot. 
Mr. Harris looks at the window. The window is closed. 
"It is a little warm in here," Mr. Harris says to himself. "I need some fresh air."
He stands up. He walks to the window. He opens the window wide. 
The fresh air comes into the room. It feels very good. It smells like flowers and green grass. Mr. Harris smiles. He takes a deep breath. 

He goes back to his desk. He sits down. He continues to write his story. 
For a long time, everything is perfect. The house is quiet. The air is fresh. The story is going well. 
But the weather outside is changing. 
The sun goes behind a big grey cloud. The sky becomes dark. The trees in the garden start to move. The leaves make a rustling sound. 
A wind is starting. 

First, the wind is gentle. It comes through the open window softly. It moves the curtains. The curtains are white. They dance a little bit in the wind. 
Mr. Harris does not notice. He is busy writing. He is thinking about his story. 

Then, the wind gets stronger. It is not gentle anymore. It is a strong, fast wind. 
It blows through the open window with a loud 'Whoosh!' 
The white curtains fly up high. They hit the wall. 
The wind hits the desk. 
Mr. Harris's papers are not heavy. They are light. The wind lifts the papers from the desk. 

"Oh no!" Mr. Harris shouts. 
One piece of paper flies into the air. Then another piece of paper flies. Soon, all his papers are flying in the room. They look like big white birds. 
They fly to the ceiling. They fly to the floor. They fly under the sofa. They fly behind the bookshelf. 

Mr. Harris jumps up from his chair. He tries to catch the papers. 
He grabs one paper from the air. He puts it on the desk. He puts a heavy book on it. 
"I must close the window!" he says. 

He runs to the window. The wind is blowing very hard now. It blows his hair into his eyes. It blows cold air into the room. It is starting to rain. Small drops of rain come through the window. 
Mr. Harris grabs the window handles. He pulls the window. It is hard to pull against the wind. But he is strong. He pulls hard. 
Bang! 
He closes the window tight. He turns the lock. 

Suddenly, the room is quiet again. The wind is outside. The rain is outside. 
But the study is a mess. 
Mr. Harris looks around his room. His papers are everywhere. 
He sighs. He is a little angry, but he is also a little amused. 
"That was a very strong wind," he says. 

He starts to collect his papers. He bends down. He picks up a paper from under the desk. He picks up a paper from the rug. He reaches behind the bookshelf and pulls out two papers. 
He finds all his papers. He puts them back on his desk. They are all mixed up. Page five is next to page two. Page ten is on top of page one. 

Mr. Harris sits down in his chair. He takes a deep breath. 
He spends the next ten minutes organizing his papers. He puts them in the right order. One, two, three, four... 
None of the papers are lost. None of the papers are wet from the rain. He is lucky. 

He looks at the closed window. He can see the rain hitting the glass. He can see the trees bending in the wind outside. 
It is cold and wet outside. But it is warm and dry inside his study. 
Mr. Harris takes his pen again. He looks at his story. 
"I will not open the window again today," he says to himself with a smile. "Fresh air is good, but flying papers are not."
He puts his head down. He starts to write again. The open window is closed, and his work continues.
