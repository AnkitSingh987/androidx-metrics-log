Title: Campfire Stories
Category: Beginner

Ben and his father walked back down the mountain much faster than they climbed up. By the time they reached the campsite, the sun was beginning to set behind the tall trees. The air temperature dropped rapidly, and the cool mountain breeze turned into a cold evening wind. 
Ben immediately put on his thick gray hoodie. 

"My legs feel like jelly," Ben groaned, collapsing into a small folding camping chair. 
"You did very well today," his father praised him. "It was a tough hike. Now, we need to build a fire before it gets completely dark, or we will freeze while we eat our dinner."

His father walked into the trees near the campsite. He returned a few minutes later carrying an armful of dry, dead branches and twigs. He placed them carefully in the metal fire ring in front of their tent. 
He built a small pyramid shape with the thin twigs in the center, and placed the thicker branches around the outside. 
"Always leave enough space for the air to flow," his father explained, striking a match. "Fire needs oxygen to breathe, just like we do."

He touched the burning match to the dry leaves at the bottom of the pyramid. The leaves caught fire quickly, crackling loudly. The small yellow flames slowly licked up the dry twigs, growing larger and hotter. Within five minutes, they had a strong, warm campfire burning brightly in the twilight. 

Ben held his cold hands out towards the orange flames. The heat felt wonderful. 
His father opened a can of beans and a packet of sausages. He cooked them in a heavy black iron pan directly over the hot flames. The smell of the cooking sausages mixed with the smell of the wood smoke, making Ben's stomach rumble loudly. 

They ate their simple dinner sitting in their folding chairs, watching the fire. The food tasted ten times better because they had worked so hard for it that day. 

When they finished eating, the sun was completely gone. The campsite was pitch black, except for the golden circle of light from their campfire. Ben looked up. Without the bright lights of the city, the night sky was spectacular. It was a deep, velvet black, completely filled with thousands of tiny, sparkling silver stars. 

"I have never seen so many stars in my entire life," Ben whispered in awe. 
"It is a beautiful sight," his father agreed, looking up. "Now, for the final part of our camping dinner."
He pulled the bag of large white marshmallows out of the food box. He handed Ben a long, thin metal stick with two prongs at the end. 

Ben pushed a soft marshmallow onto the stick. He held it carefully over the hot orange coals of the fire. 
"Don't hold it in the yellow flames, or it will burn and turn black," his father instructed, roasting his own marshmallow. "Hold it above the coals until it turns a nice, golden brown."

Ben watched his marshmallow carefully. It began to bubble slightly, turning a perfect golden color. He pulled it away, let it cool for a few seconds, and ate it. It was hot, sticky, sweet, and absolutely delicious. 

"Do you know any scary stories, Dad?" Ben asked, wiping sticky sugar from his mouth. 
His father smiled mysteriously in the firelight. The flickering shadows made his face look different. 
"I know a very old story," his father said softly. "A story about a creature that lives deep in these very woods. They call it the Shadow Walker."

Ben felt a small thrill of fear. He looked over his shoulder at the dark, silent trees beyond the campfire. 
His father told a story about a strange, tall creature that moved without making a single sound. He lowered his voice, making it deep and spooky. Just as his father reached the scariest part of the story, a branch snapped loudly in the dark woods behind them. 'CRACK!'

Ben jumped out of his chair, his heart pounding rapidly in his chest. 
His father laughed out loud. "It is just the wind, Ben, or maybe a small deer stepping on a twig. There is no Shadow Walker."
Ben laughed too, feeling a little embarrassed but very entertained. Sitting by a crackling fire, eating sweet marshmallows, and telling stories under a blanket of stars was the perfect end to a long, adventurous day.
