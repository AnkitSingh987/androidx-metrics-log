Title: The Tall Giraffes
Category: Beginner

Sam folded the colorful zoo map and put it in his pocket. He pointed down the wide, yellow path. 
"Look over there!" Sam said to his sister, Lily. 
They had walked for ten minutes from the main entrance. Now, they stood in front of a very large enclosure. It did not have a high metal fence like some of the other cages. Instead, there was a deep, wide ditch separating the visitors from the animals. 

On the other side of the ditch, the ground was covered in yellow sand and dry grass. There were several very tall trees with no branches near the bottom. 
And standing near the trees were the giraffes. 

"Wow," Lily breathed quietly. "They are so tall."
There were three giraffes in the enclosure. Two of them were adults, and one was a younger, smaller giraffe. Even the small one was much taller than a grown man. 

The family walked closer to the wooden railing and watched the amazing animals. The giraffes moved very slowly and gracefully. Their legs were long and thin. Their coats were covered in beautiful, large brown spots with light yellow lines between them. No two giraffes had exactly the same pattern of spots. 

"How tall are they, Dad?" Sam asked, taking his camera out of his bag. 
"An adult male giraffe can be more than five meters tall," his father explained. "They are the tallest land animals in the whole world. Most of their height comes from their incredibly long necks and legs."

One of the adult giraffes walked slowly toward a tall tree. It lifted its head high into the branches. The people watching could clearly see the giraffe's face. It had large, gentle brown eyes with very long, thick eyelashes. On top of its head were two small, furry horns. 

"What is it doing?" Lily asked. 
"It is eating," her mother replied. "Giraffes are herbivores. That means they only eat plants, not meat. They use their long necks to reach the fresh, green leaves at the very tops of the trees, where other animals cannot reach."

The giraffe opened its mouth. A very long, dark purple tongue reached out and wrapped around a small branch. It pulled the branch into its mouth and stripped all the green leaves off with its teeth. 
"Its tongue is purple!" Sam laughed. He quickly pressed the button on his camera. 'Click!' He took a perfect picture of the giraffe eating. 

"Yes, it is purple, and it is very long," his father said. "A giraffe's tongue can be fifty centimeters long. The dark color acts like sunscreen. It protects their tongue from getting sunburned while they eat all day in the hot African sun."

The baby giraffe trotted playfully over to the adult. It looked clumsy on its long, thin legs. It tried to reach the leaves on the tree, but it was too short. The adult giraffe dropped a few leaves from its mouth, and the baby ate them off the ground. 

Lily felt a sudden burst of affection for the gentle giants. 
"They look very peaceful," she said. "They do not make any loud noises."
"They are usually very quiet," her mother agreed. "They do not roar like lions or chatter like monkeys. They communicate with each other using very low sounds that human ears cannot even hear."

They stood by the wooden railing for another twenty minutes. They watched the giraffes eat, walk around the sandy enclosure, and swish their long tails to chase away flies. It was very relaxing to watch them. They moved with such elegance, despite their enormous size. 

"Okay, explorers," their father said eventually. "The giraffes are wonderful, but there are many other animals waiting for us. Where to next, navigator Sam?"
Sam took the map out of his pocket again. He looked at the yellow area. 
"We are still in the savanna zone," Sam announced. "If we follow this path to the left, we will find the lions."
"The lions!" Lily said, her eyes widening. "The kings of the jungle! Let's go!"

They waved goodbye to the tall, spotted giraffes. The giraffes did not wave back; they just continued pulling green leaves from the high branches with their long purple tongues. The family turned and walked down the path, ready to see the fierce and mighty lions.
