Title: My New Friend
Category: Beginner

It is a warm Saturday afternoon. The sun is shining, and there are a few white clouds in the sky. Seven-year-old Ben is at the local park with his mother. The park is a busy place on a Saturday. Many families are there. 

Ben's mother is sitting on a wooden bench under a large tree. She is reading a thick book. 
Ben is playing in the sandbox. He has a red plastic bucket and a yellow plastic spade. He is trying to build a sandcastle, but the sand is a little too dry. The walls of his castle keep falling down. 
"Oh, man," Ben says to himself. He feels a little frustrated. 

Then, he hears a voice behind him. 
"You need some water," the voice says. 
Ben turns around. There is a boy standing there. The boy looks about the same age as Ben. He has brown hair and is wearing a green T-shirt. 

"Water?" Ben asks. 
"Yes," the boy says. "If the sand is wet, it sticks together. Then you can build a very tall castle."
"But I do not have any water," Ben says. 
The boy smiles. "I have a big bottle of water. We can use some of it."
The boy runs to another bench where his father is sitting. He comes back with a large blue water bottle. 
He carefully pours a little bit of water onto the sand in Ben's bucket. 
"Now mix it," the boy says. 

Ben uses his yellow spade to mix the wet sand. It feels heavy and sticky. It feels perfect for a castle. 
He turns the bucket upside down on the ground. He taps the top of the bucket three times. Tap, tap, tap. Then he slowly lifts the red bucket. 
A perfect, solid sandcastle stands on the ground. 

"Wow!" Ben says. "It worked! Thank you."
"You are welcome," the boy says. He sits down in the sandbox next to Ben. "My name is Sam. What is your name?"
"My name is Ben," Ben replies. "Do you want to help me build a bigger castle? We can make a wall around it."
"Yes, I would love to," Sam says. 

The two boys start working together. They use the wet sand to build a long wall. They use small stones they find in the grass to make windows for the castle. They work quietly for a few minutes, focused on their building. 

"Do you come to this park often?" Sam asks. 
"Yes, I come here every Saturday with my mother," Ben says. "She likes to sit on that bench and read." He points to his mother. 
Sam looks over. "My father is sitting on that bench over there. He is looking at his phone."
"Do you live near the park?" Ben asks. 
"No, I live on Oak Street. It is a ten-minute walk," Sam explains. 
"I live on Maple Street!" Ben says excitedly. "That is very close to Oak Street."

"Really? Maybe we can play together again," Sam says. 
"That would be great," Ben says. "I have a new football at home. We could bring it to the park next week."
"I love football," Sam says. "I am not very good at kicking, but I can run very fast."
"I can teach you how to kick," Ben offers. "My older brother taught me."

They finish the wall around the castle. It looks very impressive. 
"We need a flag for the top," Sam suggests. 
He stands up and walks to a nearby bush. He finds a small, straight stick and a green leaf. He pushes the stick through the leaf. He brings it back to the sandbox and pushes the stick into the top of the sandcastle. 
"Perfect," Ben says. 

They sit back and look at their work. They are both very dirty. They have sand on their hands, their knees, and even on their faces. But they do not care. They are very happy. 

"Sam! It is time to go home!" a man calls out. It is Sam's father. 
Sam stands up and brushes the sand off his trousers. 
"I have to go now," Sam says. 
"Okay," Ben says. He stands up too. 
"Will you be here next Saturday?" Sam asks. 
"Yes, I will bring my football," Ben promises. 

"Great. I will see you then, Ben," Sam says. He smiles and waves his hand. 
"Goodbye, Sam," Ben says, waving back. 

Ben watches his new friend run to his father. He turns back to look at the impressive sandcastle. It is much better than the one he tried to build alone. 
He runs over to his mother's bench. 
"Mother, look at my castle!" he says loudly. 
His mother puts her book down. She looks at the sandbox. "That is a very big castle, Ben. Did you build it all by yourself?"
"No," Ben says proudly. "I built it with my new friend, Sam. And we are going to play football next week."
His mother smiles warmly. "That is wonderful, Ben. It is always nice to make a new friend."
