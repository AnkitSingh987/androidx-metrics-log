Title: A Summer Camping Trip
Category: Beginner

It was the beginning of August, and the weather was incredibly hot. In the city, the air felt heavy and uncomfortable. 
Fourteen-year-old Ben was sitting on the floor of his bedroom, trying to stay cool in front of a small electric fan. His father walked into the room, holding a large, dusty green backpack. 

"I cannot stand this city heat anymore," his father announced, dropping the heavy backpack onto the bed. "I think it is time for us to escape to the mountains. How do you feel about a weekend camping trip?"

Ben sat up straight. A camping trip sounded fantastic. He loved being outdoors, away from the noise and the concrete of the city. 
"I think that is a brilliant idea," Ben agreed enthusiastically. "Where should we go? Should we go to Crystal Lake?"
"I was thinking about Pine Mountain," his father suggested. "It is higher up, so the air will be much cooler. And there are some excellent hiking trails we haven't explored yet. What do you think?"
"Pine Mountain sounds perfect," Ben said. "When are we leaving?"
"Tomorrow morning, very early," his father replied. "Which means we have a lot of packing to do today. We must be organized. If you forget something important in the mountains, you cannot just walk to a supermarket to buy it."

They walked down to the garage to gather their camping gear. Ben's father was very serious about preparation. He opened a large plastic box and pulled out a rolled-up green tent. 
"We haven't used this tent since last summer," he said, inspecting the material carefully. "We must make sure there are no holes in it, and we must check that we have all the metal poles and pegs. A tent without poles is just an expensive blanket."

Ben found his blue sleeping bag and a thick foam mat. Sleeping on the hard ground was uncomfortable, so the foam mat was essential for a good night's sleep. 
"Don't forget to pack warm clothes," his father reminded him. "It is very hot here in the city, but the temperature drops significantly in the mountains at night. You will need a thick sweater and long trousers."

"I know, Dad," Ben said, carrying his sleeping bag into the house. He went to his bedroom and started packing his clothes. He packed t-shirts and shorts for the daytime, but he also packed his warmest gray hoodie and thick socks for the evenings. 

Meanwhile, Ben's mother was in the kitchen preparing the food. She was not going on the camping trip. She preferred reading books in a comfortable chair, not sleeping on the ground. But she wanted to make sure Ben and his father had plenty of delicious food. 
"I am packing sausages and beans for your dinner tomorrow," she told them as they walked into the kitchen. "And I bought a bag of large marshmallows. You cannot have a campfire without roasting marshmallows."

"Thank you, Mom," Ben smiled. "You are the best."
By eight o'clock in the evening, everything was packed. The large green backpack was full of clothes, sleeping bags, and flashlights. The food was packed in a cool box with ice. The green tent was waiting by the front door. 

Ben went to bed early. He listened to the traffic outside his window, but in his mind, he was already imagining the quiet rustle of pine trees and the smell of a wood fire. He was very excited to leave the hot city behind and spend a weekend living in nature.
