Title: The Meeting I Was Nervous About

Category: Intermediate

Leo woke up early on Thursday morning. The sun was not even up yet, but he could not sleep. His heart was beating fast. Today was a very important day at his job. He worked as a junior architect at a busy firm in the city center. For the last six months, he had worked on a design for a new community center. It was a beautiful building with large windows and a green roof. However, until today, his boss, Mr. Henderson, had always presented their designs to the clients.

"Today is your turn, Leo," Mr. Henderson had said the day before. "You know this project better than anyone else. I want you to show it to the city council."

Leo was terrified. He was a quiet person who loved drawing and planning, but he hated speaking in front of a crowd. When he thought about the ten important members of the city council looking at him, his hands started to sweat.

He got out of bed and went to the kitchen to make some coffee. While he waited for the water to boil, he practiced his speech. "Good morning, ladies and gentlemen," he said to the empty kitchen. "Thank you for coming today. I am excited to show you our vision for the new community center." He cleared his throat. His voice sounded thin and nervous. He tried again, speaking louder this time.

After breakfast, Leo put on his best dark blue suit and a light blue tie. He looked at himself in the mirror. He looked professional, but his face was pale. He took a deep breath. "You can do this," he whispered to himself.

When Leo arrived at the office, it was only 8:00 AM. The meeting was scheduled for 10:00 AM. The office was quiet. He went to his desk and opened his laptop to review his presentation one last time. He checked every slide. The pictures were clear, the numbers were correct, and the colors looked great. But what if they asked a question he could not answer? What if he forgot his words?

At 9:30 AM, Mr. Henderson walked into the office. He looked relaxed and confident, as always. "How are you feeling, Leo?" he asked, putting a hand on Leo's shoulder.

"I am a little nervous, to be honest," Leo replied, smiling weakly.

"That is completely normal," Mr. Henderson said. "A little nervousness is good. It keeps you focused. Just remember that you did the work. You know every detail of this building. If they ask a difficult question, take your time, breathe, and give an honest answer. If you don't know something, just say you will check and let them know later. You don't have to be perfect."

Leo nodded. His boss's words made him feel a bit better.

At 9:45 AM, they walked to the large conference room at the end of the hall. The room had a huge glass table and comfortable black chairs. Leo connected his laptop to the projector. The first slide of his presentation appeared on the large screen on the wall. It showed a beautiful picture of the community center surrounded by trees.

Exactly at 10:00 AM, the doors opened. The members of the city council walked in. They were older men and women, wearing expensive business clothes. They looked serious. The most important person was Mrs. Gable, the head of the council. She had a strict face and carried a thick notebook.

"Good morning, everyone," Mr. Henderson said with a warm smile. "Thank you for joining us. Today, our talented architect, Leo, will present the design for the new community center. He has worked very hard on this project."

Everyone looked at Leo. The room was completely silent. Leo stood up. His legs felt like jelly. He looked at Mrs. Gable, who was already holding her pen, ready to take notes.

"Good... Good morning," Leo started. His voice was a little shaky. He cleared his throat and tried to remember his practice in the kitchen. "Good morning, ladies and gentlemen. Thank you for coming today. I am excited to show you our vision for the new community center."

He clicked the button to change the slide. He started to explain the main idea of the building. For the first two minutes, he spoke too quickly. He forgot to pause. But as he looked at his drawings, something changed. He stopped thinking about the people in the room and started thinking about the project. He loved this design. He knew why he chose every material and why every window was placed in a specific spot.

His voice became stronger and more confident. "As you can see on this slide," Leo said smoothly, "we designed a large open space on the first floor. This area will have a cafe and comfortable seats. We want people to feel welcome as soon as they walk through the doors."

He looked at the council members. A few of them were nodding. Mrs. Gable was looking at the screen with interest.

"On the roof," Leo continued, "we included a community garden. This not only makes the building look beautiful, but it also helps the environment by reducing the heat in the summer."

After twenty minutes, Leo finished his presentation. "Thank you for your attention," he said. "Are there any questions?"

Immediately, Mrs. Gable raised her hand. "The design is very modern," she said. Her voice was sharp. "But I am worried about the cost. These large glass windows look very expensive. Will this project stay within our budget?"

Leo felt a moment of panic, but he remembered his boss's advice. He took a deep breath. "That is an excellent question, Mrs. Gable," he said calmly. He clicked to a different slide that showed a chart of costs. "The glass we plan to use is a special energy-saving material. It is a bit more expensive to buy, but it will save the city a lot of money on heating and cooling in the future. Over ten years, the building will actually cost less than a traditional brick building."

Mrs. Gable looked at the chart for a few seconds. Then, her strict face relaxed into a small smile. "I see," she said. "That is a very smart approach. Thank you."

For the next ten minutes, the other members asked more questions. They asked about the parking lot, the materials for the floors, and the size of the meeting rooms. Because Leo had spent six months thinking about these exact things, he answered every question easily. The conversation became friendly and natural. He was no longer a terrified young man; he was an expert explaining his work.

Finally, Mrs. Gable closed her notebook. "Well, Leo," she said, "I must say, I am very impressed. The design is thoughtful, and your presentation was very clear. I think this community center will be a wonderful addition to our city."

"Thank you very much, Mrs. Gable," Leo said. He felt a huge wave of relief and happiness wash over him.

The council members shook hands with Leo and Mr. Henderson before they left the room. When the door closed behind the last person, Mr. Henderson turned to Leo and smiled broadly.

"Excellent job, Leo," he said. "You handled the questions perfectly. I knew you could do it."

Leo sat down in a chair and let out a long breath. He felt tired but incredibly proud. The meeting he had been so nervous about was over, and it had been a great success. He realized that the fear before the meeting was much worse than the meeting itself. He had faced his fear, trusted his knowledge, and succeeded.

"Thank you, Mr. Henderson," Leo said, packing up his laptop. "I think I am actually looking forward to the next one."
