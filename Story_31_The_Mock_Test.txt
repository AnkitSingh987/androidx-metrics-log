Title: The Mock Test

Category: Intermediate

Leo looked at the clock on the wall of the library. It was exactly two o’clock on a Saturday afternoon. Most of his friends were probably relaxing at the park or playing video games. However, Leo was sitting at a large wooden table with a thick book open in front of him. In two weeks, he had to take a very important English exam. If he passed, he could apply for a special study program in London.

"Are you ready?" asked Maya. Maya was his classmate and his study partner. She was usually very calm and organized.

"I don't think so," Leo replied, rubbing his eyes. "I studied all the grammar rules, and I memorized a lot of new vocabulary. But I am still very nervous."

"That is completely normal," Maya said with a warm smile. "That is why we are taking this mock test today. It is just practice. It will help us understand what the real exam feels like."

A mock test is a practice exam. It has the same types of questions, and you have to finish it in the same amount of time as the real exam.

"Okay, let's do this," Leo said. He took a deep breath and picked up his pencil.

Maya set a timer on her phone for exactly ninety minutes. "Three, two, one, start," she whispered.

Leo opened the test booklet. The first section was Reading Comprehension. He read the first paragraph of the text. It was about the history of coffee in Europe. The words were not too difficult, but the sentences were very long. He read the first question, but he could not find the answer easily. He read the paragraph again. Three minutes had already passed. Panic started to grow in his stomach.

"Come on, Leo, focus," he thought to himself. He decided to skip the first question and move to the second one. This one was easier. He marked his answer and continued.

The next section was Listening. Since they were in the library, they had to use headphones. Leo pressed play on his audio file. A woman with a fast British accent started talking about a museum exhibition. Leo tried to write down notes, but she was speaking too quickly. He missed the answer to the third question. He felt a drop of sweat on his forehead.

"If I cannot understand this, I will never survive in London," he worried.

The final section was Writing. He had to write an essay about the benefits of public transportation. He knew many things about this topic, but his mind went completely blank. He stared at the empty lines on the paper. Ten minutes passed, and he had only written one sentence. He looked at Maya. She was writing quickly, and her paper was already half full.

Suddenly, the timer on Maya’s phone made a soft sound. Time was up.

Leo dropped his pencil. He felt completely exhausted, as if he had run a marathon. He looked at his answer sheet. There were many empty spaces, especially in the writing section.

"How was it?" Maya asked, taking off her headphones.

"It was a disaster," Leo said quietly. "It was much harder than I expected. I did not finish the reading section, I missed several listening questions, and my essay is terrible. I am going to fail the real exam."

Maya shook her head. "Leo, you need to change your perspective. The purpose of a mock test is not to get a perfect score. The purpose is to find your weaknesses."

"Well, I found them," Leo sighed. "My weakness is everything."

"That is not true," Maya said. She pulled her chair closer to his. "Let's check our answers together. We can learn from our mistakes."

They spent the next hour going through the test. When they checked the reading section, Leo realized that he actually got most of the questions right. The problem was not his English skills; the problem was his time management.

"You spent too much time on the difficult questions," Maya explained. "In a real exam, you should answer the easy questions first. If you don't know an answer, guess and move on. You can always come back to it later if you have time."

Leo nodded. "That makes sense. I panicked when I could not understand the very first question, and it ruined my concentration."

Then, they looked at the listening section. Maya showed him her notes. She did not write full sentences. She only wrote keywords, like dates, names, and places.

"You were trying to write down everything the speaker said," Maya told him. "That is impossible. You just need to listen for the specific information that answers the questions. Before the audio starts, always read the questions carefully. That way, you know what to listen for."

"I see," Leo said. He felt a little better. His mistakes were not because he was bad at English. They were because he was using the wrong strategies.

Finally, they looked at his essay. It was only three sentences long.

"Why did you stop writing?" Maya asked.

"I wanted to write a perfect introduction," Leo admitted. "I was trying to think of advanced vocabulary and complex grammar structures. But I could not think of anything good, so I got stuck."

Maya smiled. "A simple, clear essay is always better than a blank page, Leo. You don't need to write like a famous author. You just need to answer the prompt and organize your ideas clearly. Next time, spend five minutes planning your essay before you start writing. Make a quick outline. If you know what you want to say, the words will come much faster."

Leo leaned back in his chair and looked at his messy test paper. He was still disappointed with his performance, but he didn't feel hopeless anymore.

"You know what?" Leo said. "I am actually glad I took this mock test today. If I made all these mistakes in the real exam, it would be a disaster. But now, I know exactly what I need to practice for the next two weeks."

"Exactly!" Maya said happily. "You have two weeks to practice time management, taking better notes, and planning your essays. You have plenty of time to improve."

"Are you busy next Saturday?" Leo asked.

"No, I am free," Maya replied.

"Great," Leo said, packing his books into his backpack. "Because we are going to take another mock test. And next time, I will be ready."

For the rest of the week, Leo changed the way he studied. He stopped memorizing lists of random words. Instead, he practiced reading articles with a timer. He learned how to read quickly to find the main idea. He also listened to English podcasts while walking to the university, and he practiced taking short, quick notes.

When Saturday arrived, Leo and Maya returned to the same table in the library. They brought another mock test.

"Ready?" Maya asked, holding her phone.

"Ready," Leo said.

This time, when he opened the booklet, he did not panic. When he found a difficult reading question, he skipped it and moved on. When the listening audio started, he closed his eyes for a moment to focus, and then he listened only for the keywords. When it was time to write the essay, he spent five minutes drawing a small map of his ideas before he wrote the first sentence.

When the timer rang, Leo put down his pencil and smiled. He had finished every section. It was not perfect, and he knew he had probably made some mistakes. But the feeling of total panic was gone. He felt confident, prepared, and ready for the real challenge. The mock test had served its purpose.
