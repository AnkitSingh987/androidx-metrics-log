Title: Our First Conversation
Category: Beginner

It is a grey and rainy morning in the city. The streets are wet, and the cars are driving slowly. 
A young woman named Sarah is standing at a bus stop. She is holding a large red umbrella to stay dry. She is wearing a warm winter coat. She is waiting for the number 12 bus to take her to her office. 

A few minutes later, a young man walks to the bus stop. His name is Paul. He is also holding an umbrella, but his umbrella is black. He stands under the small roof of the bus stop. He looks at his watch. He sighs. 

Sarah looks at Paul. Paul looks at Sarah. They do not know each other. They are strangers. But they are both waiting for the same bus, and the bus is late. 

Sarah decides to be polite. She smiles a small smile. 
"Good morning," she says. Her voice is friendly. 
Paul is a little surprised, but he smiles back. 
"Good morning," he replies. 

There is a short silence. They listen to the sound of the rain hitting the roof. Pitter, patter, pitter, patter. 
"The weather is very bad today," Sarah says. This is a common way to start a conversation in English. Everyone talks about the weather. 
"Yes, it is terrible," Paul agrees. "It is very cold and wet. I do not like the rain."
"I don't mind the rain if I am inside," Sarah says. "But waiting for a bus in the rain is not fun."

"Is the number 12 bus usually late?" Paul asks. 
"Sometimes," Sarah answers. "If the traffic is heavy, the bus is late. And the traffic is always heavy when it rains."
Paul nods his head. "I understand. I am new to this city. I do not know the bus schedules very well."

Sarah looks at him with interest. "Oh, you are new here? Welcome to the city. Where are you from?"
"Thank you," Paul says. "I am from a small town in the north. I moved here last week for a new job."
"That is exciting," Sarah says. "What is your job?"
"I am a computer programmer," Paul explains. "I work for a bank in the city center. What about you? What do you do?"
"I am a nurse," Sarah says. "I work at the City Hospital. I am on my way there now."

"A nurse is a very important job," Paul says with respect. "You help people."
"Thank you," Sarah says. She is pleased. "I like my job very much. It is hard work, but it is rewarding."

They talk for a few more minutes. The conversation is easy and natural. They ask simple questions and give clear answers. It is good practice for Paul, who is still a little nervous in the big city. 

"Do you like living in the city so far?" Sarah asks. 
"It is very busy," Paul says honestly. "And it is very noisy. My small town was very quiet. But there are many things to do here. There are many restaurants and museums."
"Yes, there is always something to do," Sarah agrees. "You must visit the National Museum. It is fantastic."
"I will," Paul says. "Thank you for the recommendation."

Suddenly, they hear a loud noise. They look down the street. A big, red bus is coming through the rain. It is the number 12 bus. 
"Finally," Paul says. "The bus is here."
"Yes," Sarah says. She closes her red umbrella and shakes off the water. Paul closes his black umbrella. 

The bus stops in front of them. The doors open with a 'hiss' sound. 
"After you," Paul says politely. 
"Thank you," Sarah says. She steps onto the bus and shows her ticket to the driver. Paul follows her and pays for his ticket. 

The bus is crowded, so they cannot sit together. Sarah finds a seat near the front. Paul finds a seat near the back. 
As Paul walks past Sarah's seat, he stops for a second. 
"It was very nice talking to you," he says. "Have a good day at the hospital."
Sarah smiles warmly. "It was nice talking to you too, Paul. Good luck with your new job. Have a great day."

They sit in their seats as the bus drives through the rainy city. They are strangers again, going to different places. But for ten minutes at a cold bus stop, they shared a pleasant conversation. They practiced their English, they learned a little about each other, and they made the waiting time feel much shorter. A simple 'good morning' can be the start of a very nice moment.
