Title: A Long Ride
Category: Beginner

Sunday morning was bright and clear. The summer sun was already warm by nine o'clock. David stood in his driveway, holding the handlebars of his shining red mountain bike. He wore a blue helmet, a comfortable t-shirt, and sports shoes. He was ready for his first real ride. 

His father walked out of the house, pushing his own silver bicycle. 
"Are you ready for a test drive?" his father asked, strapping on his helmet. 
"I am ready," David said confidently. He threw his leg over the seat and pushed off the ground. 

As soon as his feet hit the pedals, David felt a surge of joy. The bicycle moved effortlessly. The newly oiled chain was completely silent. The gears shifted smoothly with a quiet 'click'. The tires rolled fast over the smooth pavement of their street. 
"It works!" David shouted over his shoulder. "It is perfect!"

They rode out of their neighborhood and headed towards the edge of town. There was a long, paved bicycle path that followed the river for many miles. It was a popular place for cyclists and runners because there were no cars allowed. 

They reached the start of the river path. The water sparkled in the morning light, and tall green trees lined the banks. 
"Let's go faster," David suggested. He felt strong and full of energy. 
He pushed harder on the pedals. His father stayed right behind him. 
David loved the feeling of speed. The warm summer wind rushed past his face, blowing his t-shirt against his chest. He watched the trees blur past him. He felt like he was flying just above the ground. 

They rode for an hour without stopping. They passed other cyclists who waved and said 'good morning'. They passed families walking dogs. The river path was wide and flat, making the riding easy and enjoyable. 

Eventually, David's legs began to burn a little bit. His breathing was heavy, and he was sweating. 
"Do you want to take a break?" his father called out from behind him. 
David nodded and slowed down. He squeezed the brake handles gently, and the bicycle came to a smooth, controlled stop. They pulled off the path and rested their bicycles against a large wooden fence. 

His father handed David a bottle of water. David drank deeply. The cold water felt fantastic in his dry throat. 
"How does the bike feel?" his father asked, taking a drink from his own bottle. 
"It feels incredible," David said, wiping the sweat from his forehead. "It is so fast. And the brakes are very strong. I feel very safe on it."
"You did a great job fixing it," his father praised him. "It is a totally different machine now."

They rested by the fence for ten minutes, looking at the calm blue river. David felt a deep sense of satisfaction. When he was bored on the front steps just a few days ago, he never imagined he would be out here, miles from home, riding a bicycle he had repaired himself. The hard work in the dirty garage was completely worth it for this feeling of freedom. 

"Okay, my legs are rested," David announced, putting his water bottle back into the holder on the bike frame. "Let's keep going. I want to see how far the path goes."
"It goes for another ten miles," his father laughed. "But we have to ride back home, remember. We cannot go too far today."

They got back on their bicycles and continued down the path. They didn't ride as fast this time. They rode side by side, talking and enjoying the beautiful scenery. David listened to the quiet hum of his tires on the pavement. He loved his new hobby. Cycling wasn't just about going fast; it was about exploring new places and feeling strong. 
As they followed the curve of the river, David smiled. He knew he was going to ride his red bicycle every single day for the rest of the summer holidays.
