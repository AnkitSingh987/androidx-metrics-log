Title: Learning to Fix It
Category: Beginner

The next morning, David woke up early. He didn't even turn on the television. He ate a fast breakfast and went straight to the garage. The red bicycle was waiting for him, propped up against the wall. 
His father came into the garage a few minutes later, holding a white plastic bag from the hardware store. 

"I bought the supplies we need," his father announced, placing the bag on the workbench. "New inner tubes, brake cables, chain cleaner, and chain oil. Are you ready to get dirty?"
"Yes, I am," David said eagerly. He put on an old pair of jeans and an old t-shirt that he didn't mind ruining. 

"Okay, lesson number one," his father said. He opened his big red toolbox. It was full of shiny metal tools of all shapes and sizes. "This is a wrench. We use it to loosen nuts and bolts. We need to take the wheels off first."
His father showed David how to attach the wrench to the nut on the front wheel. 
"Now, push down hard," his father instructed. 
David pushed with all his weight. The nut was very tight because it had been there for five years. Finally, it squeaked loudly and turned. David loosened it completely and pulled the front wheel off the frame. He felt a small sense of accomplishment. 

They took the old, flat rubber tires off the metal wheels. They pulled out the old inner tubes, which were full of holes. David pumped a little air into the new inner tubes and carefully placed them inside the tires. Using strong thumbs and a plastic tool, they pushed the tires back onto the wheels. 
Then, David used a bicycle pump to fill the new tires with air. He watched the flat rubber slowly expand until it was round and hard. 
"Perfect," his father said, squeezing the tire. "Step one is finished."

"What is next?" David asked. His hands were already black with dirt, but he didn't care. 
"Next is the nasty job," his father laughed. "The chain."

The chain was covered in a thick paste of black grease and dirt. It did not move smoothly at all. 
His father gave David a stiff wire brush and a spray bottle full of strong-smelling cleaner. 
"Spray the cleaner on the chain, and scrub it hard with the brush," his father directed. "You have to remove all the old, sticky dirt before we can put new, clean oil on it."

David sprayed the strong liquid. It smelled like chemicals and lemon. He took the wire brush and scrubbed the metal chain. The black dirt began to melt away, dripping onto an old newspaper they had placed on the floor. He scrubbed for twenty minutes. His arm ached, and his face was sweaty. 
When he finished, he used an old rag to wipe the chain dry. 
"Wow," David said in surprise. "The chain is actually silver. I thought it was black metal."
"Good job," his father praised him. "Now, we add the new oil."
David carefully squeezed small drops of clear oil onto every link of the silver chain. When he turned the pedals, the chain moved silently and smoothly. It was a huge improvement. 

For the rest of the afternoon, they worked on the brakes. His father showed David how to pull the old, stiff metal wires out of the plastic tubes. They pushed shiny, new wires in their place. They adjusted the brake pads so they clamped tightly onto the wheels when David squeezed the handles. 

"Try the brakes now," his father said. 
David stood over the bicycle and pushed it forward. He squeezed the brake handles tightly. The bicycle stopped instantly. The brakes worked perfectly. 

"I think we are finished mechanically," his father said, wiping his oily hands on a rag. "Now for the fun part."
He handed David a bucket of warm, soapy water and a large sponge. 
David washed the bicycle from top to bottom. He washed away five years of dust and dirt. He washed the wheels, the pedals, and the bright red frame. 

When he rinsed the soap away with a hose, the bicycle sparkled in the afternoon sun outside the garage. It looked completely different from the broken machine they bought yesterday. The tires were full and hard. The chain was silver and quiet. The red paint was bright and shiny. 
David felt an incredible wave of pride. He had fixed this machine with his own hands. He had learned how to use a wrench, how to change a tire, and how to oil a chain. 
"It looks amazing," David said softly. 
"It does," his father agreed. "And tomorrow, we will see how it rides."
