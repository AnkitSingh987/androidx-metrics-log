Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Waffle

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. He loved to eat soft waffles for breakfast. One day, his mom made him a big, soft waffle. Tim was very happy. Tim saw his little sister, Sue, playing with her toy. He wanted to play too, but Sue said no. Tim was angry and took her toy away. Sue started to cry. Their mom heard the cry and came to see what happened. Mom saw Sue crying and Tim with the toy. She knew Tim was not nice to his sister. Mom decided to punish Tim. She took away his soft waffle and gave it to Sue. Tim was sad and learned that being mean to others is not good. From that day, Tim always shared and was nice to his sister. The moral of the story is to be kind and share with others.

Mia and Ben were twins who loved chocolate. One day, their mom gave them a big bar of chocolate to share. She said, "You can eat this after lunch, but you have to be good and play nicely." Mia and Ben nodded and ran to their room. They put the chocolate on their bed and looked at it. It was shiny and brown and smelled sweet. They wanted to eat it right away, but they knew they had to wait. They decided to play with their toys. Mia picked up her doll and Ben picked up his car. They made noises and stories with them. They had fun and forgot about the chocolate for a while. But then, Ben saw the chocolate again. He felt hungry and curious. He said to Mia, "Can we have a little bite? Just a little one. Mom won't know." Mia shook her head. She said, "No, Ben. We have to remember what mom said. We have to be good and play nicely. If we eat the chocolate now, we will be naughty and sad." Ben frowned. He said, "But I want some chocolate. It looks so yummy. Please, Mia. Just a little bite." Mia said, "No, Ben. We have to wait. If we wait, we will be happy and proud. And the chocolate will taste even better." Ben thought about it. He knew Mia was right. He said, "Okay, Mia. You are smart. We will wait. Let's play some more." Mia smiled. She said, "Okay, Ben. You are kind. Let's play some more." They put the chocolate away and played with their toys again. They were peaceful and good. They remembered what mom said. They waited for lunch and then they ate the chocolate. It was delicious and they shared it. They hugged each other and said, "We love chocolate and we love mom and we love each other."

Sara and Ben are twins. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big truck with many animals. The truck says "Zoo". Sara and Ben want to see the animals. They run to the truck and peek inside. They see a lion, a monkey, a zebra and a snake. The lion is sleeping. The monkey is jumping. The zebra is eating. The snake is hissing. "Wow, look at the animals!" Sara says. "They are so cool!" Ben says. But then, the snake sees Sara and Ben. It is angry. It wants to escape. It pushes the door of the truck with its tail. The door opens. The snake slithers out. "Help! The snake is out!" Sara screams. "Run, run, run!" Ben shouts. Sara and Ben run away from the snake. They are scared. The snake chases them. It is fast. It wants to bite them. But then, a man sees the snake. He is the zookeeper. He has a net. He throws the net over the snake. He catches the snake. "Got you, you naughty snake!" the zookeeper says. He puts the snake back in the truck. He closes the door. He locks it. "Are you okay, kids?" the zookeeper asks Sara and Ben. "Yes, we are okay. Thank you, mister," Sara says. "That was a close one. You should not go near the truck. The animals are not for playing. They are for the zoo. They can be dangerous," the zookeeper says. "Sorry, mister. We were curious. We wanted to see the animals," Ben says. "I understand. But you have to be careful. The animals are not like your toys. They have their own feelings and needs. They can be weird sometimes. They can gain or lose their temper. They can escape or attack. You have to respect them," the zookeeper says. "We will, mister. We will respect the animals. We will not go near the truck again," Sara and Ben say. "Good. Now, go back to your parents. They must be worried. And have fun in the park. But stay safe," the zookeeper says. Sara and Ben nod. They run back to their parents. They hug them. They tell them what happened. Their parents are glad they are okay. They are also proud of them. They learned a lesson. They learned to respect the animals. They learned to be careful. They learned to be brave.
