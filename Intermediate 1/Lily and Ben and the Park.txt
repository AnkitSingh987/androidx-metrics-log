Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily and Ben and the Park

Lily and Ben were playing in the park. They liked to search for bugs and flowers. They had a jar to keep their finds. "Look, Lily, I found a ladybug!" Ben said, holding up the jar. "It is red and black and has spots." "Wow, Ben, it is so pretty!" Lily said, looking at the jar. "Can I hold it?" "Sure, Lily, but be gentle. It is a living thing." Ben said, handing her the jar. Lily took the jar and opened the lid. She put her hand inside and let the ladybug crawl on her finger. She smiled and brought it closer to her face. "Hello, little ladybug. You are so cute. Can I give you a kiss?" Lily asked, puckering her lips. Ben watched her and giggled. "Lily, you are silly. You can't kiss a bug. It might bite you." Lily shook her head. "No, Ben, it won't bite me. It likes me. See, it is tickling me." She said, feeling the ladybug's legs on her skin. She leaned forward and kissed the ladybug gently. Then she put it back in the jar and closed the lid. "Did you like my kiss, little ladybug?" Lily asked, tapping the jar. The ladybug did not answer. It just moved around in the jar. Lily and Ben decided to look for more bugs and flowers. They had a lot of fun in the park. They also found a butterfly, a worm, and a daisy. They put them all in the jar and took them home. At home, they showed their mom their jar. She smiled and praised them for their search. "That is a very nice jar, Lily and Ben. You found some beautiful and interesting things. But you know what? You can't keep them in the jar for too long. They need air and water and food. They belong in the park, not in the jar." She said, gently. Lily and Ben nodded. They understood. They loved their bugs and flowers, but they did not want to hurt them. "Can we take them back to the park tomorrow, mom?" Lily asked, hopefully. "Of course, Lily. Tomorrow, we can take them back to the park and let them go. They will be happy to be free. And you can say goodbye to them and thank them for letting you see them." Mom said, kindly. Lily and Ben agreed. They hugged their mom and kissed her cheek. "Thank you, mom. You are the best. And you are delicious." Lily said, sweetly. Mom laughed and kissed them back. "You are welcome, Lily. And you are delicious, too. And so are you, Ben. You are both my delicious kids." Mom said, lovingly. They all smiled and cuddled. They were a happy family.

One day, a silly dancer named Lily went to the park. She loved to dance and make people smile. As she danced, she saw a big tree with a small cat stuck in it. The cat was scared and wanted to get down. Lily had an idea. She said to the cat, "Don't worry, I will help you! I will dance and make the tree shake. Then you can jump down." The cat looked at her and said, "Okay, but please be careful." Lily started to dance near the tree. She danced and danced, but the tree did not shake. She thought, "I must try harder!" So, she danced even more. Finally, the tree shook a little, and the cat jumped down. The cat was happy and thanked Lily. From that day on, they became the best of friends. And whenever Lily danced, the cat would be there to watch and cheer her on.

Once upon a time, there was a cheerful boy named Tim. Tim had a toy model car that he loved very much. One day, the car broke and Tim was very sad. Tim's mom saw him crying and asked, "Why are you sad, Tim?" Tim showed her the broken car and said, "My car is broken, Mom." His mom wanted to help restore the car to make Tim happy again. They tried to fix the car, but it was too hard for them. Tim's mom said, "I'm sorry, Tim. We can't fix your car." Tim was still sad, but he learned that sometimes things can't be fixed. The moral value of the story is to be careful with the things you love.
