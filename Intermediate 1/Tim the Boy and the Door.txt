Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Door

One day, a little boy named Tim went for a walk. He saw a big orange door with a shiny lock on it. Tim felt curious and wanted to know what was behind the door. Tim tried to open the door, but it was locked. He looked around and saw a girl named Sue. Tim asked, "Do you know how to open the lock?" Sue said, "Yes, I have the key, but I lost it in the park." Tim and Sue went to look for the key in the park. They looked under the trees and near the swings. They found the key near the slide. Tim and Sue were happy. They went back to the orange door and unlocked the lock. The door opened, and they found a beautiful garden with colorful flowers. Tim and Sue played together in the garden, and they became good friends.

Ben and Sam were playing with their toy cars in the yard. They liked to make them go fast and crash into each other. Sometimes they pretended they were cops and robbers, and chased each other around. "Stop, robber, or I'll shoot!" Ben shouted, holding a stick like a gun. "Never, cop, you'll never catch me!" Sam said, running away with his car. Ben followed him, but he did not see the big puddle of water in front of him. He stepped into it and slipped. He fell down and got wet all over. His car also fell into the water and got dirty. "Ha ha, I got away!" Sam said, turning around and seeing Ben on the ground. "You lose, cop, you have to surrender!" "No, I don't!" Ben said, feeling angry and sad. He tried to get up, but he slipped again. He started to cry. Sam laughed and ran to get his car. He did not help Ben or say sorry. He was a mean robber. Ben cried louder and louder. He wanted his mom to come and help him. He wanted his car back. He wanted Sam to be nice. But no one came. He was alone and wet and cold. He wished he had not played with Sam. He wished he had a better friend. He wished he had a better day.

Sara likes to write stories. She writes with a big pencil and a book. She writes about animals and fairies and friends. She writes every day after school. One day, Sara's aunt comes to visit. Sara's aunt is nice, but she talks a lot. She talks to Sara's mom and dad and Sara. She asks Sara many questions. She asks Sara what she likes to do, what she learns at school, what she wants to be when she grows up. Sara feels uncomfortable. She does not like to talk so much. She wants to write her stories. She tries to be polite, but she is bored. She wishes her aunt would go away. "Sara, do you want to show me your stories?" her aunt asks. "I love to read stories. Maybe you can read them to me." Sara does not want to show her stories. She does not want to read them to her aunt. She thinks her aunt will not understand them. She thinks her aunt will laugh at them. She thinks her aunt will tell her to write something else. "No, thank you," Sara says. "I don't want to show you my stories. They are mine. I write them for me." Sara's aunt looks surprised. She looks sad. She says, "Oh, I see. Well, maybe another time. I'm sorry, Sara. I didn't mean to upset you. I just wanted to share your stories." Sara feels bad. She does not want to hurt her aunt's feelings. She does not want to be rude. She likes her aunt, but she likes her stories more. She does not know what to say. She looks at her mom and dad. They look at her. They look at her aunt. They look uncomfortable too. They do not know what to say either.

Once upon a time, in a calm little town, there was a silver cat named Fluffy. Fluffy was very sleepy, so she let out a big yawn. She was looking for a nice place to take a nap. Fluffy saw a big tree with a cozy spot to sleep. She climbed up the tree and found a bird named Chirpy sitting on a branch. Chirpy said, "Hi Fluffy, why are you yawning so much?" Fluffy replied, "I am very sleepy and want to nap here." Chirpy was a kind bird and said, "You can nap here, but promise me you will not chase me or my friends when you wake up." Fluffy agreed and said, "I promise, Chirpy." They both became good friends. Fluffy took a long nap and when she woke up, she saw Chirpy and his friends playing. Fluffy remembered her promise and did not chase them. Instead, they all played together and had lots of fun. The moral of the story is to always keep your promises and be kind to others.
