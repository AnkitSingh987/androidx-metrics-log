Category: Intermediate
Title: The Fairy and the Rabbit

Once upon a time there was a fairy who lived in a deep forest. All of the animals in the deep forest would go to the fairy's house to ask for help. One day, a little rabbit hopped to the fairy's house and was asking for help. The fairy offered to help the little rabbit. She offered to find him a new home that was safe and warm. So, the fairy went on a long journey to find the perfect home for the little rabbit. After a while, the fairy came across a warm and cozy burrow. The fairy offered the little rabbit the burrow as a home. The rabbit was so happy he thanked the fairy and hopped in. The fairy flew away happy, knowing she had made the little rabbit happy.

Lily and Ben are friends. They like to play in the garden. They see many flowers and bugs. They also see a big carrot. The carrot is orange and long. "Look, Ben, a carrot!" Lily says. "Can we eat it?" "I don't know, Lily. Maybe it belongs to someone. Maybe it is a smart carrot," Ben says. "A smart carrot? What do you mean?" Lily asks. Ben thinks for a moment. He says, "Maybe the carrot can talk and think. Maybe it has a name and a family. Maybe it does not want to be eaten." Lily laughs. She says, "That is silly, Ben. Carrots cannot talk and think. They are just food. They do not have names and families. They do not care if they are eaten." "How do you know, Lily? Have you ever asked a carrot?" Ben says. Lily shakes her head. She says, "No, I have not. But I know because carrots are plants. Plants do not have mouths and brains. They do not have feelings and wishes. They just grow and make seeds." Ben is not convinced. He says, "Maybe you are right, Lily. But maybe you are wrong. Maybe plants have a different way of talking and thinking. Maybe they have a different way of feeling and wishing. Maybe they just do not show it to us." Lily and Ben look at the carrot. They wonder if it is a smart carrot or not. They do not know what to do. They decide to leave the carrot alone. They go to play with something else. They are still friends.

Once upon a time, there was a bossy cat named Tom. Tom had a plan to get a big cherry from a tall tree. He asked his friend, a little bird, to help him. The little bird did not like the bossy cat, but he said yes. The little bird flew up and got the big cherry. Tom was happy and said, "Give it to me!" The little bird did not like how Tom talked to him. He thought of a new plan. The little bird dropped the big cherry on Tom's head. "Ouch!" said Tom. He was mad and chased the little bird. They did not get to eat the cherry. Tom's plan did not work and the little bird was not his friend anymore.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. Lily was a happy girl who loved to play with her toys. One day, she found an amazing new toy in her room. It was a magic ball that could talk and play with her. The magic ball had a special power. It could gain memory from the things it saw and heard. Lily and the magic ball played games, sang songs, and watched the birds outside. The magic ball was learning from Lily and they were becoming good friends. As days went by, the magic ball gained more and more memory. It could now tell Lily amazing stories about the things they did together. Lily and the magic ball were very happy and they played together every day. They knew that they would always be best friends.

Lily and Ben were playing in the kitchen. They saw a big jar on the shelf. It was full of cookies. They wanted to eat some. "Let's get the jar," Ben said. "Mom is not here. She will not know." Lily was not sure. She remembered what Mom said. "Mom said no cookies before dinner. She said they are bad for our teeth. She will be angry if we take them." Ben did not listen. He was ignorant. He thought he knew better. He climbed on a chair and reached for the jar. "Don't be scared, Lily. It will be fun. Just one cookie." Lily tried to warn him. "Ben, be careful. The jar is heavy. It can fall and break. You can hurt yourself." But it was too late. Ben grabbed the jar and pulled it. The jar slipped from his hand and crashed on the floor. Cookies and glass flew everywhere. Ben fell off the chair and cried. Mom heard the noise and ran to the kitchen. She saw the mess and Ben's cut on his arm. She was very upset. She hugged Ben and cleaned his wound. Then she scolded him and Lily. "Why did you do this, Ben? You were very naughty. You did not listen to me or Lily. You could have been hurt worse. And you wasted all the cookies. You are in big trouble, mister." Lily felt sorry for Ben, but she also felt angry. She did not want to eat the cookies. She tried to stop him. She was not naughty. She was good. "Mom, it was not my fault," Lily said. "I did not want the jar. I warned Ben. He was ignorant. He did not listen." Mom looked at Lily and sighed. She knew Lily was telling the truth. She was proud of her for being honest and brave. She hugged her and kissed her. "I know, Lily. You did the right thing. You are a good girl. I am sorry I yelled at you. You are not in trouble. But Ben is. He has to learn his lesson. He has to say sorry to you and me. And he has to clean up the mess. No cookies for him tonight." Lily nodded. She felt better. She hoped Ben would learn his lesson too. She loved him, but he was ignorant sometimes. She wished he would listen more.
