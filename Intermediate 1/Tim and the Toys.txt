Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim and the Toys

One day, a little boy named Tim went on a search for his toy car. He looked under his bed, in his toy box, and even in the kitchen. Tim could not find his car anywhere. He felt a little crazy not knowing where it was. Tim saw his mom and asked, "Mom, can you help me find my car?" His mom smiled and said, "Let's sort your toys, and maybe we will find it." They started to sort all the toys. They put the big toys in one pile and the small toys in another pile. As they sorted, Tim found his toy car hiding under a big teddy bear. He was so happy to find it! Tim said, "Thank you, Mom, for helping me sort and search. That was a little crazy, but now I can play with my car again!" From that day on, Tim always made sure to put his toys away so he would not have to search for them again. And he never felt crazy looking for his toys anymore.

Once upon a time, there was a grumpy monster who lived in the forest. He loved to take naps during the day and would get very mad if anyone woke him up. One day, a little girl named Lucy decided to look for the monster. She wanted to see if it was really as grumpy as everyone said. She found the monster deep in the forest, sleeping in a tree. She was curious and quietly poked him with her tiny finger. The monster woke up with a start and was very angry! He scowled at Lucy and waved his tiny teaspoon in the air to scare her away. But Lucy wasn't scared. She stood her ground and held out her hand, as if she was offering a gift. The monster was so stunned that he forgot to be grumpy and his eyes softened. He put down the teaspoon and let Lucy give him a hug. From then on, they were good friends. The monster looked forward to visiting with Lucy every day and he was never grumpy again.

Mai and her mom were at the city. Mai was so excited! Everywhere Mai looked, there were people marching. She tugged at her mom's shirt and said, "Mom, look! What are they doing?" Her mom smiled and said, "Those people are marching to the park. They have tickets to go see the show." Mai asked, "Can I get one too?" Her mom replied, "Yes, you can. But it's a bit expensive. Are you sure you want to go?" Mai smiled and nodded. So, she and her mom got a ticket and marched to the park. Mai was so happy. But once they got there, things were different. The park was dirty and the show wasn't very good. Mai was disappointed but she was still glad she took the march with her mom.

There was a little girl named Lily. She liked to play in her room with her toys. One day, she found an old box in her room. She was very curious about what was inside the box. She opened the box and saw a mirror. The mirror was old and had a pretty frame. Lily picked up the mirror and looked at her face. She saw something in the mirror that made her feel surprise. She saw a funny clown face in the mirror! The clown face made her laugh a lot. She showed the mirror to her mom and dad, and they laughed too. Lily loved her old mirror so much. She played with it every day. The mirror always made her happy and gave her surprise. She shared the mirror with her friends, and they all had fun looking at the funny faces. It was a very special mirror for Lily.

Tim and Sue were adventurous kids. They liked to explore new places and try new things. One day, they found a big box of paint in the garage. They decided to have some fun with it. "Let's paint the wall!" Tim said. He grabbed a blue paint can and a brush. "OK!" Sue said. She picked a red paint can and a brush. They ran to the wall and started to paint. They made swirls and dots and stripes. They laughed and giggled. Soon, the wall was covered with paint. It looked very colorful and messy. Tim and Sue were proud of their work. "Look what we did!" Tim said. "It's beautiful!" Sue said. They heard a noise. It was their mom. She came into the garage and saw the wall. She was not happy. "Tim! Sue! What have you done?" she asked. She looked angry and sad. "We painted the wall, Mom!" Tim said. He smiled. "We wanted to make it pretty, Mom!" Sue said. She smiled too. Mom shook her head. She sighed. "You can't paint the wall, kids. That's not how you use paint. Paint is for paper and canvas, not for walls. You made a big mess. You have to clean it up. And you have to say sorry." Tim and Sue felt bad. They did not mean to make Mom mad. They did not know they did something wrong. "We're sorry, Mom," they said. Mom hugged them. She was not mad anymore. She was still sad. "It's OK, kids. I know you were just being adventurous. But you have to be careful and respectful. Paint is not a toy. It can ruin things and hurt people. Do you understand?" Tim and Sue nodded. They understood. "Can we still paint, Mom?" Tim asked. "Yes, you can still paint. But only on paper and canvas. And only with my permission. And only in a safe place. OK?" Tim and Sue agreed. They wanted to paint, but they also wanted to be good. Mom gave them some paper and canvas. She also gave them some meat for lunch. They ate the meat and then they painted. They made pictures of flowers and animals and stars. They showed Mom their paintings. Mom smiled. She was happy. "You are very talented, kids," she said. "You can paint anything you want. As long as you do it the right way."
