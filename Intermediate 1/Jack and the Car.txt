Category: Intermediate
Title: Jack and the Car

Once upon a time there was a small engine car called Jack. Jack lived in the countryside and liked to explore. One day Jack decided he wanted to go on an adventure. He climbed into his car and drove around searching for exciting places. Jack soon spotted a strange looking house. He drove towards it and stopped near the entrance. "Who lives here?" he wondered. Suddenly, Jack heard a voice. "You have to unlock the door to get in," it said. Jack looked around and spotted a big red key. He grabbed the key and used it to unlock the door. Jack ventured inside the house. He noticed that the floor was dry and there were lots of big bottles full of fuel. Jack thought he had found the perfect spot to take a rest. He filled up his car with fuel and prepared to drive off. Just before he was about to go, he heard the voice again. "Come back soon," it said. "I have more adventures to show you!" Jack happily drove off, his engine humming with anticipation. He couldn't wait to come back and explore the house he had just unlocked!

One day, a curious little boy named Tim saw a light outside his window. He wanted to know where the light was coming from. He asked his mom a question, "Mom, what is that light?" His mom looked out the window and said, "I don't know, let's go find out." They went outside and followed the light. Tim was excited and kept asking questions. "Is it a star? Is it a firefly?" His mom just smiled and said, "We will find out soon." They walked and walked until they found a big tree. The light was coming from behind the tree. When they looked behind the tree, they saw a big, shiny balloon. It was not a star or a firefly. The balloon had a light inside it. Tim was surprised and happy. He had never seen a balloon like that before. His mom said, "It must have flown away from a party. Let's take it home and show your dad." They took the balloon home, and Tim's dad was just as surprised as they were. They all laughed and played with the balloon together.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She had a toy telephone that she loved to play with. One day, she was playing with her toy telephone in her room. Suddenly, she heard a loud sound coming from the telephone. Lily was surprised and said, "Hello? Who is there?" A voice from the telephone said, "Hello, Lily! I am a magic telephone. I can make your wishes come true if you rub me." Lily was very excited. She rubbed the telephone and wished for a big ice cream cone. Just like that, a big ice cream cone appeared in her hand. Lily was so happy and shared the ice cream with her friends. They all played together and had a great day.

Tom and Sue like to play in the park. They like to swing, slide and run. One day, they see a big hill. They want to climb the hill and see what is on the other side. They start to climb the hill. It is hard and steep. They hold hands and help each other. They are almost at the top when they hear a loud roar. They stop and look around. They see a big, hairy monster. It has sharp teeth and claws. It is angry and hungry. It wants to eat them. "Run!" Tom shouts. "Run back to the park!" They turn around and run. But the monster is faster. It chases them down the hill. Tom and Sue are scared. They scream for help. They see a big rock. They think they can hide behind it. They run towards the rock. But they do not see a hole. They fall into the hole. They hit the ground. It hurts. The monster sees them fall. It laughs. It thinks it has them. It runs to the hole. It bends down to grab them. But the hole is not empty. It is full of lemons. Big, yellow, sour lemons. The monster does not like lemons. It hates lemons. It puts its mouth on the hole. It bites a lemon. It spits it out. It makes a face. It cries. "Yuck! Yuck! Yuck!" the monster says. "These are not kids. These are lemons. I hate lemons. They are sour. They hurt my mouth. Go away, lemons. Go away!" The monster runs away. It does not want to see lemons ever again. It leaves Tom and Sue alone. Tom and Sue are happy. They are safe. They hug each other. They thank the lemons. They climb out of the hole. They go back to the park. They do not want to climb the hill ever again. They have had enough adventure for one day. They just want to swing, slide and run. And maybe eat some candy. Candy is sweet. Candy is good. Candy is not sour. Candy does not hurt their mouth. Candy does not make them fall. Candy does not chase them. Candy is not a monster. Candy is their friend. The end.
