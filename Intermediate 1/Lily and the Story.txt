Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily and the Story

Lily liked to create things with her crayons and paper. She made drawings of her family, her friends, and her pets. She also liked to create stories with her words. She told stories to her mom, her dad, and her brother. One day, Lily decided to create a story about a unique princess who lived in a castle. She drew the princess with a purple dress, a green crown, and a pink horse. She told her mom the story of how the princess saved the kingdom from a dragon. "That's a very nice story, Lily," her mom said. "But is it true?" Lily thought for a moment. She knew that dragons were not real, and that princesses did not wear green crowns. But she also knew that her story was fun and made her happy. "Yes, mom, it is true," Lily said. "It is true in my imagination." Her mom smiled and hugged her. "I see. Well, your imagination is very unique and creative, Lily. I love your story and your drawing. You are a very talented girl."

Lily liked tuna very much. She liked to eat tuna with bread and cheese. She liked to smell tuna when Mom opened the can. She liked to share tuna with her cat, Mimi. One day, Lily saw a big tuna at the store. It was in a glass box with ice. It had shiny scales and a long tail. Lily wanted to take the tuna home. She asked Mom, "Can we buy the tuna, please?" Mom said, "No, Lily. The tuna is too big and too expensive. We have to pay a lot of money for it. We can buy a small can of tuna instead." Lily was sad. She did not want a small can of tuna. She wanted the big tuna. She thought of a plan. She waited until Mom was busy with other things. She took the big tuna from the glass box. She hugged it tight. She ran to the door. But the tuna was very heavy. Lily could not run fast. She slipped on the ice. She fell down. The tuna fell on top of her. It hurt. Lily cried. Mom heard her cry. She came to help her. Mom was angry and worried. She said, "Lily, what did you do? You can't take the tuna without paying. That is wrong. And you could have hurt yourself. The tuna is not a toy. It is food." Lily was sorry. She said, "I'm sorry, Mom. I just wanted the big tuna. I like tuna very much. Can we still buy a small can of tuna?" Mom said, "Yes, Lily. We can buy a small can of tuna. But you have to promise me to never do that again. And you have to say sorry to the store man. He works hard to sell the tuna. He needs the money to buy food for his family." Lily nodded. She said, "I promise, Mom. And I'm sorry, store man." She gave the big tuna back to the store man. He smiled and said, "It's okay, little girl. I'm glad you like tuna. Here, have a small can of tuna for free. But be careful next time." Lily thanked the store man. She took the small can of tuna. She gave some to Mimi. She ate some with bread and cheese. She was happy. She learned a lesson. She never tried to take the big tuna again.

Once upon a time, there was a red ball. The red ball loved to play with the kids in the park. One day, a new boy came to the park. The boy saw the red ball and wanted to play with it too. The red ball said, "Will you care for me and play nice?" The boy said, "Yes, I will care for you and play nice." The red ball was happy and they played together all day. But the boy did not care for the red ball like he said. He threw it too hard and it hit a tree. The red ball broke and could not play anymore. The kids in the park were sad and the boy was sorry. The moral of the story is to always care for the things you play with, or they might break and you can't play with them anymore.
