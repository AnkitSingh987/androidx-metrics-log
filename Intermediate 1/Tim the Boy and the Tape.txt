Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Tape

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was very excited because today he would spend the day with his mom. They were going to the store to buy a special tape. When they got to the store, Tim saw many colorful tapes. There was red tape, blue tape, and even yellow tape! Tim's mom said, "Pick one tape, Tim." Tim picked the blue tape because blue was his favorite color. Tim and his mom went home. They used the blue tape to make a big, pretty picture on the wall. Tim was so happy and proud of his picture. He loved spending the day with his mom and using the exciting blue tape.

Lily and Tom are playing with paper. They like to fold the paper and make different shapes. Lily makes a bird, Tom makes a boat. "Look, my bird can fly!" Lily says. She throws the paper bird in the air and watches it flap its wings. "Wow, that's cool!" Tom says. He tries to throw his paper boat, but it falls to the ground. "My boat can't fly." Lily laughs. "Your boat can sail on the water. Let's go to the sink and try it." They run to the kitchen and fill the sink with water. They put their paper shapes in the water and watch them float. "Be careful, don't splash too much," Lily says. "The paper is fragile. It can break if it gets too wet." Tom nods. He likes his paper boat. He moves it gently with his finger, making waves. Suddenly, they hear a loud noise from the living room. It sounds like something falling and breaking. "What's that?" Lily asks, scared. "I don't know," Tom says. "Let's go see." They leave the sink and run to the living room. They see their mom standing by the couch, holding a broken vase. "Oh no, what happened?" Lily asks. "I'm sorry, kids," their mom says. "I was dusting the shelf and I knocked over the vase. It was very fragile. It shattered on the floor." Lily and Tom feel sorry for their mom. They know she liked the vase. It was a gift from their grandma. "It's okay, mom," Lily says. "We can make you a new vase." "Yeah, we can fold paper and make a vase," Tom says. Their mom smiles. She hugs them and thanks them. "That's very sweet of you, kids," she says. "But you don't have to. It's just a vase. You are more important to me than any vase." Lily and Tom hug their mom back. They love her very much. They decide to fold paper and make her a card instead. They write "We love you, mom" on it and draw flowers and hearts. They give it to their mom and she puts it on the shelf. She says it's the best gift ever.

One day, a little girl named Sara went for a walk. She wore her favorite yellow sweater. It was a sunny day and she felt happy. As she walked, she saw a big tree. She thought it would be fun to play under the tree. Sara heard a small sound. She looked around and discovered a little kitten. The kitten was stuck in the tree. "Meow," said the kitten. "Don't worry, I will help you," said Sara. She tried to reach the kitten but she was too small. Just then, a big wind came. It blew Sara's yellow sweater up into the tree. The kitten grabbed the sweater and climbed down. The kitten was safe! Sara was sad that her sweater was dirty, but she was happy she could help. The kitten said, "Thank you!" and they became best friends.

Lily loved to write letters to her grandma, who lived far away. She would draw pictures of her toys, her dog, and her friends, and tell her grandma what she did every day. She would use her best crayons and stickers, and fold the paper very carefully. She knew her letters were delicate, and she did not want them to break. One day, Lily's mom said they had to go to the post office to mail the letters. Lily was very excited, and put on her coat and hat. She held her mom's hand as they walked to the big building. She saw a lot of people and boxes and machines. She felt a little scared, but her mom smiled at her and said, "Don't worry, Lily. The post office is a safe place. They will take good care of your letters and send them to grandma." Lily nodded, but she still clutched her letters tightly. She did not want to lose them or let anyone touch them. She followed her mom to a counter, where a man in a blue shirt greeted them. He looked at Lily and said, "Hello, little girl. What do you have there?" Lily showed him her letters, and said, "These are for my grandma. She lives far away. I love her very much." The man smiled, and said, "That's very sweet of you. I'm sure your grandma will love your letters, too. Can I see them?" Lily hesitated, and looked at her mom. Her mom nodded, and said, "It's okay, Lily. The man works at the post office. He will help us mail your letters. He won't hurt them, I promise." Lily trusted her mom, and handed her letters to the man. He took them gently, and said, "Wow, these are very pretty. You are a good artist. And you used a lot of stamps. That's very smart. Do you know what stamps are for?" Lily nodded, and said, "They are for paying. They tell the post office where to send the letters." The man nodded, and said, "That's right. You are a smart girl. And you know what? I have a special stamp for you. It's a sticker that says 'Air Mail'. Do you know what that means?" Lily shook her head, and said, "No. What does it mean?" The man said, "It means your letters will go on a plane, and fly very fast to your grandma. It's a special way to mail letters that are very important. Do you want me to put this stamp on your letters?" Lily's eyes widened, and she said, "Yes, please. That sounds fun. Thank you, mister." The man smiled, and said, "You're welcome, Lily. Here you go." He peeled off the sticker, and put it on each of Lily's letters. He showed them to her, and said, "See? Now your letters are ready to fly. All we have to do is put them in this big box. Do you want to help me?" Lily nodded, and said, "Yes, please. I want to help." She took her letters, and followed the man to a big box with a slot. The man said, "This is where we put the letters. They will go down this slot, and then they will go on a conveyor belt. That's a machine that moves things around. It will take your letters to the plane. Do you want to see?" Lily nodded, and said, "Yes, please. I want to see." She lifted her letters, and put them in the slot. She watched as they disappeared into the box. She heard a noise, and saw them come out on the other side, on a conveyor belt. She saw them move along with other letters and packages, until they were out of sight. The man said, "There they go. They are on their way to your grandma. She will get them soon, and she will be very happy. You did a great job, Lily. You are a very good letter-writer." Lily smiled, and said, "Thank you, mister. You are very nice. You helped me a lot." The man said, "You're welcome, Lily. It was my pleasure. I hope you come back soon, and write more letters. Maybe you can write one to me, too. I would like that." Lily said, "Okay, mister. I will write one to you, too. I promise." The man said, "Thank you, Lily. I promise to write back. Goodbye, Lily. Have a nice day."
