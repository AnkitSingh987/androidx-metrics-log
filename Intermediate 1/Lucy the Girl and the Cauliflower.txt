Category: Intermediate
Title: Lucy the Girl and the Cauliflower

One day, a little girl named Lucy went to the store with her mom. They needed to buy some food for dinner. While they were at the store, Lucy saw a big, white cauliflower. She asked her mom, "Can we get this cauliflower, please?" Her mom looked at the cauliflower and said, "That's not a regular cauliflower, it's too big! But we can get it if you want." So they put the cauliflower in their cart and went to pay. When they got home, Lucy wanted to help her mom cook dinner. They were making a yummy soup with the cauliflower. But Lucy was not careful and she dropped the cauliflower on the floor. The cauliflower was now dirty and spoiled. Lucy was sad and said, "I'm sorry, mom. I spoiled the cauliflower." Her mom smiled and hugged her. "It's okay, Lucy. We can clean it and still use it for our soup. We'll be more careful next time." Together, they cleaned the cauliflower and made a delicious soup for dinner. Lucy learned to be more careful and she was happy that her mom helped her fix the mistake.

Once upon a time, there was an enormous tree. It had many twigs on it. The tree was very big and very old. One day, a little bird came to the tree. The bird wanted to build a nest on the tree. The tree was very kind and allowed the bird to live there. The little bird looked for twigs to build its nest. It found many twigs on the ground. The bird picked up the twigs with its beak. It flew back to the enormous tree and put the twigs on a branch. The bird worked very hard to make the nest. Soon, the little bird had a cozy nest on the enormous tree. The tree was very happy to have a new friend. The bird sang a beautiful song every day. The tree and the bird lived happily together. And they became the best of friends.

Once upon a time there was a bird whose wings were fragile. One day the bird flew too close to a tree and got its fragile wings caught in a branch. "Help me," the bird said. A small girl, who was three years old, heard the bird and ran to help. She reached up and carefully picked the wing loose. "Thank you," said the bird. The little girl smiled and said, "No problem." The bird flew away, free again. The little girl waved goodbye, feeling glad that she had been able to help the bird.

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play with toys and books. One day, they have a fight. Anna wants to play with the red car. Ben wants to play with the red car too. They pull and push the car. They say mean words to each other. Anna and Ben are angry. They do not want to be friends anymore. They go to their rooms and slam the doors. They do not say sorry. They do not kiss and hug. Mom and Dad are sad. They want Anna and Ben to be happy. They knock on the doors and talk to them. They say, "Anna and Ben, you are both good children. You should not fight over toys. You should share and take turns. You should be kind and gentle. You should say sorry when you are wrong. You should kiss and hug when you are sorry. That is how friends are." Anna and Ben listen to Mom and Dad. They think about what they did. They feel sorry. They open the doors and go to each other. They say sorry. They kiss and hug. They smile and laugh. They play with the red car together. They take turns and share. They are happy. Mom and Dad are happy too. They say, "Well done, Anna and Ben. You are good friends. You learned a good lesson. Now it is time to go to bed. You must be sleepy after playing so much. We love you very much. Good night." Anna and Ben go to bed. They are sleepy. They are happy. They are friends. They say, "Good night, Mom and Dad. Good night, friend. We love you too." They close their eyes and sleep.
