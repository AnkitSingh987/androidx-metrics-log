Category: Intermediate
Title: Amy the Girl and the Coat

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. Amy was very anxious to see a comet. Her mom said she could go outside to look for one when it was dark. One night, her mom permitted her to go outside. Amy was so happy. She put on her warm coat and ran outside. She looked up at the sky and saw many stars. She knew the comet would come soon. Amy waited and waited. Then, she saw it! A bright comet flew across the sky. Amy was so excited. She ran inside to tell her mom. The comet was even more amazing than she thought it would be.

One day, a little boy named Tim was playing in the kitchen. He saw a big bowl of sauce on the table. He wanted to protect the sauce because his mom made it for dinner. Tim was worried that his dog, Max, would eat the sauce. Tim said, "Max, you cannot eat the sauce. It is for dinner." Max looked at Tim and wagged his tail. Tim put a chair in front of the sauce to protect it. But Max was very sneaky. He jumped on the chair and tried to eat the sauce. Tim saw Max and said, "No, Max! Stop!" Tim pulled Max away from the sauce. Max was sad, but Tim knew he had to protect the sauce for his mom. In the end, Tim and Max played with a ball instead, and the sauce was safe for dinner.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a rare white cat. The cat liked to play and run all day. One day, the cat went to a house and saw a big cake. The cat ate the cake. The mom saw the cat eat the cake. She was not happy. She wanted to punish the cat. But the cat looked at her with big, sad eyes. The mom felt bad and did not punish the cat. The mom and the cat became friends. They played and had fun together. The rare white cat was happy, and so was the mom. They lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a hairy dog named Max. Max liked to play with his ball and run in the park. One day, Max saw a big party. It was a marriage party for two people who loved each other. Max went to the party and saw a little girl named Lily. Lily was sad because she lost her toy. Max wanted to help Lily find her toy. Max asked Lily, "What does your toy look like?" Lily said, "It is small and green." Max went to look for the toy. Max looked and looked, but he could not find the toy. So, Max went back to Lily and asked her to repeat what she said about her toy. Lily said, "It is small and green." Max looked again and found the toy under a chair. Lily was so happy, she hugged Max and they played together at the marriage party.

Once upon a time, there was a big, incredible statue in the middle of the park. The statue was of a kind man who did many good things for the town. Everyone who saw the statue would smile and say, "Wow!" One sunny day, a little girl named Lily went to the park with her mom. Lily saw the statue and said, "Mom, who is that?" Her mom replied, "That is a man who helped many people. He was very kind, and everyone loved him. Now we have this statue to remember him." Lily nodded and said, "I want to be like him when I grow up!" As the days went by, Lily would always go to the park and look at the statue. She would tell her friends about the kind man and how she wanted to be like him. Her friends wanted to be kind too. They all knew that being kind was the best way to be. And they all lived happily ever after.

One day, a little girl named Ann wanted to go to the store with her mom. Her mom said, "We will go soon, but first we need to be calm and wait for dad." Ann was excited but tried to be calm. She knew something fun was going to happen at the store. Ann and her mom waited for dad to come home. When dad got home, they all went to the store together. Ann was happy and calm. She looked around and saw a big box. She knew the big box was for her, but she didn't know what was inside. At last, they got to the big box, and dad said, "Surprise!" Ann opened the box and found a new toy inside. She was so happy and excited. Her mom and dad knew she would love the toy. They had planned this fun day for Ann, and she had a great time at the store with her family.
