Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily the Girl and the Spot

One day, a girl named Lily went to her mom and said, "Mom, I want spaghetti for lunch." Her mom smiled and said, "Okay, let's make spaghetti!" They went to the kitchen and started cooking. While they were cooking, Lily's dog, Spot, came in. Spot was very smelly because he had been playing in the mud. Lily said, "Ew, Spot is so smelly!" Her mom said, "Let's give Spot a bath after we make the spaghetti." But when they were not looking, Spot jumped up and ate some of the spaghetti. Lily's mom said, "Oh no, Spot will spoil the spaghetti!" They were sad, but they made more spaghetti for lunch. After they ate, Lily and her mom gave Spot a bath. Now, Spot was not smelly anymore, and they all had a fun day.

Lila and Ben are playing with their cars. They have many cars of different colors and sizes. They like to sort their cars by color or by wheel. Sometimes they sort by big and small, or fast and slow. "Look, I have all the red cars here," Lila says, pointing to her pile. "They are the fastest and the coolest." "I have all the blue cars here," Ben says, showing his pile. "They are the strongest and the smoothest." They both think their cars are the best. They do not want to share or trade with each other. They are spoiled. Then, Mom comes in and sees their mess. She is not happy. "Kids, you have too many cars. You need to give some away to other children who have no cars. You can keep only five cars each. Pick your favorites and put the rest in this box," Mom says, holding a big cardboard box. Lila and Ben are shocked. They do not want to give away their cars. They love all their cars. They start to cry and whine. "But Mom, these are our cars. We sorted them by color and by wheel. We worked hard. We do not want to share," they say. Mom shakes her head. She is firm. "Sorry, kids, but you have to learn to share and be grateful. There are many children who have nothing to play with. You are lucky to have so many cars. You can still have fun with five cars each. Now, hurry up and choose, or I will choose for you," Mom says. Lila and Ben are scared. They do not want Mom to choose for them. They quickly pick their five favorite cars and put the rest in the box. They are still sad and angry, but they do not want to make Mom more upset. Mom smiles and hugs them. "Good job, kids. I am proud of you for being generous and brave. You will make some other children very happy with your cars. And you will still have fun with your cars. Maybe you can even play together and trade sometimes," Mom says. Lila and Ben nod. They are still not sure, but they trust Mom. They look at their cars and then at each other. Maybe they can play together and trade sometimes. Maybe they can sort their cars by color and by wheel again. Maybe they can be less spoiled.

One day, a boy named Tim found a big, dull box. He was very happy. He took the box to his friend, Sam. Tim wanted to see what was inside the box. Tim and Sam were very excited. Tim said, "Let's part the box and see what's inside!" Sam agreed. They opened the box, and inside they found many toys. Tim and Sam played with the toys all day long. They did not want to stop playing. Soon, it was time to go home. Tim and Sam did not want to part with the toys. They took the toys without asking. This made their parents very sad. The toys were not theirs to keep. In the end, Tim and Sam had to give the toys back. They learned that it was wrong to take things without asking. The moral of the story is to always ask before taking something that is not yours.

Once upon a time, in a sunny park, there was a big tree. The tree had many leaves and gave nice shade. Under the tree, there was a little ant. The ant was very hot and tired. He was grateful for the tree's shade. It helped him stay cool. One day, a small bird came to the tree. The bird was thirsty and needed help. The ant wanted to help the bird. The ant told the bird, "I know where to find water." The bird was happy and said, "Thank you, little ant." The ant and the bird went to find water. They found a big puddle near the tree. The bird drank the water and felt much better. The bird was very grateful for the ant's help. They both went back to the tree's shade and became good friends. They helped each other and played together every day.
