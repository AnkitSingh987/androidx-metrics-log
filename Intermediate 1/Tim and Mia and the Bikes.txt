Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim and Mia and the Bikes

Tim and Mia like to ride their bikes in the park. They have red bikes with two wheels and pedals. They wear helmets and gloves to be safe. One day, they see a big boy with a blue bike. He has no pedals on his bike. He can go very fast and do tricks. Tim and Mia want to try his bike. "Can we ride your bike, please?" Tim asks the big boy. "No, this is my bike. Go away!" the big boy says. He is mean and rude. Tim and Mia are upset. They like their bikes, but they want to try something new. They think the big boy is not fair. They have an idea. They wait until the big boy goes to the water fountain. They run to his bike and remove the seat. They hide it behind a tree. The big boy comes back and sees his bike. He is angry and confused. He cannot ride his bike without a seat. "Who did this? Where is my seat?" he shouts. Tim and Mia giggle behind the tree. They think they are clever and funny. But then, a park ranger comes. He sees the big boy and his bike. He sees Tim and Mia and the seat. "What is going on here?" he asks. Tim and Mia are scared. They know they did something bad. They are sorry and ashamed. They come out from behind the tree. They give the seat back to the big boy. They say sorry to him and to the park ranger. The park ranger is stern but kind. He tells them that they should not take things that are not theirs. He tells them that they should respect other people and their bikes. He tells them that they should share and be nice. Tim and Mia nod and agree. They learn their lesson. They promise to be good and polite. The big boy is still mad, but he accepts their apology. He lets them ride his bike for a little while. He shows them how to do tricks. Tim and Mia are happy and grateful. They have fun and make a new friend. They ride their bikes together in the park. They have red bikes and blue bikes. They have pedals and no pedals. They have helmets and gloves. They have smiles and laughs.

Once upon a time, there was a charming little girl named Lucy. She loved to play with her blocks. She would build tall towers and small houses with them. Lucy had a little brother named Tim. Tim liked to play with the blocks too. One day, Lucy and Tim both wanted to play with the blocks at the same time. Lucy tried to grab all the blocks for herself. Tim got upset and started to cry. Their mom heard the noise and came to see what was wrong. Mom told Lucy and Tim that they needed to share the blocks. She said, "When you share, everyone is happy." Lucy and Tim listened to their mom and started to share the blocks. Soon, they built a big castle together. They were both very happy. The moral of the story is that sharing makes everyone happy.

Anna likes to print pictures with her crayons. She has many crayons of different colors. She prints flowers, animals, and stars on paper. She is very happy when she prints. One day, Anna goes to the station with her mom. The station is a big place where trains come and go. Anna sees many people and things at the station. She sees a big machine that prints tickets. She wants to print with the machine. Anna runs to the machine and tries to print. But the machine is difficult. It has many buttons and numbers. Anna does not know what to do. She pushes some buttons, but nothing happens. She gets sad and cries. Mom sees Anna and runs to her. She hugs Anna and says, "Don't cry, Anna. The machine is not for printing pictures. It is for printing tickets. You need money and a card to print tickets. The machine is difficult for you, but easy for me. I can help you print a ticket." Mom takes out some money and a card and puts them in the machine. The machine makes a noise and prints a ticket. Mom gives the ticket to Anna and says, "Look, Anna. You printed a ticket. Now we can go on the train. Do you want to go on the train?" Anna smiles and says, "Yes, Mom. I want to go on the train. Thank you for helping me print a ticket. I love you, Mom." Mom smiles and says, "I love you too, Anna. Come on, let's go on the train." Mom and Anna hold hands and walk to the train. Anna is happy. She likes to print, but she likes to go on the train more.
