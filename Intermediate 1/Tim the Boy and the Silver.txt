Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Silver

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim had a silver hat that he loved to wear. He wore it every day. The silver hat was very special to him. One day, Tim met a girl named Sue. Sue wanted to wear the silver hat too. Tim said no, but Sue was persistent. She asked him again and again to let her wear the hat. Tim was sad because he didn't want to share his special hat. Tim and Sue argued over the silver hat. They both wanted to wear it. Then, Tim had an idea. He said, "Let's take turns wearing the hat." Sue agreed, and they both got to wear the silver hat. They were happy and became good friends.

Once upon a time, there was a girl named Jane. She liked to sell things. One day, she wanted to sell prunes. She thought prunes were yummy. Jane's friend, Tim, saw her selling prunes. Tim was jealous. He wanted to sell prunes too. So, he went to Jane and asked if he could help her. Jane said yes, and they sold prunes together. They were both happy. They sold many prunes and had fun. In the end, they were not jealous anymore. They were good friends who shared everything.

Once upon a time, there was a lively little dog named Spot. Spot loved to go to the market with his best friend, a girl named Sue. They would carry a big basket to buy food and toys. Spot was very happy when they went to the market. One day, while they were at the market, a big dog came near them. Spot felt scared of the big dog. He tried to hide behind Sue, but the big dog saw him. Sue told Spot not to worry, but Spot could not stop shaking. The big dog came closer and closer. Spot tried to be brave, but he was too scared. He dropped the basket and ran away. Sue tried to catch him, but she could not. The big dog took the food and toys from the basket. Spot and Sue were very sad and went home with nothing.

Once there was a little girl who had a toy. She loved her toy so much and would always mind it carefully. But one day, while she was playing, the toy slipped out of her hands and it rolled into a deep puddle. She was very scared and she tried to catch it but the water was too mild to grab it with her hands. When the little girl saw that her toy was gone forever, she cried and cried sadly. Her toy was a reminder of how special she was and she was so sad to lose it. She knew that no matter how hard she tried, she could not get her toy back. The little girl was so upset that she decided to never play with that kind of toy again. She minded her toys carefully from then on and was always extra careful whenever she played. However, the bad ending for her toy stayed with her for a long time.

Tim and Lily went to the zoo with Mom and Dad. They saw many animals, like lions, monkeys, and birds. Tim liked the lions best, because they roared loud. Lily liked the monkeys best, because they swung from trees. Then they came to the otter pool. The otters were brown and furry, and they swam and played in the water. Tim and Lily watched them with big eyes. They saw one otter slide down a rock and splash into the pool. They saw another otter catch a fish and eat it. They saw a third otter carry a shiny stone in its mouth. "Look, Mom, look!" Lily said. "The otter has a stone!" "Yes, dear, it does," Mom said. "Some otters like to collect stones and other things. They are very smart animals." "Can I have a stone, too?" Tim asked. "Sure, honey, you can," Dad said. He picked up a small, clear stone from the ground and gave it to Tim. "Here you go. It's a pretty stone." Tim smiled and held the stone in his hand. He felt its smoothness and saw its sparkle. He wanted to show it to the otter. He walked to the edge of the pool and waved the stone at the otter. "Hello, otter!" he said. "Look at my stone! Do you like it?" The otter looked at Tim and saw the stone. It tilted its head and made a squeaky sound. Then it swam closer to Tim and reached out its paw. It wanted to touch the stone. "Be careful, Tim," Mom said. "Don't get too close to the water. The otter might bite you." Tim nodded and moved back a little. He still held the stone, but he did not let the otter touch it. He said, "Sorry, otter. This is my stone. You have your own stone." The otter made another squeaky sound. It looked sad and turned away. It swam back to its friends and joined them in their games. Tim felt sorry for the otter, too. He wished he could share his stone with it. He said, "Bye, otter. Maybe next time we can play together." Then he heard a loud sound. It was the zookeeper. He was blowing a whistle and calling the otters. He said, "Come on, otters. It's time for your snack. Follow me." The otters heard the whistle and the voice. They stopped playing and swam to the zookeeper. They followed him to a big bucket full of fish. They lined up and waited for their turn. "Look, Lily, look!" Tim said. "The otters are marching!" "Yes, they are," Lily said. "They are marching to get their fish. They are hungry." Tim and Lily watched the otters march and eat. They clapped their hands and laughed. They liked the otters very much. They said, "Thank you, otters, for the fun. We hope to see you again."
