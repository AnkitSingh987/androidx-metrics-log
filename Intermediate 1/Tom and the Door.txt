Category: Intermediate
Title: Tom and the Door

Once upon a time, there was a big, strong man named Tom. Tom had a job to deliver things to people. He was very good at his job because he was mighty. People liked Tom and always said "thank you" when he gave them their things. One day, Tom had to deliver a very big and heavy thing. He put the thing on his back and walked to the house. It was a long walk, but Tom was strong and he did not stop. When he got to the house, he knocked on the door. A little girl opened the door and looked at Tom. She said, "You are not the one who delivers things to us. My mommy does that." Tom was surprised, but he gave the little girl the big thing. The girl said "thank you" and Tom went home. He was happy to help, even if it was not his job.

Once there was a girl who loved to write. Everyday, she would take out her pencil and paper and write stories. She wrote about animals and people, and even wrote letters to her friends. One day, she wrote a special story about a smiling bear. She wrote about how the bear looked so happy, with a big smile on his face. The story made the girl smile too. But then she noticed something wrong. The bear was not really smiling. He was dead. The girl stopped smiling and started to cry. Why was the bear so dead? She wished she could write a story to make the bear alive again. But nothing she wrote would bring the bear back to life. So, the girl just kept writing stories and kept her fingers crossed that one day the dead bear would smile again.

Sara was a big girl who liked to play with her dolls. She had many dolls, but her favorite was Lily. Lily had long hair, a pink dress and a smile. Sara always invited Lily to have lunch with her. She made sandwiches and juice for both of them and put them on a small table. One day, Sara saw a new girl in the park. She had short hair, a blue dress and a frown. Sara wanted to be her friend, so she went to say hello. "Hi, I'm Sara. What's your name?" she asked. The new girl looked at Sara and said, "I'm Mia. I don't want to talk to you. Go away." Sara felt sad and hurt. She didn't understand why Mia was so mean. She ran back to her dolls and hugged Lily. "Don't worry, Lily. You're my best friend. I don't need anyone else," she said. The next day, Sara saw Mia again in the park. She was sitting on a bench, holding a sandwich. She looked weak and tired. Sara remembered how her mom told her that some people are mean because they are unhappy or lonely. She decided to try again. She walked to Mia and said, "Hi, Mia. Do you want to have lunch with me and Lily? We have sandwiches and juice. They are yummy." Mia looked at Sara and Lily. She felt hungry and curious. She didn't have any friends or toys. She said, "OK. I'll have lunch with you. But I don't like dolls. They are silly." Sara smiled and said, "That's OK. You don't have to like dolls. You can like whatever you want. Come on, let's go to the table." Sara, Mia and Lily went to the table and ate their sandwiches and juice. They talked about the park, the birds and the flowers. Sara told Mia stories about her dolls and Mia told Sara stories about her family. They laughed and had fun. Sara felt happy and Mia felt less weak and more friendly. They invited each other to play again the next day. Sara and Mia became friends and Lily was happy too.

Once upon a time, in a land far away, there was a bald king. The king liked to march with his big army. He wore a shiny hat and big boots when he marched with them. One day, the king's feet began to suffer. They hurt so much that he could not march with his army. The king was very sad. He did not want to be left behind. So, the bald king asked his friend, a wise old owl, for help. The owl gave the king a soft pair of socks. The king put them on and his feet stopped suffering. The bald king was happy again. He could march with his big army and wear his shiny hat and big boots. And they all lived happily ever after.
