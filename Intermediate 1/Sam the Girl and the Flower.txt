Category: Intermediate
Title: Sam the Girl and the Flower

Once upon a time there was a little girl named Sam. Sam loved to play outside in the sun. One day Sam wanted to make something cool. She put it into a freezer. The freezer was so cold that it made her cool. Sam forgot about her cool stuff in the freezer for many days. When she finally remembered and opened it, the stuff inside was all rotted. Sam was sad. Then she remembered that she could make something cool again. She went outside and found a flower to put in the freezer. The flower stayed cool and didn't rot. Sam was happy again.

Lily was a lively lamb who loved to run and play with her friends. She had soft white wool that kept her warm and cozy. Her mother was proud of her wool and told her to take good care of it. One day, Lily saw a big pair of scissors in the barn. She was curious and wanted to try them. She picked them up and snipped a little bit of her wool. It felt funny and made a noise. She snipped some more and some more, until she had a pile of wool on the floor. She looked at herself in the mirror and gasped. She had no wool left on her body. She was bald and cold and felt silly. She ran to her mother and cried. "Mommy, mommy, look what I did! I cut all my wool off!" Her mother looked at her and hugged her. She was not angry, but sad. "Oh, Lily, why did you do that? Your wool was so beautiful and warm. Now you have none." Lily sobbed and said she was sorry. She wanted her wool back. "Don't worry, my dear. Your wool will grow back. But it will take some time. You have to be patient and careful. And you have to promise me never to touch the scissors again. They are not for playing. They are for shearing. Do you understand?" Lily nodded and said she understood. She promised to be good and to wait for her wool to grow back. Her mother gave her a blanket and a kiss and told her she loved her. Lily felt a little better and hoped her wool would grow back soon. She learned her lesson and never touched the scissors again.

Polly the pig was walking through the woods one evening. The air was thick and heavy. She saw a butterfly and followed it through the trees. Suddenly, Polly heard a voice counting. She looked around, but she couldn’t see anyone. Polly followed the voice and she soon saw a frog sitting by a pond. He had many little stones in his hands and was counting them. “What are you doing?” asked Polly. “Counting,” replied the frog. “I am counting my stones. I need to count them in the evening so I can go to sleep.” Polly thought it sounded like a very good idea. She said goodbye to the frog, thanked him for counting and continued on her way through the thick woods.

Once upon a time, in a big forest, there lived a stubborn little squirrel named Sammy. Sammy loved to watch the big oak tree near his home. One day, Sammy saw a new friend, a bird named Billy, sitting on the oak tree. Sammy said, "Hi, Billy! Let's play near the oak tree." Billy looked at Sammy and said, "No, I want to fly in the sky. You can watch me." Sammy felt sad but agreed to watch Billy fly. As Sammy watched Billy fly, he wanted to try flying too. He climbed to the top of the oak tree and jumped. But instead of flying, he just fell. Luckily, he landed on a soft pile of leaves. Sammy learned that even though he was stubborn, he couldn't fly like Billy. From that day on, Sammy and Billy played together, each doing what they loved best.

Once there was a boy named Tim. Tim was very smart. One day, Tim went to the store with his mom to buy a toy. Tim saw a big, scary toy. He wanted to buy it, but his mom said no. Tim was sad but he had an idea. He thought that if he could show his mom how smart he was, she would let him buy the toy. So, Tim did many smart things. He helped his mom find things in the store. He even helped a lady who was lost. But then, Tim saw the big, scary toy again. He wanted it so much. Tim thought that if he took it, his mom would see how much he wanted it and let him buy it. So, Tim took the toy and hid it. When Tim's mom found out, she was very sad and angry. She made Tim give the toy back. Tim did not get to buy the toy and he was in big trouble. Tim learned that being smart does not mean he can do bad things.
