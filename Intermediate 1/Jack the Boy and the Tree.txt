Category: Intermediate
Title: Jack the Boy and the Tree

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Jack. Jack was three years old and he liked to explore his backyard. On one of his adventures, he saw a parrot perched on a tree. The parrot was pretty and Jack waved at it. The parrot looked down at him and squawked, “Hello!” Jack was surprised and he asked the parrot, “What’s your name?” The parrot replied, “My name is Beaky and I don’t like cats. Cats hate me!” Jack laughed and asked, “Why do cats hate you?” Beaky said, “Because I am so pretty! All of the cats want to be as pretty as me! But they can never be as nice looking as me.” Jack smiled and waved goodbye. He promised Beaky that he would come back to visit him soon. Jack was happy he had made a new friend.

Ben and Lily went to the zoo with Mom and Dad. They saw many animals. They saw lions, monkeys, birds, and more. Ben and Lily liked the animals. They wanted to sort them by size, color, or sound. "Look, Lily, these birds are big and red. They are macaws. Let's sort them by color," Ben said. "OK, Ben, I like red. But what about these birds? They are small and blue. They are budgies. Can we sort them by size?" Lily asked. "Sure, Lily, we can sort them by size. The macaws are bigger than the budgies. But what about these birds? They are big and white. They are swans. They are bigger than the macaws. How can we sort them?" Ben wondered. "Maybe we can sort them by sound, Ben. The macaws are loud and squawky. The budgies are quiet and chirpy. The swans are silent and graceful. Do you hear them?" Lily suggested. "Yes, Lily, I hear them. You are right. We can sort them by sound. That is a perfect way to sort them. We are good at sorting, Lily. We are smart," Ben said. "Thank you, Ben. You are smart, too. I like sorting with you. Sorting is fun. Do you want to sort more animals?" Lily said. "Yes, Lily, I want to sort more animals. Let's go and see the monkeys. They are funny and noisy. Maybe we can sort them by what they do," Ben said. "OK, Ben, let's go and see the monkeys. I like monkeys. They are cute and silly. Maybe we can sort them by what they eat," Lily said. Ben and Lily ran to see the monkeys. They had fun sorting them. They had fun at the zoo. Mom and Dad smiled and watched them. They were proud of Ben and Lily. They were happy.

Once upon a time, there was a bald man named Bob. He lived in a small house near a big tree. Bob was a nice man, but he liked to quarrel with his friend, Tom. One day, Bob and Tom went for a walk. They saw a beautiful flower and both wanted it. They started to quarrel over the flower. They could not decide who should have it. Then, a little bird came and said, "Stop quarreling! Look at the wonder around you!" Bob and Tom looked around and saw many more beautiful flowers. They stopped quarreling and enjoyed the wonder of the flowers together.

Once upon a time, there was a mill. The mill was a place where people sort things. One day, a little boy named Tim went to the mill. He wanted to help sort things too. Tim was very happy to be at the mill. While sorting, Tim found a little box. He was curious about what was inside. He opened the box and found a toy. The toy was upset. Tim wanted to help the toy feel better. Tim talked to the toy and played with it. The toy started to feel better. Then, something unexpected happened. The toy turned into a real, big dog! Tim and the dog became best friends. They played together at the mill every day.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was worried because he had to prepare for a big test at school. He knew he needed to get a good score to make his mom and dad proud. Tim's mom gave him a new eraser for his pencil. She said, "This eraser will help you do your best on the test." Tim took the eraser to school and showed it to his friends. They all liked it and wished they had one too. On the day of the test, Tim used his new eraser to fix his mistakes. He remembered what his mom said, and he tried his best. When he got his test back, he saw that he got a good score! Tim was so happy and knew that the eraser his mom gave him had helped him do his best.
