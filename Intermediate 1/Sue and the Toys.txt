Category: Intermediate
Title: Sue and the Toys

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. She loved to imagine and play with her toys. One day, she found a pretty frame in her room. The frame was not harsh, it was soft and nice. Sue put a picture of her family in the frame. She imagined going on a big adventure with them. They went to the park, played on the swings, and had a yummy picnic. Sue was so happy in her adventure. At the end of the day, Sue gave the frame a big hug. She was glad she found it. The frame made her feel close to her family, even when they were not there. And every night, she would imagine new adventures with them.

Once upon a time, there was a playful person named Tim. Tim liked to play with his red ball all day. He would throw the ball and run after it, laughing and smiling. One day, while Tim was playing, he saw a big fire. The fire was hot and it could burn things. Tim knew he should not go near the fire, but he wanted to keep playing with his ball. Tim was careful and stayed away from the fire. He kept playing with his ball, but made sure not to let it go near the fire. Tim was safe and happy, and he played all day long.

Sara and Ben were twins who lived on a farm. They liked to play with the animals in the barn. One day, they saw a big truck with a sign that said "Circus". The truck stopped near the barn and some people came out. "Hello, we are looking for a place to stay for the night," one of they said. "Can we use your barn?" "Sure, you can," said Sara and Ben's mom. "But please be careful with our animals. They are not used to strangers." "Thank you, ma'am. We will be very gentle," the man said. He and his friends took some cages and tents from the truck and went to the barn. Sara and Ben were curious. They wanted to see what was in the cages and tents. They waited until their mom was busy in the kitchen and then they sneaked to the barn. They saw a lion, a tiger, a monkey, a bear, and a clown. "Wow, look at all these animals!" Sara said. "They are not regular animals. They are circus animals!" "Let's go closer and touch them," Ben said. He walked towards the lion's cage. "Ben, no! That's dangerous!" Sara said. She tried to pull him back, but it was too late. The lion roared and jumped at the cage. Ben screamed and ran away. Sara followed him. They ran out of the barn and bumped into their mom. She was angry and scared. "What were you doing in the barn?" she asked. "Didn't I tell you to stay away from the circus people and their animals?" "We're sorry, mom. We just wanted to see them," Sara said. "Well, you saw them, and you almost got hurt. You should be thankful that the cage was strong enough to stop the lion. Now go to your room and think about what you did," their mom said. Sara and Ben nodded and went to their room. They felt sorry and scared. They learned their lesson. They decided to never go near the barn again when the circus was there. They also decided to be more thankful for their regular animals, who were friendly and safe.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to the park. He saw a humble bird sitting on a cable. The bird was sad because it could not find food. Tim wanted to help the bird. He observed the park and saw a big tree with red fruits. He thought the bird would like to eat the fruits. Tim picked up the fruits and gave them to the bird. The bird was very happy and ate the fruits. Tim and the bird became good friends. They played in the park every day.

Once upon a time, there was a famous train. This train was very big and strong. It could make a lot of steam. The train liked to plan trips for all the people in the town. One day, the train planned a very fun trip. The people were happy and excited. They got on the train and went far away. But then, something unexpected happened. The train saw a big hill and it was scared. The train tried to go up the hill, but it could not. It needed help. A small, not famous train came and said, "I can help you!" The big train was surprised. The small train pushed the big train up the hill. The people were happy again. The moral of the story is that even if you are not famous, you can still help others and do big things.
