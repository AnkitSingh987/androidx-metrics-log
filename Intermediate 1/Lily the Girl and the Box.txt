Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily the Girl and the Box

One day, a little girl named Lily went to get the mail with her mom. They walked to the big blue box at the end of their street. Lily liked to help her mom with the mail. She felt like a big girl. As they got the mail, Lily saw a hidden letter with her name on it. She was very excited. "Mom, look! A letter for me!" she said. Her mom smiled and said, "Wow, Lily! Let's go home and read it." When they got home, Lily opened the letter. It was an invitation to a friend's birthday party. Lily was so happy. She couldn't wait to go to the party and play with her friends.

---

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to eat prunes. She would eat them all day. One day, her mom said, "Sue, let's serve prunes to our friends at the party!" At the party, Sue was impatient. She wanted to share her prunes with her friends. So, she said, "Mom, can we serve the prunes now?" Her mom said, "Yes, Sue, let's serve them." But when they opened the prune box, they found a little kitten inside! The kitten had eaten all of the prunes. Sue was sad, but then she laughed. She said, "Now we have a new friend at our party!" Everyone laughed, and they all played with the kitten. And from that day on, Sue loved kittens just as much as she loved prunes.

---

One day, a strong bear named Bobo found a little bird with a hurt wing. The bird could not fly. Bobo wanted to help the bird, so he picked it up and took it to his home. In his home, Bobo found a soft wrap. He wrapped the bird's hurt wing carefully. "Don't worry, little bird. I will take care of you," said Bobo. The bird looked at Bobo and gave a small smile. After some days, the bird's wing got better. The bird was very happy and said, "Thank you, Bobo! You are a good friend." The bird gave Bobo a little kiss on his nose. They both laughed and played together, now the best of friends.

Ben and Mia are twins. They like to play with their toys and books. One day, they find a big map in their dad's office. The map has many colors and shapes. They think it is a map of the land. "Look, Ben, this is where we live," Mia says, pointing to a red shape. "And this is where grandma lives," she says, pointing to a blue shape. "Wow, this map is so cool," Ben says. "I wonder what is in the other shapes. Maybe there are animals or treasures or castles." They decide to pretend they are explorers. They take their backpacks and hats and a magnifying glass. They start to walk around the map, looking at the different shapes. "Here is a green shape. It looks like a forest," Mia says. "Maybe there are monkeys or birds or snakes." "Here is a yellow shape. It looks like a desert," Ben says. "Maybe there are camels or cacti or sand." They keep walking until they reach a purple shape. It looks like a mountain. They see something shiny on top of it. "Look, Mia, there is something sparkling. Maybe it is a jewel or a star or a crown," Ben says. "Let's go and see," Mia says. "Maybe it belongs to a king or a queen or a fairy." They climb the mountain, using the map as a ladder. They reach the top and see a big pile of coins and gems and pearls. They think it is the most beautiful thing they have ever seen. They think they are very wealthy. "Wow, Ben, we found a treasure," Mia says. "We are so lucky and brave and smart." "Yes, Mia, we are," Ben says. "But we have to share it with our family and friends and the people who need it." They agree to share the treasure. They fill their backpacks with some of the coins and gems and pearls. They leave the rest on the mountain. They climb down and run to their dad's office. They show him the treasure and tell him their story. He smiles and hugs them. He thinks they are very creative and kind and generous. He thinks he is very proud and happy and lucky.
