Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Peanut

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to write. He would write all day. One day, Tim wanted to write a story about a peanut. He thought it would be a fun story. Tim looked for a peanut to write about. He asked his mom, "Do we have any peanuts?" His mom said, "I am sorry, Tim, but we have no peanuts available." Tim was sad. He really wanted to write about a peanut. Then, Tim had an idea. He asked his friend, Lily, if she had a peanut. Lily said, "Yes, I have a peanut!" Tim was happy. He took the peanut and wrote a great story about it. Tim and Lily read the story together and laughed. Tim learned that even when things are not available, you can still find a way to have fun.

One day, a gifted girl named Penny went to the park with her mom. Penny loved to play on the swings and slide down the big slide. She also liked to make new friends at the park. Penny saw a boy sitting all alone. She went up to him and said, "Hi, I'm Penny! Do you want to play with me?" The boy looked sad and said, "I can't. I lost my toy car and I can't find it." Penny wanted to help, so they looked for the car together. They looked in the sandbox, under the slide, and behind the swings. But they could not find the toy car. Then, Penny had an idea. She said, "Maybe the car is up in the tree!" The boy looked up and saw his toy car stuck in the tree. He was very happy, but they didn't know how to get it down. Penny tried to climb the tree, but she was too small. The boy tried to throw a stick to knock the car down, but he missed. Then, a big wind came and blew the car out of the tree. They were so excited! But when the car fell, it broke into pieces. The boy was very sad and cried. Penny felt bad and said, "I'm sorry we couldn't save your car." They both learned that sometimes, things don't always go as planned.

Once upon a time there was a young boy who loved to play. One day, he heard a loud sound and decided to follow it. It was pouring really hard outside and the ground was covered with water. The young boy saw that the water was getting higher and higher. He started to worry because he did not want to be in a flood. The young boy saw his mom, and he ran towards her. She told him to stop and he did. His mom explained that they were having a flood and that they had to be careful. She said that they would need to stay in their house until the water went down. So, the young boy and his mom stayed in their house until the water stopped and went away. After the flood, everything was back to normal and the young boy was safe. He was so happy that he and his mom were both safe from the flood. The end.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Sam. Sam loved to paint. One day, he found a wild bird in his yard. The bird was very pretty. Sam had a question. He asked his mom, "Can I paint the wild bird?" His mom said, "Yes, but be gentle and kind." Sam got his paint and started to paint the bird. The bird liked the paint. It made the bird look even more pretty. Sam was happy, and the wild bird was happy too. They played together all day.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there lived a boy named Tim. Tim was a pale little boy who loved to play outside. One day, he found a small prune on the ground. He picked it up and thought it was a fun toy. Tim decided to play hide and seek with the prune. He hid the prune in different places like under his bed, behind the tree, and inside his toy box. Tim had so much fun finding the prune each time he hid it. But then, Tim forgot where he hid the prune last. Tim searched and searched, but he could not find the prune. He felt sad and missed his little prune. Tim's mom saw him looking sad and asked what was wrong. Tim told her about the lost prune. His mom said, "Tim, it's important to take care of our things and remember where we put them." From that day on, Tim learned to take care of his toys and things. He always remembered where he put them, and he never lost anything again. The moral of the story is to take care of our things and remember where we put them, so we don't lose them.
