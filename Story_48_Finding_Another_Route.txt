Title: Finding Another Route

Category: Intermediate

David stood on the busy street corner, staring at his smartphone screen with a growing sense of panic. He was in Chicago, a city he had moved to only three days ago. Today was supposed to be a great day. He had an interview for a junior architect position at one of the top design firms in the city. He had ironed his best suit, printed five copies of his resume, and left his apartment an hour early to ensure he arrived on time.

The plan was simple: take the Blue Line subway straight to the city center, walk two blocks, and arrive at the firm with twenty minutes to spare. But as he had walked down the steps into the subway station, he was met with a large metal gate and a sign written in bold red letters: 'STATION CLOSED DUE TO EMERGENCY CONSTRUCTION. PLEASE USE ALTERNATIVE TRANSPORTATION.'

"You have got to be kidding me," David whispered, stepping back onto the street.

He checked his map app. The firm was five miles away. Walking would take too long, and he would arrive sweaty and tired. He tried to request a taxi through an app, but the prices were incredibly high, and the wait time was over thirty minutes because everyone else from the closed subway station was also trying to book a ride.

It was 8:15 AM. The interview was at 9:00 AM. He had exactly forty-five minutes to figure out a new plan.

David took a deep breath to calm his racing heart. He looked around and saw a bus stop across the street. He ran over to it and looked at the complicated route map printed on the glass shelter. There were colored lines crossing over each other, numbers, and lists of stops that meant nothing to him.

"Excuse me," David said to a woman wearing a yellow raincoat who was waiting at the stop. "I am trying to get to the financial district, near the tall black tower. Do you know which bus I should take?"

The woman looked at the map and then at David. "Well, the 22 bus goes down that way, but it is very slow in the morning traffic. You might want to take the 14 bus to the river, and then switch to the 6 bus. That is usually faster."

"The 14, then the 6," David repeated, trying to memorize the information. "Thank you so much."

Just then, a large city bus pulled up with the number 14 glowing on the front. David thanked the woman again and climbed aboard. He paid his fare and found a seat near the back. The bus was crowded with people going to work. David watched the streets go by, feeling anxious every time the bus stopped at a red light.

After twenty minutes, the bus approached the river. "Next stop, River Street," the automated voice announced.

David jumped up and got off the bus. Now, he needed to find the stop for the number 6 bus. He looked up and down the street, but he didn't see another bus shelter. The area was loud with construction noise, and people were walking quickly in every direction.

He started walking down the street, hoping to see a sign. Ten minutes passed. It was now 8:45 AM. He was running out of time, and he was completely lost. The tall buildings blocked the morning sun, making it hard to figure out which direction was which.

"Okay, think," David told himself. He remembered his university professor telling him that a good architect always knows how to read their environment. He looked up at the buildings. He noticed that the glass on the buildings was reflecting the sun from the east. That meant he was walking west, away from the center.

He quickly turned around and walked in the opposite direction. After a few blocks, he finally saw a bus stop sign with the number 6 on it. A bus was already there, its doors open.

David ran as fast as he could. "Wait! Please wait!" he shouted, waving his hand.

The driver saw him in the mirror and kept the doors open. David jumped inside, breathing heavily. "Thank you," he gasped, wiping a small drop of sweat from his forehead.

"No problem," the driver said. "Take a seat."

David sat near the front and watched his map app closely. The blue dot representing his location slowly moved closer to his destination. At 8:56 AM, the bus stopped two blocks away from the architecture firm.

David thanked the driver, stepped off the bus, and power-walked down the sidewalk. He pushed through the heavy glass doors of the building at exactly 8:59 AM. He walked up to the reception desk and smiled at the receptionist, trying to look calm and collected.

"Good morning," David said, his voice a little shaky but clear. "My name is David Miller. I am here for a 9:00 AM interview with Mr. Harrison."

"Good morning, David," the receptionist replied. "Please take a seat. Mr. Harrison will be right with you."

David sat in the waiting area, smoothing his tie and taking deep breaths to slow his heart rate. A few minutes later, a tall man with grey hair walked out of an office.

"David? I am Thomas Harrison. Please come in," the man said, shaking David's hand firmly.

They went into a large office with a beautiful view of the city. Mr. Harrison looked at David's resume. "Your portfolio is impressive, David. But in this job, we don't just need people who can draw well. We need people who can solve problems under pressure. Construction projects never go exactly as planned. How do you handle unexpected challenges?"

David smiled. He couldn't have asked for a better question.

"Actually, Mr. Harrison, I can give you a very recent example," David said. "When I left my apartment this morning, I discovered that my subway line was closed due to an emergency."

Mr. Harrison raised his eyebrows. "Really? That line is usually very reliable. What did you do?"

David explained his entire morning. He told Mr. Harrison about studying the complex bus map, asking a stranger for advice, getting lost near the river, using the position of the sun on the buildings to find his direction, and running to catch the final bus.

"I had to quickly analyze the situation, gather information, adapt to the changing circumstances, and keep moving forward," David concluded. "And I managed to walk through your doors with one minute to spare."

Mr. Harrison leaned back in his chair and laughed. It was a warm, genuine laugh. "I have interviewed twenty people for this position over the past week, David. Most of them would have just called and asked to reschedule. The fact that you navigated a new city's bus system, got lost, and still made it on time tells me a lot about your character."

They spent the rest of the interview discussing architecture, design software, and the firm's current projects. David felt confident and relaxed. The stress of the morning had completely disappeared.

When David left the building an hour later, he felt a strong sense of accomplishment. He didn't know yet if he had gotten the job, but he knew he had done his absolute best.

He walked back to the bus stop, no longer in a rush. As he looked at the tall buildings around him, they didn't seem as intimidating anymore. He realized that sometimes, the original path is blocked for a reason. Finding another route not only gets you to your destination, but it also proves to yourself what you are capable of achieving.
