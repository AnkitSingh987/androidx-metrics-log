Lily and Tom were playing in the sand. They had a bucket and a spade each. They wanted to make a big castle with towers and walls. "Look, I made a door for the castle," Lily said, showing Tom her spade. "You can go in and out." "That's nice," Tom said, "but I made a flag for the castle." He stuck a stick with a leaf on it on top of his tower. "You can see it from far away." Lily and Tom were proud of their castle. They stared at it and smiled. They did not see the big wave coming. The wave was fast and strong. It crashed on the shore and washed away the castle. Lily and Tom were wet and sad. They cried and hugged each other. "Where is our castle?" Lily asked. "Where is the door and the flag?" "Maybe the fish took them," Tom said. "Maybe they liked them." Lily and Tom felt a little better. They decided to make another castle. But first, they ran to their mom and dad. They wanted a hug and a snack. The water was cold and bitter. They wanted something warm and sweet.

One day, a boy named Tim wanted to sell yummy cookies. He set up a small table outside his house. He put a big sign that said, "Yummy Cookies for Sale!" A girl named Sue walked by and saw the cookies. She asked, "How much are the cookies?" Tim said, "Two coins for one cookie." Sue gave Tim two coins and took a cookie. She took a bite and said, "This is the yummiest cookie ever!" More kids came and bought cookies. Soon, all the cookies were gone. Tim felt happy for his victory in selling all the cookies. He thanked everyone and went home to make more yummy cookies.

One day, a little girl named Lily had a plan. She wanted to make a surprise for her mom. Lily took a napkin and drew a big heart on it. She was so excited to show her mom. Lily went to find her mom in the kitchen. She hid the napkin behind her back. When she got there, her mom was frustrated. "Oh no," her mom said. "I spilled the milk!" Lily had an idea. She gave her mom the napkin with the heart. Her mom smiled and used the napkin to clean the milk. "Thank you, Lily," her mom said. "Your surprise helped me!" And they both laughed, happy that the plan worked in a different way.

Once upon a time there was a boy who loved elevators. He loved the way the big metal box moved up the big building. Whenever the boy got into the elevator, he always felt brilliant. One day, the boy was standing in the elevator when it suddenly stopped. He was stuck! The boy started to worry, but then he remembered how much he loved elevators. He imagined the elevator starting to move again, and sure enough it did! The boy was so happy. He thanked the elevator for being so brilliant and giving him a ride that day. From then on, the boy loved elevators even more than before. Whenever he was feeling sad, all he had to do was think about the elevator and he would feel better.

Lily and Ben like to play in the park. They have many toys and equipment. They have balls, bikes, scooters, and kites. They have fun with their friends and make noise. But when it is time to go home, they have to organize their equipment. They have to put everything in the right place. They have to make it tidy. They do not like this part. They want to keep playing. Mom and Dad help them. They say, "Organizing is easy and fun. You just have to sort and match. Look, this ball goes with this basket. This bike goes with this helmet. This scooter goes with this pad. This kite goes with this string. Can you do it?" Lily and Ben try to do it. They sort and match their equipment. They put them in the car or in the bag. They see that it is not hard. They see that it is fun. They see that it makes Mom and Dad happy. They see that it makes the park tidy. They say, "We did it! We organized our equipment. We made it tidy. We are good helpers. Can we play again tomorrow?" Mom and Dad smile and hug them. They say, "Yes, you can play again tomorrow. You are good helpers. You are good organizers. We are proud of you."
