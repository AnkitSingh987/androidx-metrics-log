Once upon a time, in a little town, there was a girl named Lily. She loved to skip and play outside with her friends. One sunny day, they found a symbol on the ground. It was a big, red heart. Lily and her friends were very curious about it. Lily felt a little ashamed because she didn't know what the symbol meant. She asked her friends if they knew, but they didn't know either. They all decided to ask their mommies and daddies about the symbol. When Lily asked her mommy about the symbol, her mommy smiled and said, "The heart is a symbol of love, Lily. It means that we should be kind and loving to everyone." Lily was happy to learn about the symbol. She went outside and skipped with her friends, sharing the meaning of the heart symbol with them. They all felt happy and loved as they played together.

Max was a three-year-old boy who loved to play outside. One day, he put on his warm jacket and rushed out the door with a big smile on his face. He was so enthusiastic that he couldn't wait to explore the world around him. Max ran around the yard, over the hills, and through the forest. He twirled and jumped and spun in circles, thrilled to be outdoors. He stopped to pick some flowers and smell the sweet fragrance of the blooming trees. When he felt tired, Max stopped to take a break and he noticed some of his friends playing nearby. So, he quickly got up and rushed over to join them. They played tag and hide and seek until the sun went down and it was time to go home. Max was so happy that he gave one last spin around the yard before he rushed back inside to get ready for bed. His parents always told him how enthusiastic he was about playing and exploring, and so he knew he would be back outside in no time the next day.

Lily and Ben were playing in the sandbox. They liked to make castles and roads with the sand. But sometimes they found pebbles in the sand. Pebbles were hard and round and not good for building. "Look, I found a big pebble!" Ben said. He held it in his hand and showed it to Lily. "Wow, that is a big pebble!" Lily said. "Can I see it?" Ben gave the pebble to Lily. She felt it with her fingers. It was smooth and heavy. "Can I keep it?" Lily asked. "I like it. It is pretty." "No, it is mine!" Ben said. He tried to take the pebble back from Lily. But Lily held it tight. "Give it back!" Ben said. He pulled harder. "No, it is mine!" Lily said. She pulled harder too. They both wanted the pebble. They both pulled and pulled. It was difficult to remove the pebble from each other. "Stop!" a voice said. It was Mom. She saw them fighting over the pebble. "What are you doing?" Mom asked. She came to the sandbox. "We found a pebble and we both want it!" Ben said. "It is mine!" Lily said. "No, it is mine!" Ben said. Mom looked at the pebble. It was not very special. It was just a pebble. "Listen, children," Mom said. "It is not nice to fight over a pebble. There are many pebbles in the sandbox. You can find another one. Or you can share this one. Or you can trade it for something else. But you cannot pull and hurt each other. Do you understand?" Lily and Ben nodded. They felt sorry for fighting. "OK, then," Mom said. "Why don't you say sorry to each other and give each other a hug?" Lily and Ben said sorry and hugged. They felt better. "Good," Mom said. "Now, do you want to keep the pebble or give it to me?" Lily and Ben looked at the pebble. It was not very important anymore. "You can have it, Mom," they said together. "Thank you," Mom said. She took the pebble and put it in her pocket. "Come on, let's go inside and have some juice," Mom said. Lily and Ben followed Mom. They left the pebble and the sandbox behind. They did not care about them anymore. They were happy to be friends.
