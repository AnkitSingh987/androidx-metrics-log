Once there was a little girl. She listened to the birds singing and the river babbling. Suddenly she saw something green! She ran over to see what it was and found a big, fine frog. The frog croaked and the little girl listened. She sat down and listened to the frog's song. It was a very happy sound and made her smile. The frog hopped across the grass and the little girl watched him. She had never seen such a fine green frog before. They became friends and the little girl listened to the frog every day. The little girl liked listening to the frog's croaks. He told her stories of distant places and introduced her to all kinds of new creatures. The frog made her laugh and smile every day. At the end of each day, the little girl thanked the frog before she went home. She would never forget the fine green frog and the wonderful stories he shared with her.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to the park with his mom. They walked on a path until they found a big tree. Tim saw a sign that said "Original Tree Pass". He asked his mom, "What is that sign?" His mom said, "It means this is a special tree. Let's imagine we are in a magic forest." Tim closed his eyes and imagined he was in a forest with big trees and bright flowers. They played under the tree all day. They made up stories about the magic forest and the animals that lived there. Tim was happy to have found the Original Tree Pass. It was a day he would always remember.

Once upon a time, there was a hunter. He was not a nice man. People thought he was ugly. He liked to gather things from the woods. One day, he went to find food. In the woods, the hunter saw a big, ugly bird. He wanted to catch it. But the bird was too fast. The bird flew away. The hunter was sad. He did not get the bird. Then, something unexpected happened. The big, ugly bird came back. It was not a bird! It was a magic creature. The creature said, "You are nice now. You are not ugly." The hunter was happy. He was nice and not ugly anymore. He still gathered things, but now he shared them with everyone.

Once upon a time there was a lonely oat in a field. It felt so alone, sunken down in the dirt surrounded by other oats. It tried and tried to get out but it was stuck in the ground. One day a farmer came and he had strong arms. He bent down and carefully removed the lonely oat out of the ground. The oat was so happy to be out. The farmer put the oat in a box with many other oats and took them all away. The oat was no longer lonely. It was with many other oats and was happy. The farmer took the oats to his home, where he used them to make oatmeal. The oat was excited to be part of something so tasty! And from then on, the oat was never lonely again.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl. She loved to collect things. One day, she went to the store to buy a cool toy. But when she got there, she saw something even better - a cauliflower! She loved it so much! She decided to collect it instead of the toy, and put it in her bag. When she got home, she showed it to her mom and dad. They were so surprised - they thought it was really cool that she wanted to collect cauliflowers! The little girl was so happy. She put the beautiful cauliflower in her room and showed it to all her friends. They thought it was so cool. From then on, the little girl always wanted to collect cauliflowers every time she went to the store. The End.

Sara and Ben are playing with clay. They make shapes and animals with their hands. Sara makes a big snake. She stretches the clay long and thin. She gives it eyes and a tongue. "Look at my snake!" Sara says. "It is big and scary!" Ben makes a small turtle. He rolls the clay into a ball and makes a shell. He makes four legs and a head. He likes his turtle. "Look at my turtle!" Ben says. "It is small and cute!" Sara and Ben show each other their clay animals. They are proud of their work. They put them on the table. "Can we paint them?" Sara asks. "Yes, we can!" Ben says. "But we need to wait for them to dry first." They go to the kitchen to get some water. They see a jar of salt on the counter. Sara has an idea. "Let's make our animals shiny!" Sara says. "We can sprinkle some salt on them!" Ben thinks that is a good idea. They take some salt and go back to the table. They sprinkle some salt on their clay animals. The salt makes them sparkle. "Wow, they look so pretty!" Ben says. "Yes, they do!" Sara says. They admire their salted clay animals. They are happy with their play. They hug each other and smile.
