Sara and Ben like to watch movies on the big TV. They have many movies to choose from, but their favorite is the one with the cars that go fast and make loud noises. They call it the zoom-zoom movie. One day, they want to watch the zoom-zoom movie again, but they cannot find it. They look in the box, under the sofa, behind the curtains, but the movie is not there. They are sad and confused. "Where is the zoom-zoom movie?" Sara asks Ben. "I don't know," Ben says. "Maybe Mommy took it." They go to Mommy, who is in the kitchen. They ask her if she has the zoom-zoom movie. Mommy smiles and says, "Yes, I have it. I put it in the drawer, because it was on the floor and I didn't want you to step on it and break it." Sara and Ben are happy and run to the drawer. They open it and see the movie. They take it out and hug it. "Thank you, Mommy!" they say. They go back to the big TV and put the movie in. They sit on the sofa and wait for the movie to start. But something is wrong. The movie is not playing the right way. The cars are going backwards, not forwards. The noises are strange and funny. The movie is in reverse! Sara and Ben laugh and giggle. They think the movie is funny in reverse. They watch the cars go the wrong way and make silly sounds. "Look, look!" Sara says. "That car is so clumsy! It bumped into the wall!" "Ha, ha!" Ben says. "That car is upside down! It flipped over!" They watch the movie in reverse and have a lot of fun. They don't mind that the movie is not playing the right way. They like it better this way. They think the zoom-zoom movie is the best movie ever.

Once upon a time there was a little boy called Robert. He was only three years old and very curious. One day, Robert went out for a walk with his mom. They were walking through an unusual forest and Robert took an old basket with him. Suddenly, Robert spotted a strangely bright light coming from a cave, and he started walking closer to investigate. His mom saw what he was doing and quickly warned him to stay away. But Robert was too curious and continued to move towards the light. "Mom, please! I just want to take a look!" he said, but his mom shook her head no. Suddenly, they heard a loud thunderclap and the ground started shaking. Robert's mom quickly held him close and moved away from the cave. When they were a safe distance away, Robert's mom explained what happened. She said, "It's important to listen to grown-ups when they warn you. If you had gone inside that cave, you would have been in danger. That's why listening is important." Robert was very grateful for his mom's warning and from then on he always listened to her advice.

Once upon a time, there was a quiet subway station. In the station, there was a little boy named Tim and his mom. They were waiting for the subway train to come. Suddenly, Tim saw a big red button on the wall. He wanted to touch it. He asked his mom, "Can I touch the button?" His mom said, "No, Tim. We don't know what it does." Tim was sad, but he listened to his mom. They waited for the train together. The train came, and they got on. As they rode the train, Tim thought about the big red button. He knew that listening to his mom was important. In the end, Tim and his mom got off the train at their stop. They walked home, hand in hand. Tim learned that sometimes, it's best not to touch things we don't know about. And they lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a young boy named Jack. He was so curious and wanted to explore the world. So one day, he decided to wander around the country. Jack loved to explore. He walked around the pathways, meadows, and forests. He made his way through the vast mountain range and by the end of the day, he reached the beach. It was a beautiful sight. He felt the sand between his toes, the warmth of the sun on his face, and the sound of waves crashing. He spent the whole day there, looking out towards the horizon. When it was time to leave, Jack continued to wander around the country. He saw all kinds of animals, plants, and wildlife. Jack was so happy with the wonderful things he had seen. He felt proud of himself, exploring the country all by himself.
