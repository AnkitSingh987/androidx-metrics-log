One day, grandma and her three year old granddaughter went to the store together. When they arrived, the little girl spotted a big bar of chocolate on the shelf. "Grandma, I want that chocolate!" she said. Grandma smiled. "That's a long bar of chocolate. Let's buy it and see if it tastes as good as it looks." The little girl eagerly agreed. They bought the chocolate and went home. When they arrived, the little girl knew just what to do. She took a knife and started to cut it into small pieces. Grandma spoke. "No, honey. Put the knife away. That chocolate belongs to both of us. We should enjoy it together." But it was too late. The little girl had already cut a long, thin piece out of the middle of the bar. She smiled, not knowing that the chocolate had gone bad. Grandma and the little girl tasted the chocolate. Both were disappointed to find that it did not taste good at all. The little girl felt sad that she had ruined the chocolate. Grandma tried to comfort her, but it was too late. The bad piece of chocolate had ruined the whole bar.

Once there were two friends, Jane and Sarah. Sarah had a large orange towel. She wanted to use it to dry off after she went in the pool. Jane asked, "Can I stay and help you dry?" "Of course!" Sarah said. "Let's work together and make it quick." So the two friends got to work. Jane rubbed the towel up and down her arms, and Sarah rubbed it all around her legs. They worked fast and soon the towel was nice and dry. "Let's take a look," said Sarah. The towel was big, soft, and dry! They both smiled and knew that their hard work had paid off. "Thank you for helping me stay dry," said Sarah. Jane smiled and said, "Let's do it again sometime!"

Once upon a time, there was a blue bird named Bob. Bob lived in a big tree. One day, Bob saw a red radish on the ground. He wanted to eat it, but it seemed too big for him to carry. Bob saw his friend, a big cat named Tom. Bob said, "Tom, can you help me get the radish? It's too big for me." Tom looked at the radish and said, "I can try, but it seems very big." Tom tried to pick up the radish, but it was too heavy for him too. They both felt sad. Just then, a big dog named Max came by. Max saw them and asked, "Why are you sad?" Bob and Tom told Max about the big radish. Max had an idea. "Let's all work together to move the radish," he said. So, Bob, Tom, and Max all worked together to pick up the radish. With their help, they were able to carry the radish back to Bob's tree. In the end, Bob, Tom, and Max shared the radish and ate it together. They were very happy and learned that working together can solve big problems.

One day, a little boy named Tim and his obedient dog, Spot, went for a walk. They saw a big cave and wanted to see what was inside. Tim said, "Let's enter the cave, Spot!" As they walked into the cave, it was very dark. Tim held Spot's leash tightly, and Spot stayed close to him. They heard a small voice say, "Hello, who is there?" Tim and Spot stopped and looked around. A little bat flew down and said, "Hi, I am Batty. This is my home. Do you want to play?" Tim and Spot were happy to make a new friend. They played hide and seek in the cave with Batty. They had a fun day together in the big cave.

Riley and her mom were walking through the farmer's market. As they looked around, Riley noticed a big pepper. "Mommy, what's that big pepper?" she asked. "That's a fat pepper," her mom said. "It looks delicious, doesn't it?" Riley nodded. "Can we get it?" Her mom smiled. "Let's look around and see if we can find something else." So Riley and her mom kept walking until they found a bunch of more peppers. Some were fat, some were small and some were skinny. Riley was excited to pick her favorite. After she found the perfect pepper, Riley was looking forward to bringing it home to eat. "See, sweetie," her mom said. "Sometimes patience can help you find what you're looking for. You just have to be open and look around until you find what you like." Riley smiled, understanding the importance of taking the time to look around. Learning to be patient and look beyond the first thing she saw was an important lesson.
