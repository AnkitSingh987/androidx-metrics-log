Once there was a mule who lived in an open field. The mule loved the sunshine and the singing of the birds. But one day, the sky got dark and it started to rain. The mule was scared and he wanted to run away. But the mule was too small to go very far. So he tried to resist the rain. Despite the mule's best efforts, he was getting wet. He was just about to give up when he heard a voice calling him. It was a farmer offering to take the mule to his barn. The farmer told the mule that it was foolish to resist the rain, and when the mule was inside he was safe and dry. The mule learned that day that it's better to be open to change than it is to resist it. No matter how hard you try, you can't resist the things that life throws at you. So it's best to accept and be open to new possibilities.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was a very hungry boy. One day, he went to the kitchen to find some food. He saw a big, red apple on the table. Tim was so happy! He took a big bite of the apple and started to laugh. In the kitchen, Tim also found some medicine. His mommy told him that medicine helps when we are sick. But Tim was not sick, so he did not need the medicine. Tim put the medicine back in the cupboard and went to play outside. While playing outside, Tim saw a funny squirrel. The squirrel was running and jumping in the trees. Tim laughed and laughed at the silly squirrel. He was not hungry anymore because he had eaten the big, red apple. And Tim knew that if he ever got sick, his mommy would give him the medicine to help him feel better.

Anna and Ben were at the zoo with their mom. They liked to see the animals and learn their names. They saw lions, monkeys, elephants and many more. But their favorite animal was the zebra. The zebra had black and white stripes all over its body. It looked like a big horse with a funny coat. "Mom, can we feed the zebra?" Anna asked. "Sure, honey. Let's go to the zebra house and get some carrots," mom said. They walked to the zebra house and mom bought some carrots from a man. He told them to be careful and gentle with the zebra. He said the zebra was shy and sometimes scared of people. Anna and Ben each took a carrot and followed mom to the fence. They saw a zebra standing in the grass. It looked at them with big brown eyes. "Hello, zebra. We have a carrot for you. Do you want it?" Ben said. He held out his carrot and waited. The zebra sniffed the air and slowly walked towards him. It stretched its neck and took the carrot from his hand. It crunched the carrot and licked its lips. "Wow, you are so brave, Ben. You made a new friend," mom said. Anna wanted to feed the zebra too. She held out her carrot and called the zebra. "Come here, zebra. I have a carrot for you too. Please don't be scared," she said. But the zebra did not come to her. It looked at her and then turned around. It ran away to the other side of the fence. It hid behind a tree and did not come out. "Where did the zebra go? It vanished!" Anna said. She felt sad and confused. She did not understand why the zebra did not like her. "Maybe the zebra is full. Maybe it wants to rest. Maybe it is shy of girls. Don't worry, Anna. It is not your fault. The zebra is just different from us," mom said. She hugged Anna and gave her a kiss. "Let's go see the other animals. Maybe we can find another zebra that likes carrots and girls," she said. Anna nodded and smiled. She still liked the zebra, even if it vanished. She hoped it was happy and warm in its home. She hoped it would come out and play again. She hoped it would be her friend too.
