Once upon a time, there was a big dog named Max and a small cat named Lily. They lived in a house with a nice family. Max and Lily did not like to share. They would always fight and take things from each other. One day, the family had a big party with lots of good food. Max and Lily saw the food and wanted to eat it. They looked at each other and started to fight. "No, this food is mine!" said Max. "No, it's mine!" said Lily. They fought and fought, making a big mess. A wise old bird saw them fighting and said, "Max and Lily, you must learn to share. If you share, there will be enough food for both of you." Max and Lily listened to the bird and decided to share the food. They found out that when they shared, they had more fun and more food to eat. The moral of the story is that sharing is better than fighting.

Once upon a time, there was a leopard who was very clever. He wanted to look the best he could, so he decided he would try to polish his fur. Every day he polished until his coat was super shiny and bright. Then one day, something amazing happened. The leopard was walking in the jungle when he noticed a tasty mango. He wanted to try it, so he went over to take a bite. But just then, a kind little monkey stepped in and said, "What are you doing? That mango doesn't belong to you! Maybe if you put some effort into polishing others, then you might get something tasty in return!". The leopard thanked the monkey for the advice and learned that it's better to help and be kind to others, rather than taking things without permission. From then on, the leopard became an even bigger helper to the animals in the jungle, and always put some effort into polishing the things (or fur!) of his friends.

Once upon a time, in a big forest, there lived a little bunny named Binky. Binky loved to play with his friends, the birds, and the squirrels. One day, Binky saw a big cloud in the sky. The cloud was very big and gray. Binky felt a little scared. Binky's mommy saw him and said, "Binky, you need to hurry home now. The big cloud means rain is coming." Binky did not want to get wet, so he started to run home. As he ran, Binky felt dizzy because he was running so fast. He had never run so fast before. Binky made it home just in time. The rain started to fall, and Binky was safe and warm inside his home. Binky's mommy hugged him and said, "Good job, Binky! You hurried home like a big bunny." Binky felt very proud and happy. From that day on, Binky knew to hurry home when he saw a big cloud in the sky.

Sara and Tom were playing with their toy cars in the garden. They liked to pretend they were racing drivers and zoomed around the grass and the dirt. Sara had a red car and Tom had a blue car. They both loved their cars very much. One day, Tom's car stopped working. He tried to push it, but it did not move. He looked at Sara and said, "My car is broken. It needs fuel. Do you have any fuel?" Sara shook her head. She did not have any fuel. She only had water in her bottle. She said, "I'm sorry, Tom. I don't have fuel. Maybe we can find some in the house." They ran to the house and asked their mom for fuel. Their mom smiled and said, "Fuel is not for toy cars, my dears. Fuel is for real cars. Your toy cars need batteries. Do you know what batteries are?" Sara and Tom nodded. They had seen batteries before. They were small and shiny and had plus and minus signs on them. Their mom said, "Batteries have power inside them. They can make your toy cars work again. But you have to be careful with them. They can be dangerous if you put them in your mouth or break them. Do you understand?" Sara and Tom said, "Yes, mom. We understand. Can we have some batteries, please?" Their mom gave them each a new battery and showed them how to put it in their toy cars. She said, "Now you can play with your cars again. But remember to be humble and share. Don't boast or fight. And don't waste the power. Turn off your cars when you are done. OK?" Sara and Tom said, "OK, mom. Thank you for the batteries. You are the best." They hugged their mom and ran back to the garden. They put the batteries in their cars and turned them on. Their cars started to work again. They were happy and excited. They said to each other, "Let's race again. But let's be humble and share. And let's thank mom for the batteries. She helped us restore our cars."
