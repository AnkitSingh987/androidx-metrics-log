Once there was a gifted girl named Maria who liked to pack radishes. Maria was always packing them away and sending them off to different places. But her neighbor Tony didn't like it. He thought that radishes weren't special and he wished Maria would pack something else. One day Maria asked Tony why he didn't like radishes. He replied that people should be packing something much more special. Tony told Maria that he wanted her to pack something that was "special". Then, Maria had an idea. The next day, Maria asked everyone in her village to donate any special things they had to her. Soon Maria had a big pile of special things like flowers, pencils, and books. She asked Tony to come help her pack them up, and Tony happily agreed. The moral of the story is that it is more important to do something to help others than to pack away something special for yourself. Everyone in the village came together to help Maria pack and donate the special items. It was a great example of the power of helping others!

One day, a girl named Lily went to the market with her mom. They wanted to buy food for dinner. Lily saw a big, red apple. She wanted to reach it, but it was too high. "Mom, can you help me?" Lily asked. Her mom reached up and got the apple for her. At the market, Lily met a boy named Tom. Tom was an honest boy. He always told the truth. Tom said, "Hi Lily! I just found a big, green ball. Do you want to play with me?" Lily said, "Yes, let's play!" They played with the ball and had a lot of fun. But then, something unexpected happened. The big, green ball bounced away and hit a stack of cans. The cans fell down and made a loud noise. Everyone at the market looked surprised. Lily and Tom were scared. They didn't know what to do. Then, Tom's mom came and said, "It's okay. Accidents happen. Let's help clean up the cans." So, Lily, Tom, and their moms helped clean up the mess. After that, they all went home with big smiles on their faces.

Once upon a time, in a big green forest, there lived a little wolf. The little wolf was always eager to play with his friends, the bunny and the squirrel. They loved to run, jump, and laugh together under the warm sun. One day, the little wolf and his friends had a quarrel. They could not agree on what game to play. The little wolf wanted to play tag, but the bunny wanted to play hide and seek, and the squirrel wanted to play catch. They all felt sad because they could not play together. Then, a wise old owl saw them and said, "Why don't you all take turns and play each game?" The little wolf, the bunny, and the squirrel thought it was a great idea. So they played tag, then hide and seek, and finally catch. They had so much fun, and they were happy they could play together again.

One day, a little girl named Mia saw a pie on the table. It was a big, yummy pie. Mia wanted to eat the pie, but her mom said, "No, Mia. That pie is fragile. We need to be careful." Mia did not eat the pie, but she could not stop staring at it. She thought about how good it would taste. Mia's mom saw her staring and said, "If you are good, you can have some pie later." Later that day, Mia's friend came over to play. They played with dolls and had lots of fun. Mia's mom saw that Mia was good, so she said, "Now you can have some pie." Mia and her friend were so happy. They ate the pie, and it was the best pie they ever had.

Once upon a time, there was a big red bus. The bus had a very important job. Every day, it would take kids to school and back home. The bus loved its job and the kids loved the bus. One sunny day, the bus was driving the kids to school. Suddenly, the bus started to spin around and around. The kids inside were scared and didn't know what to do. The bus didn't know why it was spinning, but it knew it had to stop and get the kids to school. Then, a little girl named Lucy found a big red button on the bus. It said, "Stop Spin." Lucy pushed the button and the bus stopped spinning. The kids were happy and thanked Lucy. The bus was able to take the kids to school again. From that day on, the bus knew that Lucy was very important too.
