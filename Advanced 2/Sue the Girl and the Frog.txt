One day, a little girl named Sue went to the grocery with her mom. They needed to buy food for dinner. Sue's mom said, "Sue, remember to stay near me." Sue nodded and promised to stay close. They walked around the grocery and saw many interesting things. Sue liked the big, round oranges and the shiny apples. But then, Sue saw a big, green frog near the vegetables! Sue was so excited, she forgot what her mom said. Sue followed the frog and when it stopped, she said, "Hello, Mr. Frog!" The frog looked at Sue and said, "Hello, Sue! I am a magic frog. I can give you a wish if you help me find my way home." Sue thought about her wish and decided to help the frog. Sue and the frog found the frog's home near a pond behind the grocery. The frog was happy and said, "Thank you, Sue. What is your wish?" Sue thought for a moment and said, "I wish to always remember what my mom tells me." The frog smiled and granted her wish. From that day on, Sue always remembered what her mom said and never got lost again. And sometimes, she would visit her new friend, the magic frog, at the pond behind the grocery.

Once upon a time, there was a little rabbit named Remy. Remy loved to spring around in the soft grass near his home. He liked to play with his friends, the birds, and the bugs. One day, Remy found a gentle stream flowing through the forest. The water was clear and cool. Remy liked to dip his paws in the water and feel the smooth rocks underneath. He would watch the fish swim by and listen to the soft sound of the water. Remy told his friends about the gentle stream. They all came to play by the water too. They would spring around, splash, and have fun. The little rabbit, the birds, and the bugs were very happy playing together by the gentle stream.

One day, a large cat named Tom was watching a video on a small box. He saw a little mouse named Jerry on the screen. Tom wanted to catch Jerry, so he tried to pinch the mouse on the screen. But he could not. Tom was sad. Tom's friend, a dog named Max, came to see what was wrong. "Why are you sad, Tom?" Max asked. Tom told Max about the video and how he could not pinch Jerry. Max had an idea. "Let's find a real mouse to play with!" he said. Tom and Max looked and looked, but they could not find a real mouse. Then, they saw a real mouse in a corner. The mouse was scared of Tom and Max. Tom wanted to pinch the mouse, but Max stopped him. "We should be nice to the mouse, not pinch it," Max said. Tom thought about it and agreed. They all became friends and played together. The moral of the story is to be kind to others, even if they are different from us.

Once upon a time, there was a smooth rock. The rock had a friend, a small bird. The bird had a tiny ear. They lived in a pretty garden with many flowers and trees. One day, the bird told the rock a secret. The bird said, "I believe I can fly high in the sky and touch the clouds." The rock was happy for the bird and wanted to see it happen. The bird flew up and up, higher and higher. The rock watched as the bird touched the clouds. The bird came back down and they were both very happy. They knew that if you believe, you can do anything.

Sara was playing in the garden. She picked up an interesting pebble from the ground and started to examine it. Suddenly, she saw something in the distance. She ran to it and observed with wonder; it was a tall tree with a nest in it filled with birds. Sara reached out to try and touch the nest, but the branches were too high. She was disappointed and started to cry, but then she noticed something else. A few meters away, there was a bright blue bird hopping around on the ground. Sara was so excited. She ran to the bird and finally reached out to grab it. The bird let out a squawk and flew away. Sara was so naughty! She had tried to catch the bird, and now it was gone. But, luckily, when Sara looked back at the tree, she saw that the birds had come back. She felt relieved and happy as she reached up again to try and touch the nest. The birds didn't fly away this time, so Sara had a chance to marvel at them in wonder.
