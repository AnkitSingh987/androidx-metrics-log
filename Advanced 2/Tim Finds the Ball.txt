Once upon a time, a small boy named Tim went outside to play in the winter. He saw smooth ice on the ground. Tim wanted to discover what was on the other side of the ice. As he walked on the ice, he saw a big red ball. Tim was very happy and wanted to play with the ball. But then, something unexpected happened. The ball started to move on its own! Tim was surprised but he followed the ball. The ball led him to a big tree. Behind the tree, there was a small, friendly dog. The dog had been moving the ball with his nose. Tim and the dog became best friends and played in the winter together.

Once upon a time, in a big green pond, there lived a noisy alligator named Ally. Ally loved to splash and play all day long. She would make big waves with her tail and laugh loudly. One sunny day, Ally was playing by herself when she saw a little yellow duck named Ducky. Ally thought it would be fun to splash Ducky with her big tail. "Splash!" went Ally's tail, and poor Ducky was all wet. Ducky looked sad, and Ally felt regret. She knew she should say sorry. "I'm sorry, Ducky," said Ally. "I didn't mean to make you sad. Let's be friends and play together." Ducky smiled and said, "That's okay, Ally. I forgive you. Let's have fun together!" From that day on, Ally and Ducky played together in the big green pond. They had lots of fun, and Ally learned to be more careful when playing with her new friend. They became the best of friends, and the pond was a happy place for everyone.

Ben and Lily were twins who liked to play together. They had many toys, but their favorite was a big book of stories. The book had many pictures and words in different languages. Ben and Lily wanted to learn the languages, so they would point at the words and try to say them. One day, they lay on the floor with the book and opened it to a page with a story about a lion and a mouse. The story was in a language they did not know, but they liked the pictures. They saw the lion sleeping and the mouse nibbling his tail. They saw the lion wake up and roar at the mouse. They saw the mouse beg for mercy and the lion let him go. They saw the mouse help the lion when he was caught in a net. "Wow, what a nice story!" Ben said. "The lion was big and strong, but he was kind to the mouse. The mouse was small and weak, but he was brave and helpful. They were friends." "Yes, they were," Lily agreed. "But what did they say to each other? I wish we knew their language." "Maybe we can learn it," Ben said. "Let's look at the words and try to remember them. Maybe we can find someone who can teach us." They looked at the words and repeated them after each other. They did not know what they meant, but they liked the sounds. They were thoughtful and curious. Their mom came in and saw them with the book. She smiled and said, "What are you doing, my sweeties?" "We are learning a new language, mom," Ben said. "It is the language of the lion and the mouse. Do you know it?" Mom looked at the book and recognized the language. It was the language of her grandparents, who came from another country. She was happy and proud that her children wanted to learn it. "I do know it, my loves," she said. "It is the language of our family. It is a beautiful and rich language. I can teach you some words and stories, if you like." "Really, mom?" Lily said. "That would be wonderful! Thank you, mom!" They hugged their mom and asked her to read the story of the lion and the mouse to them. She did, and they listened with joy and interest. They learned some new words and meanings. They learned that the story had a moral value: that kindness and friendship are more important than size and strength. They learned that language is a gift that connects people and cultures. They learned that they were lucky to have a mom who loved them and taught them. They were happy and grateful.
