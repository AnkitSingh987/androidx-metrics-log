One day, Tom saw a beautiful sunrise. The sky was full of colors. Tom was very happy. He wanted to go outside and play with his friends. Tom put on his jacket and tried to zip it. But the zip was fake. He asked his mom for help. His mom fixed the zip and he was ready to go outside. Tom played with his friends all day. They ran, jumped, and laughed. When the sun went down, Tom went home. He was tired but happy. He couldn't wait for another fun day.

One day, a little girl named Sally was walking down the street. She saw a big dog running very fast. The dog looked terrible and sad. Sally wanted to help the dog, but she was scared. Sally's mom saw her and said, "Sally, don't be scared. We can help the dog together. Just say 'stop' and the dog will listen." Sally took a deep breath and shouted, "Stop!" The big dog stopped running and looked at Sally. Sally and her mom took the dog to their home. They gave the dog food and water. The dog was happy and not terrible anymore. Sally learned that if she is brave and kind, she can help others.

Once upon a time, there was a tall tree in the forest. All the animals liked the tall tree. The tall tree had big scales on its trunk. The animals would play on the scales every day. One day, a small bird came to the tall tree. The bird wanted to scatter seeds for the animals to eat. The bird was scared because the tree was so tall. But the bird was brave and flew up to the top of the tree. As the bird scattered the seeds, the wind blew them all over the forest. The animals were happy to find the seeds everywhere. The bird was happy too. The tall tree's scales helped the bird fly higher. The animals learned that even small things can do big things.

Anna and Ben were twins who liked to play outside. They had a big yard with a swing, a slide and a trampoline. They also had many clothes to wear for different games. Sometimes they wore capes and masks to be superheroes. Sometimes they wore hats and scarves to be explorers. Sometimes they wore dresses and crowns to be princesses and princes. One sunny day, they decided to play on the trampoline. They put on their jumping clothes: colorful socks, shorts and t-shirts. They ran to the trampoline and climbed on. They started to bounce up and down, higher and higher. They felt happy and free. "Look at me, Anna!" Ben said. "I can spring very high and do a flip!" "That's good, Ben!" Anna said. "But I can spring higher and do two flips!" They both wanted to show how good they were at jumping. They became competitive. They tried to spring higher and higher, faster and faster. They did not look at each other or the trampoline. They only looked at the sky. But then, something bad happened. They sprang too high and too fast. They bumped into each other in the air. They lost their balance and fell off the trampoline. They landed on the grass with a thud. They felt hurt and sad. They looked at their clothes. They were dirty and torn. They looked at each other. They had scratches and bruises. They started to cry. "Anna, I'm sorry!" Ben said. "I was too competitive. I should have been more careful." "Ben, I'm sorry too!" Anna said. "I was also too competitive. I should have been more fun." They hugged each other and said sorry again. They walked back to the house, holding hands. They asked their mom to help them with their clothes and their wounds. They learned their lesson. They decided to play nicer and safer next time. They still liked to spring, but not too high or too fast.

Once upon a time, there was a kind girl named Sue. Sue had a rag that she loved. She took the rag with her everywhere she went. The rag was old and dirty, but Sue did not care. She thought it was the best rag in the world. One day, Sue was playing with her rag in the park. Suddenly, the rag started to move! It began to grow and become bigger and bigger. Sue was very surprised. She did not know what was happening. Then, the rag turned into a big, friendly dog! Sue was so happy. She now had a new friend to play with. The dog was kind, just like Sue. They played together all day and had lots of fun. From that day on, Sue and her dog were the best of friends.
