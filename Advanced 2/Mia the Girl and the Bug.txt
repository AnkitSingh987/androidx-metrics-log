Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Mia. Mia was very creative. She liked to draw, sing, and play with her toys. One day, Mia found a small bug in a tiny cell. The bug was sad and wanted to go home. Mia said to the bug, "Don't worry, I will help you. Let's pray together and ask for help." So, Mia and the bug closed their eyes and prayed. They wished for the bug to find his way home. After praying, Mia had an idea. She used her toys to make a path for the bug to follow. The bug was very happy and followed the path. At the end of the path, the bug found his family. They were all so happy to be together again. The bug thanked Mia for her help, and they all lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a girl named Lily. She loved to eat spicy food. Every Sunday, her mom made spicy soup for lunch. Lily was very happy on Sundays because she could eat her favorite food. One Sunday, Lily wanted to help her mom in the kitchen. Her mom said, "Lily, you can cut the vegetables for the soup." Lily was excited to help. She took a knife and carefully cut the carrots and potatoes into small pieces. When the soup was ready, Lily and her mom sat down to eat. The spicy soup was very yummy! Lily was proud that she helped her mom make it. From that day on, Lily always helped her mom cook on Sundays. They had lots of fun together in the kitchen.

One day, a girl named Lucy and her friend, Tom, found a game called chess. They wanted to play, but they did not know how. Lucy's big sister, Jane, saw them and said, "I can teach you how to play chess." Jane taught them the rules and they began to play. At first, it was hard for Lucy and Tom to manage the game. They made mistakes and sometimes moved their pieces the wrong way. But they kept trying and soon, they became better at chess. One day, they were playing chess outside when a loud dog came running by. The dog knocked over the chess board and all the pieces fell on the ground. Lucy and Tom were sad, but Jane said, "Don't worry, we can pick up the pieces and play again." So, Lucy, Tom, and Jane picked up the pieces and started a new game. They learned that even when something unexpected happens, they can always start over and keep trying. This made them happy and they played chess together every day, becoming better and better at the game.

Once upon a time, there was a small, fragile pencil. It lived in a big box with many other pencils. One day, a little girl named Lily found the box of pencils. She wanted to draw a pretty picture for her mom. Lily's brother, Tim, saw her looking at the pencils. He said, "I recommend you use the big, strong pencil. The fragile one might break." But Lily liked the small, fragile pencil best. She thought it was special. Lily started to draw with the fragile pencil. She pressed very hard on the paper. All of a sudden, the pencil broke! Lily was sad because now she could not finish her pretty picture. She wished she had listened to Tim's recommendation.

Sara and Ben were twins who liked to play with their toys. One day, they found a big box in the garage. It had a frame inside, with many colors and shapes. Sara and Ben wanted to make a picture with the frame. "Let's order the shapes by size," Sara said. "The big ones go on the bottom, and the small ones go on the top." "OK," Ben said. He liked to do what Sara said. He was a good brother, but sometimes he was lazy. He did not want to work hard. He wanted to have fun. Sara and Ben started to order the shapes. Sara worked fast and neat. She put the big shapes on the bottom, and the small shapes on the top. She made a nice picture with the frame. Ben worked slow and messy. He put the shapes wherever he wanted. He did not care about the size or the color. He made a silly picture with the frame. "Look, Sara, I'm done!" Ben said. He showed his picture to Sara. It had a big red circle on the top, and a small blue square on the bottom. It had many other shapes in between, but they did not match. "Ben, that's not how you order the shapes," Sara said. She was not happy with Ben. She wanted him to do it right. "You have to follow the rule. The big ones go on the bottom, and the small ones go on the top." "But I like my picture," Ben said. He did not want to change it. He thought it was fun. "It's different and funny. Can we hang it on the wall?" Sara shook her head. She did not like Ben's picture. She thought it was ugly and wrong. She wanted to hang her picture on the wall. She thought it was pretty and right. She had an idea. She said to Ben, "How about we play a game? We can take turns to hang our pictures on the wall. But we have to use a timer. Whoever hangs their picture faster wins. Do you want to play?" Ben nodded. He liked to play games. He thought he could win. He did not know that Sara had a plan. She wanted to trick him. She wanted to hang her picture first, and then hide the timer. She wanted to make Ben lose. She was not a good sister, but sometimes she was smart. She wanted to have her way.
