Sara loved to dance. She danced every day with her friends. She danced at home, at school, and at the park. She danced with music, with songs, and with her own sounds. She was very creative. One day, Sara saw an old man with a cane. He walked very slow and looked very sad. Sara wanted to make him happy. She ran to him and said, "Hello, sir. Do you want to dance with me?" The old man was surprised. He said, "Dance? With you? But I have a cane. I can't dance." Sara smiled. She said, "You can dance with your cane. You can use it as a partner, or a drum, or a wand. You can be creative. Dancing is fun." The old man thought for a moment. He said, "Well, why not? You are very kind. Let's dance." Sara and the old man danced together. They danced with his cane. They danced with joy. They danced with laughter. They made a crowd of people watch and clap. They made each other happy. The moral of the story is: Dancing can bring happiness to anyone, no matter how old or young, how fast or slow, how creative or not. Dancing is a way of sharing kindness and joy.

One day, a boy and a girl were playing chess. They were in a big room with wide windows. The sun was shining through the windows. The boy said, "I want to win!" The girl said, "No, I want to win!" They started to rush and play the chess game very fast. They did not think about their moves. They did not play well. The boy knocked over the chess pieces by accident. The girl got mad and said, "You made a mess!" The boy and the girl did not finish the chess game. They were both sad and upset. The chess pieces were all over the floor. The boy and the girl did not win. They learned that rushing is not good when playing chess.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl called Mary. Mary had a big blue table and she loved to eat her dinner at it. One day, Mary decided to make a picnic to enjoy at the table. She ran to the kitchen and gathered up some bread, cheese, tomatoes and her special spicy sauce. She put the food into a bag and zipped it up tightly. Then, she raced outside with her picnic bag and sat at the bright blue table. She opened it up, took out the bread and started to make her sandwich. She spread the spicy sauce on it and smiled as she took her first bite. Suddenly, her tummy started to grumble and she jumped up. "What was that?" She asked. "Well, Mary," a voice said, "that was your tummy saying 'yummy, this sandwich is so spicy!'"

Once upon a time, in a small house, a boy named Tim had a plan. He wanted to make toast for his mom's breakfast. Tim was very serious about his plan. Tim woke up early and went to the kitchen. He found bread and put it in the toaster. The toast popped up, and Tim smiled. He knew his mom would be happy. When his mom came to the kitchen, she saw the toast on the table. She gave Tim a big hug and said, "Thank you!" Tim felt proud of his plan and was happy to help his mom.

Once upon a time, there was a big alligator. He was very healthy and strong. He lived in a pond with many fish. The alligator liked to eat the fish, but he wanted to make friends too. One day, a little bird came to the pond. The alligator wanted to be friends with the bird. The bird said, "I will be your friend if you promise not to eat me." The alligator promised not to eat the bird. They played together and had fun. But the alligator started to feel hungry and suffer. The alligator thought about eating the bird, but he didn't want to break his promise. He tried to eat more fish, but it didn't help. One day, the alligator couldn't take it anymore. He ate the bird. After that, no other animals wanted to be friends with the alligator. The alligator felt very sad and lonely. He learned that breaking promises can make you lose friends. He wished he had kept his promise to the bird. The bad ending was that the alligator had no friends and was all alone. The moral value of the story is to always keep your promises.
