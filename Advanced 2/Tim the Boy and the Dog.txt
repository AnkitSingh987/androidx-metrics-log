One day, a boy named Tim wanted to ride his bike to the store. He put on his helmet and started riding. The sun was shining and he felt happy. Tim liked to ride his bike fast. As he got closer to the store, he saw a small dog. The dog was alone and looked sad. Tim felt bad for the dog and wanted to help. He stopped riding his bike and walked over to the dog. Tim tried to pick up the dog, but the dog was not a dog at all! It was a big toy dog. Tim felt ashamed for not knowing it was a toy. He laughed and got back on his bike. He rode to the store with a big smile on his face, knowing he had a funny story to tell.

Once upon a time, in a big universe, there was a test. This test was to find the most dependable friend. Many animals came to try the test. In the test, there was a big tree. On the tree, there was a yummy fruit. The animals had to help each other to get the fruit. They all tried their best. They climbed, they jumped, and they helped each other. Then, something unexpected happened. A little ant came to the test. The other animals laughed. They thought the ant was too small to help. But the little ant was very smart. It found a long stick and gave it to the other animals. They used the stick to get the fruit. The animals learned that even a little ant can be a dependable friend. They all became friends and lived happily in their big universe.

One day, the sun was shining very bright in the sky. A little girl named Lucy went outside to play. She saw a smelly sock on the ground. "Yuck!" said Lucy. She picked up the sock and threw it in the trash. Lucy wanted to cool off, so she went inside her house. She found a fan in her room. The fan blew air on her face and made her feel better. She was very happy. The next day, Lucy went outside again. The sun was still shining, but the smelly sock was gone. She played all day with her friends. They had lots of fun under the shining sun.

One day, a little boy named Tim went outside to play. The wind was blowing, making the trees dance. Tim saw his friend, the silly bird, and they started to play together. While playing, the bird said, "I heard someone mention a stupid cat." Tim was curious and asked the bird where the cat was. The bird pointed to a tree where the cat was stuck. Tim and the bird went to the tree and saw the cat. The cat looked scared and sad. Tim said, "Don't worry, stupid cat. We will help you." The bird flew up and helped the cat get down from the tree. The cat was happy and thanked Tim and the bird. From that day on, they all became good friends and played together every day. The wind would blow, and they would laugh and have fun.

There was once a girl with hair as smooth as silk. Everywhere she went people stopped and admired her beautiful hair. One day, the girl dared to go on a big adventure. She was very brave and the sun was nice and warm. So she set off on her journey and it was a lot of fun. Everywhere she went she found new and interesting things. But she was careful not to get her hair tangled in the trees and bushes. Eventually, the girl arrived at a wonderful place. The sun was shining brighter than ever and the ground was smooth as she walked. It was a magical place and the girl knew she had been very brave to dare to explore alone. She smiled and felt so proud of herself for being so brave and daring. The girl stayed in the magical place for a while and made many wonderful memories that she would never forget. The end.

Once there was a kind bear. He wanted things to be peaceful and happy. One day, while he was walking, he saw two other animals having a fight. The bear was sad, so he stopped and said, "Why are you fighting? Can't you be kind?" The two animals stopped and looked at the bear. One of the animals said, "We just don't get along". The kind bear responded, "OK, why don't you both take a deep breath and freeze. That way you won't be fighting anymore". The two animals were surprised, but they did as the kind bear said. So, both the animals froze and the fight was over. The bear smiled and said, "Turns out, it's not so hard to be kind".
