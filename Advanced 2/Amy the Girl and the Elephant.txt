Title: Amy the Girl and the Elephant

One day, a little girl named Amy went to the park with her mom. She saw a big, fake elephant. It was gray and had a long nose. Amy wanted to play with it, so she asked her mom, "Can I play with the elephant, please?" Her mom said, "Yes, but be careful." Amy ran to the elephant and started to pull its long nose. She pulled and pulled, but the nose did not move. Amy was sad and didn't know what to do. Suddenly, the fake elephant began to talk! It said, "Hello, Amy! I'm a magic elephant. If you want to play with me, you need to say the magic word." Amy was very surprised. She thought hard and then said, "Please?" The elephant's nose moved, and they played together all day long.

Once upon a time, in a big palace, there lived a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play with his toys and run around the palace. Every morning, Tim would wake up early and feel very alert. He was always ready to start his day with a big smile. One day, Tim heard a noise outside the palace. He ran to the window and saw a cute puppy. Tim wanted to play with the puppy, so he went outside. The puppy was very happy to see Tim, and they played together all day long. When the sun started to go down, Tim knew it was time to go back to the palace. He said goodbye to the puppy and went inside. That night, Tim went to bed feeling tired but happy. He couldn't wait to wake up the next day and play with his new friend again.

Once upon a time there was a three year old called Ann. Ann had a big problem; she didn't know how to share. One day, she was playing in the garden and saw a beautiful shadow. She grabbed it and held on tight. Ann's mother walked past. "Ann, share the shadow with your friends," her mother said. Ann shook her head. "No," she said firmly. Her mother tried to take the shadow away but Ann held on harder. Her grip was hard. Her mother sighed. "If you don't share, you won't receive any treats later," she said. Ann frowned but reluctantly let go of the shadow. She watched as her friends shared the shadow equally. When they finished, Ann received the biggest share. Ann was happy. From now on, she would be sure to remember the importance of sharing.

One day, a little squirrel named Sam was looking for nuts in the wide forest. Sam loved nuts very much. He would eat them all day long. As he looked for nuts, he saw a big tree with lots of nuts on the ground. Sam started to pick up the nuts, but a little bird named Ben flew down and said, "Stop! These are my nuts!" Sam looked at Ben and said, "I love nuts too. Can we share the nuts?" Ben thought for a moment and said, "Okay, we can share the nuts." So, Sam and Ben picked up the nuts together and put them in a big pile. They were both very happy to have so many nuts to eat. They became good friends and shared nuts every day in the wide forest.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. She loved to bake with her mom. One sunny day, they decided to bake a yummy cake together. Amy was very excited and felt like a big girl. First, they put all the ingredients in a big bowl. Then, they mixed it all up. Amy's mom gave her a pan and said, "Now, we are ready to bake the cake!" They poured the mix into the pan and put it in the oven. They waited and waited. When the cake was ready, they took it out of the oven. It smelled so good! Amy and her mom shared the cake with the whole family. Everyone loved it and Amy felt very proud of her baking skills.

Tom and Mia liked to swim in the pool. They were very competitive. They always wanted to be the fastest and the best. They did not like to share or help each other. One day, they had a big race. They swam as fast as they could. They pushed and splashed each other. They did not care about the rules or the other swimmers. They only wanted to win. But they swam too hard and too long. They got tired and sore. They could not finish the race. They felt sad and angry. They blamed each other for losing. They did not want to be friends anymore. The next day, they saw a new swimmer in the pool. He was very friendly and kind. He smiled and waved at them. He asked them if they wanted to swim with him. He said he liked to swim for fun and to learn new things. He said he did not care about winning or losing. He said he wanted to make new friends. Tom and Mia felt curious and shy. They decided to swim with him. They had a lot of fun. They learned new tricks and games. They laughed and cheered for each other. They did not push or splash each other. They cared about the rules and the other swimmers. They did not want to win or lose. They wanted to have fun and be friends. They realized that swimming was not only about being competitive. It was also about being happy and kind. They thanked the new swimmer for showing them a new way to swim. They apologized to each other for being mean and rude. They hugged and made up. They restored their friendship. The moral of the story is: Being competitive is not always good. It can make you tired and angry. It can make you lose your friends. Being happy and kind is better. It can make you healthy and joyful. It can make you gain new friends.
