Title: Spot the Dog and the Ball

Once upon a time, there was a little dog named Spot. Spot had a big nose. One day, Spot received a big red ball from his friend, Tom. Spot and Tom loved to play with the ball. They would run and jump and laugh all day. But one day, a terrible big cat came to their yard. The cat wanted the big red ball too. Spot and Tom were scared. They did not want to share their ball with the terrible big cat. They wanted to keep it for themselves. So, Spot and Tom came up with a plan. They would hide the ball in a safe place. They dug a hole and put the ball inside. The terrible big cat could not find the ball. Spot and Tom were happy. They had saved their ball from the big cat. From that day on, they played with their big red ball without any trouble.

Once upon a time, there was a clumsy bear named Bob. Bob had a big box to unpack. Inside the box, there was a pretty light. Bob wanted to put the light in his room. Bob said, "I need help to unpack the box." His friend, a little bird named Lily, came to help. They opened the box together. Lily said, "Wow, this light is so pretty!" They took the light out of the box. Bob tried to hang the light up, but he was very clumsy. The light fell down and broke. Bob was sad, but Lily had an idea. They fixed the light together and hung it up. Now, Bob's room was bright and happy.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Ava. Ava was sad and gloomy because she lost her favorite toy, a green avocado. She looked for it all day, but could not find it. One day, while playing outside, she saw a big bird with her avocado in its beak. Ava wanted to keep her avocado, so she asked the bird, "Please, can I have my avocado back?" The bird did not want to give it back. Ava thought of a plan. She found some yummy worms and offered them to the bird. "If you give me my avocado, I will give you these worms." The bird liked worms, so it agreed. The bird gave Ava her avocado, and she gave the bird the worms. Ava was happy to have her avocado back, and the bird was happy to eat the worms. They both learned that sharing and being kind could make everyone happy. And from that day on, Ava and the bird were the best of friends.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Mia. Mia loved to play with her toy, a round ball with tight strings around it. The ball had a pretty pattern on it. Mia liked to show her ball to her friends. One sunny day, Mia went to the park with her ball. She saw her friend, Tim, and wanted to show him the pattern on her ball. Tim thought the pattern was nice, too. They played catch with the ball, laughing and having fun. After playing, they sat on the grass and looked at the tight strings on the ball. They talked about the pretty pattern and how much they liked it. Mia and Tim were very happy. They knew they would play with the ball again soon.

Lily and Ben were twins who loved to sing and dance. They liked to perform for their family and friends. They practiced every day in their room, making up songs and moves. One day, they heard about a talent show at their school. They wanted to join and show everyone their skills. They asked their mom if they could sign up. Mom said yes, but she also said they had to be kind and respectful to the other kids who wanted to perform too. Lily and Ben agreed, but they secretly thought they were the best. They did not listen to the other kids' acts or clap for them. They only cared about their own performance. They thought they would win the prize for sure. When it was their turn, they went on the stage and started to sing and dance. But something was wrong. Their voices sounded harsh and their moves looked clumsy. They forgot some of the words and steps. They felt nervous and embarrassed. The audience did not cheer or clap. They looked bored and confused. Lily and Ben ran off the stage and cried. They did not understand what happened. Mom came to hug them and comfort them. She said, "I'm sorry, my loves. You have a lot of talent, but you also need to have a good attitude. You did not show respect or kindness to the other kids who performed. You did not enjoy their sound or their art. You only thought about yourselves. That made your performance lose its joy and beauty. You need to learn to appreciate and support others, and then you will also perform better and happier." Lily and Ben felt sorry for what they did. They realized Mom was right. They decided to apologize to the other kids and congratulate them for their acts. They also decided to practice more and have fun with their singing and dancing, not just to win a prize. They learned that being humble and generous makes their sound more peaceful and their performance more wonderful.
