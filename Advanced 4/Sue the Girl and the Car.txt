Title: Sue the Girl and the Car

One day, a little girl named Sue found a magic knob. This knob was very important. It could reverse time. Sue wanted to show her friend Tim the magic knob. Sue said to Tim, "Look at this important knob! It can reverse time!" Tim was very happy to see the magic knob. They both wanted to try it out. They decided to turn the knob and see what happens. As they turned the knob, time started to reverse. They saw their toy car go back to where it was before. Sue and Tim were very excited. They played with the magic knob all day. They learned that it was important to be careful with magic things.

Jack was playing in the garden when he saw something big. He wasn't sure what it was, so he asked his mom. "Mom, what's that?" He asked. "It's a hurricane, Jack," she said. Jack couldn't believe it. "Really?" He asked. "But what's a hurricane?" "It's a big storm that can be very powerful," his mom replied. Jack tried to imagine what that would look like. Suddenly the wind began to blow and the sky grew dark. It felt like the hurricanes his mom had told him about. Jack looked around. He had never seen anything so big and powerful before. The hurricane was scary but also exciting. Finally it was over and the sun shone again. Jack was glad it had gone away, but he would never forget it.

Sammy and Lucy were best friends. They loved to play together in the garden. One day, Sammy's mom tested him on his colors. He knew them all! Lucy was jealous and she wanted her mom to test her, too. Lucy asked, "Can you test me on colors too, mommy?" Sammy's mom said, "I don't want to make you feel sad, Lucy. You can practice your colors with Sammy in the garden and then you can show me how much you know." Lucy was very excited. She practiced her colors very hard with Sammy in the garden. By the end of the day, Lucy was proud to show her mom about how many colors she knew. Sammy was proud, too!

One day, a cat and a dog were talking. The cat said, "I went to a stupid lecture yesterday." The dog asked, "What was it about?" The cat replied, "I don't know. I didn't understand it." Later, they saw a big tree. They wanted to climb it. The cat climbed up fast, but the dog had a hard time. The cat laughed at the dog and said, "You are not good at this!" The dog felt sad. In the end, the cat fell out of the tree. The dog tried to help, but it was too late. The cat got hurt, and they both learned that making fun of others is not nice.

Once upon a time, there was a chubby monkey called Timmy. He was always climbing trees and exploring the jungle. One special day, Timmy came across a huge pile of newspapers. He had never seen newspapers before. Curious Timmy grabbed a newspaper and said, “What’s this?” He shook the newspaper fiercely and saw something cool. Timmy said, “Wow! Look at all these shapes! I must climb up and take a closer look.” Timmy started to climb the tallest tree in the jungle and suddenly everything went dark. As he hung onto the branch, the darkness started to light up with colourful shapes and patterns. “It’s the same shapes I saw on the newspaper!” exclaimed Timmy. When Timmy arrived home, he showed his friends the shapes he found, and they all laughed. “What silly shapes! Those are actually fireworks! We’re going to have a party tomorrow and they’ll be lit up!” And that’s why Timmy was so excited when he saw the newspaper. He had unknowingly seen a sneak peek of the wonderful fireworks that would be lit up and enjoyed by everyone tomorrow night!

One day, a little boy named Tim had a big itch on his leg. He tried to scratch it, but it would not go away. Tim felt sad because the itch was very bad. He wanted to find a way to make the itch go away. Tim went to his mom and said, "Mom, I have a big itch on my leg. Can you help me?" His mom looked at his leg and saw the itch. She knew it was important to help Tim feel better. She said, "Tim, let's count the number of times we scratch the itch. Maybe that will help it go away." So, Tim and his mom started to scratch the itch. They counted the number of times they scratched. One, two, three, four, five. The itch started to feel better. Tim smiled and said, "Thank you, Mom. The itch is gone now." His mom smiled back and said, "You're welcome, Tim. I'm glad we could make the itch go away by counting the number of scratches." And from that day on, Tim knew that counting could help make things better.
