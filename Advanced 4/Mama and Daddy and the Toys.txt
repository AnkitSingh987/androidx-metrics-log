Title: Mama and Daddy and the Toys

Mama and Daddy were getting ready to leave. Mama told 3-year-old Alex to come give them a hug goodbye. Alex jumped up and ran over to hug them both. "Bye Mama! Bye Daddy!" he said, squeezing them tight. Daddy smiled and said, "See you soon, buddy. And remember to be good for Mama while we're gone." Mama's eyes grew wide. "Hold on, we almost forgot something!" She ran back inside an with an armful of food and toys. "These are for that poor family down the road. Can you help me bring them to their door?" Alex beamed and grabbed the food from Mama's hands. He carefully held it all in his arms and followed Mama down the street. When they reached the family's house, Alex gently knocked on the door. A little girl opened it and immediately smiled. Alex and Mama handed her the food and toys and she thanked them with a big hug. Mama and Alex watched as she ran back inside with her arms full and smiled knowing that they had brought some joy to a poor family. Together, they turned and left to go back home.

Once upon a time, there was a big yard. In the yard, there were many fun things to play with. There was a ball, a swing, and a flexible jump rope. A little boy named Tim loved to play in the yard every day. One day, Tim saw a big shadow in the yard. He thought it would scare him. Tim told his mom about the shadow. His mom said, "Don't worry, it won't scare you. Just wait and see." Tim was still a little scared, but he listened to his mom. He played with the ball and the swing, but he did not go near the shadow. The next day, Tim went to the yard again. He saw the shadow was still there. This time, he was brave and went closer to the shadow. When he got close, he saw that it was just a big tree. The tree had a long, flexible branch that was perfect for his jump rope. Tim smiled and played with the jump rope on the tree branch. He was not scared anymore.

Once upon a time, in a big green forest, there lived a little red ladybug named Lulu. Lulu had a lot of spots on her back. She had one big spot that she did not like. All her friends had small spots, and she felt different. One sunny day, Lulu met a new friend, a tiny green caterpillar named Carl. Carl said, "Hi Lulu, I like your big spot. It is very clear and pretty." Lulu was surprised and asked, "You really like my big spot?" Carl nodded and said, "Yes, I do. It makes you special." From that day on, Lulu learned to accept her big spot. She understood that it was okay to be different. Lulu and Carl played together every day, and they were very happy. The other bugs saw how happy Lulu was and learned that it is good to accept everyone, no matter how they look. And they all lived happily ever after.

One day, a little girl named Sally went to the doctor. She felt sick and was a bit frightened. The doctor was very nice and told her to be patient. The doctor said, "Sally, I need to poke your arm to make you feel better." Sally was scared, but she let the doctor poke her arm. After the poke, Sally started to feel better. Later, Sally went home and told her mom about the poke. Her mom was proud that Sally was brave. From that day on, Sally was no longer frightened of the doctor and knew that being patient was important.

Anna liked to print pictures of animals on her mom's computer. She had a folder where she kept all her prints. She was very careful with her folder, because she knew it was fragile and could tear easily. One day, she wanted to print a picture of a lion. She asked her mom for help. "Mom, can you help me print a lion?" she said. "Sure, Anna, let me see what you have," her mom said. She looked at the computer screen and saw a picture of a lion. "That's a nice lion, Anna. Do you want to print it in color or black and white?" "Color, please, mom. I want to see the lion's mane," Anna said. "Okay, let me change the settings. There, it's ready. Press this button to print," her mom said, pointing to a button on the keyboard. Anna pressed the button and waited. She heard the printer make noises and saw a paper come out. She was excited. She ran to the printer and grabbed the paper. "Be careful, Anna, the paper is hot and wet. You have to let it dry first," her mom said. "Oops, sorry, mom. I was too eager," Anna said. She put the paper on the table and waited for it to dry. She looked at the lion and smiled. It was beautiful. When the paper was dry, she picked it up and put it in her folder. She closed the folder and hugged it. "Thank you, mom, for helping me print a lion. I love it," she said. "You're welcome, Anna. I'm glad you like it. You have a very nice folder of animal prints. Maybe you can show it to your friends at preschool," her mom said. "Maybe, mom. But I have to be very careful. My folder is fragile and I don't want it to break," Anna said. "I know, Anna. You're a very good and responsible girl. I'm proud of you," her mom said. She kissed Anna on the cheek and hugged her back.
