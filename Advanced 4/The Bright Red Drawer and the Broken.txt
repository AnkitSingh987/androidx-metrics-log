Title: The Bright Red Drawer and the Broken

Once upon a time, there was a bright red drawer. It was broken and needed repair. A girl named Amy saw the broken drawer and wanted to help fix it. Amy asked her friend, Tom, "Can you help me repair the drawer?" Tom said, "Yes, I can help!" So, they tried to fix the drawer together. They pushed and pulled, but the drawer was still broken. A big storm came and the wind blew the bright red drawer away. Amy and Tom were sad. They could not repair the drawer. The drawer was lost in the storm, and they never saw it again.

Once upon a time, in an ordinary little town, there lived a mailman named Bob. Every day, Bob would walk up and down the streets, giving people their mail. He liked his job and always had a big smile on his face. People in the town would wave at him and say "Hi, Bob!" One sunny day, as Bob was giving out the mail, he saw a big box on the ground. It had a tag on it that said "For Bob." He was very excited and said, "Wow, I never get mail!" He picked up the box and went to his friend Sue's house. Sue was a kind lady who liked to help people. Bob showed her the box and said, "Look, Sue! I got mail!" Sue was happy for Bob and asked, "What's in it?" Bob opened the box and, to his surprise, found a big bow inside. He didn't know what to do with it, so he put it on his head. Bob and Sue started to laugh, but then something unexpected happened. The bow began to glow and Bob started to float in the air! Sue was amazed and said, "Bob, you can fly!" Bob was scared at first, but then he realized how fun it was. He flew all around the town, delivering mail faster than ever before. Everyone in the town cheered and clapped for Bob, the flying mailman. From that day on, Bob's job was even more fun, all thanks to the magic bow.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there was a thoughtful boy named Tim. He loved to sit by the fireplace and think. One day, it was raining outside, and Tim's mom said, "Tim, please put the clothes inside so they don't soak." Tim brought the clothes inside and hung them near the fireplace to dry. As he sat down, he heard a tiny voice say, "Thank you!" Tim looked around and saw a small, wet bird by the fireplace. The bird was happy that Tim saved it from the rain. Tim and the bird became good friends. They played and talked by the fireplace every day. The bird taught Tim to be kind and help others. Tim learned that being thoughtful and caring is important. And they lived happily ever after.

Sara likes to go to the zoo with her mom and dad. She likes to see all the different animals and learn their names. Her favorite animal is the zebra, because it has black and white stripes and a long tail. One day, Sara sees a sign that says "Zebra feeding time: 10:00 am". She is very excited and asks her mom and dad if they can go and watch. They say yes and take her to the zebra enclosure. There are many zebras there, eating hay and grass. Sara sees a big bucket of carrots near the fence. She loves carrots and thinks the zebras do too. She asks her mom and dad if she can give a carrot to a zebra. They say no, because the carrots are not for her and the zebras might bite her. Sara is sad and says she just wants to be nice to the zebras. Her mom and dad say they understand, but she has to mind the rules and stay safe. They say there are other ways to be nice to the zebras, like talking to them or drawing them a picture. Sara thinks for a moment and then smiles. She says she has an idea and runs to the gift shop. She comes back with a zebra sticker and a piece of paper. She draws a big heart on the paper and sticks the zebra sticker on it. She shows it to her mom and dad and says this is her gift for the zebras. They say it is very sweet and help her hold it up to the fence. One of the zebras sees the paper and comes closer. It looks at the sticker and the heart and snorts. It seems to like the gift and nuzzles the paper with its nose. Sara is very happy and says thank you to the zebra. The zebra says nothing, but wags its tail. Sara says she made a new friend and hugs her mom and dad. They say they are proud of her and ask her if she wants to see more animals. Sara says yes, but only if they are available.
