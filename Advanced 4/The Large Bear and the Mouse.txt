Title: The Large Bear and the Mouse

Once upon a time, there was a large bear who loved to keep his cave clean. The bear made sure to keep things tidy and put away each time he finished using something. One day, his friend, a small mouse, came to visit. The mouse watched the bear clean and it liked the way the bear cleaned. But then the bear said something that surprised the mouse. The bear said that it was important to make sure the cave was clean, but it was more important to be kind to others. That way, he explained, you would make sure that others had a good opinion of you. The mouse liked this idea and it shared it with its friends. Soon, all the animals in the forest were taking care to keep their dens clean, but they were also being kind to each other and helping one another out. The moral of this story is that it is important to be clean, but it is even more important to be kind to each other. Kindness is a very large quality and it is something that will make sure others think nice things about you.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl called Jane. She was only three years old and loved playing with her ornaments. Her favourite ornament was a big, shiny snowman. One cold and frosty day, Jane asked her mum if she could go outside and play in the snow. Her mum said, “No, it’s too cold.” Jane was sad, but then her mum said, “I know, why don’t you take your snowman outside and let him play in the snow.” Jane was so excited and quickly put on her warm coat and hat. She went outside, looked up at the sky and said, “Snowman, I allow you to go and play in the snow. Have fun!” A few minutes later, Jane spotted her snowman crumbling in a pile of snow. She ran to him, hugged him and said, “You are the best ornament ever!” And they both stayed in the snow together, playing until night fell.

One day, a little girl named Ann found a journal. She liked to shake it and watch the pages move. Ann thought the journal was very pretty and graceful. She wanted to write in it, but she did not know how. Ann asked her mom, "Can you help me write in my journal?" Her mom said, "Yes, but first we need a pen." They looked all over the house but could not find one. Ann was sad. Then, something unexpected happened. Ann's dog, Spot, came in with a pen in his mouth. Ann was so happy! She said, "Thank you, Spot!" Now Ann could write in her journal with her mom's help. They wrote a fun story together and Ann loved her journal even more.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there was a little girl named Anna. She loved to play with her toys and run around the furniture. One day, while playing, she saw a tiny, honest bug on the floor. The bug looked lost and scared. Anna said, "Hi, little bug! Do you need help?" The bug looked up at her and nodded. Anna put out her hand and the bug climbed on. She held the bug gently and walked around the house, looking for a safe spot for it. As Anna was walking, something unexpected happened. The bug started to grow bigger and bigger! Soon, it was as big as Anna! She was so surprised, but the bug was still kind and honest. They became best friends and played together every day. And they all lived happily ever after.

Ben liked to wonder about many things. He wondered why the sky was blue, why the birds could fly, and why the flowers smelled nice. He liked to ask his mom and dad many questions, and they tried to answer him as best as they could. One day, Ben saw a man walking down the street with a whistle in his mouth. The man was wearing a red hat, a blue shirt, and a yellow pants. He looked very attractive to Ben, because he liked bright colors. Ben wondered what the man was doing with the whistle, and why he was blowing it. Ben ran after the man, and asked him, "Hello, mister, why do you have a whistle? What does it do?" The man smiled at Ben, and said, "Hello, little boy, I have a whistle because I am a traffic cop. I use it to tell the cars when to stop and when to go, so they don't crash into each other." Ben was amazed by the man's job, and said, "Wow, that sounds very fun and important. Can I try your whistle?" The man laughed, and said, "Sure, why not? Here, take it, and blow it gently." He handed Ben the whistle, and Ben put it in his mouth. Ben blew the whistle, and made a loud sound. He felt very happy and proud, and the man clapped his hands. But then, Ben heard a loud honk, and saw a car coming fast. The car had not stopped at the red light, because it did not hear the man's whistle. The man quickly grabbed Ben, and pulled him out of the way. The car zoomed past them, and the man shouted, "Hey, you, stop! You broke the law!" Ben was scared, and said, "I'm sorry, mister, I didn't mean to make trouble. I just wanted to try your whistle." The man hugged Ben, and said, "It's okay, little boy, you didn't know. But you have to be careful, and not play with things that are not yours. Whistles are not toys, they are tools. They can help, but they can also hurt." Ben nodded, and said, "I understand, mister. Thank you for letting me try your whistle, and for saving me from the car. You are very nice and brave." The man smiled, and said, "You are very welcome, little boy. And you are very curious and smart. But now, you should go back to your mom and dad, and tell them what happened. They might be worried about you." Ben agreed, and gave the whistle back to the man. He said goodbye, and ran back to his mom and dad, who were waiting for him at the park. He told them everything, and they were glad that he was safe, but also angry that he had run away. They scolded him, but also hugged him, and told him that they loved him. Ben learned his lesson, and never played with whistles again. He still liked to wonder about many things, but he also listened to his mom and dad, and followed their rules.
