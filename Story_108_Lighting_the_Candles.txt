Title: Lighting the Candles
Category: Beginner

Mrs. Clark returned from the hallway carrying a large cardboard box. She placed it carefully on the coffee table in the center of the dark living room. 
Mark and his father pointed their flashlights at the box. Inside, there were dozens of thick, white candles and several small glass holders. 

"These are special emergency candles," Mrs. Clark explained, taking them out of the box. "They are very thick, so they will burn for many hours. And they do not have a strong smell."
"Good," Mr. Clark said. He pulled a small box of wooden matches from his pocket. "Let's light them up and save our flashlight batteries."

Mrs. Clark placed four glass holders on the coffee table. She put a thick white candle into each one. She placed two more candles on the fireplace mantel and one on the bookshelf. 
"Safety first," she warned Mark. "We must never put a candle near the curtains or near any loose papers. And we must never leave a burning candle in an empty room."
"I understand, Mom," Mark said seriously. 

Mr. Clark struck a wooden match against the side of the small box. It flared with a sudden hiss and a tiny puff of grey smoke. He carefully lit the wick of the first candle on the coffee table. The small string caught fire quickly. 

He moved from candle to candle, lighting them all. As he lit more candles, the harsh, bright beams of the flashlights were no longer necessary. 
"You can turn your flashlights off now," his father said. 

Mark pushed the button on his flashlight. The room was bathed in a completely different kind of light. 
The candlelight was soft, warm, and golden. The small flames danced and flickered constantly because of tiny movements in the air. The flickering light made the shadows on the walls dance too. The living room did not look spooky anymore; it looked incredibly cozy and peaceful. 

"Wow," Mark said, looking around. "It looks like we are in an old castle, or a cabin in the woods. It is actually really nice."
"It is very relaxing," his mother agreed, sitting down on the sofa and pulling a thick woolen blanket over her legs. "It makes you feel calm."

Without the constant hum of the refrigerator, the hum of the heating, and the noise of the television, the house was profoundly quiet. The only sound was the fierce wind roaring outside and the soft crackling of the candle flames. 

"It is getting a little bit cold," Mark noticed, rubbing his arms. The central heating had only been off for twenty minutes, but the freezing winter storm was already chilling the house. 
"Go upstairs and bring down all the thick blankets from your bed," his father instructed. "And put on a thick sweater. We will all stay in the living room tonight to share our body heat."

Mark took his flashlight and walked upstairs. His bedroom was pitch black and very cold. He grabbed his thickest winter sweater and pulled it over his head. He pulled his heavy duvet off the bed and carried it awkwardly back downstairs. 

He returned to the golden living room and sat in his armchair. He wrapped the heavy duvet tightly around his body like a cocoon. He felt instantly warmer. 
His parents were also wrapped in blankets on the sofa. 

"Well," Mr. Clark said, looking at his family in the flickering candlelight. "We have light, we have blankets, and we are safe from the storm. But we still have a long evening ahead of us, and nobody is tired enough to go to sleep."
"I am a little bored," Mark admitted. "I can't read a book because the light is too dim. And my phone battery is almost dead."
"This is the perfect opportunity to do something we haven't done in a very long time," his mother smiled. Her eyes twinkled in the candlelight. "It is time to open the games cupboard."
