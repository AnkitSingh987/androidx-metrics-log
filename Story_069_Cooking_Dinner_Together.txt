Title: Cooking Dinner Together
Category: Beginner

It is six o'clock in the evening. Jack and his father are in the kitchen. They just came back from the small grocery store. They bought pasta, tomato sauce, lettuce, and tomatoes. Now, it is time to cook dinner. 

"Are you ready to cook, Jack?" his father asks. 
"Yes, Chef!" Jack says loudly. He likes to pretend they are in a real restaurant. 
"Okay, Chef Jack," his father laughs. "First, we must wash our hands. We always wash our hands before we touch food."
They go to the sink. They use warm water and white soap. They wash their hands for twenty seconds. Then, they dry them with a clean towel. 

"What is the first step?" Jack asks. 
"The first step is the water for the pasta," his father explains. 
His father takes a large metal pot from the cupboard. He fills it with cold water from the tap. He puts the heavy pot on the stove. He turns the stove on. 
"We must wait for the water to boil," he says. "It needs to be very hot. While we wait, we can prepare the salad."

He takes the green lettuce and the red tomatoes out of the shopping bag. 
"Jack, your job is to wash the vegetables," his father says. 
Jack takes the lettuce. He pulls the leaves apart. He holds them under the cold running water. He washes all the dirt away. Then, he washes the two red tomatoes. He puts the clean vegetables on a wooden cutting board. 

"Good job," his father says. "Now, I will cut them."
His father takes a sharp knife. He is very careful. He cuts the lettuce into small pieces. He cuts the tomatoes into small circles. He puts all the pieces into a big glass bowl. 
"Can I mix it?" Jack asks. 
"Yes, take these two large spoons and mix it gently," his father says. 
Jack uses the spoons to turn the lettuce and tomatoes over and over in the bowl. The salad looks fresh and healthy. 

Suddenly, the large metal pot on the stove makes a noise. It sounds like a low rumble. Then, bubbles start to appear in the water. 
"Look, Jack," his father says. "The water is boiling."
His father opens the packet of pasta. He carefully pours the dry, hard pasta into the boiling water. 
"Now we set the timer for ten minutes," his father says. He presses the buttons on the kitchen clock. Beep, beep. Ten minutes. 

"What about the tomato sauce?" Jack asks. 
"Ah, yes. The sauce," his father says. He takes a smaller pot. He opens the jar of tomato sauce and pours it into the small pot. He puts it on the stove next to the pasta, but on a low heat. 
"We just need to make the sauce warm," he explains. "Jack, you can stir the sauce so it does not burn."
He gives Jack a wooden spoon. Jack stands carefully next to the stove. He stirs the red sauce slowly in circles. It smells delicious. It smells like garlic and herbs. 

"It smells like Italy," Jack says. 
His father laughs. "Yes, it does. You are a very good assistant chef, Jack."

They wait in the kitchen. They talk about Jack's day at school. They talk about the football game Jack played at recess. Jack stirs the sauce, and his father watches the pasta. 
Beep, beep, beep! 
The kitchen timer rings loudly. Ten minutes are finished. 

"The pasta is ready," his father says. He turns off the stove. He puts on thick oven gloves to protect his hands from the heat. He lifts the heavy pot of boiling water and pasta. He carries it to the sink and pours it into a strainer. The water goes down the drain, and the pasta stays in the strainer. Huge clouds of white steam rise into the air. 
"Wow, look at all that steam!" Jack says. 

His father shakes the strainer to remove the last drops of water. He pours the soft, hot pasta back into the big metal pot. 
"Now, pour the warm tomato sauce over the pasta, Jack," his father says. 
Jack carefully lifts the small pot and pours the red sauce into the big pot. His father uses a large spoon to mix everything together. The yellow pasta turns red. 

"Dinner is served," his father announces. 
He puts a large portion of pasta on Jack's plate and a portion on his own plate. He puts the bowl of salad in the middle of the table. 
They sit down to eat. 
Jack takes a big bite of the pasta. It is hot, soft, and very tasty. 
"This is delicious," Jack says with his mouth full. 
"It is delicious because we cooked it together," his father smiles. "Food always tastes better when you make it with a friend."
Jack agrees. Cooking is fun, and eating the food you cooked is the best part of all.
