Title: The Sleepy Lions
Category: Beginner

Sam led his family away from the tall giraffes. They walked further down the yellow path, deeper into the savanna section of the zoo. The sun was getting higher in the sky, and the air was getting warmer. 

"We are almost there," Sam said, looking closely at his map. "The lions should be right around this corner."
Lily walked a little faster. She was very excited to see the lions. In her favorite cartoons, lions were always running, hunting, and roaring very loudly. She imagined them to be very fierce and active. 

They turned the corner and saw a large crowd of people standing in front of a thick glass wall. 
"This must be it," their father said. "Let's find a spot where we can see."
They gently pushed their way to the front of the crowd. Lily pressed her hands against the cool, thick glass and looked inside the large enclosure. 

The lion enclosure was designed to look like the dry African plains. There were large rocks, tall yellow grass, and a small pool of water in the corner. 
Lily looked eagerly for the fierce lions. She looked by the water. Nothing. She looked in the tall grass. Nothing. 
Then, she saw them. 

On top of a large, flat rock in the middle of the enclosure, three lions were lying down. There was one large male lion with a thick, dark mane of hair around his neck and head. There were two female lions, called lionesses, without manes. 
They were not running. They were not hunting. They were not roaring. 
They were completely, totally, deeply asleep. 

"They are just sleeping," Lily said. She sounded very disappointed. 
The large male lion was lying on his back with his big paws in the air. His stomach moved slowly up and down as he breathed. One of the lionesses had her head resting on his back. They looked exactly like large, lazy house cats sleeping in the afternoon sun. 

Sam laughed. "They don't look very scary right now. They look very boring."
"Why are they sleeping so much?" Lily asked her mother. "It is the middle of the day. They should be awake and doing things."

Her mother smiled and pointed to the sleeping animals. 
"Actually, lions sleep a lot," she explained. "In the wild, lions can sleep for up to twenty hours a day. The weather is very hot in Africa, so they save their energy during the day. They usually hunt for their food at night when it is much cooler."

"Twenty hours?" Sam repeated, surprised. "That means they are only awake for four hours. That is a very lazy life."
"It seems lazy to us," his father said. "But when they are awake, they are incredibly strong and fast. They are top predators. It takes a lot of energy to catch a zebra or a buffalo."

The crowd of people watched the sleeping lions for a few minutes. Some people took photos, but the lions did not move. It was not a very exciting picture. 

Suddenly, one of the lionesses opened her eyes. She lifted her large, heavy head. 
"Look! She is waking up!" Lily whispered excitedly. 
The lioness let out a long, loud yawn. She opened her mouth very wide, showing her enormous, sharp, yellow teeth. Her tongue was rough and pink. 

Then, she stood up slowly. She stretched her front legs out long, just like a normal cat stretching after a nap. She walked lazily across the rock and stepped down into the dry grass. She walked over to the small pool of water in the corner. 
She lowered her head and began to drink. Her rough tongue lapped the water loudly. 'Slurp, slurp, slurp.'

The large male lion opened one yellow eye. He looked at the lioness drinking water. He did not want to get up. He just rolled over onto his side, let out a deep, rumbling sigh, and went back to sleep. 

"Well," Sam said, putting his camera away. "That was not very much action. They really are just big, sleepy cats."
"They are beautiful, even when they sleep," his mother said. "Look at the size of their paws and the muscles in their legs. They are very powerful animals."

"I am glad there is a thick glass wall between us," Lily admitted. She liked the sleeping lions, but she did not want to be near a waking one. 
"Are you ready to move on?" their father asked. "We have been in the yellow zone for a long time. Should we go to the green jungle zone?"
"Yes!" Sam said, opening his map again. "Let's go find some animals that are awake. Let's go see the monkeys!"
They left the sleeping kings of the jungle to their sunny nap and headed towards the noisy jungle.
