Title: When a Friend Disagreed

Category: Intermediate


Sarah and Emily had been best friends since their first year of university. Despite having very different personalities, they had always complemented each other perfectly. Sarah was the practical one; she was organized, logical, and always carefully planned everything in advance. She worked as an accountant and was excellent with numbers and budgets. Emily, on the other hand, was the creative spirit. She was spontaneous, artistic, and always full of wild, exciting ideas. She worked as a graphic designer and saw the world through a lens of bright colors and endless possibilities.

For years, they had shared a dream of opening a business together. They both loved the idea of creating a welcoming space where people could gather, relax, and enjoy good food and drinks. After saving money for five long years, they finally felt ready to turn their dream into a reality. They decided to open a small café in their city. At first, the planning process was incredibly exciting. They spent hours sitting in Sarah’s living room, drinking tea and brainstorming names for their future business. 

However, as they moved from casual dreaming to serious planning, their differences in personality began to cause significant problems. The first major disagreement arose when they started discussing the concept and atmosphere of the café.

"I have been thinking about the interior design," Emily announced one evening, unrolling a large sheet of paper on the table. She had drawn a vibrant, detailed sketch of a café filled with brightly colored furniture, hanging plants, and neon signs. "I want it to have a lively, energetic atmosphere. We should play upbeat music, host local bands on the weekends, and focus on selling exotic, colorful tea blends and fusion pastries. It will be the trendiest spot in the city!"

Sarah stared at the sketch, feeling a sudden wave of anxiety. "Emily, this looks beautiful, but it seems very expensive and risky. Trends come and go so quickly. I was imagining something much more classic and cozy. We should have comfortable armchairs, soft lighting, and quiet background music. People want a peaceful place to read or work on their laptops. We should focus on high-quality, traditional coffee and classic baked goods, like croissants and muffins. It is safer and guarantees a steady flow of regular customers."

"Classic is just another word for boring," Emily replied, crossing her arms defensively. "If we do what everyone else is doing, we will never stand out. We need to be unique."

"We need to be profitable," Sarah countered, her voice rising slightly. "If we spend all our money on expensive decorations and strange teas that no one wants to buy, we will be bankrupt in six months."

The atmosphere in the room grew tense, but they managed to change the subject before it turned into a real fight. However, the tension exploded three days later when they went to view potential commercial spaces for rent.

The real estate agent showed them two very different locations. The first was a small, charming space located in a quiet, family-oriented neighborhood. The rent was very affordable, but it was slightly far from the city center, and the building needed some minor repairs. The second location was a large, modern storefront situated right in the heart of downtown. It had huge glass windows and a massive amount of foot traffic, but the rent was astronomically high—nearly double their planned budget.

As soon as they stepped out of the downtown location, Emily turned to Sarah, her eyes sparkling with excitement. "This is it, Sarah! This is the perfect place. Can you imagine the neon sign in that window? Everyone walking by will want to come inside. We have to sign the lease today."

Sarah felt a tightness in her chest. She had already calculated the numbers in her head, and they simply did not make sense. "Emily, we cannot afford this place. The rent is outrageous. If we take this location, we won't have enough money left for equipment, inventory, or paying ourselves for the first few months. It is financial suicide. The first place in the quiet neighborhood is much smarter. It is safe, manageable, and within our budget."

Emily threw her hands in the air in frustration. "You are always so focused on playing it safe! If you never take a risk, you will never achieve anything great. You are letting your fear ruin our dream. The neighborhood place is hidden and depressing. I refuse to open a business there."

"And I refuse to sign a lease that will put us into massive debt!" Sarah shouted back, finally losing her temper. "You only care about making things look pretty, and you completely ignore the reality of running a business. I am the one who actually understands how money works!"

The harsh words hung in the air between them. Emily looked deeply hurt, while Sarah immediately felt a sharp pang of guilt. Without saying another word, Emily turned around and walked away toward the nearest bus stop. Sarah stood alone on the busy sidewalk, feeling as though their dream—and their friendship—had just shattered into pieces.

For the next four days, neither of them called or texted. The silence was heavy and uncomfortable. Sarah missed her friend terribly. She sat at her kitchen table, looking over her financial spreadsheets. She realized that while her numbers were realistic, her vision for the café was indeed a little dull. Without Emily's creative spark, the business lacked personality. 

Meanwhile, Emily was sitting in her apartment, looking at her colorful sketches. She knew deep down that Sarah was right about the budget. If she had tried to open the downtown location on her own, she would have run out of money before opening day. She realized she needed Sarah’s practical wisdom to turn her wild ideas into a sustainable reality.

On the fifth day, Sarah finally picked up her phone and sent a message: "Can we talk? Meet me at the park at 2 PM."

When they met, they both looked nervous. They sat on a wooden bench under a large oak tree. 

"I am so sorry for what I said," Sarah began, her voice shaking slightly. "I was stressed, but I shouldn't have insulted your ideas. Your creativity is the whole reason I wanted to do this with you."

Emily smiled softly, relieved. "I am sorry too. I was being stubborn and ignoring the financial reality. I know you were just trying to protect us from going bankrupt. I need your logic just as much as you need my creativity."

They hugged, the tension finally leaving their bodies. They spent the next two hours talking calmly, actually listening to each other's perspectives. They realized that their disagreement was not a disaster; it was an opportunity to find a better solution. 

By the end of the afternoon, they had reached a perfect compromise. They decided to reject both locations they had seen. Instead, they would look for a mid-priced location in an up-and-coming, artistic neighborhood—somewhere with reasonable rent but a younger, more vibrant crowd. 

As for the café's concept, they agreed to blend their visions. The menu would feature Sarah’s high-quality, traditional coffee and classic pastries, but it would also include a special menu of Emily’s unique, colorful teas. The decor would be cozy and comfortable, as Sarah wanted, but Emily would design custom murals for the walls and choose bright, artistic accents to give the space character. They would keep the music quiet during the week, but host live acoustic music on Friday nights to draw a larger crowd.

A few months later, their café, 'The Balanced Blend,' opened its doors to the public. It was a massive success. Customers loved the comfortable seating just as much as they loved the unique artwork on the walls. Sarah managed the finances perfectly, ensuring they made a profit in their first quarter, while Emily managed their social media, attracting hundreds of new customers with her beautiful photographs. 

Standing behind the counter on a busy Saturday afternoon, Sarah looked at Emily, who was laughing with a customer while serving a brightly colored iced tea. Sarah smiled, realizing that their biggest argument had been a necessary step. It had taught them that disagreeing did not mean they were incompatible. Instead, by combining their different strengths, they had built something far more beautiful and successful than either of them could have created alone.
