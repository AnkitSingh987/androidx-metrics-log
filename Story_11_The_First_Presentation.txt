Title: The First Presentation

Category: Intermediate

Leo worked at a modern marketing agency in the bustling city center. He was a junior graphic designer, and he absolutely loved his job. He spent his days creating beautiful images, selecting colors, and designing modern websites for many different clients. He was very good at his work, but he was a quiet person. He usually sat at his desk in the corner with his headphones on, listening to soft music while he worked. He did not like talking in front of many people, and he usually avoided large meetings if he could.

One Monday morning, his manager, Sarah, called him into her spacious office. The office had large windows looking out over the city. 
"Good morning, Leo," Sarah said with a warm, encouraging smile. "Please, sit down and make yourself comfortable."
Leo sat down slowly, feeling a little nervous. He wondered if he had done something wrong, or if a client did not like his recent work. 
"You have done excellent work on the new 'Sunrise Coffee' project," Sarah continued. "The client absolutely loves your initial designs. Because of this, I want you to present the final designs to the client team this Friday in the main conference room."

Leo felt his heart jump into his throat. "Present? You mean, stand up and talk to them in the meeting room?"
"Yes, exactly," Sarah replied calmly. "You know these designs better than anyone else in the building. You can explain your creative ideas and answer their questions directly. It is a great opportunity for you to grow in your career."
Leo smiled weakly, though his stomach was twisting. "Thank you, Sarah. I will do my best."

When Leo returned to his desk, he could not focus on his computer screen. He was terrified. He had never given a professional presentation before. He was afraid he would forget his words, or that the client would not understand his explanations. He imagined terrible situations, like dropping his notes, freezing in silence, or breaking the expensive projector. 

At lunchtime, Leo went to the office break room to get some coffee and clear his head. His coworker, Emma, was there, eating a healthy salad. Emma was a senior project manager, and she was known for being very confident. She gave important presentations every single week without breaking a sweat.
"Hi Leo, you look very pale. Are you feeling okay?" Emma asked, noticing his worried face.
Leo sighed heavily and sat down across from her. "Sarah wants me to give a presentation to the Sunrise Coffee client on Friday. It is my first time presenting to a client. I am very nervous about it."
Emma smiled kindly. "Oh, I remember my first presentation. I was so scared that my hands were shaking! But you will be fine, Leo. You just need to prepare properly."
"How do I prepare?" Leo asked eagerly, hoping she would share some magical secret trick.
"First, write down what you want to say. Do not write every single word, just the main points on small cards. If you read from a piece of paper, people will get bored and stop listening," Emma explained. "Second, practice out loud. Practice in front of your mirror at home. And most importantly, remember that you are the expert here. They are paying for your ideas, and they want to hear what you have to say."

Leo felt a little better after talking to Emma. Her advice made sense. That evening, he stayed late at the office to create a beautiful, clear slideshow. He made sure the images were large and bright. He only used a few words on each slide, just like Emma suggested. He wanted the pictures to speak for themselves. 

When he finally went home, he stood in his small bedroom and practiced. 
"Hello everyone, my name is Leo, and I am the lead designer for this project," he said loudly to his empty room. He felt very silly, but he forced himself to keep going. He practiced his speech on Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday evenings. Every time he practiced, he felt a little more comfortable with his words. He used a timer on his phone to make sure the presentation was not too long or too short. He aimed for exactly fifteen minutes.

Finally, it was Friday morning. Leo woke up very early. The sun was barely up. He took a long shower to calm his nerves. He put on his best dark blue suit and a nice blue tie. He wanted to look as professional as possible. He only managed to eat a small piece of toast for breakfast because his stomach felt tight with anxiety. 

When he arrived at the office, he went straight to the large meeting room. He wanted to check the technical equipment before anyone else arrived. He connected his silver laptop to the large screen and clicked through his slides. Everything worked perfectly. The colors looked fantastic on the big screen.

At exactly ten o'clock, the clients arrived. There were three of them: a senior manager and two marketing assistants. They looked very serious in their dark, formal suits. Sarah greeted them warmly at the door and offered them fresh coffee and water. Then, they all sat down around the large glass table. 
"Good morning, everyone," Sarah said, starting the meeting. "Today, our talented designer, Leo, will show you the final designs for your new brand identity. Leo, whenever you are ready, the floor is yours."

Leo stood up slowly. His hands were shaking a little bit, so he placed them flat on the cool glass table. He took a slow, deep breath, just like Emma had told him to do. 
"Good morning," Leo said. His voice was a little quiet and shaky, so he cleared his throat and tried again, speaking much louder. "Good morning. My name is Leo, and I am very excited to show you the new designs today."
The clients smiled politely and nodded their heads, waiting for him to continue.

Leo clicked a button, and his first slide appeared. He started talking about the warm colors and the modern fonts he had chosen. As he talked about his hard work, he slowly started to forget his nerves. He was not just talking; he was sharing something beautiful that he had created. He explained why the warm brown and orange colors would make customers feel welcome and relaxed in their coffee shop. He felt his confidence growing with every sentence.

Suddenly, without any warning, the large screen went completely black. 
Leo stopped talking. His heart started beating fast again. The clients looked at the empty screen in confusion, and then they looked directly at him. The room was very quiet.
"I am very sorry, please give me one moment," Leo said. He tried his best to stay calm. He checked the black cable connecting his laptop to the screen. It was slightly loose. He pushed it in firmly, and the screen instantly lit up again, showing his beautiful designs. 
"Ah, there we go," Leo said, smiling naturally at the clients. "Technology always likes to test us when we are busy, doesn't it?"
The clients laughed lightly. The small, unexpected joke broke the tension in the room. They relaxed in their chairs.

Leo continued his presentation. He felt much better now. The worst thing had happened, and he had survived it easily. He realized that the clients were just normal people, and they were genuinely interested in what he had to say. When he reached the final slide showing the new logo on a coffee cup, he felt a strong sense of pride. 
"Thank you very much for listening to my presentation," Leo said confidently. "Do you have any questions about the designs?"

The client manager, a tall man wearing round glasses, nodded slowly. "Leo, I really love the logo design. It is very unique. But can you explain why you chose this specific modern font instead of a more traditional one?"
Because Leo had practiced so much, he knew exactly how to answer this question. "Yes, of course. We want to attract younger customers who appreciate modern, clean spaces. However, we still want to keep the traditional, warm coffee feeling. This specific font perfectly balances modern clean lines with a friendly, classic look."

The manager smiled widely. "That makes perfect sense. I completely agree with you. Excellent work, Leo."
After the meeting finished and the clients happily left the office, Sarah turned to Leo. "You did an absolutely fantastic job," she said, beaming with pride. "You handled that unexpected technical problem very well, and you answered their questions perfectly. I am very proud of you, Leo."

Leo felt a huge weight lift from his shoulders. He was mentally tired, but he was also incredibly happy. He walked back to his desk with a big, proud smile on his face. Emma walked past with a stack of papers and gave him a high-five. 
"See? I told you it would be completely fine!" she laughed.
"You were definitely right," Leo replied, laughing too. "It was very scary at first, but it was actually a great experience."

Leo sat down at his computer. He looked at his designs on the screen. He was no longer just the quiet designer hiding in the corner. He knew he could share his ideas, solve problems, and speak confidently to important people. He was already looking forward to his next creative project, and perhaps, his next big presentation.
