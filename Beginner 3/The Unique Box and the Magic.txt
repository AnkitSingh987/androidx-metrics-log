Category: Beginner
Title: The Unique Box and the Magic

Once upon a time, in a small house, there was a unique box. It was big, red, and shiny. A little girl named Lucy lived in the house. She liked to play with the box. One day, Lucy's friend Tom came to play. They both looked at the box and wondered what was inside. Lucy and Tom tried to open the box, but it was shut very tight. They pushed and pulled, but the box did not open. They knew that there was a joke inside the box, and they wanted to hear it. They thought and thought about how to open the box. Lucy had an idea. "Let's ask mom for help," she said. So, Lucy and Tom went to Lucy's mom and asked her to help them open the box. Lucy's mom smiled and said, "You need to say the magic word." Lucy and Tom looked at each other and said, "Please!" The box opened, and they heard the joke. They laughed and laughed, happy that they finally heard the joke. The magic word was the key to open the unique box, and they would always remember to say "please" from now on.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. She lived in a modern house with her mom, dad, and her pet dog, Max. Sue loved Max very much. They played together every day. One day, Sue's mom said, "Sue, you should learn to teach Max some tricks." Sue thought it was a great idea. She said, "Yes, mom. I want to learn to teach Max tricks." So, Sue and Max went outside to learn. Sue said, "Max, sit!" Max sat down. Sue was happy. Then she said, "Max, jump!" Max jumped high in the air. Sue clapped and laughed. Every day, Sue and Max learned more tricks together. They had lots of fun. Sue's mom and dad were proud of them. And Sue and Max were the best of friends.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play together. One day, they find a big tape measure in the garage. They want to see how long they are. They take turns to lie down on the floor and hold the tape measure. Anna says, "I am 36 inches long. I am big!" Ben says, "I am 35 inches long. I am almost as big as you!" Then they have an idea. They want to see how far they can crawl with the tape measure. They start from one end of the garage and crawl to the other end, holding the tape measure between them. They look at the number on the tape measure. It says 50 inches. They are happy. They say, "We can crawl 50 inches together. We are strong!" But then they hear a noise. It is their big brother, Sam. He is 10 years old. He sees them crawling with the tape measure. He laughs. He says, "You are silly. You are not strong. You are small. I can crawl 100 inches by myself. Watch me!" He takes the tape measure from them and crawls across the garage. He looks at the number on the tape measure. It says 100 inches. He is proud. He says, "See? I am 100 inches long. I am big and strong. You are nothing!" Anna and Ben are sad. They feel bad. They say, "You are mean. You are not nice. You are not humble. You should share and be kind." Sam does not listen. He does not care. He thinks he is better than them. He says, "I don't need to share or be kind. I am the best. You are the worst. Go away!" He pushes them away and keeps the tape measure. He crawls around the garage, smiling. But then he hears another noise. It is their dad. He is very tall. He sees Sam crawling with the tape measure. He is angry. He says, "What are you doing? Why are you being rude to your siblings? Why are you taking their toy? Give it back to them now!" He takes the tape measure from Sam and gives it back to Anna and Ben. He says, "You should be ashamed of yourself. You are not big and strong. You are small and weak. You need to learn to be humble and respectful. You need to apologize to your brother and sister and play nicely with them." Sam is scared. He feels bad. He says, "I'm sorry. I was wrong. I was mean. I will be humble and respectful. I will share and be kind. Can we play together?" Anna and Ben are happy. They forgive him. They say, "Yes, we can play together. We love you. You are our brother. We are a family." They hug and smile. They play with the tape measure. They have fun. They are humble and respectful. They are strong together.
