Category: Beginner
Title: Anna and Ben and the Box

Anna and Ben like to play in the park. They have a ball and a bat. They take turns to hit the ball and run. They have fun. One day, they see a big box near the slide. It is brown and has a hole. They are curious. They go to the box and look inside. "Hello, who are you?" a voice says from the box. Anna and Ben are surprised. They do not see anyone. "We are Anna and Ben. We play in the park. Who are you?" Anna says. "I am Tom. I live in the box. It is my home. Do you want to play with me?" the voice says. Anna and Ben are not sure. They do not know Tom. He sounds friendly, but he is mysterious. They cannot see him. "Can you come out of the box? We want to see you," Ben says. "OK, I will come out. But you have to close your eyes and count to ten," the voice says. Anna and Ben agree. They close their eyes and count to ten. They hear some noise from the box. When they open their eyes, they see a boy with a hat and a coat. He has a big smile. He holds a pitch in his hand. It is shiny and has a star on it. "Hi, I am Tom. This is my pitch. It is magic. It can make things appear and disappear. Do you want to see?" he says. Anna and Ben are amazed. They want to see the magic pitch. They nod and say yes. Tom throws the pitch in the air. It spins and sparkles. Then, it lands on the ground. Something appears next to it. It is a cake with candles. "Wow, a cake! How did you do that?" Anna says. "It is easy. You just have to wish for something and throw the pitch. Then, it will appear. But you have to be careful. The pitch can also make things disappear. If you throw it again, the cake will be gone," Tom says. Anna and Ben want to try the pitch. They take turns to wish for something and throw the pitch. They make a balloon, a teddy bear, a bike and a flower appear. They are happy. They play with the pitch and the things they make until it is time to go home. They thank Tom for sharing his pitch and his magic. They say goodbye and promise to come back. Tom waves and smiles. He picks up his pitch and goes back to his box. He is happy too. He has made new friends. He likes to play in the park.

One day, a girl named Lily went to the park with her mom. In the park, there was a big plant. Lily saw a man with a truck. The man was trying to load the plant onto the truck. Lily was sad because she loved the plant. Lily asked the man, "Why are you taking the plant away?" The man said, "I need to move this plant to a new place where it can grow better." Lily thought for a moment and then had an idea. Lily said, "I can help you!" She went home and got her sweet candy. She gave the candy to the man. The man was happy and said, "Thank you, Lily!" The man let the plant stay in the park. The next day, Lily went to the park and saw the plant was still there. She was very happy. The moral of the story is that being kind and helpful can make a difference. And sometimes, a sweet treat can save the day.

Once upon a time, there was a big armchair that lived near a pond. It was a fearful armchair, always afraid of the water. One day, a little frog came to sit on the armchair. The frog wanted to teach the armchair how to swim. The armchair was very scared, but the frog was kind. They went to the pond together. The frog showed the armchair how to jump and splash. The armchair tried to swim too. It was hard at first, but soon the armchair was having fun. Then, something unexpected happened. The armchair turned into a big, soft boat! The frog and the armchair were so happy. They swam together all day, and the armchair was not fearful anymore. The armchair and the frog became best friends, and they swam in the pond every day.
