Title: Tim and Sam and the Hill

Tim and Sam were playing in the garden. They found a big bucket of water and some twigs. They had an idea. "Let's pour water on the ants!" Tim said. "Yeah, that will be fun!" Sam agreed. They picked up some twigs and dipped them in the water. Then they ran to the ant hill and poured water on it. The ants ran out, wet and angry. "Ha ha, look at them!" Tim laughed. But Sam did not laugh. He felt guilty. He liked the ants. They were small and busy. He did not want to hurt them. "Stop, Tim, stop!" Sam said. "This is not nice. The ants are sad. They need their home." Tim looked at Sam. He saw that Sam was not happy. He felt guilty too. He did not mean to be mean. He just wanted to play. "Sorry, Sam, sorry, ants," Tim said. "We won't do it again. We will help you fix your home." They put down the twigs and the bucket. They found some leaves and grass and sticks. They helped the ants rebuild their hill. The ants were happy. They thanked Tim and Sam. Tim and Sam felt better. They learned a lesson. They decided to be kind to the ants and to all the animals. They played with the twigs and the water in a different way. They made a boat and a bridge. They had fun.

One day, a little girl named Mia and her sister, Lily, went to the park. They liked to play there together. Mia was grateful to have a sister to play with. Mia and Lily were playing when they saw a big, red ball. Mia said, "Let's hold the ball and play with it!" Lily said, "Yes! Let's play!" They played with the ball, but they did not know whose ball it was. They played and played, and they were very happy. Then, a little boy came to the park. He looked sad. Mia and Lily saw the boy and stopped playing. The boy said, "That is my ball. I lost it." Mia and Lily looked at each other. They did not know it was his ball. Mia said, "We are sorry. Here is your ball back." The boy was surprised and happy. Mia and Lily felt bad, but the boy said, "Thank you for finding my ball. Let's all play together!" They all played with the ball and had a great day. Mia and Lily were grateful for their new friend and the fun they had at the park.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She had a big, soft pillow that she loved very much. One day, Lily wanted to find a way to make her pillow more useful. Lily thought and thought. Then, she had an idea! She went to her friend, Tim, and said, "I want to lift my pillow up high! Can you help me?" Tim said, "Yes, I can help you, Lily!" Together, Lily and Tim lifted the pillow up high and put it on top of a box. Now, the pillow was a nice place for their toys to sit. They were very happy with their useful pillow, and they played together all day long.

Once upon a time, there was a vendor named Tom. He sold soft and comfortable things. He had a big, friendly smile. Every day, he would set up his shop near the park. One sunny day, a little girl named Lily came to the park with her mom. Lily saw Tom's shop and ran to see what he had. She saw a big, comfortable pillow. Lily touched it and said, "Mom, this is so soft!" Her mom smiled and said, "Yes, it is very comfortable. It will make you feel good when you sleep." Tom heard them and said, "You can take the pillow. It will comfort you at night." Lily hugged her new pillow and thanked Tom. She felt happy and comfortable with her new pillow. From that day on, Lily slept well every night, and she always remembered the kind vendor who gave her the comfortable pillow.

Once upon a time, there were two trees. They were beside a pond full of water. The bigger tree was always foolish and teasing the smaller tree. It would call him names and laugh. One hot summer day, the bigger tree noticed the smaller tree looked very sad. The bigger tree said, "Why do you look sad?" The little tree said, "I am so thirsty and I can't reach the water in the pond." The bigger tree felt sorry for his friend. He asked the little tree to stand by the pond and he leaned over as far as he could. The bigger tree said," I will drop the leaves I have in my branches into the water so you can have a drink." The bigger tree felt proud of himself and the little tree thanked him. From that day forward, they were friends and the bigger tree never called his little friend foolish again.
