Category: Beginner
Title: Wisdom the Cat and the Mouse

Once upon a time, there was a big cat named Wisdom. He lived in a small house with his friend, a little mouse. They played together every day. One day, Wisdom felt uncomfortable. His tummy hurt a lot. He told the mouse, "I need to stop playing. My tummy hurts." The mouse was sad, but he let Wisdom rest. Days went by, and Wisdom's tummy still hurt. He couldn't play with the mouse anymore. The mouse tried to help, but nothing worked. In the end, Wisdom's tummy hurt so much that he had to leave his friend and his small house. The little mouse was very sad and missed his friend.

Once there was a little rabbit who wanted to make a rag. She searched everywhere until she found some soft cloth. She took it and began to sew it together. She was very gentle, so the cloth didn't get hurt. She made some stitches, and the rag was finished. The little rabbit was so happy! With the rag in his hand, he hopped home. He looked for something soft to put on the couch. He found the perfect spot for his rag and put it there. The little rabbit smiled proudly at his creation. The rag made the couch look so cozy and gentle. He hopped happily around it. From that day on, the little rabbit always kept a gentle rag nearby.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue had a pretty blouse. She loved to wear it all the time. One day, Sue played in the mud with her blouse. Her mom saw this and said, "Sue, you need to wash your blouse." Sue felt sorry. She did not want to wash her blouse. But she knew she had to. So, she took her blouse and went to the river. She started to wash it. As she washed, she saw a big fish. The fish looked at her blouse and swam away. Sue finished washing her blouse and put it on a rock to dry. She went to play some more. The big fish came back. He saw the blouse on the rock. The fish took the blouse in his mouth and swam away. Sue came back to get her blouse. But it was gone. She looked and looked, but she could not find it. Sue was very sad. She knew she should have listened to her mom. Now her pretty blouse was gone forever.

Once upon a time, there was a mighty uncle. He was big and strong. He lived in a small house near the woods. He loved to help people with their problems. One day, a little boy came to the mighty uncle. The boy was sad because he lost his toy. The mighty uncle wanted to help the boy find his toy. They went into the woods to look for it. They searched and searched, but the toy was not there. The mighty uncle told the boy to remain with him so they could keep looking. As they kept looking, it started to get dark. The mighty uncle and the boy were very tired. They did not find the toy, and now they were lost in the woods. They were scared and sad. The mighty uncle tried to find the way back home, but he could not. The bad ending was that the mighty uncle and the boy remained lost in the woods. They never found the toy or their way back home. The moral value of the story is that sometimes, even the mightiest people need help and that it's important to ask for help when you need it.

Lily and Ben were best friends. They liked to play in the park every day. They liked to swing, slide and run. But one day, they had a fight. Lily wanted to swing first, but Ben said no. He said he was there first. Lily got angry and pushed Ben. Ben fell down and hurt his knee. He cried and ran away. Lily felt bad. She did not mean to hurt Ben. She wanted to say sorry, but Ben did not come back. She sat on the swing and felt cold. She wished Ben was there to play with her. She missed him. The next day, Lily saw Ben in the park. He had a band-aid on his knee. He was playing with a ball. Lily walked up to him and said, "Hi, Ben. I'm sorry I pushed you. Can we be friends again?" Ben looked at Lily. He was still mad, but he also missed her. He said, "Hi, Lily. I'm sorry I was mean to you. You can swing first if you want." Lily smiled and hugged Ben. She said, "Thank you, Ben. You are very kind. I forgive you." Ben smiled and hugged Lily back. He said, "Thank you, Lily. You are very sweet. I forgive you too." They went to the swing and took turns. They laughed and had fun. They were best friends again. They learned to share and forgive. They were graceful and happy.
