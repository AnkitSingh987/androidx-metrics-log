Title: The Story of the Roof and the Coat

Once upon a time, there were two kids. One was walking in the rain, and his coat was getting very wet. The other kid was dry, because she was standing under a roof, away from the rain. She noticed him and said, “Hey, why don’t you come here so you won’t get wet?” The first kid thought and then said, “I don’t mind getting wet. I like the rain!” The other kid smiled and said, “Me too! Let’s enjoy it together!” So the two kids stayed under the roof and watched the rain fall. They smiled and laughed, and the wet kid reminded the other kid how important it is to be kind and helpful, even if it’s just a small memory. The moral of the story is that kindness and helpfulness are important, no matter how small the act may be. It’s the little things that can make everyone’s day brighter.

Once upon a time, there was a lonely dog named Spot. Spot had no friends to play with. He liked to wear his red helmet when he went for a walk. One sunny day, Spot saw a cat named Fluffy playing with a ball. Spot wanted to play too, so he decided to invite Fluffy to play with him. He walked up to Fluffy and asked, "Do you want to play with me?" Fluffy looked at Spot and said, "Yes! I would love to play with you!" They played together all day, and had lots of fun. Spot was not lonely anymore, and he made a new friend. From that day on, Spot and Fluffy always played together, and Spot always wore his red helmet.

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Jack. Jack's family was not wealthy. They had a cow. One day, Jack's mom said, "Bring the cow to the market and sell it." Jack said, "Yes, mom." On the way to the market, Jack met a man. The man said, "I will give you magic seeds for your cow." Jack thought the seeds would bring a better life. So, Jack gave the man the cow and got the seeds. Jack went home and showed the seeds to his mom. His mom was mad. She threw the seeds out the window. The next day, a big beanstalk grew. Jack climbed it and found a giant's home. The giant had lots of gold. Jack took some gold and went back down. Now, Jack's family was wealthy and happy. And they lived a good life.

Sara and Ben are brother and sister. They like to play together, but sometimes they fight. One day, they are playing with blocks in their room. Sara wants to build a tall tower, but Ben wants to knock it down. He thinks it is fun to see the blocks fall. "Stop it, Ben!" Sara says. "I want to make a tower. You are being rough and mean." "No, I'm not. You are being boring and bossy," Ben says. He pushes Sara's tower again and it crashes to the floor. Sara feels angry and sad. She starts to cry. Mom hears the noise and comes to the room. She sees the blocks and Sara's tears. She is not happy. "What is going on here?" Mom asks. "Why are you fighting?" "Ben is ruining my tower. He is rough and mean," Sara says. "Sara is boring and bossy. She won't let me play," Ben says. Mom sighs. She sits down with them and hugs them both. "Listen, Sara and Ben. You are brother and sister. You should love each other and play nicely. You should not be rough and mean, or boring and bossy. You should share and take turns. You should help and respect each other. Do you understand?" Sara and Ben nod. They feel sorry for making Mom unhappy and for hurting each other. "I'm sorry, Sara. I won't knock down your tower anymore. Can I help you build it?" Ben asks. "I'm sorry, Ben. I won't be bossy anymore. Can we play together?" Sara asks. They hug and smile. They start to build a new tower with the blocks. Mom smiles too. She is proud of them. "Good. I'm glad you are getting along. You know, you remind me of me and my brother when we were little. We used to fight too, but we always made up. He is my best friend now. I hope you will be best friends too," Mom says. Sara and Ben look at Mom and then at each other. They think maybe Mom is right. They are brother and sister, and they love each other. They want to be best friends too. They say thank you to Mom and keep playing. They have fun and they don't fight anymore.
