Category: Beginner
Title: Lila and Sam and the Bird

Lila and Sam were best friends. They liked to play outside in the park. One day, they saw a big puddle on the grass. They ran to the puddle and jumped in it. They splashed and laughed and made waves. "Look, Lila, a bird!" Sam said, pointing to the sky. Lila looked up and saw a big white bird with long wings. It was soaring high above the trees. It looked different from the other birds they knew. "What kind of bird is that?" Lila asked. "I don't know. Maybe it's a swan. Or a goose. Or a cloud-bird." Sam guessed. "A cloud-bird? What's that?" Lila wondered. "A cloud-bird is a bird that lives in the clouds. It can fly very fast and very far. It can make rain and snow and thunder. It's very special." Sam explained. "Wow, I wish I could see a cloud-bird up close. Do you think it would be friendly?" Lila said. "Maybe. Maybe we can be friends with the cloud-bird. Maybe we can soar with it." Sam said. They both smiled and imagined flying with the cloud-bird. They held hands and jumped in the puddle again. They felt happy and free. They felt different from the other kids. They felt like cloud-birds.

Once upon a time there was a busy little girl named Mary. Mary was walking down the street with her mom. Mary spotted a cute necklace in a store window. "Mom, can I have the necklace in the window?" Mary asked. Her mom shook her head. "No Mary, it is too expensive," she said. Mary was disappointed, but she could understand why her mom said no. Mary stopped walking and looked around. She was amazed at how many people were walking around her. Everyone was so busy. Then she noticed a little ant marching up her arm. She smiled and watched it. "Mom, look at this little ant. It is so busy. I can see it values hard work!" Her mom smiled at her and said, "Yes, Mary, hard work is important. Just like this little ant, you should value hard work too." Mary nodded and said, "I understand, mom. I will value all the hard work I do." Mary and her mom continued on their walk, but Mary could not stop thinking about the little ant. She knew that it was important to value hard work, just like the ant. Mary smiled to herself and held onto her mom's neck, feeling very content and happy. The end.

Once upon a time, there was a wealthy cat named Tom. He loved to dance and play all day. One sunny day, Tom put on his hat and went outside to find his friends. Tom saw a hedge near his house. Behind the hedge, he heard a sound. It was his friend, the little bird, singing a happy song. Tom wanted to dance with the bird, so he asked, "Bird, will you dance with me?" The bird was happy to dance with Tom. They danced and played near the hedge all day long. They had so much fun and laughed a lot. Tom and the bird became the best of friends. And from that day on, they always danced and played together near the hedge.

Lily and Anna were sisters who liked to play dress-up. They had many clothes and hats and shoes to try on. One day, they found a veil in their mom's closet. It was white and long and had flowers on it. "Wow, look at this!" Lily said. "It is so pretty! Can I wear it?" "No, I want to wear it!" Anna said. "It matches my dress!" They both grabbed the veil and pulled it. They did not want to share it. They were persistent and did not listen to each other. "Stop it, girls!" their mom said. "You are tearing the veil! It is not a toy, it is a special thing. I wore it on my wedding day. It is very important to me." Lily and Anna felt sorry. They did not know the veil was special. They let go of the veil and hugged their mom. "We are sorry, mom," they said. "We did not mean to hurt the veil. We just wanted to play." "I know, girls," their mom said. "But you have to be careful with other people's things. And you have to learn to share and take turns. That is how you show respect and love." Lily and Anna nodded. They learned their lesson. They gave the veil back to their mom and thanked her. Then they went to play with their own clothes. They matched their outfits and had fun. They also shared and took turns. They were happy sisters.
