Category: Beginner
Title: Amy the Girl and the Marble

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. She had a big, ugly marble. She found it in her yard. Amy loved her marble, even if it was ugly. One day, Amy's friend, Tom, came over to play. Tom saw the ugly marble and said, "Amy, why do you like that ugly marble?" Amy smiled and said, "I like it because it's special to me." Amy and Tom decided to clean the ugly marble. They used water and soap. They scrubbed and scrubbed. When they were done, the ugly marble was shiny and pretty. They played with the clean marble all day and had lots of fun.

Once upon a time, there was a dolphin named Dolly. Dolly lived in the big, blue sea. She had many friends who liked to play with her. They would jump and swim all day. One day, Dolly found a toy in the water. It was a cheap toy, not very nice. But Dolly liked it and wanted to show her friends. She swam fast to find them. When Dolly showed her friends the toy, they laughed at her. Dolly felt embarrass. She swam away with the toy, feeling sad. But then, her friends came to say sorry. They played with the toy together, and everyone was happy again.

Sara and Ben were playing with their toys in the living room. They had a big box that they used as a house, a car, or a boat. They liked to pretend that they were on adventures. "Let's go to the beach!" Ben said, putting on his hat and sunglasses. He grabbed a towel and a bucket. "We can make sand castles and find shells." "OK!" Sara agreed, following him. She took her doll and a shovel. They dragged the box to the backyard, where there was a sandbox. They put the box in the sand and climbed inside. They pretended that they were sailing on the ocean. They used the shovel and the bucket to make sand castles and dig holes. They found some rocks and sticks and pretended that they were shells and fish. They had a lot of fun, but they also got very dirty. Sand was everywhere: on their clothes, in their hair, and on their toys. The sun was very hot and harsh. It made them thirsty and sweaty. "Let's go inside and wash," Sara said, feeling sticky and itchy. "We can have some juice and cookies." "OK," Ben said, feeling tired and thirsty. He grabbed the towel and the bucket. He helped Sara carry the box and the doll. They left the sandbox and went to the door. But when they got there, they saw Mom waiting for them. She looked angry. She saw the sand and the dirt on them and on the floor. "What have you done?" she asked, in a loud and harsh voice. "You made a big mess! You can't bring the box and the toys inside. They are too dirty. You have to wash them outside first. And then you have to wash yourselves. Go to the hose and spray everything. Now!" Sara and Ben felt scared and sad. They did not mean to make Mom angry. They just wanted to have fun. They did not like the hose. It was cold and wet. They did not want to wash their toys. They liked them the way they were. They started to cry. They dropped the box and the toys. They hugged each other. "Please, Mom, don't be harsh," Sara said, in a small and soft voice. "We are sorry. We love you." Mom saw their tears and heard their words. She felt sorry too. She realized that she was too harsh. She knelt down and hugged them. "I'm sorry too," she said, in a gentle and kind voice. "I love you too. I know you were just playing. But you have to be careful. Sand can hurt your skin and your eyes. And dirt can make you sick. You have to wash it off. It's good for you. I'll help you. Come on, let's go to the hose. It will be fun. And then we can have some juice and cookies. OK?" Sara and Ben nodded. They felt better. They smiled. They took the box and the toys. They followed Mom to the hose. They washed everything. They laughed and splashed. They had fun. They learned that washing was not so bad. And that Mom was not so harsh. She was just trying to keep them safe and clean. They loved her. And she loved them.
