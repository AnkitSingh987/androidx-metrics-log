Category: Beginner
Title: Molly the Girl and the Garden

Once upon a time there was a little girl named Molly. She was only three years old and she was very curious. Every day she would explore her garden. One day, Molly saw a pretty cap lying in the middle of the garden. She was so happy that she picked it up and put it on her head. Suddenly, Molly's mum came out of the house and shouted, "Molly, what are you doing with that cap?" Molly replied, "I found it in the garden, Mum." Molly's mum said, "No, that cap belongs to me. You must give it back to me." Molly started crying and said, "No, it's so pretty, I want to keep it." Molly's mum said, "You can keep it, but you must remember to take it back to me." Molly was so happy and she hugged her mum. She said, "Yes, Mum, I will remember to take back the pretty cap to you." The end.

One day, a cat and a dog were in a big house. They liked to talk and play. The dog said, "I am tired. Let's nap." The cat said, "Yes, I like naps too." So, they lay down in a warm, mild spot near the window. As they slept, a big wind blew the door open. A little bird flew inside. The bird saw the cat and dog napping. The bird said, "Wake up! I need help!" The cat and dog woke up and asked, "What is wrong?" The bird said, "I lost my way. Can you show me how to go home?" The cat and dog felt love for the little bird. They said, "We will help you." They all went outside and found the bird's home. The bird was happy and said, "Thank you, friends!" The cat and dog were happy too, because they made a new friend.

Once upon a time there was a daisy who was worried. She was stuck in a pot and was very sad. But then one day, a kind man came along and saw her. He smiled at her and held out his hand to her. With a gentle touch, he released her from the pot and held her in his hands. The daisy was so happy that she felt like she could jump for joy! She thanked the kind man for releasing her and she held onto his hand for the rest of the day. From then on, the daisy was no longer worried. She was free to fly wherever she wanted and to feel the wind on her petals. She was so happy to be released from the pot and thanked the kind man every day. The end.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play in the park with their mom and dad. One day, they see a big truck with a picture of a muffin on it. The truck stops near the park and a man opens the back door. "Who wants a muffin?" the man shouts. "I have chocolate, blueberry, banana and more!" Anna and Ben love muffins. They rush to the truck with their mom and dad. They see many muffins in boxes and bags. They smell sweet and yummy. "Can we have a muffin, please?" Anna and Ben ask. "Sure, you can have one each," the man says. "What flavor do you want?" Anna picks a chocolate muffin. Ben picks a blueberry muffin. They say thank you to the man and take their muffins. They sit on a bench and bite their muffins. They are soft and warm and delicious. "Wow, these are the best muffins ever!" Anna says. "I agree," Ben says. "They make me happy." They finish their muffins and smile. They look at their mom and dad. They are pale and quiet. They have not eaten their muffins. "Are you not hungry?" Anna asks. "Are you not feeling well?" Ben asks. Their mom and dad shake their heads. They point to the truck. The truck has a sign that says: "Muffins for Mice. Not for Humans." Anna and Ben gasp. They look at their muffins. They see tiny holes and crumbs. They see little tails and whiskers. They have eaten muffins for mice! They feel sick and scared. They drop their muffins and run to their mom and dad. They hug them and cry. "Sorry, kids," the man says. "I should have told you. These muffins are for my pet mice. They love them. But they are not good for humans. They have cheese and nuts and seeds and other things that humans don't like." He gives them a bottle of water and a napkin. He tells them to drink and wipe their mouths. He says they will be fine. He says he is sorry. He says he will give them real muffins next time. Anna and Ben drink and wipe their mouths. They feel a little better. They say they are sorry too. They say they will not eat muffins for mice again. They go back to the park with their mom and dad. They play and laugh and forget about the muffins. They are happy again. But they are careful when they see a truck with a picture of a muffin on it. They always ask what kind of muffin it is. They do not want to eat muffins for mice again. They only want muffins for humans.
