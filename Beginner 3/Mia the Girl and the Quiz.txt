Category: Beginner
Title: Mia the Girl and the Quiz

Once upon a time, in a small noisy town, there lived a little girl named Mia. Mia loved to play with her friends and learn new things. One day, her teacher told her that they would have a quiz at school. Mia was excited about the quiz. She wanted to do her best and reach the top score. She studied with her mom and dad at home, and they helped her learn all the simple words she needed to know. On the day of the quiz, Mia was a little scared, but she knew she could do it. She listened to the teacher and answered all the questions. When the quiz was over, Mia had reached her goal and got the top score. She was very happy, and her friends and family were proud of her.

Sara and Ben were twins who liked to play pretend. They had a fancy tea set with cups and saucers and spoons. They also had a big tablecloth that they spread on the floor to make a picnic. They invited their stuffed animals to join them. "Would you like some coffee, Mr. Bear?" Sara asked, pouring some water from a kettle into a cup. "Yes, please, Sara. You are very kind," Mr. Bear said in a deep voice. "Me too, me too!" Ben said, holding out his cup. "I want coffee!" "Okay, Ben. Here you go," Sara said, filling his cup. "But be careful, it's hot!" Ben nodded and took a sip. He made a face and spat it out. "Yuck! This is not coffee! This is water!" Sara giggled. "Of course it's not coffee, silly! We don't have coffee. We're just pretending!" Ben frowned. "But I want real coffee! Mommy and Daddy drink coffee every morning. It smells good!" Sara shook her head. "No, Ben. Coffee is not for kids. It's only for grown-ups. It's too strong and bitter for us. We have juice or milk instead." Ben pouted. He wanted to try coffee. He had a plan. He waited until Sara was busy talking to Mr. Bear. Then he got up and sneaked into the kitchen. He saw a big jar of coffee on the counter. He reached for it and opened it. He smelled it. It smelled good. He poured some into his cup. He added some water from the sink. He stirred it with his spoon. He took a sip. He smiled. It tasted good. He drank some more. He felt happy. He went back to the picnic. "Look, Sara! I have real coffee!" Ben said, showing his cup. Sara gasped. "Ben! Where did you get that? That's Mommy and Daddy's coffee! You can't have that!" Ben shrugged. "I can. I like it. It's yummy!" Sara shook her head. "No, Ben. You can't. Coffee is not for kids. It's bad for you. It will make you sick. And Mommy and Daddy will be mad. You have to stop!" Ben ignored her. He drank some more. He felt funny. His heart was beating fast. His hands were shaking. His stomach was hurting. He felt sick. He dropped his cup. He started to cry. Sara ran to him. She hugged him. She felt sorry for him. She called Mommy and Daddy. They came and saw what happened. They were worried. They took Ben to the doctor. The doctor gave him some medicine. He said Ben would be okay. He said coffee was not for kids. He said Ben had to rest. Sara and Ben learned a lesson. They never drank coffee again. They only played pretend. They had juice or milk instead. They were happy. The end.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She loved to play hide and seek with her friends. One day, Lily's mom made a big cup of cocoa for her. Lily was very glad because it was her favorite drink. Lily wanted to share the cocoa with her friends. She thought, "I will play hide and seek with the cocoa!" So, she went outside to find the best spot to hide the cocoa. She found a big tree with lots of leaves and put the cocoa there. Her friends came over to play. Lily told them, "I have hidden the cocoa! Let's see who can find it!" They all started looking for the cocoa. After some time, Lily's friend, Tim, found the cocoa under the big tree. Everyone was so happy and glad. They sat under the tree and enjoyed the cocoa together.
