Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Bench

One day, a little boy named Tim went to the park with his mom. It was a wet day, and there were puddles everywhere. Tim loved to jump in the puddles and make a big splash. As Tim was playing, he saw a little girl named Sue sitting on a bench. She looked sad. Tim walked over to her and asked, "Why are you sad?" Sue said, "My toy fell in the water and now it's all wet." Tim wanted to help Sue, so he went to the puddle and picked up her wet toy. He shook it until it was not wet anymore. Sue's face lit up with a big smile. She said, "Thank you, Tim! You made my toy dry again!" They played together until the day had to end. Tim and Sue became best friends. They learned that a kind heart can make even the wettest day feel warm and sunny.

Anna and Ben are twins. They like to play together. Sometimes they play with cars, sometimes they play with dolls, sometimes they play with blocks. But their favorite game is to race to the bathtub. Every night, when their mom says it is time for a bath, they run to the bathroom as fast as they can. They shout, "Ready, set, go!" and try to be the first one to reach the bathtub. They are eager to splash in the water and make bubbles with the soap. One night, Anna and Ben are racing to the bathtub as usual. But they are so excited that they do not see the toy truck on the floor. Anna steps on the truck and slips. She falls down and hurts her knee. She cries, "Ow, ow, ow!" Ben hears her cry and stops running. He turns around and sees his sister on the floor. He feels sorry for her. He runs to her and hugs her. He says, "Are you okay, Anna? I'm sorry you fell. Do you want me to help you?" Anna stops crying and smiles at her brother. She says, "Thank you, Ben. You are a good brother. I'm okay, it's just a little scratch. Can you help me get up?" Ben helps Anna get up and they walk to the bathtub together. They hold hands and say, "We are both winners. We love each other. And we love bath time."

Once upon a time, there was a boy who liked to lean on the ground when he was outside. He would lean down and look at the little bugs that were crawling around. He was so fascinated by them. One day, he leaned down too far and he fell! He tried to get up, but he couldn't. He was very scared. Then, a serious-looking man came to help him. The man picked him up and asked him if he was okay. The boy was very scared and shook his head no. The man helped the boy walk back home. When they got there, the man asked to speak to the boy's mom. The mom was very serious. She was mad at the boy for leaning too far. She told him that next time he should be more careful or he could get hurt. The boy listened to his mom, and he never leaned down on the ground that way again. He was more careful and stayed safe.

Ben and Mia were twins who liked to play in the park. They saw a big bridge that crossed a stream. They wanted to go over the bridge and see what was on the other side. "Let's crawl under the bridge first," Ben said. "It will be fun and scary." "OK," Mia said. "But be careful. There might be bugs or mud." They crawled under the bridge, holding hands. They saw some stones, some sticks, and some leaves. They also saw a small hole in the wall of the bridge. "Look, a hole!" Ben said. "I wonder what's inside." He put his hand in the hole and felt something soft and furry. He pulled it out and saw a baby rabbit. "Wow, a rabbit!" Mia said. "It's so cute and humble. Can we keep it?" Ben smiled and nodded. He held the rabbit gently and stroked its ears. The rabbit looked at them with big eyes and twitched its nose. They crawled out of the bridge, happy and excited. They wanted to show their new friend to their mom and dad. They did not see the big sign that said "Danger: Do not touch the rabbits. They are sick and can bite." They walked towards their parents, who were sitting on a bench. They did not notice the rabbit's teeth getting sharper and its eyes getting redder. They did not hear the rabbit's growl getting louder and louder. They did not feel the rabbit's bite until it was too late.
