Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Tree and the Yield

Once upon a time, there was a little tree named Tim. Tim was a troubled tree. He did not know how to yield good fruits. All the other trees could yield big and sweet fruits, but not Tim. One day, a wise old tree told Tim, "You need to breathe in more oxygen to yield good fruits." Tim did not know what oxygen was, but he tried his best to breathe in more air. He took big breaths and hoped it would help him. After some time, Tim started to feel better. He saw that he started to yield good fruits too! All the other trees were happy for Tim. Now, Tim was not a troubled tree anymore. He learned that oxygen was important for him to grow and yield good fruits.

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big tree with green balls on it. Anna says, "What are those?" Ben says, "They are olives. My mom puts them on pizza. Do you want to try one?" Anna nods. She is excited. She likes pizza. She climbs the tree and picks an olive. She bites it. She makes a face. She spits it out. She says, "Yuck! This is not pizza. This is sour and bitter." Ben laughs. He says, "You have to wait. Mom says olives need salt and oil to taste good. She buys them in a jar. Do you want to see?" Anna says, "Yes, please. Maybe I will like them then." They climb down the tree and run to Ben's house. Ben shows Anna the jar of olives. They are black and shiny. Anna says, "They look different. Can I have one?" Ben says, "Sure. Here you go." Anna takes an olive and pops it in her mouth. She smiles. She says, "Mmm, this is good. It is salty and soft. I like it." Ben says, "Me too. Do you want more?" Anna says, "Yes, please. Can we have some pizza too?" Ben says, "Maybe later. Mom says we have to eat lunch first. Come on, let's go." They leave the jar of olives on the table and go to the kitchen. They are happy. They are friends. They like olives.

Once upon a time, in a big forest, there was a kangaroo. The kangaroo liked to jump and play all day. He had many friends, like the bird, the cat, and the dog. They all liked to play together in the sun. One day, the kangaroo got very tired. He wanted to sleep. He looked for a comfortable place to lie down. He found a soft, green spot under a big tree. The kangaroo lay down and closed his eyes. While the kangaroo was sleeping, a big storm came. The wind blew and the rain fell hard. The kangaroo woke up and was very wet and cold. He tried to find his friends, but they were all hiding from the storm. The kangaroo was sad and alone in the rain.

One day, a boy named Tim and a girl named Sue went to play outside. They saw a big sheet hanging on a line. Tim said, "Let's make a tent with this sheet!" Sue agreed, and they started to build their tent. While they were playing, a spicy smell came from the house. Sue said, "I smell something spicy!" Tim replied, "Me too! Let's go see what it is." They went inside and saw their mom cooking. "What are you making, Mom?" asked Tim. Mom replied, "I'm making spicy soup for lunch." Tim and Sue wanted to try the spicy soup, but when they tasted it, it was too spicy for them! They started to cry. Mom said, "I'm sorry, I didn't know it would be too spicy for you. Let's make something else." Just then, the sheet from their tent flew inside the house through the window. It landed on the table and covered the spicy soup. Tim and Sue laughed as they realized the sheet had saved them from the spicy soup.

One day, a flower named Lily was in a race with her friends. They were all running very fast. Lily was small and not as fast as the others, but she tried her best. As they raced, a loud noise came from the sky. "What is that?" asked Lily's friend, Bee. They all stopped to look. A big, colorful bird flew down and landed next to Lily. The bird said, "I can help you win the race. Just hop on my back!" Lily got on the bird's back and they flew ahead of her friends. Lily was so happy to win the race, she started to bloom with pretty colors. Lily's friends clapped and cheered. They were all happy that Lily won the race, even with the help of her new friend, the big, colorful bird.
