Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and Ben and the Toys

Sara and Ben are going on a trip with Mom and Dad. They are very happy. They have their own luggage. Sara has a pink one and Ben has a blue one. They put their toys and clothes inside. They go to the airport. It is very big and noisy. They see many people and planes. Mom and Dad check in their luggage. Sara and Ben want to keep theirs. "No, you have to give them to the man," Mom says. "He will put them on the plane. You will get them back later." "But I want my luggage!" Sara says. She hugs her pink one. "It has my teddy bear inside!" "And I want my luggage!" Ben says. He holds his blue one. "It has my dinosaur inside!" Mom and Dad try to explain. They say the luggage is too big and heavy to take on the plane. They say the man will be careful with them. They say they will have fun on the trip. Sara and Ben are not convinced. They think their luggage is not too big or heavy. They think it is hard to say goodbye to their toys. They think the trip is scary. They start to cry. They do not want to give their luggage to the man. They do not want to go on the plane. They want to go home. Mom and Dad are sad. They do not know what to do. They want to make Sara and Ben happy. They want to go on the trip. They look at each other. They have an idea. They smile. They whisper something to the man. The man nods. He takes Sara and Ben's luggage. He puts a sticker on each one. He says, "Don't worry, kids. Your luggage will be right next to you on the plane. You can see them from your seats. They will be safe and sound." Sara and Ben stop crying. They look at the man. They look at their luggage. They see the stickers. They have their names and pictures on them. Sara sees a teddy bear. Ben sees a dinosaur. They smile. They say, "Thank you, man!" They follow Mom and Dad to the plane. They find their seats. They look out the window. They see their luggage. They wave to them. They are happy. They are ready to go on the trip. They are excited to see new things. They are glad they have their luggage.

True was a very honest and kind 3 year old who had a big heart. One day, True's mommy asked her to take a bath. True didn't want to do it, so she started to cry. She was scared to bathe because it felt so strange. Mommy tried to make it better. She told True that she would stay and bathe together with her. True was so happy and she stopped crying. True and Mommy enjoyed bathing together, and True felt better afterwards. The two were very honest with each other, and they learned that bathing together could be a fun and special thing to do. True learned that it was OK to be scared, because she had Mommy to help her feel better.

Once upon a time, there was an adorable little dog named Spot. Spot loved to play with his toys and run around in the park. One sunny day, Spot found a shiny coin on the ground. He picked it up with his mouth and showed it to his best friend, a little girl named Lucy. "Look, Lucy!" said Spot. "I found a coin!" Lucy looked at the coin and had an idea. "Let's measure how far you can jump with the coin in your mouth!" she said. Spot wagged his tail and agreed. Lucy found a long stick and used it to measure how far Spot could jump. They played all day, measuring Spot's jumps and laughing together. At the end of the day, Spot and Lucy were tired but happy. They went home, excited to play more games with the coin the next day.

Once upon a time, there was a lonely dog named Spot. Spot had no friends to play with, so he would play with his shampoo bottle. He liked to push it around the floor with his nose. One day, Spot saw a little girl named Sue. Sue was playing with a ball. Spot wanted to play with Sue, so he brought his shampoo bottle to her. "Do you want to play with me and my shampoo?" asked Spot. Sue looked at Spot and said, "No, I don't want to play with your shampoo. I want to play with my ball." Spot felt sad and didn't know what to do. He tried to take the ball from Sue, but she said, "Stop! That's my ball!" Spot felt even sadder and walked away with his shampoo bottle. Spot never found a friend to play with, and he stayed lonely. He kept playing with his shampoo bottle, but it wasn't the same as having a friend.
