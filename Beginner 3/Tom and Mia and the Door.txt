Category: Beginner
Title: Tom and Mia and the Door

Tom and Mia were twins who liked to play in the forest. One day, they saw a big house with a black roof and a red door. They were curious and wanted to look inside. "Maybe there is someone nice who lives here," Mia said. "Or maybe there is a witch who will eat us," Tom said. They knocked on the door, but no one answered. They pushed it open and walked in. The house was dark and messy. There were pots and pans, books and bottles, and a big black cat. "Hello?" Mia called out. "Who are you?" a voice said. It was a witch who was lying on a couch. She had a long nose, a pointy hat, and a green dress. She looked angry and tired. "We are sorry, we just wanted to see your house," Tom said. "See my house? How rude! This is my house, and you are not welcome here. Go away, or I will turn you into frogs!" the witch said. The twins were scared and ran to the door. But the door was locked. They tried to open it, but it did not move. "Please, let us go. We won't bother you again," Mia said. "No, you will stay here and learn. You will learn to clean my house, to cook my food, to feed my cat, and to do my spells. You will be my helpers, and I will be your teacher. You will work hard, and I will be lazy. That is the deal," the witch said. The twins did not like the deal. They did not want to be the witch's helpers. They wanted to go home and play. They looked around and saw a window. It was small and high, but maybe they could reach it. "Tom, I have an idea. Let's climb on that table and jump out of the window. Maybe we can escape," Mia whispered. "OK, let's try. But be careful, the witch might see us," Tom whispered back. They waited until the witch was snoring on the couch. Then they tiptoed to the table and climbed on it. They pushed the window open and jumped out. They landed on the grass and ran away. They ran until they reached their home. They hugged their mom and dad and told them what happened. Their parents were shocked and glad that they were safe. "Never go to that house again. That witch is mean and dangerous. You were lucky to get away," their mom said. "We know, mom. We learned our lesson. We will never be curious again," the twins said. But they did not really mean it. They still liked to play in the forest and explore new things. They just avoided the witch's house and hoped she would not find them. They were happy and brave, and the witch was lonely and lazy. That was the end.

Once upon a time, there was a big, red balloon. The balloon was very flexible. It could bend and twist in many ways. The balloon lived in a small town with a little boy named Tim. One day, the balloon saw a big, green apple. The apple was starting to rot. The balloon said, "Oh no, Mr. Apple! You don't look so good!" The apple replied, "Yes, I am not feeling well. I am starting to rot. Can you help me?" The flexible balloon had a great idea. It bent and twisted itself into a nice, soft bed for the apple. The apple lay on the balloon bed, and soon it felt much better. The rot went away, and the apple was happy again. Tim saw the balloon and the apple, and he was so happy. He said, "Thank you, Mr. Balloon, for helping Mr. Apple!" The balloon and the apple became best friends, and they all lived happily ever after.

One day, a boy named Tim went to play at his friend's house. They ran upstairs to find a big box of toys. Tim saw a tight rope and wanted to play with it. His friend, Sam, wanted to play with it too. They both held on to the rope and did not want to let go. "No, it's mine!" said Tim. "No, I want it!" said Sam. They pulled and pulled on the tight rope, not wanting to part with it. They were both very upset. Suddenly, the rope broke into two pieces. Tim and Sam looked at each other, surprised. Then, they both started to laugh. They realized they could each have a piece of the rope to play with. They were happy again and played together for the rest of the day.
