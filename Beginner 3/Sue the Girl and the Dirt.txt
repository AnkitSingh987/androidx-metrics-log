Category: Beginner
Title: Sue the Girl and the Dirt

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to play outside in the dirt. One day, she decided to bury her toys in the dirt. She dug a big hole and put her toys in it. Then, she covered them with dirt. Sue felt very impressive. Sue's mom was doing laundry and saw Sue playing outside. She called Sue to come inside and help with the laundry. Sue came in and started folding the clothes. While folding, she noticed that her favorite shirt was missing. She looked everywhere but could not find it. Then, Sue remembered that she had buried her toys outside. She ran back to the hole and started digging. To her surprise, she found her favorite shirt buried with the toys! Sue was so happy that she found her shirt. She learned to be more careful with her things and not to bury them with her toys.

Lily loved to help her mom make bread. She liked to touch the soft dough and watch it grow in the oven. She admired her mom for knowing how to make such yummy food. One day, Lily wanted to surprise her mom with a special bread. She found some dough in the fridge and put it on the table. She looked for something to make it more fun. She saw some red and green candies in a jar. She thought they would look pretty on the bread. She took some candies and pressed them into the dough. She put the dough in the oven and waited. She was very excited to show her mom her creation. She hoped her mom would admire it too. When the timer beeped, Lily opened the oven. She was confused. The bread did not look like she expected. It was brown and hard. The candies had melted and made a sticky mess. It smelled bad. Lily started to cry. She heard her mom coming. She was afraid her mom would be angry. Her mom saw the bread and Lily. She hugged Lily and said, "What happened, sweetie?" Lily sobbed, "I wanted to make you a special bread, but it turned out bad. I'm sorry, mom." Her mom smiled and said, "It's okay, Lily. I appreciate your effort. You tried to do something nice for me. But next time, please ask me before you use the dough and the candies. They are not meant for bread. They are for cookies and cakes." Lily nodded and said, "I'm sorry, mom. I love you." Her mom said, "I love you too, Lily. How about we make some cookies together? You can choose the candies you like." Lily smiled and said, "Yes, please. That sounds fun." They went to the kitchen and made some cookies. They ate some and shared some with dad. Lily learned that it is good to be creative, but it is also important to follow the rules and ask for help when needed. She also learned that her mom admired her for being kind and brave, not for being perfect.

Anna and Ben are friends. They like to play outside. One day, they see a big hill near their house. They want to climb the hill and see what is on the other side. "Let's go to the hill!" Anna says. "Maybe we can find something fun!" "OK!" Ben says. "But we have to be careful. The hill is very high and steep." They hold hands and start to walk to the hill. They see some flowers, some rocks, and some bugs on the way. They stop to look at them and touch them. They are happy and curious. They reach the top of the hill. They look around. They see a beautiful lake with some ducks and fish. They see some trees with some birds and squirrels. They see some clouds with some shapes and colors. "Wow!" Anna says. "This is so beautiful! Look at the lake and the trees and the clouds!" "Wow!" Ben says. "This is so beautiful! Look at the ducks and the fish and the birds!" They sit on the grass and watch the lake and the trees and the clouds. They smile and laugh. They are amazed and grateful. They hear a voice. It is their mom. She is calling them from their house. "Anna! Ben! Time to come back! It is getting dark!" They look at each other. They do not want to leave the hill. They want to stay and play. "Can we stay a little longer?" Anna asks. "Please?" Ben adds. They hear their mom again. She sounds more serious. "Anna! Ben! Come back now! It is not safe to be on the hill at night!" They sigh. They know they have to listen to their mom. They stand up and start to walk back to their house. They look at the hill one more time. They shrug. They know they can come back another day. They know the hill will be there, waiting for them. They know the hill is their friend.
