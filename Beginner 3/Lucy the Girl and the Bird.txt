Category: Beginner
Title: Lucy the Girl and the Bird

Once there was a girl named Lucy. She was three years old and she was very bossy. She liked to boss her friends around and tell them what to do. One day she was outside and saw a bird. She wanted to play with the bird and chased after it. She ran a long way and her tummy started to growl. She was so hungry! Just then, a kind lady called out to her. The lady had a big container of rice. Lucy was very happy. The lady said, "I will give you some rice if you promise to go home." Lucy was so excited. She gave the lady her word that she would go home. The lady gave Lucy the rice. Lucy ran back home and ate the rice with a big smile on her face. Lucy was very happy that the kind lady kept her promise. Lucy learned that it was important to keep her promises too.

One day, a boy named Tim went to his friend Sam's house to play. They liked to play a game called chess. Chess had many little pieces that they moved on a board. Tim and Sam took turns moving the pieces, trying to win the game. As they played, Tim's mom came in with a big plate of sour lemon cookies. She said, "Here you go, boys! I made these for you." Tim and Sam smiled and said, "Thank you!" They each took a cookie and ate it. The cookies were very sour, but they liked them a lot. Tim's mom sat down and watched them play chess. She asked, "Who is winning?" Tim and Sam looked at the board and then at each other. They both said, "I don't know!" They laughed and kept playing, happy to have a fun day with chess and sour cookies.

Once upon a time there was a fast little Spike. He lived in a small green house with his two friends, Pinky and Porky. One day the little friends decided to race each other. "Whoever gets to the tree first wins!" said Spike. So they started to race. Pinky was very fast and flew through the grass. Porky was running close behind, and Spike was jumping after them. "Oh no!" thought Spike. Pinky and Porky had already reached the tree, but Spike was still way behind. He had missed out on the race and was sad. Luckily, his friends saw that he was sad. "Don't worry Spike," said Porky in a very friendly voice. "We can race again tomorrow if you want!" "Yes!" said Spike. He was very happy that his friends were so understanding, and he gave them both a big hug. The end.

Sara and Ben were playing in the garden. They liked to dig in the soil and make mud pies. They had a lovely time until they saw a big hole in the fence. "Oh no!" Sara said. "Dad will be mad. He said we should not break the fence." "Maybe we can fix it," Ben said. He picked up some sticks and tried to put them in the hole. But they were too short and fell out. "That won't work," Sara said. She looked around and saw some flowers. "Maybe we can use these." She picked some flowers and tried to stick them in the hole. But they were too soft and wilted. "That won't work either," Ben said. He felt sad and scared. "What can we do?" Just then, they heard a voice. It was Dad. He came to the garden and saw the hole in the fence. He also saw the sticks and the flowers. "What happened here?" he asked. Sara and Ben looked at each other. They knew they had to tell the truth. "We were playing in the soil and we made a big hole in the fence," Sara said. "We tried to fix it with sticks and flowers, but it didn't work," Ben said. They waited for Dad to be angry. But he was not. He smiled and hugged them. "I'm glad you told me the truth," he said. "And I'm glad you tried to fix it. But sticks and flowers are not good for fences. We need nails and wood." He took them to the shed and got some tools and materials. He showed them how to fix the hole in the fence. He let them help him hammer and saw. They had fun and learned something new. When they were done, the fence looked good again. Sara and Ben were happy and proud. "Thank you, Dad," they said. "You're welcome, Sara and Ben," he said. "You are lovely children. And you have lovely flowers. Why don't we plant them in the soil and make a nice garden?"
