Category: Beginner
Title: Bob the Light Blue Bird and the Park

Once upon a time, there was a light blue bird named Bob. Bob liked to play near a fountain in the park. Every day, he would fly around the fountain and splash in the water. He loved how the sun made the water sparkle. One day, a big dog named Max came to the park. Max saw the fountain and wanted to play too. But when Max jumped into the fountain, he made a big mess. The water became dirty, and the pretty fountain was spoiled. Bob was sad because his favorite place to play was spoiled. But Max felt bad and wanted to help. Together, they cleaned the fountain until it was sparkling again. Now, Bob and Max play together at the fountain every day, and they became the best of friends.

Once upon a time, there was a cute little cat. The cat lived in a big castle with a king. The king loved the cat very much. They played together every day. One day, the cat saw a bird stuck in a tree. The cat wanted to help the bird. The cat said, "Don't worry, bird. I will rescue you." The cat climbed the tree and helped the bird get free. But then, the cat got stuck in the tree too! The king saw the cat stuck in the tree. He got a big ladder and climbed up to rescue the cat. The king said, "Be careful next time, little cat." The cat and the bird were happy and safe. The king, the cat, and the bird all became good friends. The moral of the story is to always help others, but be careful and ask for help when you need it too.

Once, there was a man and his wife. The man knew he could always count on his dependable wife. They lived in a small house with their dog, Spot. Every day, they would play with Spot and have fun together. One day, the man and his wife went to the park with Spot. They played with a big red ball. They threw the ball and Spot would run and catch it. They were all very happy. But then, something unexpected happened. As they were playing, a big wind came and took the red ball away. The man, his wife, and Spot did not know what to do. They looked and looked for the ball, but it was gone. They were sad and started to go home. But then, Spot saw something. It was the red ball! It was stuck in a tree. The man and his wife didn't know how to get it down. Spot had an idea. He jumped and jumped until he got the ball down from the tree. The man and his wife were so happy! They knew that they could always count on their dependable dog, Spot. From that day on, they played with the red ball every day, and they knew that they could always trust Spot to help them when they needed it.

Sara loves to ride her bike. She has a unique bike with pink and purple stripes and a basket with flowers. She likes to go to the park with her bike and play with her friends. One day, she sees a boy with a big bike. His bike has no stripes, no basket, and no flowers. It has a bell and a light. He rides very fast and makes loud noises. Sara thinks his bike is boring and ugly. "Hey, you!" the boy shouts at Sara. "Your bike is silly and girly. You can't ride fast like me. You should get a better bike." Sara feels angry and sad. She likes her bike. She thinks it is pretty and fun. She does not want a better bike. She wants the boy to leave her alone. "No, you!" she shouts back. "Your bike is noisy and dull. You can't ride smooth like me. You should get a nicer bike." The boy feels angry and proud. He likes his bike. He thinks it is cool and strong. He does not want a nicer bike. He wants Sara to admit he is better. They start to argue and ride around the park. They try to show off their bikes and skills. They do not see the big rock on the path. They both hit the rock and fall off their bikes. Their bikes get hurt. Sara's tire pops. The boy's light breaks. They both cry and look at their bikes. They feel sorry and scared. They do not care about their bikes being unique or better. They just want their bikes to be okay. They look at each other and stop crying. They see that they are both hurt and sad. They see that they both love their bikes. They see that they both made a mistake. "I'm sorry," Sara says. "I was mean to you. Your bike is not ugly. It is different. Different is good." "I'm sorry, too," the boy says. "I was rude to you. Your bike is not silly. It is special. Special is good." They smile and help each other. They walk their bikes to the bike shop. They ask the bike man to fix their bikes. They wait and talk. They find out they have some things in common. They like the same ice cream. They like the same animals. They like to ride their bikes. They become friends and ride their bikes together. They learn to appreciate their bikes and each other. They learn that unique is good.
