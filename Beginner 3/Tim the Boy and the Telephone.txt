Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Telephone

One day, a big boy named Tim found a telephone. He thought it was impressive. He showed it to his friend, Sue. Sue was very happy to see it. They played with the telephone, pretending to talk to each other. But then, their friend, Sam, came and wanted to add his toy car to their playtime. Tim and Sue did not want to play with the car. They only wanted to play with the telephone. This made Sam sad and upset. Sam walked away and found a big box. He put his toy car in the box and started to push it. Tim and Sue saw this and thought it was fun. They asked Sam if they could add their telephone to the box and play together. Sam agreed, and they all played happily with the box, the car, and the telephone.

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim had a favorite shirt. It was a modern shirt with many colors. He wore it all the time. One day, Tim's shirt got a big hole in it. Tim felt very sad. He started to weep. His mom saw him crying and asked, "Why do you weep, Tim?" Tim showed the hole in his shirt to his mom. She said, "Don't worry, I can fix it." She took the shirt and went to work. Tim waited and hoped his shirt would be like new. After some time, his mom came back with the shirt. The hole was gone! Tim was so happy. He hugged his mom and said, "Thank you!" He put on his modern shirt and played all day, feeling very glad.

One day, a red cat and a little mouse were in the park. The cat saw the mouse and wanted to play. The mouse had a big piece of cheese. The cat said, "Can I have some cheese?" The mouse said, "No, it's mine!" The red cat was sad and wanted the cheese. So, the cat tried to take it from the mouse. The mouse said, "No, no, no!" and started to run. The cat ran after the mouse. They ran and ran around the park. Then, something unexpected happened. A big dog came and saw the cheese. The dog took the cheese and ran away. The red cat and the little mouse stopped running. They looked at each other and said, "We need to get the cheese back!" So, they worked together to get the cheese from the big dog.

One day, a creative little girl named Lily went to the park. She saw a big leopard statue. She loved the statue so much, she wanted to play with it. Lily said to the statue, "Leopard, let's play freeze!" The leopard didn't move because it was just a statue. But Lily pretended it was her friend. Lily ran around the statue, laughing and having fun. She would shout, "Freeze, leopard!" and then stop moving. She played this game all day long until it was time to go home.

One sunny day, a fast car and a big truck had a race. The car was red and the truck was blue. They both wanted to see who was the fastest. They started at the big tree and raced to the little house. As they raced, the red car said, "You are so slow, blue truck! I will win this race!" The blue truck just smiled and kept going. The red car was being silly and started to make sparks with its wheels. The blue truck saw the sparks and knew it was not safe. In the end, the blue truck won the race. The red car was sad and asked, "How did you win?" The blue truck said, "I did not make sparks and I was not silly. I was careful and slow." The red car learned that being safe and not silly was more important than winning a race.

One day, a boy named Tim wanted to go out and play. He put on his shoes with the buckle. He saw that it was very dry outside. Tim went to the park and saw his friend, Sam. "Hi, Sam!" said Tim. "Do you want to play with me?" "Yes, Tim!" said Sam. "Let's play near the big tree." They played near the big tree for a long time. They had a lot of fun. But then, they saw dark clouds in the sky. Tim and Sam knew that rain was coming. They needed to find a place to hide. Tim remembered his shoes with the buckle. He thought that if he closed the buckle very tight, maybe something good would happen. So, Tim closed the buckle on his shoes as tight as he could. Suddenly, a big umbrella appeared over Tim and Sam! They were safe from the rain. The boys were very happy and surprised. They stayed dry under the umbrella, and they could keep playing even when it rained.
