Category: Beginner
Title: Sue the Girl and the Bear

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to serve tea to her friends. One sunny day, she had a tea party with her teddy bear, Mr. Cuddles. Sue said, "Mr. Cuddles, would you like some tea?" Mr. Cuddles nodded his head. Sue poured tea into his cup and gave it to him. They both enjoyed the tea and had a fun time. Sue's mom saw her playing and said, "Sue, you are very good at serving tea. You are successful at making your friends happy." Sue smiled and felt proud. The next day, Sue served tea to her friends at school. They all loved it and thanked her. Sue learned that when you share and serve others, everyone is happy. The moral of the story is: Serving others and sharing can make you and your friends happy.

One day, a little girl named Amy was playing in her room. She was scared because she heard a funny noise. The noise made her laugh, but she was still scared. She called her mom and said, "Mom, I hear a funny noise and I'm scared!" Her mom came to the room and listened. She laughed too and said, "Amy, that's just the toaster. It's making toast for us to eat." Amy felt better and went to the kitchen with her mom. When they got to the kitchen, they saw that the toast had jumped out of the toaster and was dancing on the table! Amy and her mom laughed and laughed. They never thought they would see dancing toast! The toast made them laugh so much that they were not scared anymore. They ate the toast and had a fun day together.

Once upon a time, there was a kind girl named True. True had a big room with many toys. One day, her room became very messy. It was so disgusting that she could not find her favorite toy. True did not like her room being dirty, so she decided to clean it. While True was cleaning, she heard a small voice say, "Can you help me?" True looked around and saw a little toy bear stuck under a big toy box. "Oh no!" she said, "I will help you, little bear." True moved the toy box and picked up the little bear. She gave him a big hug. True and the little bear kept cleaning the room together. They put all the toys back where they belonged. When they were done, True's room was very clean and pretty. The little bear said, "Thank you, True. You saved me!" True smiled and said, "You're welcome, little bear. Let's keep the room clean so we can always find each other." From that day on, True and the little bear were the best of friends and always kept the room clean.

Once upon a time, there was a fat pig. He lived in a farmyard with lots of other animals, and he was always very happy. One day, the pig heard a ringing noise. The noise was coming from a phone in the corner of the farm. The pig had never seen a phone before, so he went to investigate. When he got to the phone, he saw a man holding it. The man smiled, looked at the pig, and made a sign. The pig didn't understand what the sign meant. The man made the sign again, this time with a friendly smile. Suddenly, the pig understood: the sign was telling him to pick up the phone. He was so excited and grabbed the phone with his hoof. The pig said hello, and heard a voice from the other end of the line. After a few minutes of talking, the pig waved goodbye and put the phone back. From that day onwards, the fat pig always smiled whenever someone made the sign. He knew it was a way to tell him something special was about to happen!

Ben and Sam were brothers who liked to play in the park. One day, they saw a big snake on a tree. Ben was scared of the snake, but Sam was not. "Look, Ben, a snake!" Sam said. "Let's go and touch it!" "No, Sam, it's dangerous!" Ben said. "It can bite us and make us sick!" "Are you a baby, Ben?" Sam said. "I dare you to touch the snake. If you do, I will give you my expensive car toy." Ben loved the car toy. It was shiny and fast. He wanted to have it, but he was also afraid of the snake. He looked at Sam and the snake. He did not know what to do. "Come on, Ben, don't be a chicken!" Sam said. "It's just a snake. It won't hurt you." Ben decided to be brave. He walked slowly to the tree and reached out his hand to touch the snake. But the snake was not happy. It hissed and moved quickly. It bit Ben's hand and made him scream. "Ow, ow, ow!" Ben cried. "The snake bit me! It hurts!" Sam was shocked. He ran to Ben and saw his hand was red and swollen. He felt sorry for Ben and scared of the snake. He dropped the car toy and called for help. "Help, help, help!" Sam shouted. "My brother is hurt! The snake bit him!" But no one heard them. They were too far from the people and the cars. They were alone with the snake. The snake was angry and hungry. It slithered towards them and opened its mouth. Ben and Sam were in trouble. They wished they had never seen the snake. They wished they had never played the dare. They wished they had listened to Ben. But it was too late. The snake was faster than them. The snake ate them both. The end.
