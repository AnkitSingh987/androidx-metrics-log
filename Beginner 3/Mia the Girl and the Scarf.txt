Category: Beginner
Title: Mia the Girl and the Scarf

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Mia. She loved to wear her red scarf every day. Mia was eager to play outside with her friends. One day, Mia heard a ring. It was the ice cream truck! She ran to get her red scarf and her money. Mia put on her red scarf and went outside. She saw her friends waiting in line for ice cream. They were all very excited. Mia stood in line and waited for her turn. But then, something unexpected happened. A big wind blew her red scarf away! Mia was sad and ran after her scarf. She saw it fly high in the sky. Suddenly, the scarf landed on the ice cream truck. The ice cream man saw the scarf and picked it up. He gave it back to Mia with a big smile. Mia was so happy to have her scarf back. She got her ice cream and shared it with her friends. They all had a great day.

One day, a little boy named Tim went for a walk in the park. It was a cool day with a soft breeze. Tim saw many trees with pretty leaves. He liked the green leaves the most. Tim saw a big leaf on the ground. He picked it up and said, "I want to let this leaf fly in the wind!" So, he threw the leaf up in the air. The wind caught the leaf and it danced in the sky. Tim laughed and clapped as he watched the leaf fly. The leaf went up and down, round and round. It was fun to see. The cool wind blew on Tim's face, and he felt happy. The leaf landed back in Tim's hand, and he took it home to show his mom.

Once upon a time, there was a girl named Lucy. She loved to go outside and slide on the slippery slides. One day, Lucy wanted to go farther than she had ever been before. She saw a big hill and wanted to take a look. When she got close, she saw a hidden fire. Lucy was surprised but excited. She wanted to feel the heat of the fire. She carefully got closer to the fire. But the fire wasn't hot. It was cold! Lucy was disappointed, but then she noticed something. At the bottom of the hill, there was another hidden slide! Lucy couldn't believe it. She quickly ran to the slide and got ready. She grabbed the sides of the slide and went zooming down the hill. Lucy was so happy she screamed with joy! She felt the wind ruffle her hair and the sun on her face. What a great adventure she had on the hidden slide!

Lily and Ben were best friends. They liked to play with bricks. They made houses, towers, and cars with bricks. They had many bricks, but they always shared them. One day, a new boy came to their school. His name was Tom. He said he was famous. He said he had been on TV and had many toys. He said he was better than everyone else. Tom saw Lily and Ben playing with bricks. He wanted to play too. He asked them if he could join them. Lily and Ben said yes. They were nice and wanted to make a new friend. But Tom was not nice. He did not share the bricks. He took all the bricks he liked and made a big castle. He said it was his castle and no one else could touch it. He said he was the king and Lily and Ben were his servants. Lily and Ben did not like that. They felt sad and angry. They asked Tom to share the bricks and play nicely. But Tom said no. He said he did not need to share. He said he was famous and he could do whatever he wanted. Lily and Ben decided to stop playing with Tom. They went to another corner and made their own things with the bricks. They had fun and laughed together. Tom saw them and felt lonely. He realized that his castle was not fun without friends. He wished he had shared the bricks and played nicely. He wished he had not been so mean and proud. He went to Lily and Ben and said he was sorry. He asked them if he could play with them again. He said he would share the bricks and play nicely. He said he did not care about being famous. He just wanted to be their friend. Lily and Ben smiled and said yes. They forgave Tom and welcomed him. They shared the bricks and played together. They made a bigger and better castle for all of them. They said they were all kings and queens and friends. The moral of the story is: Sharing is caring. Being famous is not as important as being kind. Friendship is more fun than pride.
