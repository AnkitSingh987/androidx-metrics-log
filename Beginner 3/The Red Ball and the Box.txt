Category: Beginner
Title: The Red Ball and the Box

Once upon a time, there was a little red ball. The little red ball was in a box with a light cable. The little red ball wanted to escape from the box. One day, a friendly dog came to the box. The dog said, "I can help you escape!" The dog used its teeth to pull the light cable. The box opened and the little red ball was free. But something unexpected happened! The little red ball was not a ball at all. It was a red bird! The red bird said, "Thank you, dog!" The red bird flew away, happy to escape the box. The friendly dog and the red bird became best friends.

Anna and Ben were friends who liked to play at the park. They both loved the swing, but there was only one swing left. Anna wanted to go first, but Ben said no. He said he was older and stronger, and he could swing higher. Anna was angry, but she did not want to fight. She said, "OK, you can go first, but only for a little while. Then it is my turn." Ben smiled and ran to the swing. He pushed it hard and jumped on. He swung back and forth, feeling proud. He shouted, "Look at me, Anna! I am the best swinger in the park!" Anna waited patiently, but she was bored. She looked around and saw a slide, a seesaw, and a sandbox. She thought they looked fun, too. She said, "Ben, can I have a turn now? You have been on the swing for a long time." Ben did not listen. He swung faster and faster, trying to touch the sky. He said, "No, Anna, you have to wait. I am not done yet. This is my swing, and I can do what I want." Anna felt sad and hurt. She did not like Ben anymore. She said, "Fine, Ben, you can have the swing. I don't want to play with you. You are mean and selfish. I will find someone else to play with." Anna walked away from the swing. She saw a girl with curly hair and a pink dress. She was sitting on a bench, holding a book. She looked lonely and shy. Anna smiled and said, "Hi, I am Anna. What is your name?" The girl looked up and said, "I am Lily. I like your shirt. It has stars on it." Anna said, "Thank you. I like your book. It has animals on it. Do you want to play with me?" Lily nodded and said, "Yes, I do. What do you want to play?" Anna and Lily became friends. They played on the slide, the seesaw, and the sandbox. They had fun and laughed. They shared their toys and stories. They were kind and polite. They did not care about the swing anymore. Ben saw Anna and Lily playing. He felt lonely and bored. He was tired of the swing. He wanted to join them, but he was afraid. He had been rude and bossy. He thought they would not like him. He felt embarrassed and sorry. He said, "Anna, Lily, can I play with you? I am sorry for being mean. I will share the swing with you. I will be nice and friendly." Anna and Lily looked at Ben. They saw he was sincere. They said, "OK, Ben, you can play with us. But you have to be fair and kind. You have to take turns and say please and thank you. You have to be a good friend." Ben nodded and said, "I will, I promise. Thank you for forgiving me." He ran to them and hugged them. They smiled and hugged him back. They played together happily. The moral of the story is: Sharing is caring. Being selfish and rude will make you lose your friends. Being kind and polite will make you gain your friends. Friendship is more important than a swing.

Once upon a time, there was a big, mild bear. The bear had a little friend, a small bird. They lived in a cold place with lots of snow. One day, they saw an igloo. The bird wanted to go inside, but the bear did not. The bird insisted they go in. The bear said no, but the bird kept asking. The bear was scared, but the bird did not care. They went inside the igloo to see what was there. Inside, they found a big, angry wolf. The wolf did not like them in his home. The bear and the bird tried to run away, but the wolf caught the bird. The bear was very sad. The moral of the story is to listen to your friends and be careful where you go.
