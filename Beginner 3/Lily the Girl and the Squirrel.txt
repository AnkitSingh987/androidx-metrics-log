Category: Beginner
Title: Lily the Girl and the Squirrel

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She loved to gaze at the big yellow squash in her garden. One day, she saw a naughty squirrel trying to take her squash away. "Hey, naughty squirrel, that's my squash!" Lily said. The squirrel looked at her and then ran away. Lily was sad because she didn't want to be mean to the squirrel, but she also didn't want to lose her squash. The next day, Lily had an idea. She gave the squirrel some nuts and said, "Let's share. You can have these nuts, and I can keep my squash." The squirrel was happy, and they became good friends. From that day on, Lily and the squirrel played together in the garden. They both learned that sharing is a good thing, and it can make everyone happy.

Lily loves jewelry. She likes to look at the shiny things on her mom's neck and ears. She wants to wear them too. One day, when mom is busy, Lily sneaks into her room and finds her jewelry box. She opens it and sees many pretty things. She puts on a necklace, a bracelet, and some earrings. She feels like a princess. But the jewelry is not for Lily. It is for mom. And some of the jewelry is very old and very hot. Lily does not know that. She only knows that they sparkle and make her happy. She runs to the mirror to see how she looks. But as she runs, she feels something hot on her ear. It hurts. She cries out and drops the earring. It is a very old earring that mom got from grandma. It has a red stone that gets hot in the sun. Lily did not see the sun coming through the window. She only saw the red stone. Mom hears Lily cry and runs to her room. She sees Lily holding her ear and the earring on the floor. She is angry and scared. She picks up Lily and hugs her. She says, "Lily, what did you do? This is my jewelry, not yours. You could have hurt yourself. This earring is very hot. It is not for playing. It is for special days. You have to ask me before you touch my things. Do you understand?" Lily nods and says, "I'm sorry, mom. I love you. I love your jewelry. I wanted to be like you." Mom sighs and says, "I love you too, Lily. But you have to be careful. Jewelry is not a toy. It is a gift. And you are my best gift. You don't need jewelry to be beautiful. You are beautiful just the way you are." She kisses Lily's ear and takes off the other jewelry. She puts them back in the box and locks it. She says, "Maybe when you are older, I will let you wear some of my jewelry. But only if you promise to be good and listen to me. OK?" Lily says, "OK, mom. Thank you. Can I have a hug?" Mom says, "Of course, my sweet Lily. You can always have a hug." She hugs Lily and smiles. She thinks, "Maybe I should get her some jewelry of her own. Something simple and safe. Something that shows her how much I love her."

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was very competitive. He always wanted to win. One day, Tim and his friend Sam were playing a game. Tim was struggling to win, but he did not give up. "Let's race to the attic!" said Tim. Sam agreed, and they both ran as fast as they could. They climbed up the stairs and into the attic. The attic was full of old toys and dusty boxes. In the attic, Tim and Sam found a big box. It was heavy and hard to open. Tim struggled to open it, but he did not give up. "I can do it!" said Tim. And with a big push, the box opened. Inside, they found many fun toys to play with. Tim and Sam played together and had a great day.

Lily and Tom were playing in the yard. They saw a big hole in the fence. They wanted to see what was on the other side. "Let's go and look," Tom said. "No, we can't," Lily said. "Mom said we have to stay in the yard. She will be mad if we go." "Mom won't know," Tom said. "We will be back soon. Come on, it will be fun. Promise you won't tell." Lily was scared, but she wanted to go with Tom. She nodded and said, "OK, I promise." They crawled through the hole and ran to the woods. They saw many trees and flowers and bugs. They also saw a big pile of bones. They were white and hard and had holes in them. "Wow, look at these," Tom said. He picked up one of the bones. It was a skull. It had teeth and eyes and a nose. "This was a dog, I think." Lily felt sick. She did not like the bones. They smelled bad and looked scary. She wanted to go back. "Tom, let's go," she said. "This is not fun. This is gross." Tom did not listen. He liked the bones. He put the skull on his head and said, "Boo! I am a dog. Woof, woof!" Lily cried. She ran away from Tom and the bones. She ran back to the hole in the fence. She saw Mom coming to the yard. She was angry. "Lily, where were you?" Mom said. "I told you to stay in the yard. What were you doing?" Lily was sorry. She broke her promise. She told Mom everything. She told her about Tom and the hole and the woods and the bones and the skull. Mom hugged Lily and said, "I'm glad you told me the truth. That was very brave of you. But you should never go to the woods alone. It is dangerous. There are wild animals and sharp things and germs. You could get hurt or sick. Do you understand?" Lily nodded and said, "Yes, Mom. I understand. I'm sorry. I won't do it again." Mom smiled and said, "I forgive you. But you have to promise me one thing. Never go with Tom again. He is a bad friend. He does not care about you. He only cares about himself. He is sour." Lily agreed and said, "I promise, Mom. I don't like Tom anymore. He is sour. I like you. You are sweet."
