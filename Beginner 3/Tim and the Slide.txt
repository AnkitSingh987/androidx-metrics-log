Category: Beginner
Title: Tim and the Slide

One day, a boy named Tim went to the park. He saw a big slide and wanted to play on it. He ran to the slide, excited to go up and down. Tim saw a girl named Sue standing by the slide. Sue looked serious. She said, "I want to go on the slide, but I am scared." Tim thought for a moment, then had an idea. Tim said, "Let's go on the slide together!" He pointed to the slide and smiled. Sue smiled too, and they both climbed up. They went down the slide, laughing and having fun. From that day on, Tim and Sue were best friends.

Once upon a time, there was a big wall in a small town. The wall was old and not pretty. Some people in the town thought the wall was bad. They wanted to take it down. Other people in the town liked the wall. They wanted it to stay. One day, two friends named Tim and Pam went to look at the wall. Tim thought the wall was bad. He said, "We should take it down." Pam did not agree. She said, "No, I like the wall. It is old, but it is a part of our town." Tim and Pam disagreed about the wall. They decided to ask their other friends what they thought. Some friends agreed with Tim, and some friends agreed with Pam. In the end, they found a way to make everyone happy. They painted a big, pretty picture on the wall. Now, the wall was not bad anymore, and everyone in the town liked it.

One day, a little boy named Mark was playing in his room. He was troubled because his mom told him not to draw on the walls. But Mark really wanted to draw a big, fun picture. He tried to resist, but it was very hard. Mark found a big blue crayon and started to draw on the wall. He drew a big sun, a house, and some trees. He was very happy with his picture. But then, his mom came into the room and saw the wall. She was not happy. "Mark, I told you not to draw on the wall!" said his mom. Mark felt sad and said, "I'm sorry, Mom. I couldn't resist." His mom was still a little upset, but she knew Mark was just a little boy. She said, "Next time, let's draw on paper instead." Mark agreed, and they cleaned the wall together.

One day, a little boy named Tim went to visit his Aunt Sue. It was a cold day, so he wore his big, warm coat. He was very excited to see his aunt because she always had fun things to do. When Tim got to Aunt Sue's house, she said, "Hello, Tim! Let's play a game outside!" Tim was very happy and said, "Yes, please!" They went outside and saw some snow on the ground. Aunt Sue had a great idea. "Let's throw snowballs!" she said. Tim and Aunt Sue made snowballs with their hands. They laughed and threw them at each other. They played and played until they were both very cold. Then, they went inside to get warm. Tim loved spending time with his Aunt Sue, and they both had a great day.

Anna liked to play with her mom's jewels. They were shiny and pretty and made her feel like a princess. But mom said she could only look at them, not touch them. Mom said they were very special and very old and very expensive. Anna thought they were very boring. One day, when mom was not home, Anna decided to sneak into mom's room and play with the jewels. She opened the big box on the dresser and saw all the sparkly rings and necklaces and earrings. She put some on her fingers and ears and neck and looked at herself in the mirror. She smiled and twirled and pretended she was a fairy. But then she heard the door open. It was mom! She came back early from the store. Anna got scared and tried to take off the jewels and put them back in the box. But she was in a hurry and dropped some on the floor. She bent down to pick them up, but she slipped on the smooth carpet and fell down. She hit her head on the dresser and started to cry. Mom heard the noise and ran to the room. She saw Anna on the floor, with jewels all around her. She was angry and worried. She picked up Anna and hugged her and asked her if she was okay. She checked her head and saw a small bump. She said, "Anna, what did you do? Why did you touch my jewels? You know you are not supposed to. You could have hurt yourself or lost them or broken them. They are very important to me. They were my grandma's jewels. She gave them to me before she died. They are not toys. They are memories." Anna felt sorry and ashamed. She said, "I'm sorry, mom. I just wanted to play. They are so pretty. I didn't mean to hurt them or hurt myself. I love you, mom. And I love grandma, too. Can you forgive me, mom?" Mom sighed and kissed Anna. She said, "I love you, too, Anna. And I forgive you. But you have to promise me you will never touch my jewels again without my permission. Okay?" Anna nodded and said, "Okay, mom. I promise. Can I have a hug, mom?" Mom smiled and hugged Anna. She said, "Of course, you can have a hug, my sweet girl. You are my most precious jewel."
