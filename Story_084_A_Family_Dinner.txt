Title: A Family Dinner
Category: Beginner

Sunday evening arrived quickly. It was the family's last night at the grandparents' house. Tomorrow morning, they had to pack their bags and catch the train back to the city. Because it was a special evening, Grandma prepared a huge, traditional Sunday dinner. 

The large, round wooden table in the kitchen was set perfectly. There was a white tablecloth, shiny silver cutlery, and tall water glasses. In the center of the table, two tall white candles were burning, creating a soft, warm light in the room. The fire in the living room crackled gently in the background. 

"Dinner is ready!" Grandma called out cheerfully. "Everyone, please take a seat."
The family gathered around the table. They were all wearing nice, clean clothes after their busy day working in the muddy garden. They sat down in their chairs, feeling very hungry. The smell in the kitchen was incredible. 

Grandpa walked over to the oven. He put on thick red oven gloves and carefully pulled out a massive roasting pan. He carried it to the table and placed it on a wooden board in the middle. 
Inside the pan was a huge, golden-brown roast beef. Around the beef, there were roasted potatoes, bright orange carrots, and soft green peas. A small jug of thick, dark brown gravy stood next to the pan. 

"Wow," Thomas said, his eyes wide. "That looks amazing, Grandma."
"It smells delicious," Mrs. Miller added, smiling at her mother. 
"Thank you," Grandma said, looking pleased. "Well, don't just look at it. Let's eat!"

Grandpa stood up with a long, sharp knife and a large fork. He carved the roast beef carefully, cutting thick, juicy slices for everyone. He put two slices on Thomas's plate, then passed the plate to Grandma, who added a large spoonful of roasted potatoes and carrots. Finally, Mr. Miller poured the hot brown gravy over the meat and vegetables. 

They passed the plates around until everyone had a mountain of delicious food. 
For a few minutes, the kitchen was completely silent. The only sound was the clinking of knives and forks against the ceramic plates. The food was so good that nobody wanted to stop eating to talk. The beef was tender, the potatoes were crispy on the outside and fluffy inside, and the gravy was perfect. 

"This is the best roast dinner I have ever had," Mr. Miller declared, wiping his mouth with a napkin. "Your cooking is better than ever, Mom."
Grandma laughed. "You always say that when you are hungry. But I am glad you enjoy it."

As they ate, the conversation started to flow. They talked about the weekend and all the fun things they did. 
"I loved planting the tulip bulbs," Sophie said proudly. "Grandma says they will grow into big, beautiful flowers in the spring."
"Yes, she worked very hard," Grandma agreed. "She was a fantastic helper. You must all come back in April to see the flowers when they bloom."
"We will definitely come back," Mrs. Miller promised. 

"And Thomas and I went for a long walk in the forest this morning," Grandpa told the family. "Thomas has very sharp eyes. He spotted a red squirrel hiding in an oak tree. I completely missed it."
Thomas beamed with pride. "The forest was so quiet, Dad," he explained to his father. "We didn't hear any cars. We only heard a woodpecker tapping on a tree."
"That sounds wonderful," Mr. Miller said. "I wish we had forests like that near our apartment in the city."

They talked and laughed as they finished their main course. The candles burned down slowly, casting long shadows on the kitchen walls. It felt very cozy and safe. 

"Who has room for dessert?" Grandma asked, standing up to clear the empty plates. 
"I always have room for dessert," Thomas answered quickly. 
"Me too," Sophie added. 

Grandma brought out a large, round apple pie. It was warm from the oven, and the top crust was golden and sugary. She placed a large scoop of vanilla ice cream next to each slice of pie. 
The cold ice cream melted slowly over the hot, sweet apples. It was the perfect end to a perfect meal. 

When everyone was finally finished, they sat back in their chairs, rubbing their full stomachs. They felt relaxed and happy. 
"Thank you for a wonderful dinner, Grandma," Sophie said softly, feeling a little sleepy. 
"You are very welcome, my darling," Grandma smiled. 

Sitting around the table, sharing good food and stories, Thomas realized something important. The new house was beautiful, the forest was exciting, and the train ride was fun. But the best part of the weekend trip was simply being together as a family. The warm feeling of laughing around the kitchen table was better than any video game or comic book. It was a perfect family dinner.
