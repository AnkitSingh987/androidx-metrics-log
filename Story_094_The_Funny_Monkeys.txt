Title: The Funny Monkeys
Category: Beginner

The family left the dry, sunny savanna area and walked towards the green zone on Sam's map. As they walked, the environment changed. There were more tall trees, and the air felt slightly cooler and more humid. They were entering the jungle section of the zoo. 

Even before they saw the enclosure, they could hear the noise. It was a loud mixture of screeching, chattering, and high-pitched calls. 
"I hear them!" Lily shouted, running ahead. "The monkeys are definitely awake!"

They reached a very large enclosure surrounded by a high, strong net. Inside the net, it looked like a real jungle. There were real trees, thick green vines, thick ropes hanging from the ceiling, and several wooden platforms. 

The enclosure was full of life. There were more than twenty monkeys moving around quickly. These were spider monkeys. They had long, thin arms, long legs, and incredibly long tails. They were mostly black with some brown fur on their backs. 

The family stood at the fence and watched in absolute amazement. Unlike the sleepy lions, the monkeys never stopped moving for a single second. 

"Look at that one!" Sam pointed to the top of a tall tree. 
A monkey was hanging upside down. It was holding a branch using only its long tail. Its arms and legs were completely free. It was casually picking leaves and eating them while hanging upside down. 
"Its tail is like a fifth arm," Sam observed. 
"You are right," his father said. "Spider monkeys have prehensile tails. That means their tails can grip things tightly. It helps them swing safely through the high branches in the jungle."

On the other side of the enclosure, two younger monkeys were playing a game of chase. One monkey ran quickly across a wooden bridge. The other monkey chased him. They jumped from the bridge onto a thick rope. They swung wildly on the rope, screeching at each other. 
Suddenly, the first monkey let go of the rope and flew through the air. 

"Oh!" Lily gasped, covering her mouth. She thought the monkey was going to fall. 
But the monkey did not fall. It reached out its long arms and easily grabbed another rope three meters away. It swung gracefully to a wooden platform. The second monkey followed him immediately, doing exactly the same jump. 
"They are amazing gymnasts," Mrs. Miller smiled. "They make it look so easy."

Down on the ground, another funny scene was happening. A mother monkey was sitting on a rock. A tiny baby monkey was climbing all over her. The baby was very curious. It pulled its mother's ears. It tried to climb on her head. 
The mother monkey looked very patient. Finally, she picked the baby up with one hand and put it on her back. The baby wrapped its tiny arms and legs tightly around its mother's fur. Then, the mother quickly climbed a rope, carrying the baby on her back. 

"They are so funny," Lily laughed. "They act like naughty little children."
"They are very intelligent animals," her father told her. "They live in large family groups, and they spend a lot of time playing and grooming each other. Grooming helps them stay clean and build strong friendships."

Suddenly, a zoo keeper in a green shirt walked into the back of the enclosure. He was carrying a large bucket. 
The monkeys saw the bucket and went crazy. The screeching noise doubled in volume. All the monkeys stopped what they were doing and ran, jumped, or swung towards the keeper. 

The keeper opened the bucket and started throwing food onto the ground and onto the platforms. He threw pieces of bananas, apples, carrots, and special monkey biscuits. 
The monkeys grabbed the food quickly. One monkey grabbed a piece of banana in each hand, wrapped its tail around a branch, and grabbed a piece of apple with its foot. It didn't want to share with anyone else. 

The family stood and watched the monkeys eat their lunch for a long time. It was impossible to be bored while watching them. There was always something funny or amazing happening in every corner of the large net. 

"I think the monkeys are my favorite," Sam decided, putting his camera away. "They are much better than the sleeping lions."
"They are very entertaining," his mother agreed, checking her watch. "But speaking of lunch, it is one o'clock. Our own stomachs are starting to rumble. It is time for us to find a quiet bench and eat our picnic."
They said a final goodbye to the busy, noisy spider monkeys and walked down the green path, looking for a perfect place to eat their sandwiches.
