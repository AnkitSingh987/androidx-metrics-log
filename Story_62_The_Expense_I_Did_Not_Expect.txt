Title: The Expense I Did Not Expect

Category: Intermediate

Martin had always considered himself to be a responsible person. He was twenty-eight years old, he worked as a manager at a local logistics company, and he always paid his bills on time. He was very proud of the fact that he had no credit card debt. In fact, just last month, he had made the final payment on his car loan. When he saw his bank statement, he smiled. He finally had an extra three hundred dollars in his pocket every month.

"This is it," Martin thought as he drove home from work on a cold Tuesday evening. "I am finally going to start saving for a nice vacation. Maybe I will go to Italy next summer."

Martin lived in a small, older house in the suburbs. It was not a modern house, but he loved it because it was quiet and comfortable. When he parked his car in the driveway, he noticed that the air outside was freezing. Winter had arrived early this year, and the temperature had dropped below freezing point. He quickly unlocked his front door, expecting to feel the warm air of his house.

Instead, the house felt like a refrigerator.

Martin frowned and kept his thick winter coat on. He walked to the thermostat on the wall in the hallway. The screen showed that the temperature inside the house was only ten degrees Celsius. He pressed the button to increase the heat, hoping to hear the familiar sound of the furnace starting up in the basement. He waited for a minute, but there was only silence. He pressed the button again. Nothing happened.

"Oh no," Martin whispered. He grabbed a flashlight and went down the wooden stairs to the dark basement. The furnace was a large, grey metal box. It looked very old. Martin had bought the house three years ago, and the inspector had warned him that the heating system was near the end of its life. However, Martin had ignored the warning. There were always other things to buy, like furniture and a new television.

He pressed the reset button on the side of the furnace. It made a strange, grinding noise and then stopped completely. A faint smell of burning plastic filled the air. Martin knew instantly that this was not a simple problem he could fix himself.

He went back upstairs, his breath visible in the cold air. He immediately called an emergency heating repair company. Because it was late in the evening, the receptionist told him there would be an extra fee for the visit. Martin agreed because he had no other choice; he could not freeze in his own home.

An hour later, a repairman named Steve arrived. Steve went to the basement and inspected the furnace for about twenty minutes. When he came back upstairs, his face was serious.
"I have bad news, Martin," Steve said, taking off his work gloves. "The motor is completely burned out, and the heat exchanger is cracked. Because this model is over twenty years old, I cannot find replacement parts easily. To be honest, it is not worth repairing. You need a completely new heating system."

Martin felt a cold sweat on his forehead despite the freezing temperature in the room. "A new system? How much is that going to cost?"
Steve pulled out a tablet and typed a few numbers. "For a house this size, including the installation and the emergency service, it will be around four thousand, five hundred dollars."

Martin felt sick. Four thousand, five hundred dollars. He had just finished paying off his car, but he had not built any savings. Every time he had extra money, he spent it on dinners, gadgets, or clothes. His checking account had about six hundred dollars in it. He did not have a special savings account for emergencies. He had always assumed that if something bad happened, he would figure it out later. Later had just arrived, and he had no plan.

"I... I don't have that kind of money right now," Martin admitted, looking at the floor.
Steve nodded sympathetically. "I understand. It is a huge expense, especially right before the holidays. My company offers a financing plan. You can pay it off over two years, but the interest rate is quite high. Would you like me to leave you some information?"
"Yes, please," Martin said quietly.

Since the new system could not be installed until Thursday, Steve told Martin he would have to find a way to stay warm for two nights. After Steve left, Martin sat on his sofa wrapped in three heavy blankets. He felt incredibly foolish. Why had he bought a new television last year instead of saving money? Why did he go out for expensive dinners when he knew his house was old?

The next morning, Martin went to work feeling exhausted and stressed. During his lunch break, he talked to his colleague, Sarah.
"You look terrible, Martin," Sarah said gently as she sat across from him in the breakroom. "Did you not sleep?"
Martin explained the whole situation. He told her about the broken furnace, the massive repair bill, and the fact that he had zero savings to cover the cost. "I have to take their financing plan," he said sadly. "I will be paying high interest for the next two years. It feels like as soon as I got rid of my car loan, I stepped right into another trap."

Sarah listened patiently. "I am so sorry, Martin. That is a terrible situation. But listen, don't beat yourself up too much. A lot of people don't have an emergency fund. I didn't have one either until my dog got sick two years ago and the vet bill was two thousand dollars."
"What did you do?" Martin asked.
"I put it on a credit card and paid it off slowly," Sarah replied. "But after that, I learned my lesson. I started transferring fifty dollars every week into a separate bank account that I never touch. It is just for emergencies. If my car breaks down or my roof leaks, I know the money is there. It gives me so much peace of mind."

Martin realized that peace of mind was exactly what he was missing. He was always living on the edge, depending on his next paycheck.

"By the way," Sarah added, "I have a large portable space heater at my house. It works really well. I can bring it over tonight so you don't freeze before they install the new furnace."
"That would be amazing. Thank you so much, Sarah," Martin smiled for the first time in twenty-four hours.

That evening, Sarah brought the heater, and Martin was able to sleep in a warm bedroom. On Thursday, the repair team came and installed the new furnace. When the warm air finally blew through the vents, Martin felt a huge sense of relief. However, he also felt the weight of the new debt he had just signed up for.

That weekend, Martin sat down at his kitchen table with a calculator and his bank statements. He looked at his spending habits honestly. He cancelled two streaming subscriptions he rarely used. He decided to bring his lunch to work instead of buying sandwiches from the cafe. He calculated that he could save an extra two hundred dollars a month if he was careful.

He opened his laptop and created a new, separate bank account. He named it "Emergency Fund." He set up an automatic transfer of fifty dollars from his main account to the emergency account every single Friday. It was a small start, but he knew it would grow over time.

He also made a strict plan to pay off the furnace debt as quickly as possible, to avoid paying too much interest. The dream of a summer vacation in Italy was gone for now. He would have to wait another year or two.

As Martin listened to the quiet hum of his new furnace, he realized that this unexpected expense was a painful but necessary lesson. He had learned that life is unpredictable. Things break, accidents happen, and emergencies never announce themselves in advance. From now on, he promised himself, he would always be prepared. He would never allow an unexpected expense to catch him empty-handed again.
