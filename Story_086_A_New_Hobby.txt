Title: A New Hobby
Category: Beginner

It was the middle of the summer holidays. The days were long, hot, and sunny. Thirteen-year-old David sat on the front steps of his house. He was incredibly bored. He had played all his video games, he had read all his comic books, and his best friend, Mark, was away on a family vacation. David rested his chin on his hands and sighed deeply. 

His father walked out of the front door, holding a cup of coffee. He looked down at David. 
"You look very sad for a boy on summer vacation," his father said. "What is the matter?"
"I have nothing to do," David complained. "I am bored."
"You need a new hobby," his father suggested. "A hobby is a great way to spend your free time. It keeps your mind and your body busy."

David thought about this. A hobby sounded like a good idea. But what kind of hobby? 
"What should I do?" David asked. "I don't like painting. I don't want to collect stamps. Collecting stamps is boring."
"Well, you like being outside," his father said. "And you have a lot of energy. How about cycling? You used to love riding your small bicycle when you were younger."

David remembered his old, small blue bicycle. He rode it everywhere when he was eight years old. But he grew very fast, and the bicycle became too small for him. It was currently sitting in the back of the garage, covered in dust and spider webs. He had not ridden a bicycle in three years. 

"I don't have a bicycle that fits me," David pointed out. 
"That is true," his father agreed. "But we could look for a used bicycle online. It would be a project. You could buy an old one, clean it, fix it up, and then ride it. It would be a hobby and a project all at once."
David's eyes lit up. He liked the idea of a project. He liked the idea of fixing something and making it useful again. Riding a fast bicycle in the warm summer wind sounded much better than sitting on the front steps. 

"Okay," David said, standing up with sudden energy. "Let's do it. Let's find a bicycle."
His father smiled and opened the front door. "Come inside. We will look on the computer."

They sat at the desk in the home office. His father opened a website where people sold old things. He typed 'bicycle' into the search bar. Hundreds of pictures appeared on the screen. 
"We need to find one that is the right size for a teenager," his father explained. "Not too big, not too small."
They looked through the pages. Some bicycles were very expensive. Some were very cheap but looked completely destroyed. 

After twenty minutes, David saw a picture that caught his eye. It was a dark red mountain bike. It looked a little dirty, and the seat looked old, but the frame looked strong. 
"Look at this one," David pointed to the screen. 
His father clicked on the picture. He read the description. 
"The seller says it has been in a shed for five years. It needs some work, but the price is very good. It is only thirty dollars."
"Thirty dollars?" David said. He had fifty dollars saved from his birthday money. "I can buy it with my own money!"

"Are you sure?" his father asked. "It will be hard work to fix it."
"I am sure," David said confidently. "I want that red bicycle."
"Alright," his father said. He picked up his phone. "I will call the seller and ask if we can go and see it this afternoon."

David felt a rush of excitement. His boring summer day had completely changed. He ran upstairs to his bedroom to get his wallet. He counted his money carefully. He had exactly fifty-two dollars. He put thirty dollars in his pocket. 
He was going to buy his very own bicycle. He was going to have a new hobby. He imagined himself riding fast down the street, the wind in his hair. He could not wait for the afternoon to arrive.
