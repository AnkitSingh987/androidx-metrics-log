Title: Meeting the Puppy
Category: Beginner

The shelter worker opened the heavy metal door of the cage. The light brown dog with floppy ears bounded out instantly. He didn't try to run away; instead, he ran straight to Leo and Maya. 

"Follow me to the meet-and-greet room," the worker said, attaching the green leash to the dog's collar. "It is much quieter in there."
She led the family into a small, square room at the end of the hallway. The room had a comfortable sofa, a few rubber toys on the floor, and a large window. It was much quieter than the main kennel area. 

When the worker took the leash off, the dog immediately began to explore. He sniffed the corners of the room, sniffed the rubber toys, and then ran back to the family. 
Leo sat down on the floor and crossed his legs. The dog walked right up to him, sniffed his shoes, and then placed his front paws on Leo's knees. 

"Hello there," Leo said softly, reaching out his hand. The dog sniffed his hand and then licked it with a warm, wet tongue. Leo smiled broadly and began to gently stroke the dog's soft brown fur behind his ears. The dog closed his eyes and leaned into Leo's hand. 

"He really likes you, Leo," Maya said, kneeling down next to her brother. She picked up a red rubber ball from the floor. "Look here, boy!"
Maya tossed the ball across the small room. The dog opened his eyes, watched the ball bounce, and ran after it. He picked the ball up in his mouth, but he didn't bring it back. He simply lay down on the floor and started to chew it happily. 

"He needs to learn how to play fetch properly," their father observed, sitting on the sofa next to their mother. "But he seems very calm and good-natured. He isn't jumping aggressively."
"He is a mix of different breeds," the shelter worker explained. "We think he might have some Labrador in him, which is why he is so friendly. But because he was a street dog, he needs some basic obedience training."

"We can train him," Maya promised, watching the dog chew the red ball. "We can teach him to sit and stay."

The dog suddenly dropped the ball, stood up, and trotted over to the sofa. He sniffed Mr. and Mrs. Clark's shoes and gave a small, quiet 'woof'. Then, he walked back to Leo, circled twice on the floor, and lay down right next to Leo's leg, resting his head on his paws. 

Leo looked up at his parents. His eyes were shining. 
"Can we keep him, please?" Leo asked. "I think he is perfect for us."
"He is very sweet," their mother agreed, looking at the dog resting peacefully. "What do you think, honey?" she asked her husband. 
Mr. Clark looked at the dog, and then at his two hopeful children. 
"I think Barnaby has made his decision," his father smiled. "He seems to have chosen us. Yes, we can adopt him."

Leo and Maya cheered, but they tried to keep their voices quiet so they didn't scare the dog. Barnaby simply lifted his head, wagged his tail twice, and went back to resting. 

"That is wonderful," the shelter worker said brightly. "I am so happy for Barnaby. I will go and prepare the adoption paperwork. It will take about twenty minutes to fill in all the forms."
"While you do that," their father said, standing up. "We need to go to the pet supply store next door. We can't take a dog home without food, a bed, and a strong leash."

The family was thrilled. They had walked into the noisy shelter hoping to find a dog, and they had found the perfect one. Barnaby was going to be the newest member of their family, but first, they needed to buy everything a new dog could possibly need.
