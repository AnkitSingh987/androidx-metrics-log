Title: Packing up Camp
Category: Beginner

Ben woke up feeling very stiff. The foam mat was better than sleeping directly on the hard ground, but it was definitely not as comfortable as his real bed at home. He unzipped his warm blue sleeping bag and felt a sudden blast of freezing morning air. 
He shivered, quickly pulled on his jeans and his thick gray hoodie, and unzipped the front door of the green tent. 

The campsite was quiet and covered in a heavy morning mist. The sun was just beginning to rise above the tall pine trees, casting long, pale shadows across the wet grass. 
His father was already awake. He was standing by the cold, grey ashes of last night's campfire, drinking a cup of hot coffee from a metal mug. 

"Good morning," his father said cheerfully. "Did you sleep well?"
"I slept okay," Ben replied, rubbing his cold arms. "But I am very cold, and my legs are still sore from climbing the mountain yesterday."
"A hot breakfast will fix that," his father smiled. He had already set up a small, portable gas stove. He was cooking eggs and bacon in a frying pan. 

They ate their breakfast quickly in the cold air. The hot food made Ben feel much better. 
"Well, today is Sunday," his father announced, putting his empty metal mug down. "Our weekend adventure is over. We have to go back to the city. It is time to pack up the camp."
Ben felt a little sad. He loved the fresh air and the quiet forest, but he also missed his comfortable bed and a hot shower. 

"Packing up is much faster than setting up, but it is less fun," his father said. "First, we need to clean the tent."
They took their sleeping bags and backpacks out of the green tent. They used a small brush to sweep all the dirt and dry grass out of the tent door. 
"Never pack a dirty tent," his father advised. "If you pack it away wet or dirty, it will smell terrible the next time you use it."

Next, Ben pulled the metal pegs out of the ground. His hands were cold, and the metal was freezing. He had to pull very hard to get them out of the dark earth. 
With the pegs removed, they carefully slid the long, flexible metal poles out of the fabric sleeves. Without the poles, the tent collapsed immediately, falling flat onto the grass like a deflated green balloon. 

"Now for the hardest part," his father grunted. "Folding it so it actually fits back into that tiny bag."
They worked together to fold the large green fabric in half, and then in half again. They rolled it very tightly, squeezing all the trapped air out. After a bit of a struggle, they managed to push the rolled tent and the metal poles back into the original bag and zip it shut. 

"Okay, the house is packed," his father said, putting the heavy bag into the trunk of the car. "Now, we have one final, very important job to do."
He handed Ben a large black plastic garbage bag. 
"We must leave no trace," his father said seriously. "That is the golden rule of camping. We must leave this beautiful place looking exactly as we found it. We cannot leave any trash behind."

Ben understood. He walked around the flat patch of grass. He picked up an empty plastic wrapper, a small piece of string, and a tissue that had blown near a tree. He put them all into the black bag. His father poured a bucket of river water over the cold ashes of the fire ring to make absolutely sure it was completely out. 

They stood together and looked at the empty patch of grass. There was no sign that they had lived there for the weekend. The forest was perfectly clean. 
"Good job," his father said, locking the trunk of the car. "We did it."

They climbed into the car and turned on the heater. The warm air felt fantastic on Ben's cold hands. 
As they drove down the winding road, away from Pine Mountain, Ben looked back at the tall trees. He was tired, dirty, and his legs ached, but he was incredibly happy. He had climbed a steep mountain, he had built a house of fabric, and he had slept under a thousand silver stars. He knew he would remember this summer camping trip for the rest of his life.
