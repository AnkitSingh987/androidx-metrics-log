Title: The Deadline

Category: Intermediate

Clara loved her job as an event planner. She enjoyed organizing beautiful parties, coordinating decorations, and making sure guests had a wonderful time. She was known in her city for being calm under pressure. However, the first week of October tested her patience more than any other time in her career.

She was hired by the City Children's Hospital to organize their annual charity gala. It was a massive and important event. Two hundred wealthy donors bought expensive tickets to attend a luxurious dinner at the Grand Hotel ballroom. The money raised from the gala would be used to build a new pediatric wing for the hospital. The event was scheduled for Friday night.

Clara had been planning the gala for six months. She had booked a famous string quartet for the music, ordered hundreds of white roses for the tables, and hired 'Silver Spoon Catering', the best catering company in the city, to prepare a delicious three-course meal. Everything was perfectly organized.

On Wednesday morning, just forty-eight hours before the event, Clara was in her office reviewing the seating chart when her phone rang. It was David, the manager of Silver Spoon Catering.

"Clara, I am so sorry," David said. His voice was shaking. "We have a disaster."

Clara stopped writing. "What is wrong, David?"

"There was a fire in our main kitchen last night," he explained quickly. "Thankfully, no one was hurt, but the kitchen is completely destroyed. The health inspectors have closed our building. We cannot cook anything. We have to cancel all our orders for the next month, including your gala on Friday."

Clara felt the room spin. "Cancel? David, the gala is in two days! I have two hundred important guests expecting a gourmet dinner. You cannot cancel!"

"I am truly sorry, Clara," David said sadly. "I will refund your deposit immediately, but there is physically nothing I can do."

The line went dead. Clara stared at her phone. Her mind went completely blank for a moment. A charity gala without food was not a gala; it was a disaster. The hospital would lose thousands of dollars in donations, and her reputation as an event planner would be ruined.

She looked at the calendar on her wall. Wednesday, 10:00 AM. The guests would arrive on Friday at 7:00 PM. She had exactly fifty-seven hours to find a caterer who could prepare a high-quality meal for two hundred people. It was a nearly impossible deadline.

Clara took a deep breath and grabbed a notebook. She could not panic. She had to act.

She opened her computer and created a list of every catering company, large restaurant, and hotel kitchen within a fifty-mile radius. She started calling them one by one.

"Hello, this is Clara from Clara's Events. I have an emergency situation and need catering for two hundred people this Friday evening."

The answers were always the same.

"I am sorry, we are fully booked for a wedding."

"Two days? That is impossible. We need at least two weeks' notice to order ingredients."

"We do not have enough staff available on such short notice."

By 3:00 PM, Clara had called twenty-five places. Every single one had said no. She was starting to lose hope. She imagined having to call the hospital director and tell him they had to serve pizza at the most important charity event of the year.

She looked at her list. There was one place left: 'Bella Napoli', a very popular, high-end Italian restaurant downtown. It wasn't a traditional catering company, but they had a large kitchen. She dialed the number.

The owner, Chef Mario, answered the phone. He had a loud, energetic voice. Clara explained her desperate situation.

Chef Mario was silent for a moment. "Two hundred people? This Friday?" he asked. "That is crazy. That is a crazy deadline."

"I know it is crazy, Mario," Clara pleaded. "But it is for the Children's Hospital. If we don't have food, the charity loses money for the kids. I have the budget, and I will pay for any extra overtime your staff needs."

Chef Mario sighed loudly. "You are very lucky, Clara," he said. "A corporate group just canceled their huge reservation for Friday night because of a travel issue. I have a kitchen full of fresh ingredients and a staff that is ready to work. If you are willing to change the menu to Italian cuisine, we can do it."

Clara almost cried with relief. "Italian is perfect! What can you make?"

For the next hour, Clara and Chef Mario worked at lightning speed. They planned a menu over the phone: a fresh Caprese salad for the starter, chicken piccata with roasted vegetables for the main course, and a classic tiramisu for dessert. It was simple, elegant, and could be prepared quickly in large quantities.

"I will send you the contract in ten minutes," Mario said. "But you have to arrange the plates, silverware, and serving staff. My waiters are busy at the restaurant."

"I will handle it," Clara promised.

The deadline was still incredibly tight. Clara spent all of Wednesday evening calling rental companies to secure two hundred sets of nice plates and forks. On Thursday, she contacted a local staffing agency and hired twelve experienced waiters for the night. She didn't sleep more than three hours.

On Friday afternoon, Clara arrived at the Grand Hotel ballroom. Her eyes were tired, but her focus was sharp. At 4:00 PM, Chef Mario's delivery vans arrived, bringing heated containers full of amazing-smelling food. The rented plates arrived soon after. Clara directed the temporary waiters, showing them the layout of the room and explaining the service schedule.

At 7:00 PM, the doors opened. The guests walked in, wearing beautiful dresses and dark suits. They admired the white roses and listened to the beautiful string music.

At 8:00 PM, the dinner was served. Clara stood in the back near the kitchen doors, holding her breath. She watched as the waiters carried the plates of chicken piccata to the tables.

She watched a man take his first bite. He smiled and nodded to the woman next to him. Soon, the room was filled with the sound of clinking forks and happy conversation. The food was a massive hit. Many guests said it was the best meal they had ever eaten at a charity gala.

At the end of the night, the hospital director gave a speech and announced that they had raised record-breaking donations for the new children's wing.

After the last guest left, Clara sat down on a chair in the empty ballroom. She kicked off her high heels and rubbed her tired feet. It had been the most stressful forty-eight hours of her life. She had faced an impossible deadline and a complete disaster, but she had refused to give up. As she looked around the beautiful, quiet room, she smiled. She knew she was exactly in the right profession.
