Title: Adopting a Pet
Category: Beginner

For many months, ten-year-old Leo and his eight-year-old sister, Maya, had been asking their parents for a pet. They did not want a goldfish, and they did not want a hamster. They wanted a dog. They promised they would take care of it, feed it, and play with it every day. 

Their parents always said the same thing: "A dog is a huge responsibility. It is not a toy. It is a living creature that needs a lot of time and attention."

However, on a quiet Sunday afternoon, their father called a family meeting in the living room. Leo and Maya sat on the sofa, looking at their parents expectantly. 

"Your mother and I have been talking a lot recently," their father began, folding his hands on his lap. "We know how much you both want a dog. And we have decided that you are finally old enough to handle the responsibility."
Leo and Maya gasped. "Really?" Leo asked, his eyes wide with hope. "Are we getting a dog?"
"We are," his mother smiled warmly. "But there are some very strict rules."

"We will follow all the rules!" Maya promised quickly, bouncing up and down on the sofa cushions. 
"Let's go over them first," her father said, holding up one finger. "Rule number one: We are not buying a puppy from a pet store. We are going to adopt a dog from the local animal shelter. There are many wonderful dogs there who need a loving home. It is the right thing to do."
"I completely agree," Leo said seriously. "I want to rescue a dog."

"Rule number two," his mother continued. "A dog needs exercise every single day. That means someone has to walk the dog in the morning before school, and someone has to walk it in the evening, even if it is raining or cold outside. Are you prepared for that?"
Leo and Maya looked at each other. Waking up early to walk a dog in the rain sounded hard, but they nodded firmly. 
"We will take turns," Leo suggested. "I will do the mornings, and Maya can do the evenings with Dad."

"Good plan," his father approved. "And rule number three: Feeding and cleaning. The dog must have fresh water every day, and it must be fed on a strict schedule. If it makes a mess in the house, you have to clean it up. It is not Mom's job, and it is not my job. It is your job."

"We understand the rules," Maya said sincerely. "We will be very responsible. We just really want a dog to love."
Her parents looked at each other and smiled. They could see that the children were taking the commitment seriously. 

"Okay, then," their father said, standing up from his chair. "The decision is made. Put on your shoes and grab your jackets. The animal shelter closes at four o'clock, so we need to leave right now."

The children cheered loudly. They ran to the hallway and put their shoes on faster than they ever had before. 
As they drove towards the shelter, Leo felt a mixture of excitement and nervousness in his stomach. He was happy they were finally getting a dog, but he also realized that their lives were about to change completely. A dog was a big responsibility, but as he looked at his sister's happy face, he knew it was going to be a wonderful adventure.
