Title: A Difficult Conversation

Category: Intermediate

David sat at his desk, staring at the closed wooden door of his office. He had been dreading this moment for the entire week. On his desk was a single piece of paper—the official decision from the upper management team regarding the new regional director position. The problem was not the piece of paper itself, but the name that was written on it. Or rather, the name that was missing.

Sarah was his oldest colleague at the company. When David had first joined the marketing firm five years ago, Sarah was the one who had shown him around. She had introduced him to the team, taught him how to use the complicated software, and even invited him to have lunch with her on his first day when he didn't know anyone. Over the years, they had successfully completed dozens of projects together. They had celebrated victories with coffee and cake, and they had supported each other through stressful deadlines. When David was promoted to department manager last year, Sarah had been genuinely happy for him. She had even organized a small surprise party in the break room.

Now, Sarah had applied for the regional director position. She had worked incredibly hard over the last six months, taking on extra responsibilities and staying late to finish reports. She had told David confidentially that she really needed this promotion. Her daughter was starting university next year, and the extra salary would make a huge difference for her family. David knew how much this meant to her. 

However, the directors had made their decision. They had chosen a candidate from outside the company, a man named Robert who had ten years of experience managing international teams. While Sarah was excellent at her current job, the directors felt she didn't have enough experience with international markets. As her direct manager, it was David's responsibility to deliver the bad news. 

He looked at the clock on the wall. It was almost two o'clock. Sarah was scheduled to come to his office in five minutes. David took a deep breath and tried to organize his thoughts. He knew that this was going to be a difficult conversation. If he handled it badly, he might lose a valuable employee and a good friend. He wanted to be professional, but he also wanted to show empathy.

At exactly two o'clock, there was a soft knock on the door. "Come in," David called out, trying to make his voice sound normal. 

Sarah opened the door and smiled brightly. She was carrying a notebook and her favorite green coffee mug. "Hi David, you wanted to see me? Is it about the new advertising campaign for the Tech Solutions client? I have some ideas I wanted to share with you."

"Hi Sarah. Please, sit down," David said, pointing to the chair opposite his desk. "Actually, it's not about the Tech Solutions campaign. It's about the regional director position."

Sarah's smile immediately disappeared, and she sat up straighter in her chair. She looked at David with a mixture of hope and anxiety. "Oh. Have they made a decision already? I thought they were going to wait until next week."

David folded his hands on the desk. "They decided yesterday afternoon. Sarah, I wanted to tell you this myself before the official company email goes out. The board of directors has decided to hire an external candidate for the position."

The silence in the room was heavy. Sarah stared at David for a long moment, as if she was waiting for him to say it was a joke. When he didn't, she slowly placed her coffee mug on the desk. "I see," she said quietly. Her voice was shaking slightly. "Can I ask who it is?"

"His name is Robert Vance. He is coming from a larger agency in London," David explained gently. 

"I don't understand," Sarah said, shaking her head. "I have been with this company for seven years. I know our clients, I know our products, and I know our team. I have increased our department's sales by twenty percent this year. What does this Robert person have that I don't?"

"Sarah, please believe me when I say that your hard work is incredibly appreciated," David said, leaning forward. "The directors recognized your excellent sales record. However, the new position requires a lot of experience with the Asian and European markets. Robert has been managing international accounts for a decade. The board felt that his specific background was exactly what the company needed for this expansion phase."

Sarah looked away, staring out the window at the gray sky. "So, all my extra hours... all the weekends I worked... it wasn't enough."

"It's not that it wasn't enough," David corrected her softly. "It's just that the requirements for this specific role changed during the process. If they had been looking for a national director, you would have been the obvious choice. But the focus shifted to international expansion."

"It feels unfair," Sarah admitted, her voice tight with emotion. "I feel like I've been giving everything to this job, and it doesn't matter. I really thought I had a strong chance. I even told my husband I was probably getting it."

David felt a pain in his chest. He hated seeing her so disappointed. "I know it feels unfair, Sarah. And I completely understand why you are upset. You have every right to be angry and disappointed. If I were in your position, I would feel exactly the same way."

Sarah took a deep breath and looked back at David. Her eyes were slightly red, but she maintained her professional attitude. "Did you agree with their decision, David? Did you fight for me?"

This was the question David had feared the most. He had to be honest. "I told the board that you are the most dedicated employee we have. I presented a detailed report of your achievements. I strongly recommended you for the role. But when they showed me Robert's portfolio and his connections in the Asian market, I had to agree that from a purely strategic business perspective, he brings a very specific set of skills that we currently lack. It wasn't a choice against you, Sarah. It was a choice for a specific market strategy."

Sarah was quiet for a long time. She looked at her notebook, tracing the edge of the cover with her finger. David didn't interrupt her. He knew she needed time to process the information. In difficult conversations, silence was sometimes more powerful than words. 

Finally, she nodded slowly. "I appreciate your honesty, David. It hurts, I won't lie. But I prefer to know the real reasons rather than hearing empty corporate excuses."

"What can we do to support you moving forward?" David asked. "I know this is a setback, but I don't want you to lose your motivation. You are essential to this team."

"Honestly? Right now, I just need to process this," Sarah replied, standing up and picking up her mug. "I'm going to take the rest of the afternoon to work from home, if that's alright with you. I need some space."

"Of course," David said immediately. "Take all the time you need. And Sarah? When you're ready, I'd like to discuss creating a professional development plan for you. If international experience is what you need for the next big step, we will find a way to get you that experience. Maybe we can put you in charge of coordinating with Robert's new international teams."

Sarah offered a small, sad smile. "Thank you, David. I'll think about it. I'll see you on Monday."

As she walked out and closed the door behind her, David let out a long breath. The conversation had been just as difficult as he had expected. It was never easy to disappoint someone you respected. However, he felt relieved that he had been honest and direct. Trust was the most important thing in a working relationship, and avoiding the truth would have destroyed that trust forever. He knew Sarah would need time to recover, but he also knew she was strong and professional. They had survived difficult times before, and they would survive this one too.
