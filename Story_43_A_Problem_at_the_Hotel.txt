Title: A Problem at the Hotel

Category: Intermediate

David dragged his heavy suitcase through the revolving doors of the grand hotel. He had been traveling for almost fourteen hours, flying from New York to Tokyo for a very important business conference. His suit was wrinkled, his back was aching, and his eyes felt like they were filled with sand. All he wanted to do was take a hot shower, order some simple room service, and sleep for ten hours.
The hotel lobby was magnificent. It had marble floors, enormous crystal chandeliers hanging from the ceiling, and beautiful flower arrangements on every table. Soft classical music played in the background. David walked to the long reception desk, trying to look professional despite his exhaustion.
A young woman wearing a sharp black uniform smiled warmly at him. She had a gold name tag that read 'Sarah'.
"Good evening, sir. Welcome to the Grand Horizon Hotel. How may I help you tonight?" she asked politely in perfect English.
"Good evening," David replied, handing her his passport and credit card. "I have a reservation under the name David Miller. It should be for a single room for five nights."
Sarah typed quickly on her computer keyboard. "Yes, Mr. Miller. I see your reservation right here. A standard room with a city view. Please give me one moment to prepare your key cards."
Within minutes, the check-in process was complete. Sarah handed him a small paper folder containing two electronic keys. "You are in room 412, on the fourth floor. The elevators are just past the restaurant on your left. Breakfast is served from 6:30 AM to 10:00 AM in the main dining hall. We hope you enjoy your stay."
"Thank you very much," David said, taking his keys.
He took the elevator to the fourth floor and walked down the quiet, carpeted hallway. He found room 412, tapped his key against the lock, and pushed the door open. The room was spacious and clean, with a large, comfortable-looking bed and a desk by the window. David let out a sigh of relief, dropped his suitcase on the floor, and immediately went into the bathroom to start the shower.
He turned the water handle all the way to the red side, waiting for the water to get hot. He went back into the bedroom, took off his jacket and tie, and unpacked a few clean clothes. After five minutes, he returned to the bathroom. He put his hand under the water. It was freezing cold.
David frowned. He turned the handle back and forth, trying to find a warmer setting, but the water remained icy. He waited another five minutes, but nothing changed. There was absolutely no hot water.
Sighing heavily, David walked to the phone on the bedside table and dialed '0' for the reception desk.
"Reception, this is Sarah speaking. How can I help you?"
"Hello, Sarah. This is David Miller in room 412. I just checked in a few minutes ago. I'm afraid there is a problem with my bathroom. There is no hot water at all."
"Oh, I am very sorry to hear that, Mr. Miller," Sarah said, sounding genuinely concerned. "Let me send someone from our maintenance team up to your room immediately to look at it. It should only take a few minutes."
"Thank you. I appreciate it," David said, hanging up the phone.
Ten minutes later, there was a polite knock on the door. David opened it to find a man in a gray uniform carrying a small tool bag.
"Good evening, sir. I am here to check the plumbing," the man said politely.
He went into the bathroom and spent several minutes examining the pipes under the sink and behind the shower wall. He tapped a few things with a wrench and listened closely. Finally, he came out shaking his head.
"I apologize, sir," the maintenance worker said. "The heating pipe connected to this specific room has a broken valve. I cannot fix it tonight because we need to order a replacement part. It won't be repaired until tomorrow afternoon."
David felt a surge of frustration. He was incredibly tired, and the thought of waiting until tomorrow afternoon for a warm shower was unbearable. He needed to look presentable and feel awake for his meetings in the morning.
"I see. Thank you for trying," David told the man.
After the worker left, David called the reception desk again. "Hello, Sarah. It's David Miller again from room 412. The maintenance worker just left. He informed me that the hot water valve is broken and cannot be fixed tonight."
"I was just reading his report on the system," Sarah replied quickly. "I am terribly sorry for this inconvenience, Mr. Miller. This is definitely not the standard of service we want to provide our guests, especially after a long flight."
"I understand it is not your fault," David said, trying to keep his voice calm and polite. "However, I really need to take a shower tonight. Is there any possibility that I could move to a different room?"
"Please hold the line for one moment while I check our availability," Sarah requested.
David waited, listening to the soft hold music on the phone. He looked out the window at the bright neon lights of the Tokyo skyline, hoping they had another room. If the hotel was fully booked, he didn't know what he would do.
After a minute, Sarah returned to the line. "Thank you for your patience, Mr. Miller. Unfortunately, because of a large medical convention in the city, all of our standard rooms are completely booked tonight."
David closed his eyes and rubbed his forehead. "So, there is nothing you can do?"
"I didn't say that, sir," Sarah said, and David could hear the smile in her voice. "Since we cannot provide the service you paid for in your current room, my manager has authorized a complimentary upgrade. We are moving you to one of our Executive Suites on the top floor, at no extra charge for the entire duration of your stay."
David was surprised. "An Executive Suite? Truly?"
"Yes, sir. It has a separate living room, a beautiful view of the harbor, and, most importantly, a fully functioning bathroom with a luxury bathtub and unlimited hot water. I will send a bellboy up to room 412 right now with your new keys to help you move your luggage."
"Sarah, that is incredibly generous. Thank you very much. You have saved my evening."
"It is our pleasure, Mr. Miller. We want you to be comfortable. Please accept our deepest apologies for the trouble."
Shortly after, a friendly bellboy arrived and helped David move his suitcase up to the twentieth floor. When David opened the door to his new suite, he was amazed. It was twice the size of his original room, with elegant modern furniture, a massive television, and huge windows overlooking the glowing city and the dark ocean beyond.
He walked straight into the luxurious marble bathroom and turned on the shower. Within seconds, thick, warm steam filled the air. David smiled. Sometimes, facing a problem calmly and politely was the best way to travel. He finally stepped into the hot water, feeling the stress of his journey washing away, ready for a perfect night of sleep.
