Title: Cleaning the Kitchen
Category: Beginner

Dinner is finished. Jack and his father ate all the pasta and all the salad. They are very full and very happy. 
But now, they look around the kitchen. 
There is a big metal pot on the stove. There is a small pot next to it. The wooden cutting board is on the counter. The glass salad bowl is empty on the table. There are dirty plates, forks, and glasses everywhere. 

"Oh dear," Jack's father says. He rubs his full stomach. "Cooking is fun, and eating is great. But cleaning the kitchen is hard work."
"I am very tired," Jack says. "Can we clean it tomorrow morning?"
His father shakes his head. "No, we cannot leave dirty dishes overnight. It is a bad habit. If we clean it now, we will wake up to a nice, clean kitchen tomorrow."

Jack sighs. He knows his father is right, but he really wants to go and watch television. 
"If we work together as a team, it will be very fast," his father says cheerfully. "I will wash the dishes, and you can dry them. How does that sound?"
"Okay," Jack agrees. He stands up from the table. "I will be the dryer."

His father goes to the kitchen sink. He turns on the hot water. He pours some green dish soap into the sink. The water fills up with many white bubbles. 
"Bring me the dirty plates, Jack," his father says. 

Jack takes the two dirty dinner plates from the table. He carries them to the sink. 
His father takes a yellow sponge. He dips the plate into the hot, soapy water. He scrubs the plate with the sponge until all the red tomato sauce is gone. Then, he rinses the plate under clean water. 
He places the clean, wet plate on the drying rack next to the sink. 

Jack takes a clean blue towel from the drawer. He picks up the wet plate. He rubs the towel all over the plate until it is completely dry. 
"Where does this plate go?" Jack asks. 
"It goes in the cupboard above the toaster," his father answers. 
Jack opens the cupboard door and puts the dry plate inside. He goes back for the second plate and dries it too. 

They work in a steady rhythm. His father washes, and Jack dries. 
Wash, rinse, place on the rack. 
Pick up, dry with the towel, put in the cupboard. 
They do the forks. They do the glasses. They do the big glass salad bowl. 

"Now for the big pots," his father says. The metal pots are very dirty with sticky sauce. His father has to scrub them very hard with the yellow sponge. 
"These pots are heavy," Jack says as he dries them with his blue towel. 
"Yes, they are," his father agrees. "You can put them in the bottom cupboard."
Jack bends down and pushes the heavy pots into the bottom cupboard. 

The sink is finally empty. The dish rack is empty. All the dishes are clean and put away in the cupboards. 
"Are we finished?" Jack asks hopefully. He looks towards the living room where the television is. 
"Almost," his father says. "We just need to wipe the table and the counters. We do not want a sticky kitchen."

His father takes a wet cloth. He gives it to Jack. 
"You wipe the table, and I will wipe the counters near the stove," he says. 
Jack takes the wet cloth. He wipes the kitchen table carefully. He wipes away some breadcrumbs and a small drop of tomato sauce. The table looks shiny and clean. 
His father wipes the counters. He wipes around the sink. He washes his hands and turns off the light above the stove. 

"Okay," his father says. He looks around the kitchen. "Everything is clean. The pots are away, the table is wiped, and the sink is empty. It looks perfect."
Jack looks around too. The kitchen looks exactly as it did before they started cooking. 
He looks at the clock on the wall. 
"That only took fifteen minutes!" Jack says, surprised. 
"I told you," his father smiles. "When we work as a team, things go very fast. Many hands make light work."

Jack puts the wet cloth by the sink. He hangs his blue drying towel on a hook on the door. 
"Thank you for your help, Jack," his father says. "You are a great assistant chef, and a great dishwasher too."
"You are welcome," Jack says. "Can I go watch television now?"
"Yes, you can," his father says. "You earned it. Go and relax."

Jack runs into the living room. He turns on the television and sits on the comfortable sofa. He is glad they cleaned the kitchen. It is a good feeling to know the work is finished and the house is tidy. Tomorrow morning, when he comes down for breakfast, the clean kitchen will be ready for a new day.
