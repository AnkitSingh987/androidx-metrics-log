Title: A Weekend Trip
Category: Beginner

It was a cold Tuesday evening in November. The wind blew hard outside, making the windows rattle, but inside the house, it was warm and comfortable. The Miller family was sitting in the living room after dinner. Mr. Miller was reading the newspaper, Mrs. Miller was knitting a blue scarf, and their two children, twelve-year-old Thomas and ten-year-old Sophie, were doing their homework on the floor. 

Suddenly, Mr. Miller folded his newspaper and put it on the coffee table. He looked at his wife and smiled. 
"I was thinking," he said slowly, getting everyone's attention. "We have not been anywhere for a long time. The children have a school holiday next Monday, so it will be a long weekend. I think we should go on a weekend trip."

Thomas and Sophie stopped writing immediately. They looked up from their books with wide, excited eyes. 
"A trip?" Thomas asked. "Where can we go?"
"Can we go to the beach?" Sophie suggested eagerly. 
Mrs. Miller laughed softly. "It is November, Sophie. The beach will be freezing cold and very windy. We would not be able to swim or build sandcastles."
"Oh, that is true," Sophie said, feeling a little disappointed. 

"I have a better idea," Mr. Miller said. "How about we go and visit your grandparents in the countryside? They moved into their new house six months ago, and we still haven't seen it. Grandma called me yesterday, and she said she misses you both very much."

The children cheered loudly. Visiting their grandparents was always a wonderful adventure. Their grandparents lived in a small, quiet village surrounded by green hills and thick forests. It was completely different from the noisy, busy city where the Millers lived. 

"That is a fantastic idea, dear," Mrs. Miller agreed. "I haven't seen your mother since the summer. It will be lovely to catch up with them. How should we travel? Should we drive the car?"
Mr. Miller shook his head. "No, the car needs some repairs, and it is a very long drive. I think it would be much more relaxing if we took the train. The children love traveling by train, and we can all sit together and enjoy the scenery."

"Yes! The train!" Thomas shouted. He loved trains. He loved the noise they made, and he loved walking up and down the aisles. 
"Okay, it is decided then," Mr. Miller said, picking up his newspaper again. "We will leave on Saturday morning and come back on Monday afternoon. I will book the train tickets online tonight."

For the rest of the evening, nobody could concentrate on their normal activities. Thomas and Sophie tried to finish their homework, but they were too distracted. They kept talking about all the fun things they wanted to do at their grandparents' new house. 
"I hope Grandpa built a treehouse in his new garden," Thomas said. 
"I hope Grandma baked her special chocolate chip cookies," Sophie added, rubbing her stomach. 

"You both need to finish your schoolwork first," their mother reminded them gently. "Then, you can start thinking about what to pack for the trip. It will be much colder in the countryside than it is here in the city, so you will need warm clothes."

The idea of a weekend trip changed the whole mood in the house. The dark, cold November evening did not feel depressing anymore. It felt exciting. Thomas and Sophie finished their math problems as quickly as they could. They wanted Friday to arrive immediately. The week was going to feel very long, but knowing that a train ride and their loving grandparents were waiting at the end of it made them incredibly happy.
