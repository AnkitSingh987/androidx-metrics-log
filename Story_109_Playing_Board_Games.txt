Title: Playing Board Games
Category: Beginner

"The games cupboard?" Mark asked, raising his eyebrows. "We haven't opened that cupboard in years. The games are probably covered in dust."
"They might be dusty, but they still work," his father laughed. He stood up, taking a flashlight, and walked into the dining room where the large wooden cupboard stood. 

A few minutes later, he returned with a stack of square cardboard boxes. He blew a thin layer of dust off the top box. 
"I found Monopoly, Scrabble, and a simple deck of playing cards," Mr. Clark announced, placing the boxes on the coffee table next to the burning candles. "Which one should we play first?"

"Let's play Monopoly," Mark suggested eagerly. "I am very good at Monopoly. I always buy the best streets."
"We will see about that," his mother challenged him with a smile. "I am a very ruthless property buyer."

They cleared a space on the coffee table between the candles. Mark opened the Monopoly box. He unfolded the large, colorful square board and placed it flat on the table. He sorted the paper money into neat piles: hundreds, fifties, twenties, and tens. He handed the money out to his parents. 

"I choose the silver car," Mark said, picking his favorite playing piece. 
"I will be the top hat," his father said. 
"And I will be the little dog," his mother chose. 

The game began. In the soft, flickering candlelight, they rolled the two white dice. The dice made a satisfying clattering sound on the wooden table. 
Mark rolled a double six. "Twelve!" he announced proudly. He moved his silver car twelve spaces around the board. "I landed on Park Place. I will buy it!" he said, handing his paper money to the bank. 

For the next two hours, the living room was filled with laughter, groans of frustration, and playful arguments. 
Without the distraction of the television or their mobile phones, they completely focused on the game and on each other. They talked and joked more than they usually did on a normal Thursday evening. 

"You landed on my hotel, Dad!" Mark cheered loudly. "You owe me one thousand dollars!"
Mr. Clark looked at the board in dismay. "Are you sure? Oh dear, I am bankrupt. I have no more money. You win, Mark."
"I told you I was good at this game," Mark grinned, collecting the colorful paper money. 

"Okay, the property tycoon wins," his mother laughed. "Let's put Monopoly away. How about a quick game of cards before we go to sleep?"
Mr. Clark shuffled the deck of standard playing cards. The sharp, snappy sound of the cards being shuffled was pleasant in the quiet room. 
They played a simple game called 'Go Fish'. It did not require deep thinking, just a little bit of luck and a good memory. 

As they played, the winter storm continued to roar outside, but nobody was paying attention to it anymore. The wind was just background noise. The cold was kept away by the thick blankets and sweaters. The darkness was pushed back by the warm, golden glow of the candles. 

Mark looked at his parents across the table. His mother was smiling, trying to hide the fact that she had a very good hand of cards. His father was squinting at his cards in the dim light. 
Mark realized something surprising. He was actually having a wonderful time. He thought losing the electricity would be a disaster, but it had turned into a very special family evening. They were spending quality time together, talking and laughing, instead of staring silently at different screens. 

"Do you have any kings, Mark?" his mother asked, interrupting his thoughts. 
Mark looked at his hand. "No," he smiled. "Go fish, Mom."
He hoped the electricity wouldn't come back on too soon. He was enjoying the candlelight and the board games far too much.
