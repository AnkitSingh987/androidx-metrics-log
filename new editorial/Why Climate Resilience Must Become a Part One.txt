Title: Why Climate Resilience Must Become a Part One

Why Climate Resilience Must Become a Priority for MSMEs

Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) form the backbone of India's manufacturing supply chains, connecting local production systems to national and global markets. The resurgence of El Niño conditions coupled with record-breaking heatwaves, extreme rainfall, and recurring floods have underscored a new reality - climate change is not just an environmental challenge but also an economic risk. Across India, businesses are increasingly grappling with disruptions caused by rising temperatures, erratic rainfall, and extreme weather events.

While much attention is given to the impact of climate change on agriculture and infrastructure, a critical pillar of India's economy is often overlooked. Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) form the backbone of India's manufacturing supply chains, connecting local production systems to national and global markets. Currently, MSMEs contribute nearly 31 percent of India's Gross domestic Product and support over 32.8 crore people.

Yet, these enterprises remain among the most vulnerable to climate risks. What the Evidence Suggests A recent WRI India report, Resilience of Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises to Climate Risks, examines how extreme heat and flooding are affecting more than 300 MSMEs across Surat, Chennai and Coimbatore. The findings point to a growing reality of climate affecting productivity, supply chains, worker well-being and long-term competitiveness.

Floods and extreme rainfall are increasingly disrupting business operations, damaging inventory and equipment, and causing production delays. More than 90 percent of enterprises directly affected by flooding reported operational disruptions, while nearly three-fourths experienced temporary business closures. However, climate impacts are no longer limited to physical damages alone.

Even facilities untouched by floodwaters can suffer substantial losses when transport networks become impassable, power supply is interrupted, supply chains are disrupted, and workers are unable to reach their workplaces. These indirect impacts create ripple effects across entire industries, amplifying economic losses. Extreme heat presents an equally pressing challenge for 92% of the surveyed MSMEs.

Rising temperatures increase energy costs, reduce operational efficiency, and affects machinery and production processes. More importantly, heat is taking a toll on the workforce. With nearly four out of five MSMEs reporting reduced worker productivity. Enterprises also reported increased absenteeism, heat-related illnesses, and rise in workplace accidents.

In labour-intensive sectors such as textiles and automotive manufacturing, these impacts directly affect output, delivery timelines, product quality, and profitability. While MSMEs recognise that climate change is affecting them, few have the resources, information, or institutional support needed to prepare for future risks. Business continuity planning, climate risk assessments, insurance coverage, and access to resilience finance remain limited.

As a result, many enterprises respond to climate shocks only after they occur, often at significant economic cost. The result is a reinforcing cycle of vulnerability where climate shocks trigger business disruptions, financial losses constrain investments, and enterprises remain exposed to future risks. Why Climate Resilience is a Business Imperative For many MSMEs, climate resilience is still viewed as an environmental or compliance requirement.

This perception needs to change as the business landscape evolves. Large corporations are increasingly evaluating climate risks across supply chains. Investors and financial institutions are beginning to incorporate climate exposure into lending and investment decisions and regulatory expectations around sustainability and resilience are also growing.

Enterprises that fail to adapt may find themselves facing higher operational costs, greater business uncertainty, and reduced market opportunities. The challenge, therefore, is not simply recovering from climate shocks, but building the capacity to anticipate, absorb, and adapt to them. Moving from Coping to Preparedness Strengthening MSME resilience requires moving beyond short-term coping measures towards more systematic and anticipatory approaches.
