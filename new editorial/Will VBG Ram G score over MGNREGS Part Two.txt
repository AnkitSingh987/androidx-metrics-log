Title: Will VBG Ram G score over MGNREGS Part Two

Will VB-G Ram G score over MGNREGS?

Given the nature of operations allowed under the Act, the actual creation of significant durable assets would also be rather limited. Primary objective Coming to the new scheme, the primary objective of the Viksit Bharat-G RAM G Act 2025 is to align the rural development framework with the national vision of Viksit Bharat @2047. Provision of enhanced statutory wage employment guarantee of 125 days in a year is expected to enable effective participation in the expanded livelihood security framework.

Its convergence with Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans and the PM Gati Shakti would be powered by geospatial systems, digital public infrastructure, district and State planning mechanisms, etc. It is in this context that the new scheme comprises categories of works identified to form Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans aggregated into the Viksit Bharat National Rural Infrastructure Stack. The four priority issues proposed to be addressed topics are: water security through water-related works, core-rural infrastructure, livelihood-related infrastructure and special works to mitigate extreme weather events.

Besides these, it will include other public works notified by the Central Government, maintenance of rural public assets works for individual beneficiaries, including housing under the PM Awaas Yojana, sanitation-related works, etc. The expanded horizon of public works in the new scheme clearly opens up the scheme to be part of the new vision of a developed India, appearing in catchy phrases. Issues and questions The vastly expanded scope of the activities to be taken up under the new Act, is intended to create and maintain productive assets discarding a demand-driven open-ended right based entitlement, as under MNREGA.

Understandably, many of the new activities would need skilled or semi-skilled workers. Also their specification can no longer be decided at the local level. In the existing scheme, the percentage of registered skilled or semi-skilled workers is under 4 per cent of the total registered for employment in 2024-25.

Also, for most unskilled women, participation was a part of the survival strategy of the family. Bhattacharya (businessline, January 6) argues that "unlike MNREGA, it will be a planned and well-integrated supply driven scheme that will enhance the efficiency of allocation, covering strategic planning, engineering, design, supervision, etc". Within this framework, ensuring engagement of unskilled or low skilled labour at its bottom layer and keeping it within the purview of the local panchayats would be extremely hard.

Furthermore, it will be difficult to time schedule the activities to exclude agricultural seasons while enhancing the number of days of employment, which is not a high priority for the households, going by the past experience. Social audits of the MGNREGA scheme often bring out issues like payments to non-existent persons, diversion of funds using fake names on muster rolls, not finding concrete work on the ground, utilization of funds without any work, assets being of poor quality, use of machinery in place of labour, as mandated, to siphon off funds, besides works for private gain. Such issues plague almost all publicly funded schemes.

Going by reports, the amount involved in these do not appear to be very large, given the huge outlay of the scheme. Over the past half-decade, the government has taken the credit of incorporating digital governance reforms aimed at improving transparency, accountability, and efficiency. The new scheme in a way attempts to strengthen this process through real-time data capture and monitoring, digital planning and payment systems.
