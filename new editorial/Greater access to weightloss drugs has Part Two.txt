Discontinuation of these drugs, after achieving desired weight loss, has been widely reported to result in rebound weight gain and loss of cardio-metabolic health benefits.

This is especially true of persons who do not adhere to healthy diets or regular physical activity, believing that the drugs have permanently cast away body fat. Prolonged therapy may be needed, with high financial costs and adverse drug effects. Use of leptin, an appetite-suppressing hormone, has been suggested for long-term therapy after initial therapy with semaglutide.

As yet, that is uncharted territory. The levels of benefits and risks associated with these relatively recent drugs still need to be carefully assessed in our patient population. So, regulatory caution is fully justified. The use of these drugs should be guided by medical wisdom and regulatory prudence.
