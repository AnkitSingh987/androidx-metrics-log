Title: Technology as terror Part Two

Technology as terror

The restrictions may include the need for a licence to use them, a heavy penalty for their unlicensed use, and even a ban on them if needed. Any depreciation in value, which companies use to reduce the tax burden, should not be allowed for them. Trial-and-error recordings in the development stage of products, access to patents, etc., are critically important data that should not fall into the hands of adversaries.

The national government should be alert to attempts by companies from certain countries to buy local firms, particularly those working in high-technology areas. The buyer can change the production pattern using AI and cause job loss in the host country, which can lead to destabilisation of its government and system as a whole. When the computer came, there was widespread fear that it would replace workers.

But it opened up new avenues of employment. AI is a different devil. While the computer has no independent mind and needs human help to arrive at a conclusion, AI has a mind of its own which can act, analyse and change course to arrive at the desired result. In short, it can replace humans completely in many areas.

Most vulnerable countries When AI plays havoc with the job market, democracies and kingdom states will be the most affected. Democracies because uncertainty about the future can trigger popular unrest; monarchies because a heightened fear of insurrection will grip them. Once the AI revolution is on, there is no turning back.

It would be a part of life whether we like it or not, just like the corona virus. However, there is a ray of hope. Who is AI finally going to replace? Human beings.
