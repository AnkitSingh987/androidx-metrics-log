Title: Will VBG Ram G score over MGNREGS Part One

Will VB-G Ram G score over MGNREGS?

Creating productive assets and curbing payment leakages appear doubtful under the new scheme. Also, it reduces flexibility in local planning MGNREGA (2005) offers a legal right-based safety net for the rural poor. The preamble to the Act says that it is meant for the enhancement of livelihood security by providing at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a year to every household whose adult members are available to do unskilled manual work, mostly in lean seasons, droughts and during other economic shocks.

This direct income support improves food security and reduces distress migration among the poor beneficiaries. It also plays a role in enhancing women's employment and social inclusion with mandated one-third engagement of women, although in many States actual female participation exceeds this. It also positively impacts marginalised groups like Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other vulnerable groups through universal, demand driven, de-bureacratic and local selection of works.

It brings a lot more people in to the labour force who otherwise would have little chance of being part of it. Age bracket As per the official data for 2024-25, age-wise distribution of persons employed in the programme during 2024-25 shows that 15.5 per cent were in the 61 to 80 age group who, in normal course, are unlikely to be in any paid employment. Next, 22.6 per cent were in the 51 to 60 age bracket.

Only 9 per cent of the workers in the scheme were in the younger age group of 18 to 30 years. It would be a serious concern, if participation of persons aged 41 years and above, comprising close to 70 per cent of the MNREGA workers, goes down. The age profile of the workers very clearly brings out the employment situation and the job demand across the states.

The percentage of workers in the younger age group (18 to 30 years) are high in Arunachal Pradesh ( 25.1), Bihar (21.2) and Jharkhand (23.6), the last two contributing significantly to migrant workers in the country. Older workers (61 to 80) are relatively very high in Andhra Pradesh (25.3 per cent), Goa (23 per cent), Kerala (36.4), Tamil Nadu (23.2), Telangana (27 per cent) and Pondicherry (23.3 per cent) that have higher life expectancy at birth than the national average. Creation of durable assets and strengthening the livelihood resource base of the rural poor was an objective of the scheme.

The stipulation that material component cost and the wages of skilled and semi-skilled workers should not exceed 40 per cent of the cost indirectly lays stress on a high manual labour component in the scheme. Under the existing Act, the Panchayats at district, intermediate and village levels are the principal authorities for planning and implementation of the schemes. This brings a local flavor to the actual field level implementation of the scheme.

Given the design of MGNREGA, the profile of workers emerges as a key concern in integrating and aligning the scheme with holistic visions like the Viksit Bharat Gram Panchayat Plans, PM Gati Shakti etc. A significant segment of the MGNREGA workers would not fit in the activities aligned with these objectives and their goals of modern infrastructure and economic engagement. This is evident from the types of work executed under the programme which were mostly in the nature of water conservation, drought proofing, afforestation, micro-level irrigation, renovation of water bodies, land development etc.
