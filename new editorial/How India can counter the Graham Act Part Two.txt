Title: How India can counter the Graham Act Part Two

How India can counter the Graham Act

Cannot bank on Washington's discretion The Act offers India more room than the headline rate suggests: tariffs capped at the top five importers, with carve-outs for "significant" diversification to reduce Russian energy dependence. The same administration that raised India's tariff to 50 per cent in August 2025 also reduced it to 18 per cent within six months, suggesting the lever is calibrated for bargaining rather than functioning as fixed law. India's response on September 17 was accordingly measured, not defiant, reaffirming diversification rather than threatening retaliation  -  the diplomatic equivalent of waiting the lever out.

The way forward Washington's discretion is not something India can rely on. Four steps matter more than hoping for restraint. First, conclude the bilateral trade agreement  - seven months overdue  -  at preferential, not reciprocal, rates: a negotiated floor beats a revocable concession. Second, treat the reserve buffer as a war chest, not a trophy  -  a swap-financed cushion needs genuine surplus behind it, not applause, before the next bout of rupee pressure.

Third, diversify crude sourcing for real, not the token shift that has kept Russia's share above 50 per cent  -  that earns the Act's own "significant steps" exemption, rather than betting on presidential goodwill. Fourth, widen export markets beyond a US corridor that has delivered two tariff shocks in thirteen months  -  concentration, not merely this law, is the risk. Strategic autonomy was never freedom from constraints; it is the capacity to prevent any single constraint from dictating policy.

By Sankalpa Bhattacharjee The writer teaches Economics at IIM Ranchi. Views are personal
