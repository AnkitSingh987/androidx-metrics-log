Title: The art of no deal Part Two

The art of no deal

At various stages, both sides have said an accord was near only for the talks to lose momentum. Over the weekend, US Ambassador Sergio Gor said most major elements have now been agreed "in principle", and what remains is working out the legal and technical language. Gor also announced Secretary of State Marco Rubio would return to India in October for a second time this year, portraying this as proof of the weight Washington attaches to its India ties.

That's encouraging. But only days before the Canada-US negotiations collapsed, Trump had declared the trade deal all but done. A US trade agreement could be immensely valuable. But New Delhi shouldn't assume agreement "in principle" means the tough part is over. Every clause matters. Provisions buried in technical language may have consequences for India's agricultural policy, its autonomy, its dealings with Russia, its digital economy or its freedom to negotiate agreements with other countries.

Canada thought it was approaching the finish line, but then Washington moved the line. New Delhi will have to watch carefully to make sure the US doesn't try the same thing. By Paran Balakrishnan
