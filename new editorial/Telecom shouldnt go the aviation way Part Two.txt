Title: Telecom shouldnt go the aviation way Part Two

Telecom shouldn't go the aviation way

This will keep the heavy investment infrastructure to be provided by the 2-3 operators, while facilitating new entrants in the services layer with light touch regulation. For the incumbent NSOs, spinning of the services arm is expected to provide regulatory relief in the form of reduced levies and taxes. This will also facilitate leasing of underused/unused assets of the NSOs including spectrum to be put in to optimal use.

The 2024 recommendation by TRAI on the framework for service authorisation is precisely along these lines to introduce light touch regulation in the telecom sector. The recent report by the leading GSM Association, indicates that MVNOs worldwide have grown by more than 60 per cent over the past decade. New MVNOs are entering the market, looking to strengthen customer acquisition and compete with traditional operators.

MVNOs are also positioning for the future through innovating in technology, capturing growing demand for Internet of Things, bundling content with connectivity, and forming partnerships along the way. The Government has been facilitating the survival of Vodafone Idea (VI) as an NSO by converting the regulatory dues into equity shares and allowing moratorium on payments. Going forward, VI should seriously consider functional separation of its entity so that the services arm can compete and offer services efficiently to become an active third private operator in the country.
