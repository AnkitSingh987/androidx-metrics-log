Title: A roadblock to tax certainty Part Two

A roadblock to tax certainty

The objective is to enhance compliance by making the law predictable and reduce arbitrariness. In this context, prioritising "spirit" over text appears regressive. It reintroduces discretion at the point where previous reforms have sought to eliminate it. The success of any new tax code depends not merely on drafting but also on consistent application.

Institutional resolution, not individual discretion None of this is to deny that statutes can produce unintended outcomes. No legislative exercise can anticipate every scenario. The question is how such situations should be addressed. The answer lies not in devolving interpretative authority to individual officers, but in centralising it within institutions such as the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).

Where literal interpretation leads to outcomes inconsistent with policy intent, the CBDT must step in. Such centralisation ensures uniformity and reduces the risk of fragmented interpretations across jurisdictions. If the issue can be resolved in favour of taxpayers, the Board should issue a clear public circular.

Alternatively, if the correct interpretation favours revenue, it should issue binding instructions to ensure uniform application. Such clarification must be followed by a legislative amendment at the earliest opportunity. This approach preserves legal integrity while ensuring anomalies are addressed systematically.

It avoids case-by-case discretion and reinforces that interpretation is an institutional function. The stakes: Certainty, credibility, and compliance Tax systems function effectively when taxpayers can predict outcomes with certainty. This predictability underpins compliance, reduces litigation, and enhances revenue stability.

Conversely, when interpretation becomes fluid and contingent on discretion, uncertainty rises, disputes proliferate, and compliance deteriorates. The Income Tax Act, 2025, represents an opportunity to reset direct taxation in India. That opportunity should not be diluted by an interpretative philosophy that undermines clarity.

The "spirit" of the law is important  -  but it must operate within, not in substitution of, the statutory text. Preserve primacy of law The guiding principle must remain clear: where the law is unambiguous, it must be applied as written. Where ambiguity exists, interpretative tools may be invoked, but only within disciplined boundaries.

Administrative convenience cannot justify departure from this framework. If the objective is to build a tax system that is fair, predictable, and modern, the path forward lies in strengthening rule-based administration, not in reintroducing discretion under the banner of "spirit". This is essential not only for legal coherence but also for sustaining long-term taxpayer confidence in the system.

By Arbind Modi Former Member, CBDT; former Senior Economist, IMF; Senior Visiting Research Fellow at XDR
