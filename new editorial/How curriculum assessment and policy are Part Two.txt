Title: How curriculum assessment and policy are Part Two

How curriculum, assessment, and policy are reshaping school learning

When teachers feel supported and trusted, students respond in kind. NEP 2020: Creating the conditions for change India's National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 has played a catalytic role in legitimising these shifts. By emphasising competency-based learning, holistic development, and student agency, the policy echoed what many educators already believed but struggled to implement.

Perhaps most importantly, NEP gave schools permission to rethink long-standing practices. Independent learning, critical thinking, collaboration, and emotional intelligence moved from being peripheral ideas to core educational outcomes. In many ways, NEP helped align Indian schooling with practices that progressive international curricula had already been developing.

Looking Ahead There is reason for cautious optimism. Independent learning is no longer merely a slogan; it is visible in pockets of the system. Yet this change remains uneven. While many international schools have embraced these approaches at scale, many Indian and State board schools remain constrained by older models of teaching and assessment.

One of the most significant barriers continues to be university admissions systems that rely heavily on rote-based entrance examinations. As long as access to higher education is determined primarily by memory-driven tests, schools will feel compelled to teach to those exams. Meaningful assessment reform at the school level cannot fully succeed unless admissions frameworks also evolve.

From my vantage point at TAISI, I see both the promise of what is possible and the limitations of the current ecosystem. For change to deepen and spread, more educators, institutions, and policymakers must believe that preparing students to think independently is not a risk, but a responsibility. Education, at its heart, is a human endeavour.

When students feel trusted, teachers feel respected, and schools feel purposeful, learning becomes meaningful. If 2025 has taught us anything, it is this: when we teach children how to learn, not merely what to learn, we prepare them not only for examinations, but for life beyond them. (Syed Sultan Ahmed, Chairperson, The Association of International Schools of India (TAISI) | MD & Chief Learner LXL Ideas | Advisor, WACE India.)
