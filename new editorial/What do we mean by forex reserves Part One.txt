Title: What do we mean by forex reserves Part One

What do we mean by forex reserves?

With forward market currency trade rising of late, the manner in which it should be reckoned in current reserves is an issue In the light of the FCNR scheme being introduced to attract dollars, a question raised is whether these funds would be used to shore up reserves, or for intervention in the market to stabilise the rupee. The two would amount to the same even when the operations are conducted independently.

Inflows can be added to the reserves as a matter of routine by swap with banks. At the same time there would be intervention in the spot market to steady the rupee, which would have been done even if FCNR scheme had not been there. Hence, what matters is the quantum of forex reserves at the end of the day.

Now, what exactly do we mean by forex reserves? There are primarily three parts to it: foreign currency assets, gold and others such as SDRs. Should the total be taken to be the number or just foreign currency? In the past, the share of gold was around 6 per cent. But of late it has risen to nearly 17 per cent by 2025-26.

This has been a conscious policy of central banks across the world to diversify forex reserves and bring in more gold which is a neutral asset. Gold has the same virtue as dollar or any other hard currency in terms of being acceptable to all. At the same time, it does not belong to any country. This being the case, reserves stood at $691 billion as of March 2026 with currency component being $553 billion and gold, $115 billion.

A metric used often to judge the strength of forex reserves is to juxtapose the same with value of imports. In FY26 imports were $775 billion which is around $65 billion a month. Hence reserves would cover around 10.6 months of imports which is very comfortable. The more conservative analysts would use only foreign currency as the appropriate reserves.

This would in turn yield a cover of 8.5 months which is still very good. RBI intervention In the last couple of years in particular, the RBI has been very active by way of intervention in terms of both spot transactions and forwards. The spot transactions directly get reflected in the movement of forex reserves and would be part of the final number.

However, the forwards are in the derivative space where the RBI goes in for swaps; dollars are sold short in the market, which sends a strong signal of intervention without any direct outflow in the spot market. This is considered by some analysts as a liability that has to be reduced from total reserves to get a fair picture of the same. The share of gold has gone up sharply in the last two years though the upward trend has been noticed since 2023-24. One reason often given for central banks increasing their holdings of gold is the compulsion to diversify and de-dollarize ever since the US impounded the reserves of Russia which were invested in US treasuries. This is now a global phenomenon.

The 'forwards' trend is also interesting. A positive number is a long position taken wherein dollars will be bought in future while a negative number is a short position of RBI selling dollars in the coming months. Taking a forward position in the market is more of a signalling mechanism to the market of the RBI's intentions.
