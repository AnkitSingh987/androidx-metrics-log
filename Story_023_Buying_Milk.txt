Title: Buying Milk
Category: Beginner

It is morning. Mrs. Jones is in her kitchen. She is making breakfast for her family. She is making cereal for her two children. 
She takes two bowls. She puts cereal in the bowls. The cereal is crunchy and brown. 
Now, she needs milk. Children like milk with their cereal. 

She goes to the fridge. She opens the door. She looks for the milk. 
She sees the eggs. She sees the butter. She sees the juice. She sees the big plastic bottle of milk. 
She takes the bottle. It feels very light. 
She shakes the bottle. There is no sound. 
She looks inside the bottle. It is empty. There is only one drop of milk left. 
"Oh no," Mrs. Jones says. "We have no milk."

She goes to the living room. Her husband, Mr. Jones, is reading the newspaper. 
"We have a problem," Mrs. Jones says. "We have no milk for breakfast."
"I will go and buy some," Mr. Jones says. He is a helpful husband. 
He puts down his newspaper. He stands up. 

Mr. Jones does not need to go to the big supermarket. The big supermarket is far away. He can go to the small shop. The small shop is at the corner of their street. It is very close. 
He takes his wallet from the table. He puts it in his pocket. 
"I will be back in ten minutes," he says to his wife. 

He walks out of the house. It is a nice morning. The air is fresh. He walks down the street. He sees his neighbor. He waves his hand. 
"Good morning!" he says. 
"Good morning!" the neighbor says. 
Mr. Jones continues to walk. He reaches the corner of the street. 

The small shop is open. The shop has a big glass window. Mr. Jones can see inside. He can see the shelves. He can see the shopkeeper. 
The shopkeeper is a friendly man named Mr. Ali. 
Mr. Jones opens the door. A small bell rings. 'Ding, ding.' 
"Good morning, Mr. Jones," Mr. Ali says. He smiles. 
"Good morning, Mr. Ali," Mr. Jones says. "How are you today?"
"I am very well, thank you. How can I help you?" Mr. Ali asks. 

"I need some milk," Mr. Jones says. "My children want cereal for breakfast, and our fridge is empty."
"I have very fresh milk," Mr. Ali says. "It arrived this morning."
Mr. Ali points to a large refrigerator at the back of the shop. 
Mr. Jones walks to the refrigerator. He opens the glass door. It is very cold inside. 
He sees many bottles of milk. There are small bottles and big bottles. There is milk with a blue cap. There is milk with a red cap. 
Mr. Jones takes a big bottle with a blue cap. His family drinks a lot of milk. 

He closes the refrigerator door. He carries the heavy bottle of milk to the counter. 
Mr. Ali takes the bottle. He looks at the price. 
"That is two dollars and fifty cents, please," Mr. Ali says. 
Mr. Jones takes his wallet from his pocket. He opens it. He takes out a five-dollar bill. It is made of paper. He gives it to Mr. Ali. 
"Here is five dollars," Mr. Jones says. 

Mr. Ali takes the money. He opens the cash register. The cash register makes a loud 'Cha-ching!' sound. 
Mr. Ali puts the five dollars inside. He takes out two one-dollar bills. He takes out a shiny silver coin. It is a fifty-cent coin. 
He gives the money back to Mr. Jones. 
"Here is your change," Mr. Ali says. "Two dollars and fifty cents."
"Thank you," Mr. Jones says. He puts the money back in his wallet. 

Mr. Ali puts the bottle of milk into a strong paper bag. He hands the bag to Mr. Jones. 
"Thank you, Mr. Ali. Have a good day," Mr. Jones says. 
"You too, Mr. Jones. Enjoy your breakfast," Mr. Ali says. 
Mr. Jones walks out of the shop. The bell rings again. 'Ding, ding.' 

He walks back up the street. The bag is heavy. The milk is cold. He holds the bag tightly. 
He arrives at his house. He opens the door. 
"I am back!" he calls out. "I have the milk!"
His children run into the hall. They are happy. 
"Yay! Milk!" they shout. 
Mrs. Jones comes from the kitchen. She smiles. 
"Thank you, dear," she says. She takes the paper bag. 

She goes to the kitchen. She takes the milk out of the bag. She opens the blue cap. She pours the cold, white milk into the two bowls of cereal. 
The children sit at the table. They start to eat their breakfast. They are very happy. 
Mr. Jones sits down too. He drinks a cup of coffee. He reads his newspaper. It was a short trip to the shop, but it was a very important trip. Now, everyone has a good breakfast.
