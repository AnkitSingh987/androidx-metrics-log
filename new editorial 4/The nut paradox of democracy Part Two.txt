Title: The nut paradox of democracy Part Two

The nut paradox of democracy

The paradox of virtue But we should not become complacent. In a democracy you never know where and when a nut will take over the government. The problem here is not merely a nut getting elected. It's also how to prevent that nut from causing massive disruption. No one knows in advance. An abiding mystery in this regard is why such madmen are not removed from office.

Even in monarchies, the powers of a mad monarch have been curbed by sequestration. It hasn't always worked fully but it's had a fair amount of success. Democracies, however, fare very badly on this score. The procedure for removing an unfit head of government may exist but it's on paper only. It's hardly ever been used because it's nearly impossible to use.

Indeed, the process is designed to fail because a democratically elected leader can only be removed democratically, that is, by the electorate. That's the reason nuts are safer in democracies than in other forms of government. The one exception was recently in Korea where a president was impeached successfully.

In fact, the Koreans have done it twice. The first time was in 1990 when they got rid of President Park Chung-hee. But apart from that do let me know if there are any other instances of a nut being removed from office by the legislature. Indeed, that's why, sometimes it takes nuts to remove nuts. By TCA Srinivasa Raghavan
