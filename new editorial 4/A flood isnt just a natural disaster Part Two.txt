Title: A flood isnt just a natural disaster Part Two

A flood isn't just a natural disaster

Education must therefore change. Children should learn not only that floods follow heavy rain, but to ask: Where did the water come from? Where did the forest go? Why was the settlement built there? Where is the floodplain? Why was the river narrowed? Was a warning issued, and who acted on it? Such education teaches children to understand the relationship between human decisions and nature rather than simply memorising environmental facts.

We speak of conquering mountains, controlling rivers and harnessing nature. But a mountain does not surrender because a road is cut into it, and a river does not become obedient because concrete walls surround it. Nature has limits. When we cross them, nature reminds us who is really in control. The Nepal tragedy should not end with rescue, compensation and reconstruction.

Rebuilding the same roads and settlements in the same hazardous places may simply prepare the next disaster. Hazard maps must guide land-use plans; watershed planning must precede isolated projects; ecological limits must be assessed before construction; environmental assessments must be genuinely independent; warnings must reach communities; and some high-risk areas may have to remain free of permanent settlement. Scientists must speak beyond their laboratories.

Engineers must sometimes say "no". Bureaucrats must reject unsafe projects. Political leaders must understand that preventing a disaster earns no ribbon-cutting ceremony  -  but may save thousands of lives. Citizens must also recognise that environmental protection is not an obstacle to development; it is the insurance that makes development durable.

The most frightening lesson from Nepal is not simply that the Himalayas are becoming more dangerous. It is that we are becoming increasingly dangerous to ourselves because we have forgotten how to live within the limits of the landscape. By DR DEBAPRIYA MUKHERJEE (The writer is former Senior Scientist, Central Pollution Control Board)
