Title: The Kitchen Helper
Category: Beginner

It is late afternoon. Mrs. Davis is in the kitchen. She is preparing dinner. She is making a big vegetable soup. 
The kitchen is a warm and busy place. Mrs. Davis is chopping onions. Her eyes are watering a little. 

Then, her daughter, Lily, comes into the kitchen. Lily is six years old. She is wearing a pink dress. 
"Mother," Lily says. "What are you doing?"
"I am making vegetable soup for dinner," Mrs. Davis answers. She wipes a tear from her eye. "Onions always make me cry."
"Can I help you make the soup?" Lily asks. "I want to be a kitchen helper."

Mrs. Davis smiles. She loves it when Lily wants to help. It is good for children to learn how to cook. 
"Yes, Lily, you can be my kitchen helper today," her mother says. "But first, what must a good helper do?"
"I must wash my hands!" Lily says loudly. 
"That is correct," her mother says. 

Lily runs to the sink. She cannot reach the tap. She pulls her small blue step stool to the sink. She stands on the stool. Now she is tall enough. 
She turns on the warm water. She pumps some soap onto her hands. She rubs her hands together. She makes a lot of white bubbles. She washes between her fingers. She rinses the bubbles away. She dries her hands on a clean towel. 
"My hands are clean!" she says. 

"Good girl," Mrs. Davis says. She takes a small green apron. She ties the apron around Lily's waist. Now Lily looks like a real chef. 
"What is my first job?" Lily asks. She is very excited. 

Mrs. Davis points to a bowl on the counter. "I have some carrots and potatoes in that bowl. They are dirty. They have soil on them from the farm. Can you wash them for me?"
"Yes, I can wash them," Lily says. 

Mrs. Davis takes the bowl. She puts it next to the sink. She gives Lily a small vegetable brush. 
Lily turns on the cold water. She takes a big orange carrot. She holds it under the water. She scrubs the carrot with the small brush. Scrub, scrub, scrub. The dirt washes away. The carrot is bright orange and clean. 
She puts the clean carrot on a plate. 

Then she takes a potato. The potato is brown and very dirty. She scrubs it hard with the brush. The water turns brown. The potato becomes clean. She puts it on the plate next to the carrot. 
She washes all three carrots and all four potatoes. 
"They are all clean, Mother," Lily says proudly. 
"Excellent job, my little helper," Mrs. Davis says. "Now, I will peel them and cut them. Knives are too sharp for you right now."

Mrs. Davis takes a peeler. She peels the skin off the carrots and potatoes. Then she takes a sharp knife. She cuts the vegetables into small squares. She puts all the squares into a big pot. 
"What is my next job?" Lily asks. She wants to do more. 

"I need to make the soup thick," Mrs. Davis says. "I need you to mix some flour and water."
Mrs. Davis takes a small blue bowl. She puts two spoons of white flour into the bowl. She adds a little bit of cold water. She gives Lily a small whisk. A whisk is a tool for mixing. 
"Mix the flour and water together, Lily," her mother says. "Mix it until it is smooth. We do not want any lumps in our soup."

Lily takes the whisk. She starts to stir the flour and water. She stirs slowly at first. Then she stirs faster. 
Whisk, whisk, whisk. 
The flour and water mix together. It becomes a thick, white liquid. She looks closely. There are no lumps. It is perfectly smooth. 
"I finished mixing," Lily says. 

"Let me see," Mrs. Davis says. She looks in the blue bowl. "That is perfect, Lily. You are a very good mixer."
Mrs. Davis pours the white liquid into the big pot of soup. She turns on the stove. The soup starts to heat up. 
"Now, we just have to stir the soup while it cooks," Mrs. Davis says. She gives Lily a very long wooden spoon. "You can stir it, but be careful. The stove is hot."

Lily stands on her blue stool. She holds the long wooden spoon with two hands. She reaches into the big pot. She stirs the soup slowly in a big circle. The soup is getting warm. She can see the orange carrots and the white potatoes moving in the water. 
"It smells good," Lily says. 
"It will taste very good," Mrs. Davis says. "Because it has a secret ingredient."
"What is the secret ingredient?" Lily asks. 
"Love," her mother says with a smile. "And the hard work of my favorite kitchen helper."

They cook the soup together. Lily is very happy. She likes wearing her green apron. She likes washing the vegetables. She likes stirring the pot. When her father and brother come home, she will tell them she made the dinner. She is the best kitchen helper in the house.
