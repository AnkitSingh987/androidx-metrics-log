Title: Battlefield where India is losing

Battlefield where India is losing

In the contemporary geopolitical landscape, the traditional metrics of national strength - military hardware and economic indicators - are no longer sufficient to secure global prominence. In the contemporary geopolitical landscape, the traditional metrics of national strength - military hardware and economic indicators - are no longer sufficient to secure global prominence. The international paradigm has shifted toward cognitive warfare, a domain where the human mind is the primary battlefield and public perception is the ultimate prize.

For India, a nation navigating a complex geopolitical trajectory, this shift presents a critical challenge. While academic debates, such as those inspired by historian Brendan Simms' conceptualisations of power dynamics, argue whether India is an "almost Great Power," a Middle Power, or risking the trajectory of a "Stagnant Power," the reality remains clear: hard power without sophisticated smart power is unsustainable. To avert strategic stagnation, India must urgently address its systemic deficit in global narrative projection.

A significant vulnerability in India's foreign policy architecture is the lack of a dominant, globally synchronized media voice. International-facing broadcasters and news organisations such as DD India, WION, NDTV World, Times Now, and India Today attempt to project New Delhi's perspective, yet their international market penetration remains limited. They have yet to achieve the global audience penetration of networks such as CNN, BBC World News, or Al Jazeera.

This vacuum allows external actors and adversarial states to fill the information void, routinely mischaracterising India's geopolitical stances, internal policies, and strategic objectives. India's point of view is largely left unportrayed, allowing others to take advantage of the gap. In the fast-paced information age, relying solely on official diplomatic demarches or formal government press releases is an outdated strategy.

The global public does not have the time to follow dense, bureaucratic pronouncements. Instead, modern audiences in the global village consume highly engaging, sensationalised, and meticulously packaged information  -  the metaphorical tadka with chatpata flavour of contemporary news. By failing to deliver high-quality, engaging content that competes effectively on the global stage, Indian media cedes critical psychological ground to international competitors.

To elevate the quality of its journalism, India must adopt the sophisticated methodologies of established global media empires. A primary structural flaw in India's current international media strategy is the overt suffixing and prefixing of the nation's name to its news platforms. In the realm of cognitive warfare, overt nationalism in international broadcasting is counterproductive.

Prominent global networks like CNN, Fox News, or Al Jazeera do not explicitly carry their country of origin in their primary branding. This architectural subtlety is deliberate. Many audiences are unlikely to consciously evaluate the strategic branding and framing choices employed by international media organisations, making subtle branding an important component of perception management.

If a media house directly portrays its nation's name, foreign audiences easily understand the actual goal. They instinctively f ilter the information through a lens of scepticism, assuming an underlying national agenda, which causes the objective to fail. Such perceptions can reduce the broadcaster's ability to project itself as a neutral source of information.

Intellectuals and geopolitical analysts understand the mechanics of cognitive warfare, but the broader global populace remains susceptible to the subtle framing of news. By utilising streamlined, abstracted acronyms or distinct brand names rather than geographical suffixes, media entities can seamlessly embed strategic perceptions into the global consciousness without triggering immediate defensive biases. If India aspires to transcend its current geopolitical constraints and project true smart power, an immediate overhaul of its international media strategy is required.

This demands a drastic upgrade in journalistic quality alongside a psychological decoupling from overt national branding. Only by mastering the nuanced, subtle art of perception management can India successfully bridge the information gap, defend its strategic interests. Also developing globally minded journalists is essential for strengthening India's narrative power and enhancing its influence in international discourse.

By SOURASISH GHOSH (The writer is President, Proyash Welfare Association)
