Title: The Medicine
Category: Beginner

After leaving the doctor's clinic, Sam and his mother walk to the pharmacy. The pharmacy is a shop that sells medicine. It is on the corner of the street. 
They walk through the automatic glass doors. The pharmacy smells clean and slightly sweet. There are many shelves with shampoo, toothpaste, and vitamins. But at the back of the shop, there is a high counter. This is where the pharmacist works. 

Sam's mother walks to the high counter. She hands the small piece of paper from Dr. Evans to the pharmacist. 
The pharmacist is a woman with glasses. She reads the paper carefully. 
"Antibiotics for Sam Miller," she says. "Please wait a few minutes. I will prepare the medicine."

Sam stands next to his mother. He is very tired. He wants to go home and lie in his bed. 
Soon, the pharmacist comes back. She holds a small brown glass bottle and a small plastic measuring cup. 
"Here is the medicine," she says. "It is a liquid. Sam must take ten milliliters every morning and ten milliliters every evening. He must take it after eating some food. And remember, you must finish the whole bottle, even if he feels better."
"I understand," Sam's mother says. "Thank you."
She pays for the medicine, and they walk back to the car. 

When they arrive home, Sam goes straight to the living room. He lies down on the soft sofa. 
His mother goes to the kitchen. She makes a piece of toast with butter. 
"Sam," she calls from the kitchen. "You need to eat a little bit of food before you take your medicine. Come and eat this toast."
Sam does not feel hungry, but he slowly walks to the kitchen. He eats the toast. It is dry, and his throat is sore, but he finishes it. 

His mother takes the small brown bottle. She shakes it well. Then, she pours the thick liquid into the small plastic measuring cup. 
The liquid is bright pink. 
"Here you go," she says. She hands the cup to Sam. 

Sam looks at the pink liquid. "What does it taste like?" he asks nervously. 
"It says 'strawberry flavor' on the bottle," his mother says. "It should be nice."
Sam does not believe her. Medicine is usually terrible. 
He holds his breath. He lifts the small cup to his mouth. He drinks the pink liquid quickly. 

He makes a funny face. It tastes like very sweet, very fake strawberry candy. It is not delicious, but it is not terrible. It is thick and leaves a strange taste in his mouth. 
He swallows it quickly. 
His mother hands him a large glass of cold water. 
"Drink this," she says. 
Sam drinks the water. The cold water washes away the sweet, strange taste. It makes his sore throat feel a little better for a moment. 

"Well done," his mother says. "Now, you must go upstairs and sleep. The medicine needs time to work. Sleep is the best thing for you right now."
Sam walks slowly up the stairs. His legs feel heavy. 
He goes into his bedroom. He pulls the blankets back and climbs into bed. The bed feels very soft and comfortable. 

His mother comes into the room. She pulls the curtains closed so the room is dark. She puts a glass of fresh water on the small table next to his bed. 
"I will be downstairs," she whispers. "Call me if you need anything."
"Okay," Sam whispers back. 
She kisses his hot forehead and leaves the room. She closes the door quietly. 

Sam lies in the quiet, dark room. His head is throbbing with a dull pain. But he feels safe in his bed. He knows the pink medicine is inside his stomach, getting ready to fight the infection. 
He closes his eyes. He listens to the quiet sounds of the house. He hears a car drive past the window outside. Slowly, his breathing becomes deep and regular. The medicine makes him feel sleepy. In ten minutes, Sam is fast asleep, resting and getting better.
