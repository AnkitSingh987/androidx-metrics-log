Title: Saying Goodbye
Category: Beginner

Monday morning arrived too quickly. It was time for the Miller family to leave the countryside and go back to the city. The weekend adventure was over. 

After a quick breakfast, everyone went upstairs to pack their bags. Thomas pushed his clothes into his blue duffel bag. He found a beautiful brown autumn leaf on his desk that he had picked up in the forest. He carefully placed the leaf inside his comic book so he could keep it safe. He wanted a small piece of the countryside to take home with him. 

In the next room, Sophie neatly folded her thick red sweater and placed it in her pink suitcase. She zipped the suitcase shut and pulled it off the bed. She felt a little sad. She liked her yellow bedroom in the grandparents' house. She wanted to stay longer to see the tulip bulbs grow. 

Downstairs, the atmosphere was quiet. Nobody was laughing or talking loudly like they did at dinner the night before. Saying goodbye was always the hardest part of a trip. 

Mr. Miller carried the heavy family suitcase down the stairs and put it by the front door. Thomas and Sophie brought their smaller bags and placed them next to it. 
Grandpa looked at his watch. "The train leaves at ten-thirty," he said. "We should put the bags in the car now. We do not want you to miss it."

Grandpa and Mr. Miller carried the luggage outside and loaded it into the trunk of the old green car. The morning air was very cold, and the sky was a dull grey color. It looked like it might rain later in the day. 

Inside the hallway, Grandma was holding Sophie's hand. 
"I will miss you very much, my little gardener," Grandma said softly. 
"I will miss you too, Grandma," Sophie replied. She hugged her grandmother tightly. "Thank you for teaching me how to plant the bulbs. I will think about them all winter."

Thomas walked over and hugged his grandmother too. 
"Thank you for the delicious food, Grandma," he said. "The roast beef was the best."
"You are welcome, Thomas," she smiled, patting his cheek. "You are a growing boy; you need good food. Come back soon, and I will make it again for you."

They all walked outside to the gravel driveway. The cold wind made them pull their coats tight around their bodies. 
Mrs. Miller hugged her parents. "Thank you for a wonderful weekend," she said. "It was so good to see the new house. You have made it very beautiful."
"It was wonderful to have you all here," Grandpa said, opening the car door for them. "The house is much happier when it is full of noise and children."

Thomas, Sophie, and Mrs. Miller climbed into the back of the car. Mr. Miller sat in the front next to Grandpa. Grandma stood on the gravel driveway, waving her hand. 
As Grandpa started the engine and the car rolled slowly down the driveway, the children looked out the back window. They waved to Grandma until the car turned the corner and the old stone house disappeared behind the tall green hedges. 

The drive to the small train station was quiet. They passed the same open fields and narrow roads they saw on Saturday, but they looked different now because they were leaving. 

When they arrived at Green Valley Station, the long silver train was already waiting at the concrete platform. 
Grandpa helped them take the bags out of the trunk. 
"Well, this is it," Grandpa said. He hugged Mr. Miller, then Mrs. Miller, and then he gave Thomas and Sophie one last big squeeze. "Have a safe journey home. Study hard at school, and be good for your parents."
"We will, Grandpa," Thomas promised. 

The train blew its loud horn. 'HONK!' It was a warning that the doors were going to close soon. 
"Go on, get on the train," Grandpa urged them. 

The family quickly climbed the metal steps into carriage number four. They found their table seat next to the large window. They sat down and looked out at the platform. Grandpa was standing there, holding his thick winter coat tight against the cold wind. He smiled and waved. 

The train doors closed with a soft 'whoosh'. Slowly and smoothly, the heavy train began to move. 
Thomas and Sophie pressed their hands against the cold glass window. They waved vigorously at their grandfather. He waved back until the train moved faster and faster, leaving the small station and the countryside behind. 

Sophie leaned back in her seat and sighed. 
"Are you sad, Sophie?" her mother asked gently. 
"A little bit," Sophie admitted. "I had a really good time."
"Me too," Thomas agreed. 
"It is okay to be sad," Mr. Miller said. "It means we had a wonderful visit. But we will go back again in the spring. We have to go back to see Sophie's tulips bloom, remember?"

Sophie smiled at the thought of the bright spring flowers. The sadness went away a little bit. She looked out the window as the green fields rushed past. The weekend was over, but she had many happy memories packed away safely in her mind, just like the warm clothes in her pink suitcase.
