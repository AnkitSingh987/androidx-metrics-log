Title: A Flat Tire
Category: Beginner

It was Wednesday afternoon. David was riding his red mountain bike alone. He was riding on a dirt path through a small park near his house. He liked the dirt path because there were small hills and bumps that made the ride exciting. 

He was pedaling fast up a small hill. When he reached the top, he rode quickly down the other side. 
Suddenly, he heard a loud, sharp noise. 
'POP! Hssssssssss.'
Immediately, the back of his bicycle felt strange. It felt heavy and difficult to control. The back wheel started to slide from side to side on the dirt. 

David squeezed the brakes carefully and stopped the bicycle. He stepped off and looked at the back wheel. 
The brand new tire was completely flat. The black rubber was squashed flat against the metal rim. 
"Oh no," David groaned. He leaned closer. He saw a large, sharp thorn from a bush sticking directly out of the thick rubber. The thorn had punctured the inner tube, and all the air had escaped. 

David felt a sudden wave of frustration. He was two miles away from his house. He could not ride the bicycle with a flat tire; it would ruin the metal wheel. 
He sighed, grabbed the handlebars, and began to push the heavy bicycle along the path. 

Pushing a broken bicycle is not fun. The bicycle felt twice as heavy as normal. The flat rubber tire dragged on the ground, making it difficult to push straight. The hot afternoon sun beat down on his back, making him sweat. 
He walked for thirty minutes. It felt like hours. He watched other children riding past him on their perfect, working bicycles. He felt a little embarrassed and very annoyed. 

When he finally reached his driveway, his arms were tired, and he was very thirsty. 
His father was washing the car in the driveway. He looked up and saw David pushing the broken bike. 
"What happened?" his father asked, dropping his soapy sponge. 
"I got a flat tire," David said miserably. He pointed to the sharp thorn still stuck in the rubber. "I had to walk all the way back from the park."

His father looked at the tire. He didn't look angry or worried. He just smiled. 
"Well, congratulations," his father said. 
David frowned. "Congratulations? Why? My bike is broken again."
"Because getting a flat tire is a normal part of cycling," his father explained. "It happens to everyone. And now, you get to learn the most important repair skill for any cyclist."

"How to fix a flat tire?" David asked, his frustration fading a little bit. 
"Exactly," his father said. "Come on. Let's go to the garage."

They took the red bicycle into the cool garage. His father showed David how to take the back wheel off the frame. It was more difficult than the front wheel because of the chain and gears, but David managed it. 
Next, his father handed him two small plastic tools called tire levers. 
"Use these to pry the rubber tire off the metal rim," his father instructed. 

David pushed the plastic tools under the tight rubber and pulled hard. Slowly, the tire popped off the edge of the rim. He pulled out the flat inner tube. 
His father gave him a small, flat box. It was a puncture repair kit. Inside were small rubber patches, sandpaper, and a tiny tube of strong glue. 

"First, we find the hole," his father said. He pumped a tiny bit of air into the broken tube. They listened closely. They heard a faint 'hiss' and felt the air escaping against their fingers. 
"There it is," David said, marking the small hole with a pen. 

"Now, rub the area around the hole with the sandpaper," his father directed. "This makes the rubber rough, so the glue will stick better."
David sanded the rubber until it looked dull. He squeezed a small drop of clear glue onto the area and waited one minute for it to become sticky. Then, he peeled the back off a small rubber patch and pressed it firmly over the hole. He held it tightly for five minutes. 

"Okay, let's test it," his father said. 
David pumped air into the inner tube. It inflated perfectly and stayed round and hard. The patch worked. The hole was sealed. 

David felt a huge sense of relief and pride. He had fixed it. 
They put the repaired inner tube back into the tire, pushed the tire back onto the rim, and attached the wheel to the bicycle frame. 
David pumped the tire up to its full pressure. It was as good as new. 

"You see?" his father said, wiping his hands. "A flat tire is not a disaster. It is just a small problem. And now, you know how to solve it yourself."
David smiled and nodded. He was tired from walking, but he felt very capable. He was a real cyclist now. He knew how to ride, and he knew how to repair. He was ready for any adventure his new hobby would bring.
