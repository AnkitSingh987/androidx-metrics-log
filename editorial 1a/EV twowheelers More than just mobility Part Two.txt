Title: EV twowheelers More than just mobility Part Two

EV two-wheelers: More than just mobility

As volumes scale, the cost of weak standards becomes systemic rather than episodic. A related challenge is dependence on imported components, particularly battery cells, motor controllers, and power electronics  -  much of it sourced from China. While imports have enabled rapid scaling and competitive pricing, they also raise concerns around quality control, supply-chain resilience, and strategic dependence.

Innovation remains uneven. Much of the ecosystem is still focused on assembly rather than deep manufacturing. True differentiation  -  whether in battery chemistry, drivetrain architecture, or power electronics  -  is limited to a handful of players. For many manufacturers, innovation remains incremental, driven more by design, form factors, and software layers than by core engineering breakthroughs.

Engineering factor The next phase of growth will depend less on subsidies or market exuberance and more on engineering depth. For EV two-wheelers to realise their full potential, innovation and indigenisation must become central priorities. India needs stronger domestic capabilities across the value chain, particularly in battery cells, power electronics, and embedded software.

Localising cell manufacturing is critical  - not only for cost reduction but also for safety, performance optimisation, and strategic autonomy. Design-led innovation is equally essential. Indian EV two-wheelers must be engineered for Indian conditions: heavy daily usage, overloading, extreme temperatures, erratic charging environments, and uneven roads.

Vehicles designed for other geographies cannot simply be adapted; they must be reimagined. This transition will require patient capital. Private institutional investors must begin viewing EV two-wheelers not merely as consumer products but as long-term infrastructure for jobs, productivity, and industrial capability.

If executed well, EV two-wheelers could become one of India's most powerful instruments of inclusive growth  -  combining clean mobility with employment, entrepreneurship, and manufacturing depth. The opportunity is no longer just about electrifying transport. It is about electrifying livelihoods. By Abhishek Sinha & Manvendra Prasad Sinha FRM, is Assistant Professor, Centre for Entrepreneurship and Innovation, Mahindra University; Prasad is a Partner at IntelliNexus Ventures LLP
