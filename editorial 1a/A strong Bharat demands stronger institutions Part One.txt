Title: A strong Bharat demands stronger institutions Part One

A strong Bharat demands stronger institutions

India stands at a defining moment in its democratic journey. It is the world's largest democracy, one of the fastest-growing major economies, and an emerging global power. Yet the true strength of a nation is not measured only by GDP growth, expressways, airports, or digital infrastructure.

It is measured by the quality of its institutions and the confidence that ordinary citizens place in them. Today, that confidence is under increasing strain. These challenges are not failures of one government or one political party. They are symptoms of institutional weaknesses that have accumulated over decades and have survived successive administrations of every political persuasion.

As Abraham Lincoln wisely observed, "When public institutions fail to deliver efficient services, public frustration inevitably rises." India's police system requires urgent modernization. Across the country, police personnel serve with courage under difficult conditions, yet they often work with inadequate manpower, outdated investigative methods, excessive workloads, limited forensic support, and insufficient technological integration. Citizens frequently perceive investigations as slow, corrupt, and legal processes as cumbersome.

Genuine police reforms must focus on professionalism, operational independence, scientific investigation, accountability, and community policing for citizens to view the police as protectors rather than merely law enforcers. Moreover, the lack of reforms has led to police stations in India turning in extortion hubs. The judiciary remains another pillar requiring comprehensive reform.

Justice delayed has become one of India's greatest institutional challenges. Millions of pending cases undermine confidence in the rule of law, delay economic activity, discourage investment, and impose enormous emotional and financial costs on ordinary litigants. Judicial reforms should include significantly increasing judicial capacity, adopting artificial intelligence for case management, strengthening digital courts, simplifying procedures, expanding mediation, and ensuring that justice is both affordable and timely.

As William Gladstone famously remarked, "Justice delayed is justice denied." The courts in India have lately become an effective tool to serve the elites; the common man is left high and dry. Urban governance presents equally serious concerns. India's cities generate economic growth but struggle with pollution, waste management, congestion, flooding, water shortages, unauthorized construction, and inadequate civic infrastructure.

Municipal bodies frequently operate with fragmented authority, weak finances, and limited professional capacity. The issue is not simply funding; it is institutional efficiency, accountability, and measurable outcomes. Surely, citizens deserve clean streets, safe drinking water, efficient public transport, and transparent local governance, contrary to what they are getting across the country.

Healthcare represents perhaps the greatest daily concern for ordinary families. Despite remarkable advances in medical science, access to quality healthcare remains highly unequal. Public hospitals in many regions continue to face shortages of doctors, nurses, medicines, equipment, and infrastructure.

A medical emergency should never become a financial catastrophe for a family. Investing in primary healthcare, preventive medicine, digital health systems, medical education, and rural healthcare infrastructure must become a national priority. Education too requires structural transformation. Expanding enrolment alone cannot define success if learning outcomes remain weak.

Schools and universities must produce critical thinkers, innovators, entrepreneurs, scientists, and skilled professionals rather than merely examination candidates. India's demographic dividend can become its greatest strength only if education equips young people with practical skills, digital literacy, ethical values, and adaptability in an AI-driven economy. The recent Gen Z agitation should be an eye-opener for the ruling class; it exposes the disconnect between the government and its young citizens.
