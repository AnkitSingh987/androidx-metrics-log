Title: AI in pharma sector Part One

AI in pharma sector

Can play a vital role in drug quality control With rising manufacturing complexity and intensified regulatory expectations, traditional quality frameworks are no longer sufficient for Indian pharmaceutical industry. Emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI) is now emerging as a critical enabler in transforming pharmaceutical quality systems by enhancing support for speed of decision making, compliance, improving manufacturing and quality consistency and strengthening patient safety. While AI has already made inroads into drug discovery and clinical research, its most profound impact may be in quality management, where consistency, traceability and accuracy are paramount.

From Documentation to Intelligence-Driven Quality Historically, pharma quality has relied heavily on documentation such as Process and Analytical Validations, batch records, deviation reports, investigations, and audits. These systems, while essential, are largely dependent on manual reviews, often leading to delays, variability, and retrospective reactive approach to quality management when required. AI enables a fundamental shift.

By digitising documentation and applying analytics to large volumes of manufacturing and analytical data, quality systems can move from manual oversight to intelligent surveillance. AI-powered tools can scan batch records, flag anomalies, detect inconsistencies and highlight emerging risks long before they become deviations and ultimately lead to failures and product rejections. This transition allows quality teams to focus less on paperwork and more on decision-making, root-cause analysis, and continuous improvement.

One of the most impactful applications of AI in quality systems is deviation management. Traditionally, deviation investigations depend heavily on manual review of historical records and expert judgement. AI changes this by analysing past deviations and CAPAs, identifying recurring failure patterns, highlighting high-risk processes or operators and suggesting probable root causes based on data trends.

An early warning feature in the data monitoring of the ongoing manufacturing and analysis activities, further enhances the reduction in deviations and failures Predictive root-cause analysis, significantly reducing investigation timelines and improving the effectiveness of CAPAs. Instead of reacting to failures, quality teams can prevent them. RFT: The New Quality Imperative One of the most important outcomes of AI adoption is the ability to achieve Right First Time (RFT) manufacturing (producing products within specifications, the first time, with minimal rework, rejection, or deviation).

RFT is critical not only for operational efficiency but also for regulatory compliance and patient safety. AI strengthens RFT by identifying patterns of failure, analysing historical deviations, and predicting potential risks before they materialise. This reduces waste, lowers costs, improves batch release timelines, and enhances regulatory confidence.

AI-enabled predictive maintenance systems analyse equipment performance data, sensor readings and historical failures and environmental and operational parameters. Through these systems, failures can be predicted before they occur, allowing preventive maintenance instead of reactive repairs. The result is reduced downtime and optimising equipment utilisation.

Strengthening Compliance and Regulatory Readiness Regulatory agencies globally including the US FDA, EMA, MHRA, and WHO are increasingly supportive of digital and AI-enabled systems, provided governance, data integrity and human oversight remain intact. AI strengthens compliance by ensuring consistent documentation, maintaining complete audit trails, supporting root cause analysis (RCA) and CAPA and enabling continuous audit readiness. Importantly, AI is positioned as a decision-support tool, not a decision-maker.

Final accountability continues to rest with qualified professionals, aligning with regulatory expectations that emphasize transparency, explainability, and human oversight. To prepare for an AI-first quality ecosystem, pharma leaders must take a time bound and strategic approach. This includes initiating pilot projects in areas such as deviation management and predictive maintenance, digitising quality documentation before introducing AI layers, and establishing strong data governance and validation frameworks.
