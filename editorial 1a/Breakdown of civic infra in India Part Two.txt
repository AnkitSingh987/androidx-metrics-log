Title: Breakdown of civic infra in India Part Two

Breakdown of civic infra in India

In the U.S., established food and coffee chains have reported a slowdown in sales because of consumer boycott movements due to the political stance of these chains. If consumer boycott movements can lead to a slowdown of global conglomerates, then Indian consumers should also boycott establishments that have brazenly violated rules that have led to loss of life. However, for an ordinary citizen who may not remember the incident, lack of access to compliance information such as publicly displayed compliance certificates in establishments and a public registry of violation norms, makes it harder for them to make an informed choice.

Lack of access to this information is a governance failure, and access to it may be a step towards strengthening compliance with regulatory norms. During the COVID-19 pandemic, establishments across India were under strict operating guidelines. Any deviation resulted in their immediate closure. The same enforcement must continue to be applied; all buildings must be monitored to ensure compliance with guidelines and the fear of loosing their licence to operate must be real.

Prachi Dutta is a corporate lawyer licensed in New York and India, advises companies and funds, and serves as an Adjunct Professor at Shiv Nadar University
