Title: A race against time to embrace quality Part One

A race against time to embrace quality

Manufacturing success will depend on India's ability to embed itself in global value chains as a high-tech, highly productive, and trusted production hub India enters the second half of the 2020s with macroeconomic fundamentals that rank among the strongest in the world. Another year of real GDP growth close to 7% is within reach. Yet India's global trade footprint remains modest relative to its scale.

In 2024, it accounted for just 2.9% of global manufacturing gross value added and 1.8% of merchandise exports - signalling a vast opportunity to expand its manufacturing presence on the world stage. Achieving the vision of Viksit Bharat @2047 will require more than growth alone. As highlighted in the Economic Survey, India must steadily climb global value chains (GVCs).

The next phase of global manufacturing will be shaped less by cost arbitrage and more by "strategic indispensability". Success will depend on India's ability to embed itself in GVCs as a high-tech, highly productive, and trusted manufacturing hub. Quality as mechanism for GVC integration Participation in global value chains requires more than competitive pricing.

Integration into global production networks increasingly depends on process reliability, consistency, and institutional trust. In this context, quality must evolve from a functional goal to a cultural anchor - shaping decisions from the boardroom to the shop floor. It must become a personal value, not just an organisational requirement.

Studies suggest several core factors are essential to cultivating a world-class quality culture: Senior leadership commitment and governance. Top-down commitment is crucial, with senior leadership taking visible ownership and organisations demanding so from their top managers. Quality does not belong to the quality department because excellence is not about ticking boxes.

It is about meeting the highest standards that customers genuinely value and that only comes through cross functional cohesion anchored by top leadership. Japan offers a long-running example: for over five decades, its top corporate leaders have participated in executive symposia hosted by the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers (JUSE). Held twice a year over 2.5 days, each session brings together some 250 chairpersons and presidents from across Japanese industry.

Cumulatively, this has amounted to around 70,000 senior executive days dedicated to improving quality. These forums provide space for leaders to reflect on organisational successes and challenges, reinforcing quality as a strategic leadership agenda. Shared accountability across the value chain. In any supply chain, the weakest link determines the strength of the whole.

For India to build a globally trusted "Made in India" brand, micro, small, and medium enterprise (MSME) clusters must consistently match the quality performance of original equipment manufacturers. Isolated excellence is not enough. A seamless ecosystem - akin to Germany's Mittelstand - is required. Germany is one of the world's top exporters and the Mittelstand (the equivalent of our MSMEs) contributes 68% of it.

The German kind of success demands adoption of national quality principles, cluster-based scale strategies, ready access to assessment and coaching tools, and widespread engagement in initiatives like quality control circles that encourage small-group problem-solving beyond individual firms or divisions. Rigorous documentation and tracking of quality. To institutionalise quality, India must establish a distinctive yet globally benchmarked approach - an "Indian way" to quality excellence - led by the industry as a nation-first objective.

This includes developing robust certification and assessment frameworks for firms and individuals, designing progressive training for trainers, and investing in data integrity and process standardisation. Quality cannot be managed without measurement, and measurement demands clarity, comparability, and trust. Bridging the employability gap.
