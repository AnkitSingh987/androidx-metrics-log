Monetary policy and persisting supply shocks

Also, WPI is closely linked with GDP deflator (correlation over 0.80). It is likely the broad-based price pressures both in CPI and WPI will push up the GDP deflator substantially, resulting in subdued real GDP growth. Rate hike likely? First, with Fed keeping rates unchanged but divided house pledging to 'deliver price stability', rupee would get support from a higher interest rate.

From February 2025 till date, WPI and rupee show a correlation of 0.81: pass-through of supply side shock to domestic inflation needs to be contained. While rising crude prices almost invariably translate into rupee depreciation, periods of crude price correction do not necessarily halt rupee depreciation, as capital account outflows can outweigh the gains from an improving current account. Second, the high credit growth (with FCNRB leading to cheaper deposits), sharper increase in inflation, and persisting adverse supply shocks indicate playing with the traditional interest rate instrument.

As monetary policy is forward looking and there is a long lag in transmission, a 25-bps increase in repo is not a distant possibility.
