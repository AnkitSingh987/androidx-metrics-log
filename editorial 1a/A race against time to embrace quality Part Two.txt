Title: A race against time to embrace quality Part Two

A race against time to embrace quality

Perhaps the most foundational shift lies in workforce development. India must strengthen its academia-industry linkage to ensure graduates are industry-ready. There is a pressing need to cultivate a cadre of "master craftsmen" in electronics, mechanical trades, and manufacturing - with the recognition, rewards, and respect that such skills deserve.

Crucially, vocational pathways must be designed with upward mobility in mind. A shop-floor apprentice should be able to see a future that could lead halfway to a doctorate or a boardroom seat. Well-resourced quality institutes must be established to define and propagate holistic quality models, drawing inspiration from global benchmarks such as ASQ in the US, the Malcolm Baldrige Foundation, JUSE and JSQC in Japan, CAQ in China, and DIQ in Germany.

A sprint, not a marathon India does not have the luxury of time. Japan and South Korea spent decades building industrial scale and quality ecosystems. India's window is narrower - and closing fast. As product and technology life cycles shorten, the time required to master a sector is shrinking. India must harness its demographic dividend within the next five to 10 years or risk stagnating as a middle-income economy, ageing before it reaches developed status.

To win this global race, India must treat quality not as a compliance burden but as a strategic economic asset. It is time to sprint. SOUMITRA BHATTACHARYA CEO & Director, Indian Foundation for Quality Management, and Chairman, Bosch Limited
