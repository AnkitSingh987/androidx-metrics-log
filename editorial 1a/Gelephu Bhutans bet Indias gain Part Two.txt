Title: Gelephu Bhutans bet Indias gain Part Two

Gelephu: Bhutan's bet, India's gain

Bhutan also hopes that the economic growth which will come with the project will create job opportunities for its educated youth, who have been exposed to the outside world via social media, and arrest their migration to other countries. Educated Bhutanese youth and skilled professionals have been migrating abroad in huge numbers, with Australia being a favoured destination. Many have also moved to the Middle East.

For a country with a small population, this large-scale migration threatens long-term demographic stability. For Bhutan, the project is a huge gamble. If it takes off, the project can be a catalyst for its economic growth, trade and physical connectivity not just with India but even beyond to the Southeast Asian region.

It needs India and other countries to help develop it and invest in it. To encourage private businesses to be part of the project, Bhutan has chosen to use Singapore's laws for corporate and commercial matters and Abu Dhabi Global Market regulations for financial services in GMC. Reflecting this broader economic opening, Bhutan also resumed negotiations in July this year to join the World Trade Organisation (WTO) after an 18-year hiatus.

With connectivity being important, an international airport is also on the anvil in Gelephu, which will be Bhutan's second such airport after the one in Paro. Simultaneously, India has also already promised assistance to Bhutan for two vital rail links. One will be the 69-km rail link from Gelephu to Kokrajhar in Assam.

This is expected to help Bhutan access Indian ports. Signalling its strong backing for the Gelephu project, India has declared this link a special railway project. The other 17-km rail link will be between Samtse in Bhutan, which is being developed as an industrial hub, and Banarhat in West Bengal. Ultimately, the GMC project represents a transformative convergence of Bhutan's socio-economic aspirations and India's core strategic imperatives.

For Bhutan, it offers a vital path to stem youth migration, modernise its economy, and integrate into global supply chains without compromising its distinct cultural identity. For India, it secures a crucial buffer against growing Chinese influence along the Himalayan frontier while cementing a high-stakes geopolitical alliance rooted in shared growth and regional stability. By Parul Chandra
