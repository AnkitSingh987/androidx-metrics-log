Title: Another way to see the world Part Three

Another way to see the world

Representation shapes perception, and perception shapes the way people understand their place in the world. The UN resolution therefore should not be interpreted as the death of the Mercator projection. It is better understood as a challenge to its universal dominance. This is a modest technical change on paper, but it carries a larger symbolic message.

The world of the twenty-first century is no longer the world of sixteenth-century ships. Satellites can photograph the Earth, computers can model it in three dimensions and digital platforms can switch between projections instantly. There is little reason to pretend that a centuries-old projection must serve every modern purpose.

For the Global South, this is ultimately about more than cartography. It is about visibility. It is about correcting a visual imbalance that has been repeated so often that it came to look natural. The UN vote gives that argument an international platform. By Santhosh Mathew (The writer is Professor, Centre for South Asian Studies, School of International Studies & Social Sciences, Pondicherry Central University)
