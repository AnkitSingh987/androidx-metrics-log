Title: Make next great digital leap sustainable Part Two

Make next great digital leap sustainable

Data centres do consume large amounts of electricity for powering and water for cooling. India is blessed with intense solar radiation and expansive wind corridors. By linking data centre parks directly to utility-scale renewable plants through green energy power purchase agreements and grid banking, such facilities can operate on 100% clean power.

Instead of draining precious inland freshwater tables, coastal data hubs can integrate seawater desalination plants and closed-loop liquid cooling. Another sustainable approach would involve municipalities developing industrial pipelines to convey treated wastewater from sewage treatment plants directly to data centre clusters. Utilising reclaimed wastewater for cooling reduces dependence on stressed groundwater aquifers and potable municipal water supplies, while enabling data centres to advance water stewardship objectives, comply with zero liquid discharge requirements where applicable, and strengthen their ESG performance.

Advanced liquid cooling systems can capture server-generated waste heat and redirect it to industrial applications or agricultural processing, minimising wasted energy and driving down power usage effectiveness metrics. To truly support 1.4 billion citizens who have collectively saved billions of tons of paper through digital identity (Aadhaar), electronic tax filings, and paperless ticketing, India urgently needs an expansive underwater network of submarine fiber-optic cables running along the ocean floor. Expanding landing stations beyond Mumbai and Chennai into cities like Visakhapatnam, Kochi, and Tuticorin creates vital network redundancy, shields from single-point network failures, and ensures ultra-low latency for users in tier-II and -III cities.

India's transition to a fully digital, AI-driven economy is irreversible. By pairing data centre expansion with aggressive renewable energy and sustainable methods, it can achieve a double victory setting a net-zero standard for the world. Anchoring hyperscale green data centres on domestic soil will unlock a wave of FDI, transform India into a resilient digital infrastructure exporter, and create high-skilled technology jobs across regions.

Pioneering a zero-carbon, seawater-cooled compute architecture positions India as a global blueprint for sustainable technology, directly advancing the nation's 2070 net-zero targets. Written by Rameesh Kailasam
