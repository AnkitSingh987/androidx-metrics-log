Title: The New Neighbor
Category: Beginner

It was a quiet Tuesday morning in the suburbs. Twelve-year-old Lily was sitting on her front porch, reading a library book. The sun was shining, and the street was very peaceful. Most of the adults were at work, and the other children were inside watching television. 

Suddenly, a loud, rumbling noise broke the silence. Lily looked up from her book. 
A massive white truck was driving very slowly down the street. It was much bigger than a normal delivery van. It had large, bold blue letters on the side that read: 'National Moving Company'. 

Lily closed her book and stood up. She was very curious. The house directly next door to hers had been empty for three months. The previous owners, an older couple, had moved away to Florida to live near the beach. Ever since they left, the house had felt a little lonely, with a 'For Sale' sign standing in the front yard. 

The large white truck slowly backed into the driveway of the empty house. The engine roared loudly before the driver finally turned it off. 
Lily watched eagerly from her porch. 
Two strong men wearing blue uniforms jumped out of the front cabin of the truck. They walked to the back, unhooked a heavy metal latch, and rolled up the large back door. Inside, the truck was completely packed with boxes, mattresses, and heavy wooden furniture wrapped in thick moving blankets. 

"Mom!" Lily called out, turning towards the open front door of her own house. "The new neighbors are here! The moving truck just arrived!"
Her mother walked out of the house, drying her hands on a kitchen towel. She stood next to Lily and looked across the small patch of green grass that separated the two houses. 

A silver car pulled up and parked on the street behind the moving truck. The doors opened. A tall woman with short brown hair stepped out of the driver's side. From the passenger side, a young boy stepped out. He looked exactly the same age as Lily. He was wearing jeans, a green t-shirt, and carrying a small, dark blue backpack over one shoulder. 

"Well, that is exciting," her mother smiled, leaning against the wooden porch railing. "It looks like a mother and her son. It will be very nice to have people living in that house again. The street has been too quiet lately."

Lily agreed. She watched the boy walk up the driveway, looking around the neighborhood. He looked a little bit nervous. Moving to a new house in a new city was a very big change. You had to leave your old friends, your old school, and your old bedroom behind. 

"I wonder what his name is," Lily thought to herself. "I wonder if he likes reading books or playing sports."

The two men in blue uniforms began to unload the heavy furniture. They carried a large grey sofa out of the truck and walked carefully towards the front door. The woman unlocked the door and held it open for them. 
"Put the sofa in the living room on the left, please," she instructed them clearly. 

"They have a lot of work to do today," Lily's mother observed, watching the men carry a heavy wooden dining table. "Moving is a very stressful and exhausting process. I remember when we moved into this house ten years ago. It took us a whole week to unpack everything."

Lily looked at the young boy again. He was standing on the lawn, watching the men carry his life in cardboard boxes. 
"We should go over and introduce ourselves," Lily suggested. "It is the polite thing to do, right?"
"Yes, it is," her mother agreed. "But not right now. They are very busy directing the moving men. We will give them a little time to settle in, and then we will go over and give them a proper neighborhood welcome."

Lily nodded and sat back down on her porch chair, but she did not open her book again. She was too interested in watching the busy scene next door. Having a new neighbor was the most exciting thing that had happened on their street all summer.
