Category: Beginner
Title: Ben and the Toys

Ben likes to store his toys in his desk. He has many toys: cars, trains, blocks, and books. He likes to play with them and make noises. Sometimes he shares his toys with his friends, but not always. One day, a new boy came to his class. His name was Sam. Sam was bald. He had no hair on his head. Ben was curious. He wanted to touch Sam's head and see how it felt. He thought it might be soft or smooth or cold. But Sam did not like it when Ben touched his head. He said, "Stop it! That hurts!" He pushed Ben away and ran to the teacher. The teacher was angry. She said, "Ben, that was not nice. You should not touch other people without asking. How would you feel if someone touched your toys without asking?" Ben felt sorry. He did not mean to hurt Sam. He just wanted to be friends. He said, "I'm sorry, Sam. I won't touch your head again. Do you want to play with my toys? I have many toys in my desk. You can choose any toy you like." Sam smiled. He said, "Thank you, Ben. That is very kind of you. I would like to play with your toys. Can I see your trains? I like trains. They are fast and loud." Ben nodded. He said, "Sure, you can see my trains. They are my favorite toys. Come, I will show you." He took Sam by the hand and led him to his desk. He opened the drawer and took out his trains. He said, "Look, this is my red train. It is the fastest. And this is my blue train. It is the loudest. And this is my green train. It is the longest. Which one do you like?" Sam looked at the trains. He said, "They are all very nice. But I like the green train the best. It is the longest. Can I play with it?" Ben smiled. He said, "Yes, you can play with it. Here, I will give it to you. And I will play with the red train. We can race them on the floor. Are you ready?" Sam nodded. He said, "Yes, I'm ready. Let's go!" He held the green train in his hand and followed Ben to the floor. They put their trains on the ground and made them go. They had fun. They laughed and cheered. They became friends.

Once upon a time, there was a little chair. The chair was sad because everyone said it was rude. The chair did not want to be rude. It wanted to be nice and make friends. One day, a little boy came to sit on the chair. The chair decided to delay being rude. Instead, it was very nice to the boy. The boy liked the chair and they became friends. The chair learned that being nice was better than being rude. It made more friends and was happy. Everyone loved the chair and it was never rude again.

Once upon a time there was a little bunny that liked to hop. The bunny's name was Toby. One day as Toby hopped through the forest, he smelt something delicious. The smell was coming from a pie in a clearing. Toby hop-skipped over to take a closer look. When he arrived, he noticed a kind old lady sitting beside the pie. "Would you like a slice of this pie, Toby?" she asked. Toby nodded eagerly and the old lady carefully cut a slice of the pie then handed it to Toby. "This pie is delicious!" said Toby as he munched away. The old lady smiled and said, "I'm glad you like it. It's very kind of me to give it to you". Then the old lady picked up her basket and hopped away. Toby was so happy that he started to hop around. He knew he'd remember this wonderful pie for a long time to come.

One day, a funny dog named Spot went for a jog in the park. He saw a little girl named Amy. Amy had a cup with her. Spot was curious about the cup and wanted to see what was inside. Spot jogged to Amy and said, "Hi! I am Spot. What is in your cup?" Amy smiled and said, "Hi Spot! I have some water in my cup for my plant. I want to make it grow big and strong." Spot and Amy became friends. They played in the park and had a fun time. Then, Spot remembered the cup. He said, "Amy, let's go give the water to your plant!" They jogged to Amy's house and found the plant. Amy poured the water from the cup onto the plant. The next day, Spot and Amy went to the park again. They saw that the plant had grown very big and strong, just like Amy wanted. They were both very happy and knew that their friendship had helped the plant grow.
