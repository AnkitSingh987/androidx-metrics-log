Category: Beginner
Title: The Story of the Box and the Brother

Once upon a time, there was a family with two children. One day, their dad came home from work carrying a big box. "What's in the box, Daddy?" asked the younger brother. "It's an oxygen machine," said their Dad. The kids were excited and the older brother said "What does it do?" "It becomes loud when it pumps oxygen around the room," said their Dad. But the younger brother wasn't so sure. He didn't like loud noises and he started to cry. "It'll be okay," said their Dad, trying to soothe him. "You won't even notice it's there." But the younger brother wouldn't be consoled. He didn't want the oxygen machine in his house. The next day, their Dad gave the younger brother an idea. He said to the brother, "What if we put the machine in the backyard, away from your bedroom? Would that help?" The younger brother thought about it for a minute and then he smiled. "Yes! That would be better," he said. So, with the help of their dad, the siblings put the oxygen machine in the backyard and it stayed there from then on.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play with chalk. He would draw on the ground with his chalk every day. One day, Tim saw a big, messy spot on the ground. He wanted to make it pretty with his chalk. Tim started to draw on the messy spot, but his friend, Tom, came by. Tom wanted to play with Tim. He said, "Let's throw the chalk!" Tim didn't want to throw his chalk. He wanted to draw. But Tom kept asking him to throw the chalk. Tim got mad and said, "No, I don't want to throw my chalk!" Tom got mad too. They both didn't know what to do. Then, Tim had an idea. "Let's draw together!" he said. Tom liked the idea. They both drew on the messy spot with the chalk. Soon, the messy spot was full of pretty drawings. Tim and Tom were happy and played together every day.

Lucy liked to travel with her mom and dad. They went to many places and saw many things. Lucy liked to collect pebbles from different places. She had a big jar of pebbles in her room. One day, they went to the beach. Lucy saw many pebbles on the sand. She wanted to pick them up and put them in her pocket. She told her mom and dad, "I want to get pebbles. I will be back soon." Her mom and dad said, "OK, but be careful. Don't go too far. Stay where we can see you." Lucy said, "OK, I will." But she did not listen. She was very independent. She wanted to explore. She walked and walked along the beach. She found many pebbles. She filled her pocket with pebbles. She did not notice that she was far from her mom and dad. She did not notice that the sun was going down. She did not notice that the tide was coming in. She did not notice that she was lost. She looked around and saw that she was alone. She did not see her mom and dad. She did not see any people. She only saw water and sand and rocks. She was scared. She cried, "Mom! Dad! Where are you?" She heard a voice. It was her mom. She said, "Lucy, where are you? We are looking for you. We are worried about you." Lucy said, "Mom, I'm here. I'm sorry. I went too far. I wanted to get pebbles. Please find me." Her mom said, "Don't worry, Lucy. We are coming. Stay where you are. We love you." Lucy waited and waited. She saw a light. It was her dad. He had a flashlight. He found her and hugged her. He said, "Lucy, we are so glad you are safe. You scared us. Why did you go so far?" Lucy said, "I'm sorry, dad. I was independent. I wanted to get pebbles. But I learned my lesson. I will not go far again. I will listen to you and mom." Her dad said, "That's good, Lucy. We are proud of you for being independent. But you also need to be careful and responsible. You need to follow the rules and stay close to us. We are here to protect you and help you. We are a team." Lucy said, "I understand, dad. I love you and mom. Thank you for finding me." They walked back to their car. Lucy gave her mom and dad some of her pebbles. She said, "These are for you. They are from the beach. They are beautiful and special. Just like you." Her mom and dad smiled and thanked her. They said, "We love you, Lucy. You are beautiful and special. Just like your pebbles." The end.
