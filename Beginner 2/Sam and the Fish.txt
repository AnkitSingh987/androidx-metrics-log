Category: Beginner
Title: Sam and the Fish

Sam is a frog who likes to hop. He hops on the grass, on the rocks, on the logs. He hops high and low, fast and slow. He is happy when he hops. One day, he sees a big rod by the pond. He thinks it is a toy. He hops to the rod and tries to play with it. He pulls it, he pushes it, he bites it. But the rod does not move. It is stuck in the mud. Sam is mad. He wants the rod to hop with him. He hops harder and harder, but the rod does not budge. He hops so hard that he falls into the pond. He is wet and cold and sad. Then he hears a splash. He looks up and sees a fish. The fish has the rod in its mouth. The fish is happy. It swims with the rod, it flips with the rod, it shakes with the rod. The rod is not a toy. It is a fish's food. Sam is sorry. He did not know the rod was for the fish. He hops out of the pond and says sorry to the fish. The fish says it is okay. The fish says it has enough food. The fish says Sam can hop with it if he wants. Sam is glad. He hops with the fish, he swims with the fish, he plays with the fish. He is happy when he hops. He is happy when he has a friend.

Once upon a time, in a big forest, there was a big oak tree. The oak tree was very old and bald. Many little animals lived near the oak tree, and they liked to play under it. One sunny day, a group of little animals decided to play a game. They wanted to count how many leaves were left on the bald oak tree. They took turns counting the leaves. The animals had fun and learned to count together. As they counted the leaves, the little animals also learned to help each other. If one of them had trouble counting, the others would help. Soon, all the little animals could count the leaves on the bald oak tree. They were very happy and proud of themselves. The moral of the story is that we can learn more and have fun when we help each other.

Sara loves to feed the ducks at the pond. She brings bread and corn and throws them in the water. The ducks swim and quack and eat the food. Sara likes to watch them and count them. She knows all their names. One day, Sara sees a big duck with a red hat and a shiny medal. He looks different from the other ducks. He walks in front of them and makes loud noises. The other ducks follow him and make noises too. Sara thinks they are having a march. Sara asks her mom who the big duck is. Her mom says he is a famous duck. He is the leader of the ducks. He helps them find food and water and keeps them safe. He is brave and smart and kind. He won the medal for saving a baby duck from a fox. Sara thinks the famous duck is very cool. She wants to feed him some bread and corn. She calls his name and waves her hand. The famous duck sees her and swims to the shore. He takes the food from her and thanks her. He says she is a nice girl and a good friend. He gives her a feather as a gift. Sara is very happy. She puts the feather in her hair and smiles. She says goodbye to the famous duck and the other ducks. She tells her mom she had a lot of fun. She says she wants to come back and feed the ducks again. Her mom says they can do that tomorrow. Sara hugs her mom and says she loves her.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim had a toy rod that he loved to play with. He would use the rod to catch pretend fish in his yard. One day, the rod broke and Tim was very sad. Tim's mom saw him crying and asked, "Why are you sad, Tim?" Tim showed her the broken rod and said, "My rod is broken, and I can't play anymore." His mom hugged him and said, "Don't worry, we will replace it with a new one." The next day, Tim and his mom went to the toy store. They found a new rod that was mild and easy for Tim to use. Tim was very happy and said, "Thank you, Mom!" They went back home, and Tim played with his new rod all day long, catching lots of pretend fish. And they all lived happily ever after.
