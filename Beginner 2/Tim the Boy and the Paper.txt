Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Boy and the Paper

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim had a toy gun that could shoot paper. He liked to play with it all day. One day, Tim saw a weak tree in his yard. He thought it would be fun to shoot the tree with his paper gun. Tim went to his room and got his paper gun. He put a lot of paper in it. He wanted to see if the weak tree would fall down when he shot it. Tim went outside and aimed at the tree. He was very excited. Tim shot the paper at the weak tree. The tree started to shake and then it fell down. But the tree fell on Tim's house! Tim's mom was very mad. She said Tim could not play with his paper gun anymore. Tim was very sad.

One day, a boy named Tim went to the park with his mom. They saw a big tree with a lot of leaves. Tim saw an impressive bird on a branch. The bird was very pretty and had many colors. Tim said, "Mom, look at the bird! It is so pretty!" His mom looked up and said, "Yes, it is very impressive!" Tim wanted to play with the bird, so he tried to strike the tree with a stick. The bird flew away and landed on a table. On the table, there was a big bowl of salad. The bird started to eat the salad. Tim and his mom laughed as they watched the bird eat.

Once upon a time, there was a jolly man named Tom. Tom had a big box with gold in it. One day, he met a little girl named Sue. Sue saw the gold and smiled. She asked Tom, "Can I see your gold?" Tom frowned and said, "No, this gold is mine." Sue was sad but walked away. As Tom walked with his gold, he met a big, scary dog. The dog growled at Tom and wanted the gold. Tom was scared and didn't know what to do. Sue saw Tom and the dog. She had an idea. She picked up a stick and threw it far away. The dog ran after the stick and left Tom alone. Tom was happy and thankful. He looked at Sue and said, "You helped me. Now, I want to share my gold with you." Sue smiled, and they became good friends. They shared the gold and played together every day.

Once upon a time, in a cute little house, there lived a nice girl named Amy. She had a small toy bunny that she loved very much. One day, Amy felt the urge to play with her friend, Timmy. Amy went to Timmy's house and asked, "Can I borrow your toy car, please?" Timmy smiled and said, "Yes, you can!" They played together all day, sharing their toys and having lots of fun. As the sun went down, Amy said, "Thank you for letting me borrow your toy car. I had a great time!" Timmy replied, "You're welcome! Let's play again soon." They hugged and Amy went back to her cute little house with her toy bunny, feeling very happy.

Ben and Mia were playing with their toy cars in the yard. They liked to make roads and bridges with sticks and stones. They also had a big cardboard box that they used as a shelter for their cars. "Look, I can load four cars in my truck!" Ben said, showing Mia his toy. "Can you do that?" Mia tried to load her cars in her truck, but she could only fit three. She felt sad and angry. She wanted to have a bigger truck like Ben. "No fair! Your truck is bigger than mine!" Mia said, pushing Ben's truck away. "Give me your truck!" Ben did not like that. He pushed Mia's truck away too. "No! It's mine! You have your own truck!" They started to fight over the trucks. They pulled and pushed and shouted. They did not see that their shelter was falling down. The cardboard box collapsed on their cars and trucks. They heard a loud crash. They stopped fighting and looked at their shelter. It was broken and dull. Their cars and trucks were buried under the cardboard. They felt sorry and scared. "Oh no! What did we do?" Ben said, tears in his eyes. "We broke our shelter and our cars!" Mia also cried. "I'm sorry, Ben. I should not have pushed your truck. Can we be friends again?" Ben nodded. "I'm sorry, too. I should not have pushed your truck either. Yes, we can be friends again." They hugged and said sorry. Then they worked together to lift the cardboard and find their cars and trucks. They were happy to see that they were not broken. They fixed their shelter and put their cars and trucks inside. They learned that fighting over toys was not good. They learned that sharing and helping was better. They learned that being friends was more fun than being enemies.
