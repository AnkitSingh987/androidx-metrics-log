Category: Beginner
Title: Oh and the Whistle

"Oh no!" said the chubby little boy. He had just dropped his shield on the ground and it had smashed into pieces. He had been so excited to play with it and now it was broken. Suddenly, he heard a whistle from above. He looked up and saw a big bird flying circles in the sky. He watched it and wished he could be up there too, flying and having fun without any worries. The chubby little boy decided to go find some twigs and stones to repair his shield. He hoped he would be able to fix it. But when the little boy returned with the pieces, he found out he had been too late. A big gust of wind had blown away the pieces of the shield and he had lost them forever. The chubby little boy sadly looked up at the bird, still whistling in the sky, and realized he should have acted faster. He should have taken action as soon as he had heard the bird's whistle, instead of watching in awe. The moral of this story is to always take action when you hear the whistle of a signal, whether it’s literally a whistle or a call to action. Don’t wait until it’s too late!

Lily and Ben are twins. They like to play with toys. One day, they go to the toy shop with Mom. They see many toys. They see dolls, cars, balls, and books. They also see a big white skeleton. It has bones and teeth and eyes. "Wow, look at that!" Lily says. "It is scary and cool. Can we buy it, Mom?" Mom shakes her head. "No, Lily, we cannot buy it. It is too big and too expensive. And it is not a toy. It is for learning. It shows how our body is inside." "But I want to learn, Mom. I want to know how my body is inside. Please, Mom, please!" Lily begs. Ben looks at the skeleton too. He thinks it is scary and cool too. But he does not want to buy it. He wants to buy something else. He sees a red car. It has wheels and doors and lights. It can go fast and make noises. "I want this, Mom. I want the red car. It is fun and shiny. Can we buy it, Mom?" Ben asks. Mom smiles. "Yes, Ben, we can buy it. It is not too big and not too expensive. And it is a toy. It is for playing. You can drive it and make noises." Ben is happy. He hugs the red car. Lily is sad. She does not hug the skeleton. She cries. Mom sees Lily crying. She feels sorry for her. She thinks of something. She takes out her phone. She takes a picture of the skeleton. She shows it to Lily. "Look, Lily, this is the skeleton. You can have it on your phone. You can look at it and learn. You can zoom in and zoom out. You can see the bones and teeth and eyes. Do you like it, Lily?" Mom says. Lily stops crying. She looks at the phone. She sees the skeleton. She smiles. "I like it, Mom. Thank you, Mom. It is scary and cool. And I can have it with me. Can I show it to Ben?" Lily says. Mom nods. "Yes, Lily, you can show it to Ben. And you can show it to Dad and Grandma and your friends. They will like it too. They will think you are smart and brave." Lily is happy. She hugs Mom. She hugs the phone. She runs to Ben. She shows him the skeleton. Ben is surprised. He thinks it is scary and cool too. He shows her the red car. Lily thinks it is fun and shiny too. They share their toys. They play together. They are happy. Mom is happy too. She pays for the red car. She takes them home. They have a good day.

Once upon a time, there was a thoughtful boy named Tim. He loved to help his mom and dad at their restaurant. One day, he made a promise to his mom that he would clean the tables. But Tim's little sister, Sue, wanted to play with him. She kept asking him to play with her dolls. Tim didn't want to break his promise to his mom, but he also didn't want to make Sue sad. So, Tim thought of a good idea. He asked Sue to help him clean the tables. Then, they could play together. Sue agreed and they cleaned the tables very fast. After that, they played with the dolls and had lots of fun. In the end, Tim kept his promise and made Sue happy too.
