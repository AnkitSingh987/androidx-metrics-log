Category: Beginner
Title: Sue the Girl and the Apple

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue was very hungry. She went to the kitchen to find some food. She saw a big bag with a zipper on it. Sue thought there might be food inside. She opened the zipper and found a big red apple. Sue was happy and took a bite of the apple. But the apple did not taste good. It tasted like a sock! Sue made a funny face and spit it out. She looked at the apple and saw a little bug. The bug was laughing. "I made the apple taste like a sock!" said the bug. Sue was surprised but she laughed too. Sue and the bug became friends. They played and had fun. The bug said, "I will make the apple taste good now." Sue took another bite of the apple. It tasted sweet and yummy. Sue was not hungry anymore. She and the bug played more games and had a great day together.

Lily and Ben were twins who liked to play with their toys. One day, they found a new toy in their room. It was a fragile glass bird that sparkled in the sun. Lily and Ben wanted to touch it and see how it felt. "Let me have it first!" Lily said. She tried to grab the bird from the shelf. "No, it's mine!" Ben said. He pushed Lily away and reached for the bird. They both pulled and tugged at the bird, but it was too fragile. It slipped from their hands and fell to the floor. It broke into many pieces. Lily and Ben gasped and cried. Mom heard the noise and came to see what happened. She saw the broken bird and the sad twins. She was angry and sad too. "Who did this?" she asked. "This was a special gift from Grandma. She gave it to me when I was a little girl. It was very precious and fragile. You should not have touched it without asking me." Lily and Ben felt sorry and ashamed. They did not know it was Mom's bird. They did not mean to break it. "We're sorry, Mom," they said. "We wanted to play with it. We did not know it was yours. We did not know it was fragile." Mom sighed and hugged them. She knew they were sorry, but she was still sad. "I forgive you, but you have to learn to be careful with other people's things. And you have to learn to share and take turns. You can't always have what you want. Sometimes you have to wait and be patient. Do you understand?" Lily and Ben nodded. They understood. They said they would try to be more careful and more kind. Mom smiled and kissed them. She said she loved them. She said they could help her make a meal for dinner. They could make Grandma's favorite soup and bread. They could also make a card for Grandma and say sorry for breaking her bird. Lily and Ben agreed. They felt a little better. They went to the kitchen with Mom and helped her raise the pot and stir the soup. They learned how to knead the dough and shape the bread. They also drew a picture of a bird and wrote "We love you Grandma" on the card. They hoped Grandma would like their meal and their card. They hoped she would forgive them too. They learned that fragile things can break, but love can heal.

One day, a little boy named Tim found a black sock. He was very happy because he lost one just like it. He wanted to wear the sock on his foot. So, he put the sock on his foot, but it was too small. Tim tried to pull it off, but it was stuck. He pulled and pulled, and the sock began to tear. Tim was sad because his black sock was tearing. He did not want the sock to have a hole. Then, something unexpected happened. As the sock tore, a little black kitten came out of the hole. Tim was so surprised! The little black kitten looked at Tim and meowed. Tim was not sad anymore. He was very happy to have a new friend. The torn sock did not matter now. Tim and the little black kitten played all day. They had lots of fun together, and Tim forgot all about the sock on his foot.
