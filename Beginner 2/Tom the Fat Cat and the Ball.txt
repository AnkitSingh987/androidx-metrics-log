Category: Beginner
Title: Tom the Fat Cat and the Ball

Once upon a time, there was a fat cat named Tom. Tom loved to play with his toy, a small glass ball. One day, Tom was playing with his glass ball in the yard. He saw a big tree with a high branch. Tom had an idea. He wanted to raise his glass ball high up in the tree. Tom climbed the tree with his glass ball in his mouth. He put the glass ball on the high branch. Tom was happy. He climbed down the tree and looked up at his glass ball. It was shining in the sun. But then, a strong wind blew. The glass ball fell down and broke into pieces. Tom was sad. He learned that it is not always a good idea to raise things high up in the tree. From that day on, Tom played with his toys on the ground.

Once upon a time, there was a friendly cat named Whiskers. Whiskers loved to walk around his big yard. One day, while walking, he met a little bird. The bird was blue and had a pretty song. Whiskers and the bird became good friends. One day, Whiskers said to the bird, "I had a fun dream last night. In my dream, we could both fly high in the sky together. We saw big trees and pretty flowers from up high." The bird smiled and said, "That sounds like a great dream, Whiskers!" Whiskers and the bird would walk and play together every day. They would talk about their dreams and all the fun things they wanted to do. They were the best of friends and loved to spend time with each other. And every night, Whiskers would dream of more fun adventures with his friendly bird friend.

There was a silly little girl who decided to pull a metal wagon. She was so excited to have a wagon of her own! She pulled it around the backyard for hours. She went up and down the driveway and even dared to go around some of the tight corners. When she got tired of pulling the wagon, she decided to fill it with silly things like rocks, leaves and twigs. She developed a route and was able to make it all the way around the backyard carrying her silly cargo. She had so much fun that day!

Once upon a time, there was a strong dog named True. True was very big and loved to play with his friends. They liked to run, jump, and fetch sticks in the park. One day, True could not remember where he left his favorite toy. He looked in the yard, under the bed, and behind the couch. True was sad because he could not find it anywhere. Then, True's friend came over to play. They looked for the toy together and found it under a bush in the yard. True was so happy and thanked his friend for helping him remember where it was. They played together all day, and True never forgot how strong their friendship was.

Tommy woke up in the morning with a big smile on his face. He remembered that today his mom promised to take him to the zoo. He quickly put on his clothes and went to the kitchen. His mom had already prepared breakfast and was warming it up in the pan. Mom: “Would you like some toast, Tommy?” Tommy: “Yes, please!” Mom proceeded to feed Tommy with a big smile. She couldn't wait to surprise him with the special place they were going to visit. Mom: “Let's go to the zoo, Tommy.” Tommy: “Yay! I'm so excited!” Tommy and his mom hopped in the car and drove off toward the zoo. The animals were so impressive that Tommy couldn't take his eyes off them. The monkeys were swinging from their tails and the giraffes were eating from tall trees. Tommy: “Mommy, this is the best morning ever!” Mom: “I'm so happy you're enjoying it!” Tommy and his mom walked around the zoo and fed the animals with special food. It was the most amazing experience!

Lily and Ben are friends. They like to play with toys and books. Sometimes they play with a football. They kick it and throw it and catch it. One day, Lily and Ben go to the park with their moms. They see a big slide and a swing and a sandbox. They want to play with everything. They run to the slide and take turns. They push each other on the swing. They make castles and tunnels in the sandbox. But then, Ben sees a boy with a football. He wants to play with it. He runs to the boy and asks, "Can I play with your football?" The boy says, "No, this is my football. Go away." Ben is sad. He says, "Please, can I play with your football? Just for a little bit?" The boy says, "No, I said go away. This is my football. You have your own football." Ben looks at his football. It is old and dirty and has a hole in it. He does not like his football. He likes the boy's football. It is new and shiny and has no hole in it. He says, "But your football is better. I want to play with your football." The boy says, "No, no, no. You can't play with my football. It is mine. Go away or I will tell my mom." Ben is angry. He does not want to go away. He wants to play with the football. He tries to grab the football from the boy. The boy pulls the football away. They start to fight. Lily sees Ben and the boy fighting. She does not like fighting. She runs to them and says, "Stop fighting. Fighting is bad. You can share the football. Sharing is good." The boy says, "No, I don't want to share. This is my football. He can't have it." Ben says, "Yes, I can. I want to play with it. It is not fair." Lily says, "Yes, it is fair. You have your own football. You can play with your football. He can play with his football. Or you can play together. You can take turns. You can be friends." The boy says, "No, I don't want to be friends. I don't like him. He is mean. He tried to take my football." Ben says, "No, you are mean. You are selfish. You don't share your football." Lily says, "Please, stop. You are both mean. You are both selfish. You don't listen. You don't care. You make me sad." Lily starts to cry. She does not like to cry. She does not like to be sad. She likes to be happy. She likes to play. The boy and Ben see Lily crying. They feel bad. They do not want to make Lily cry. They do not want to make Lily sad. They want to make Lily happy. They want to play with Lily. The boy says, "I'm sorry. I was mean. I was selfish. I should share my football. You can play with my football. Do you want to play with my football?" Ben says, "I'm sorry. I was mean. I was selfish. I should not take your football. You can have your football. Do you want to play with your football?" Lily says, "Thank you. You are nice. You are kind. You should share your football. You should not take your football. You should play with your football. Do you want to play with me?" The boy and Ben say, "Yes, we want to play with you. You are our friend. You are patient. You are smart. You are good. You make us happy. You make us play." Lily smiles. She stops crying. She is happy. She is happy to play with her friends. She is happy to play with the football. She kicks it and throws it and catches it. The boy and Ben do the same. They play with the football. They play with Lily. They have fun. They are happy.
