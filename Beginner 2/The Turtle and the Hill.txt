Category: Beginner
Title: The Turtle and the Hill

Once upon a time, there was a small turtle. He loved exploring his yard and finding bugs to eat. One day, he decided to crawl to the top of the hill near his home. As he made his way up the hill, he saw a shiny medal on the ground. The medal was so bright and shiny that he wanted to take it. But he stopped and thought, "If I take this medal, someone else might want it." So the turtle decided to be open and leave the shiny medal where it was. At the top of the hill, he found the most beautiful flowers. He admired their beauty and decided to pick some to take home. As soon as he grabbed the flowers, he heard a big "Thank You!" He looked around and couldn't find anyone. He was so confused until he felt something soft on his back. It was the medal! The medal had thanked him for being open and generous so it could be there for someone else to find. From that day on, the turtle taught others the moral value of being open and generous and never taking what isn't yours.

Tom and Lily were playing in the garden. They had a big red ball that they liked to kick and throw. They also liked to look at the flowers, especially the poppies. The poppies were bright and pretty, and they had soft petals and black seeds. One day, Tom kicked the ball too hard and it flew over the fence. Lily ran after it, but she could not see it. She heard a loud pop and a hiss. She found the ball in the neighbor's yard, but it had a hole in it. It was flat and sad. "Oh no, Tom! The ball is broken!" Lily cried. She picked up the ball and ran back to the fence. Tom was waiting for her. He saw the ball and felt sorry. "I'm sorry, Lily. I didn't mean to break the ball. Can we repair it?" Tom asked. Lily shook her head. "I don't know how to repair it. It has a deep hole. Maybe we need a patch or some glue. But we don't have those things." Tom thought for a moment. He had an idea. He ran to the flower bed and picked some poppies. He brought them to Lily and said, "Look, Lily. We can use these poppies to repair the ball. They are red like the ball, and they have sticky juice. Maybe they can cover the hole and make the ball happy again." Lily smiled. She liked Tom's idea. She took the poppies and pressed them on the hole. The juice came out and stuck to the ball. The poppies made the ball look colorful and cheerful. "Wow, Tom! You are smart! The ball looks better now. Thank you for repairing it!" Lily said. Tom hugged Lily. "You're welcome, Lily. I'm glad you like it. Let's play with the ball again. But this time, let's be careful not to kick it too hard." Lily nodded. She threw the ball to Tom and they played happily in the garden. They didn't mind that the ball had poppies on it. They thought it made the ball special and fun.

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim had a violin. He loved to play it every day. One day, Tim's violin string broke. He was sad. Tim tried to tie the string back together. He tried and tried, but it was hard. Tim did not give up. He was very persistent. He kept trying to tie the string. Finally, Tim tied the string and fixed his violin. He was happy. Tim played his violin again. The music was beautiful. The moral of the story is to never give up and keep trying.

Ben and Mia liked to play chess. They had a big board with black and white pieces. They learned how to move them from their dad. He said chess was a smart game that made you think. One day, Ben and Mia were playing chess in their room. They were having fun and trying to catch each other's king. But then they heard music from the living room. It was loud and happy. They looked at each other and smiled. "Let's go dance!" Mia said. She jumped up and ran to the door. Ben followed her. They left their chess board on the floor. In the living room, they saw their mom and dad dancing. They were holding hands and spinning around. They looked happy and silly. Ben and Mia joined them. They danced and laughed and hugged. But then they heard a crash from their room. They stopped dancing and ran back. They saw their chess board on the floor. The pieces were scattered and broken. Their cat, Luna, was sitting on the board. She looked at them with her pale green eyes. She had knocked over the board with her paw. "Bad cat!" Ben and Mia shouted. They were sad and angry. They loved their chess game. They tried to pick up the pieces, but some were too small or too sharp. Their mom and dad came to their room. They saw what happened. They hugged Ben and Mia and said they were sorry. They said they would buy them a new chess game. They said they were proud of them for playing chess. They said they loved them. Ben and Mia felt better. They hugged their mom and dad and said they loved them too. They said they also liked to dance. They said they forgave Luna. They looked at their cat. She was still sitting on the board. She looked at them with her pale green eyes. She purred.
