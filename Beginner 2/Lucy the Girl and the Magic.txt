Category: Beginner
Title: Lucy the Girl and the Magic

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lucy. She lived near the sea with her mom and dad. They loved to walk on the beach and look for pretty things. One day, they found a big oyster. Lucy's mom said, "I promise, we will open it when we get home." When they got home, Lucy's dad opened the oyster with a special tool. Inside, they found a beautiful pearl. Lucy's eyes got big and she said, "Wow! Can I keep it, please?" Her mom and dad were very thoughtful and said, "Yes, you can keep it, but you must take care of it." Lucy kept the pearl in a small box and took care of it every day. One day, she opened the box and the pearl was gone! She was sad and cried. But then, she saw a note in the box that said, "I am a magic pearl. I will give you one wish. What do you want?" Lucy thought for a moment and said, "I wish for my family to be happy forever." The pearl came back and they all lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a big church. Many people went to the church to sing and be happy. A little boy named Tim loved to watch the people go in and out of the church. Tim had a loyal dog named Max. Max was always with Tim. They liked to play, run, and jump together. Max would watch the church with Tim, too. One day, Tim and Max went to the church. They sang, laughed, and had fun with the people. After that, Tim and Max always watched the church with a big smile. They knew they were part of something special.

Anna and Ben are best friends. They like to play in the park every day. They have a lot of fun on the swings, the slide and the sandbox. One day, Anna sees a big dog in the park. She is scared of dogs. She runs to Ben and says, "Ben, Ben, there is a big dog! It has sharp teeth and loud bark. I am afraid!" Ben looks at the dog. He is not scared of dogs. He likes them. He says, "Anna, don't worry. The dog is nice. It is not mean. It just wants to play. Look, it has a red shirt on. It is incredible!" Anna looks at the dog again. She sees the red shirt. It has a picture of a bone on it. She thinks it is funny. She says, "Ben, you are right. The dog is incredible. It has a shirt like us. Maybe it is friendly. Can we pet it?" Ben says, "Yes, we can. But we have to ask the owner first. Come on, let's go and say hello." He takes Anna's hand and they walk to the dog and the owner. The owner smiles and says, "Hi, kids. This is Rover. He loves to meet new friends. Do you want to pet him?" Anna and Ben nod. They pet Rover gently. Rover wags his tail and licks their faces. Anna and Ben giggle. They are not scared anymore. They are happy. They have a new friend.

Once upon a time there was a girl. She was very fast. She loved to run and play. One day she was playing with her friends and she forgot something. She had forgotten to close her mouth when she ran. She couldn't believe it! She ran so fast that the wind blew through her open mouth and it was uncomfortable. She stopped and closed her mouth tight. When her friends saw it they laughed and laughed. But the girl didn't mind. It was just a funny mistake. From then on she was more careful. She remembered to keep her mouth closed when she ran. Whenever she forgot, her friends would remind her and she'd say thank you. And so the girl lived happily ever after, never forgetting to keep her mouth closed when she was running fast.

One day, a little boy named Tim found a small black rock. He did not know it was coal. He thought it was an adorable rock. He took it home and showed it to his mom. Tim said, "Look, Mom! I found a cute rock!" His mom looked at the rock and smiled. She told him, "That's not just a rock, Tim. That's coal. It can be used to make a fire." Tim was very happy to have found coal. He went to his friend, Lily, and told her about it. Lily said, "Wow, I have never seen coal before! Can you show it to me?" Tim showed Lily the coal. They both thought it was very special. They played with the coal all day and had lots of fun. Tim and Lily never forgot the day they found the adorable coal.
