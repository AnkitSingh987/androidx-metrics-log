Category: Beginner
Title: Rosie and the Door

Once upon a time there was a little girl. Her name was Rosie and she was brave. One day, Rosie heard some music. She knocked on the door to find out where the music was coming from. A friendly voice answered, "Come in!" Rosie opened the door and walked in. Her eyes were bright. In front of her, a musician was playing an instrument. "I like the music!", Rosie said. "Thanks!", the musician replied. "Music's special. It makes people feel like they belong." Then the musician asked Rosie if she wanted to join them. She said yes. Rosie was very brave, so she tried to play the instrument. It wasn't easy at first, but she kept trying. Soon, her music sounded just like the musician's. The musician smiled and said, "You belong here, Rosie. Welcome to our band!" Rosie felt happy and brave. She loved playing music with her new friends.

Once upon a time, there was a tidy monkey named Max. Max loved to mix things up. One day, he found a big bowl and some fruit in his tree house. Max said, "I will make a yummy fruit salad!" He put apples, bananas, and grapes in the bowl. Then, he used his hands to mix all the fruit together. Max's friend, a little bird, came to visit. The bird saw the fruit salad and said, "Wow, Max! That looks so good!" Max smiled and shared his fruit salad with his friend. They both loved it and had a fun day together.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. He had a toy ball that he loved to play with. One day, Tim saw a big crack in his ball. He felt sad and asked his mom, "Mom, can you fix my ball?" His mom looked at the crack and said, "Tim, this ball is very dry. It needs some water to help fix the crack." So, Tim and his mom went outside and put water on the ball. They waited for a while and then the crack was gone! Tim was so happy that he could play with his ball again. Tim learned that when something is dry and has a crack, you can return it to being good with a little help. The moral of the story is that sometimes things need help to be fixed, and it's okay to ask for help.

Sam was a brown bear who loved to swim. He swam in the big lake every day, splashing and diving and having fun. He had many friends who swam with him, like the ducks, the fish and the frogs. They all liked Sam and played with him. One day, a new bear came to the lake. He was big and black and mean. He saw Sam swimming and said, "Hey, you! Get out of the water! This is my lake now! You don't belong here!" Sam was scared and sad. He did not want to leave his friends and his lake. He said, "But I have always swum here. This is my home. Why can't we share the lake?" The black bear growled and said, "No! I don't share with anyone! I am the strongest and the best! You are a weak and silly brown bear who can't do anything! Go away or I will hurt you!" Sam did not know what to do. He did not want to fight, but he did not want to lose his lake. He looked around and saw his friends watching him. They looked worried and angry. They wanted to help Sam, but they were afraid of the black bear. Then, Sam had an idea. He remembered something his mother had taught him. He said, "Okay, I will go away, but only if you can do something better than me. Can you swim as fast as me?" The black bear laughed and said, "Of course I can! I can do anything better than you! You are a joke!" Sam said, "Then let's have a race. From here to that big rock and back. The winner can stay in the lake, and the loser has to leave. Do you agree?" The black bear agreed. He was sure he would win. He did not know that Sam had a trick up his sleeve. He did not know that the big rock was the home of the beavers, who were Sam's friends too. Sam and the black bear got ready to swim. The ducks, the fish and the frogs cheered for Sam. The black bear snorted and said, "You are wasting your time. I will beat you easily!" Sam said, "We'll see about that. On your mark, get set, go!" Sam and the black bear swam as fast as they could. Sam was ahead, but the black bear was close behind. They reached the big rock and turned around. But as they did, the beavers came out of their hole and bit the black bear's tail. The black bear yelped and stopped swimming. He was in pain and angry. He tried to shake off the beavers, but they held on tight. Sam saw what happened and smiled. He swam back to the shore and won the race. He said, "I did it! I won! You have to leave now!" The black bear was ashamed and furious. He said, "No fair! You cheated! You had help from the beavers!" Sam said, "No, I didn't cheat. I used my brain and my friends. You see, the lake belongs to everyone who lives here and cares for it. You don't belong here because you are mean and selfish. You don't have any friends. You only have enemies. You should learn to be nice and share, or you will always be alone and unhappy." The black bear realized that Sam was right. He felt sorry for himself and for what he had done. He said, "You are right. I was wrong. I'm sorry. Can you forgive me?" Sam said, "Yes, I can forgive you. But you have to promise to be nice and share from now on. And you have to apologize to the beavers and the others too." The black bear agreed. He apologized to the beavers and the others. He asked them if he could swim with them and be their friend. They said yes, but only if he was nice and shared. The black bear said he would. He thanked Sam for teaching him a lesson. Sam was happy. He had saved his lake and his friends. He had also made a new friend. He said, "You're welcome. I'm glad you learned your lesson. Come on, let's swim and have fun!" Sam and the black bear swam and had fun. The ducks, the fish and the frogs joined them. They all played and laughed and enjoyed the lake. They all belonged there. They were all happy.
