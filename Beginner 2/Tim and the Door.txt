Category: Beginner
Title: Tim and the Door

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tim. Tim loved to paint. One day, he found an ancient door with a big lock. Tim wanted to see what was behind the door. Tim tried to open the lock, but it was too hard. He asked his friend, Sam, for help. Sam said, "Let's paint the door, maybe it will open then." So, they started to paint the door with bright colors. As they painted, the door started to shake. Suddenly, the lock fell off and the door opened. Behind the door, they found a big room filled with toys. Tim and Sam were so happy. They played with the toys all day long.

Anna and Ben were playing with their crayons and paper. They liked to print pictures of animals and flowers. Anna printed a big cat with orange and black stripes. Ben printed a small bird with blue and yellow feathers. "Look at my cat!" Anna said. "It is very strong and fast. It can run and jump and roar." "Look at my bird!" Ben said. "It is very pretty and sweet. It can fly and sing and chirp." They showed each other their pictures and smiled. They were proud of their prints. Suddenly, they heard a loud noise outside. They ran to the window and saw a big truck. The truck was spraying water on the street. The water made a lot of mist in the air. The mist was white and wet and cold. "Wow!" Anna said. "What is that?" "It is a truck," Ben said. "It is cleaning the street. The water makes mist." "Can we go and see?" Anna asked. "OK, but we have to be careful," Ben said. "The truck is big and loud and the mist is wet and cold." They put on their coats and shoes and ran outside. They held hands and stayed close to each other. They saw the truck and the mist. They felt the water on their faces and hands. They laughed and shouted. "Hello, truck!" Anna said. "Hello, mist!" "Hello, kids!" the truck driver said. He smiled and waved at them. He stopped the truck and the water. He got out and walked to them. "Are you having fun?" he asked. "Yes, we are!" Anna and Ben said. "Would you like to see my truck?" he asked. "Yes, please!" Anna and Ben said. He showed them his truck. He showed them the hose and the tank and the buttons. He let them sit in the driver's seat and honk the horn. They were very happy. "Thank you, truck driver!" Anna and Ben said. "You're welcome, kids!" the truck driver said. "But you should go back inside now. It is getting cold and dark." "OK, bye, truck driver!" Anna and Ben said. They ran back to their house. They took off their coats and shoes and went to their room. They looked at their prints. They were wet and torn. The cat and the bird were faded and smudged. "Oh, no!" Anna said. "Our prints are ruined!" "Oh, no!" Ben said. "They are fragile!" They felt sad and sorry. They liked their prints. They worked hard on them. "It's OK," Anna said. "We can make new prints tomorrow." "It's OK," Ben said. "We can make better prints tomorrow." They hugged each other and smiled. They were still proud of their prints. They were more proud of their adventure. They had fun with the truck and the mist. They learned something new. They were brave and curious. They put their prints under their pillows. They said good night to each other. They closed their eyes and dreamed of cats and birds and trucks and mist.

Once upon a time there was a girl named Anna. One day, Anna wanted to go to the park so she could enjoy some fresh air. She put on her coat and went outside. When she arrived, Anna saw a huge bubble machine! She clapped her hands together and squealed with excitement. She ran to the bubble machine and watched in awe as the colourful bubbles rose up and floated away. Anna laughed with joy. She tried to touch the bubbles with her hands, feeling the bubbles glide lightly through her fingers. She loved it! But then a sad thing happened. Anna's bubble popped and she started crying. She was so sad that she didn't want to play anymore. At that moment her mother came up to her and said, "Don't be so sad, Anna. Look, I brought you a bottle of bubbles!" Her mother gave her a big hug and Anna smiled. Together, they enjoyed blowing bubbles and watching them float away into the sky. Anna felt joy and happiness again. She learned that sometimes things don't work out, but that doesn't mean you can't still enjoy life.
