Category: Beginner
Title: Tom and Anna and the Ball

Tom and Anna like to play catch with their ball. They throw the ball to each other and try not to drop it. Sometimes they run and jump to catch the ball. They have a lot of fun. One day, they see a shiny comb on the ground. It is silver and has many teeth. They think it is pretty and want to play with it. They pick it up and look at it. They see a tag on the comb. It says "expensive". They do not know what that means. They decide to use the comb as a new toy. They throw the comb to each other and try to catch it. They think it is more fun than the ball. They laugh and giggle. But the comb is not a good toy. It is sharp and heavy. It hurts their hands when they catch it. It also makes a loud noise when it hits the ground. It scares the birds and the cats. It also scratches the grass and the flowers. Soon, a lady comes running. She is angry and sad. She says the comb is hers and she lost it. She says the comb is expensive and special. She says the comb is a gift from her grandma. She says the comb is not a toy and they should not play with it. Tom and Anna feel bad. They say they are sorry and give the comb back to the lady. They say they did not know it was hers and they did not mean to hurt it. They say they will not play with it again. The lady says thank you and smiles. She says she is glad they found her comb and she is not mad at them. She says they can play with her ball instead. She says the ball is a good toy and they can have fun with it. Tom and Anna say thank you and smile. They take the ball and play catch with it. They think the ball is better than the comb. They have a lot of fun.

Once upon a time, there was a large tree. This tree had many lemons. One day, a big lemon fell from the tree. A little girl named Lily saw the lemon. Lily picked up the lemon and said, "Wow, this is a big lemon! I will take it home and make lemon juice." She took the lemon home and put it on the table. Her mom saw the lemon and smiled. She said, "That is a very large lemon, Lily. But be careful, it might spoil if we don't use it soon." The next day, Lily and her mom made lemon juice. They had fun making it. They drank the lemon juice and it was so yummy. Lily was happy she found the large lemon and that it did not spoil.

One day, a thin cat named Max saw a big pile of wood. Max wanted to move the wood to his home. He thought, "I can use this wood to make a warm bed." Max tried to move the wood, but it was too heavy for him. He felt sad. Max saw his friend, a big dog named Sam. Max asked Sam, "Can you help me move this wood?" Sam said, "Yes, I can help you." They worked together to move the wood to Max's home. When they were almost done, they found a surprise under the wood. It was a little bird with a broken wing. Max and Sam decided to help the bird. They used some of the wood to make a safe home for the bird. The bird was happy, and Max and Sam were happy too. They learned that helping others can be more fun than getting things for themselves.

Tom and Sam were brothers who liked to play outside. One day, they saw a big scale in the park. They wanted to know how much they weighed. "Let's take turns and see who is heavier," said Tom. "OK, I'll go first," said Sam. He stepped on the scale and saw a number. "I weigh 30 pounds," he said proudly. Tom laughed. "That's nothing. I'm older and bigger than you. I'll weigh more for sure." He pushed Sam off the scale and got on. But before he could see his number, a loud thunder boomed in the sky. Tom was scared. He hated thunder. He jumped off the scale and ran to their mom, who was sitting on a bench nearby. "Mom, mom, there's thunder! I want to go home!" he cried. Sam followed him, but he was not scared. He liked thunder. He thought it was exciting and fun. He looked at the scale and saw Tom's number. "Tom, you weigh 29 pounds," he said. "You're lighter than me!" Tom was shocked. He thought he was heavier than Sam. He felt silly and ashamed. He realized that being older and bigger did not mean being better. Mom hugged them both and said, "It doesn't matter how much you weigh, boys. What matters is how you behave. You should not push or laugh at each other. You should be kind and respectful. That's what makes you clever and good." Tom and Sam learned their lesson. They said sorry to each other and hugged. They decided to play together and share the scale. They also learned to enjoy the thunder and not be afraid. They were happy and clever brothers.
