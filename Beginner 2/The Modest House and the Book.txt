Category: Beginner
Title: The Modest House and the Book

Once upon a time, there was a modest little house near a big field. In the house lived a kind mom, a strong dad, and a happy little boy named Tim. They loved to play in the field and have fun together. One sunny day, Tim went to the field to play. He saw a big tree with a small box under it. When he opened the box, he found a book inside. Tim loved to read, so he took the book home to his mom and dad. His mom and dad were happy to see the book. They all sat down and read the book together. The book was about a big bear who lived in a forest. Tim and his parents had fun reading the story. After they finished, they went outside to play in the field again, feeling happy and loved.

---

Anna and Ben are twins. They are three years old. Today is their birthday. They are very happy. They want to celebrate with their friends. They put on their party hats and go to the living room. There are balloons, cake, and presents. Their friends are waiting for them. They say hello and hug them. "Happy birthday, Anna and Ben!" their friends say. "Thank you, thank you!" Anna and Ben say. They play games, sing songs, and eat cake. They have a lot of fun. They open their presents. They get books, toys, and clothes. They say thank you to their friends. But then, something bad happens. Ben trips over a balloon and falls down. He hurts his hand. He cries. He feels helpless. "Ow, ow, ow!" he says. Anna hears him. She runs to him. She hugs him. She kisses his hand. "Don't cry, Ben. I'm here. I love you," she says. Ben stops crying. He smiles. He hugs her back. He loves her too. Their friends come to them. They say sorry and ask if he is okay. They give him more hugs and kisses. They make him feel better. Anna and Ben are happy again. They celebrate with their friends. They are not helpless. They have each other. They are twins. They are three years old. Today is their birthday. They are very happy.

Once upon a time, in a small village, there was a weird-looking tree. This tree had a big, shiny jewel on it. All the kids in the village wanted to take the jewel, but their moms and dads did not permit them. One day, a little boy named Tim went to the tree. He saw the jewel and wanted to take it. He asked his mom if he could take the jewel. His mom said, "No, Tim. We must not take things that are not ours." Tim was sad but listened to his mom. He went to play with his friends and told them about the jewel. All his friends wanted to take the jewel too, but Tim told them what his mom said. They all agreed not to take the jewel. As time went by, the kids in the village grew up and had their own kids. They told their kids the story of the weird tree and the shiny jewel. No one ever took the jewel, and it became a symbol of the village. The moral of the story is to always listen to your parents and not take things that are not yours.

Ben and Lily were playing pirates in the garden. They had a big box for a ship, a stick for a sword, and a scarf for a flag. They also had a skull that Ben found in the shed. It was white and hard and had no eyes or nose. "Look, Lily, this is our treasure!" Ben said, holding the skull. "We have to hide it from the bad pirates!" "Where can we hide it?" Lily asked. Ben looked around and saw a big tree with a hole in it. "There! In the tree!" he said. He ran to the tree and put the skull in the hole. Then he tied the scarf around the hole to cover it. "Now no one can find it!" But as he was tying the scarf, he did not see a big black crow watching him from a branch. The crow had a shiny eye and a sharp beak. It liked shiny things and hard things. It wanted the skull. The crow waited until Ben and Lily went back to their box. Then it flew down to the tree and pulled the scarf with its beak. The scarf came off and the skull fell out. The crow grabbed the skull and flew away. "Hey! That's our treasure!" Ben shouted, seeing the crow. He and Lily ran after it, waving their stick. "Give it back, you bad bird!" But the crow was too fast and too high. It flew to the roof of the house and landed there. It looked at the skull and pecked it. It was bitter and hard. The crow did not like it. It dropped the skull and flew away. The skull fell from the roof and landed on the ground. It broke into many pieces. Ben and Lily saw it and stopped running. They were sad and angry. "That was our treasure!" Lily cried. "The bad bird broke it!" Ben hugged Lily and said, "Don't cry, Lily. We can find another treasure. Maybe a shiny coin or a pretty shell. Come on, let's go back to our ship." They went back to their box and played pirates again. They forgot about the skull and the crow. But they did not forget to tie their scarf around their treasure.
