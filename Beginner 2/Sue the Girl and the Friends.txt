Category: Beginner
Title: Sue the Girl and the Friends

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue was in a class with her friends. They were all ready to play a game. The game was to pass a ball. Sue was very excited. But, there was a problem. A big boy named Tom did not want to pass the ball. He wanted to keep the ball for himself. Sue and her friends were sad. They did not know what to do. Sue had an idea. She asked Tom if he wanted to play too. Tom said yes. They all played the game together. They passed the ball and had fun. Sue and her friends were happy.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. Lily loved to eat celery. One day, Lily's mom gave her a new snack to try. It was a mild spread. Lily was very excited to taste it. Lily's mom said, "Lily, you can put the mild spread on your celery. It will taste very good!" Lily took her celery and spread the mild spread on it. She took a big bite and smiled. Later, Lily went to play with her friend Tim. She told Tim about the mild spread on her celery. Tim wanted to try it too. Lily and Tim went to Lily's house and shared the yummy snack. They both loved the mild spread on their celery and became best friends forever.

Lily and Ben are twins. They like to play in their room. They have many toys and books. They also have a big bookcase. The bookcase is tall and heavy. One day, Lily and Ben are bored. They want to have some fun. They see the bookcase. They have an idea. They want to hang from the bookcase. They think it is a game. Lily goes first. She climbs on a chair. She grabs the top of the bookcase. She swings her legs. She laughs. Ben watches. He wants to try too. "Stop, Lily!" Mom says. She comes into the room. She sees Lily hanging from the bookcase. She is scared. She runs to Lily. She pulls her down. She hugs her. "That is dangerous, Lily. You could fall. You could hurt yourself. You could make the bookcase fall. That is not a game. That is bad." Lily feels sorry. She did not know. She says sorry to Mom. Ben says sorry too. He did not hang from the bookcase. But he wanted to. He knows it is dangerous now. He does not want to do it. Mom is calm. She loves Lily and Ben. She says it is okay. She says they can play with their toys and books. But they have to be careful. They have to listen to Mom. They have to be good. Lily and Ben nod. They hug Mom. They say they love her. They say they will be good. They go to play with their toys and books. They do not hang from the bookcase anymore. They are happy. Mom is happy too.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. Sue loved to help her mom in the kitchen. One day, Sue's mom said, "Sue, can you please unpack the big box?" Sue said, "Yes, Mom!" and went to unpack the box. Inside the box, Sue found a big, shiny pot. "Look, Mom!" Sue said, "I found a pot!" Sue's mom smiled and said, "That is an honest pot. It will help us cook yummy food." Sue was very happy and put the pot on the table. Sue and her mom used the honest pot to cook a big soup. They put in carrots, peas, and corn. When the soup was ready, Sue and her mom sat down to eat. They both loved the soup and the new pot. Sue knew that the honest pot would help them cook many more yummy meals.

Once upon a time, in a small house, there lived a boy named Tim. Tim was an adventurous boy. He loved to play outside and find new things. One day, Tim saw a big box in his room. He thought, "I want to unpack that box!" Tim called his friend, Sam. "Sam, come help me unpack this big box!" Sam came over and they started to unpack the box. Inside the box, they found a big, shiny ball. They were very happy and decided to play with it outside. As they played, the ball rolled to the tip of a hill. Tim and Sam looked at each other and thought it would be fun to roll the ball down the hill. They pushed the ball and watched it roll down the hill. But the ball rolled too fast and went into the street. A car came and hit the ball. The ball popped and was gone. Tim and Sam were very sad. They learned that being adventurous can sometimes lead to bad endings.
