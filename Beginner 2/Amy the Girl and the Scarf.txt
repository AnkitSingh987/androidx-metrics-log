Category: Beginner
Title: Amy the Girl and the Scarf

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Amy. She had a cheap scarf that she loved very much. It was red and soft. She wore it every day, even when it was hot outside. Amy thought her scarf was the best thing ever. One day, Amy saw a big box. She wanted to see what was inside. Amy tried to lift the box, but it was very heavy. She put her scarf on the ground and tried again. This time, she could lift the box a little bit. She was so happy! But when Amy put the box down, she saw something bad. Her cheap scarf was under the box. The scarf was not soft and red anymore. It was flat and dirty. Amy was very sad. She could not wear her favorite scarf anymore. The end.

One day, a pale bird named Lily was flying in the sky. She loved to sing her happy songs. She flew over a big tree and saw her friend, Tom the squirrel. "Hi, Tom!" said Lily. "Do you want to hear my new song?" "Yes, please!" said Tom with a big smile. Lily began to sing her new song. It was a very pretty song. Tom clapped his hands and danced to the music. But while they were having fun, Tom did not see the fall in front of him. He tripped and fell down. "Oh no, Tom!" said Lily. "Are you okay?" "Yes, I am fine," said Tom, getting up. "Your song was so nice that I forgot to watch where I was going." Lily and Tom laughed together. They played and sang songs all day long, being careful of the fall next time.

Once upon a time, there was a mighty dog named Max. Max loved to eat sandwiches. One day, Max went to the park to play with his friends. At the park, Max saw a new friend. He wanted to introduce himself. So, Max walked up to the new friend and said, "Hi, I'm Max, the mighty dog. Do you like sandwiches too?" The new friend smiled and said, "Yes, I love sandwiches! My name is Lily." Max and Lily played together all day, and they shared a big sandwich for lunch. From that day on, Max and Lily became best friends. They always played together at the park, and they always shared their sandwiches. They were very happy, and everyone loved to watch the mighty dog and his new friend have fun.

One day, a little girl named Sue went to the park with her mom. They saw a man making thick ice cream cones. Sue wanted one, so her mom bought her a big cone. While Sue was eating her ice cream, a funny clown came to the park. He made the kids laugh and applaud. Sue liked the clown, so she went to say hi. "Hi, Mr. Clown, you are funny!" said Sue. The clown smiled and said, "Thank you, Sue! I like your ice cream cone." As Sue was talking to the clown, her ice cream cone fell on the ground. She was sad, but the clown said, "Don't worry, Sue. I can make you a new cone!" The clown took out a big balloon and made a cone shape. He put the thick ice cream on top. Sue was happy again and thanked the clown. The other kids saw Sue's balloon cone and wanted one too. The clown made cones for all the kids. They all learned that when something bad happens, you can still find a way to make it better.

Anna and Ben are playing in the park. They see a big pile of leaves. They run to the pile and jump in. They laugh and have fun. "Look, Ben, I have a big leaf!" Anna says. She holds a leaf that is bigger than her head. "Wow, Anna, that is a big leaf!" Ben says. He picks up a small leaf. "Can you throw your leaf, Ben?" Anna asks. She throws her leaf in the air. It floats down slowly. "I can throw my leaf, Anna!" Ben says. He throws his leaf as hard as he can. But his leaf is very light. It does not go far. "Your leaf is too light, Ben. It is easy to throw, but it does not fly well," Anna says. "Your leaf is too big, Anna. It is hard to throw, but it flies better," Ben says. They look at each other and smile. They have an idea. "Let's trade leaves, Ben!" Anna says. "OK, Anna, let's trade leaves!" Ben says. They swap their leaves and try again. They throw their new leaves in the air. Anna's leaf goes farther and Ben's leaf floats longer. They clap and cheer. They have more fun. They learn that different leaves have different weights. They learn that some things are easy to throw and some things are hard to throw. They learn that they can share and try new things. They are happy.
