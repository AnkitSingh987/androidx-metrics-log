Category: Beginner
Title: The Goose and the Park

Once there was a goose. The goose was important because it had a big job. The goose had to find food for all its friends. The goose went to the park to find food. It looked under a tree and found some bread. The goose was very happy. It picked up the bread and went back to its friends. The friends were happy too. They all ate the bread and thanked the goose. The important goose was proud of its job. They all played together and had a fun day.

Once upon a time, there was a little dog named Spot. Spot loved to play near the river. One day, Spot saw a bridge. He wanted to cross it, but it looked tough to walk on. Spot tried to balance on the bridge. He took one step, then another. He wobbled a bit, but he did not fall. He was very happy. He barked, "I can do it! I can cross the bridge!" A big, friendly bear saw Spot on the bridge. The bear wanted to help Spot. He said, "Hold on, little dog. I will help you." The bear walked slowly behind Spot, making sure he was safe. Together, Spot and the bear crossed the tough bridge. They made it to the other side. Spot was so happy and proud. He thanked the bear and they played by the river all day.

---

One day, a girl named Sue went to the beach with her mom. They had fun playing in the sand and looking at the water. The water was cold, so they didn't swim. Sue saw the tide coming in and wanted to see it up close. She asked her mom, "Can I go see the tide?" Her mom said, "Yes, but be careful and don't go too far." Sue walked closer to the water and saw a big, pretty shell. She picked it up and heard a voice call out, "Hello!" Sue was surprised and looked around. She didn't see anyone. Sue looked at the shell and said, "Did you call me?" The shell said, "Yes, I did! I am a talking shell!" Sue was very happy to have a new friend. They talked and played by the cold water and the tide all day. When it was time to go home, Sue took the talking shell with her, and they had many more fun days together.

Tom likes to drive his toy car. He has a red car that makes noises and has lights. He drives it around the house and the yard. He pretends he is a racer or a firefighter or a policeman. One day, Tom brings his car to class. He wants to show his friends his car and play with them. He puts his car on his desk and waits for the teacher to start. He is very happy and proud of his car. But when the teacher comes, she is not happy. She sees Tom's car and says, "Tom, what is that? You know you are not allowed to bring toys to class. This is not a time for playing. This is a time for learning. Give me your car right now." Tom feels very embarrassed. He does not want to give his car to the teacher. He likes his car and he thinks his friends like it too. He thinks the teacher is mean and does not understand. He says, "No, this is my car. I want to drive it. Please let me keep it." The teacher is angry. She says, "Tom, you are being rude and naughty. You have to follow the rules. If you do not give me your car, you will have to go to the office and talk to the principal. Do you want that?" Tom is scared. He does not want to go to the office. He does not want to talk to the principal. He thinks the principal is scary and will be mad at him. He says, "No, I do not want that. I am sorry. Here is my car." The teacher takes Tom's car and puts it in a drawer. She says, "Thank you, Tom. You can have your car back at the end of the day. But you have to promise me you will not bring it to class again. Do you understand?" Tom nods. He says, "Yes, I understand. I promise. I am sorry." He feels very sad and embarrassed. He wishes he did not bring his car to class. He wishes he could drive it and play with his friends. He hopes the day will end soon.
