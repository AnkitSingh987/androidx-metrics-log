Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Orange Cat and the Park

Once upon a time, in a small town, there lived a happy orange cat named Tim. Tim loved to play with his friends, the blue bird and the green dog. They had so much fun together, running and jumping in the park. One day, it was Tim's birthday. His friends wanted to celebrate with him. They decided to have a party in the park. They brought balloons and a big cake. Tim was so happy to see his friends and the cake. But there was a lot of waste after the party. So, Tim and his friends cleaned up the park. They picked up the trash and put it in the bin. The park was clean again. Tim and his friends were happy to help. They learned that it is good to celebrate, but also important to clean up after.

Once upon a time there was a girl named Sarah. She loved gaze at the stars in the night sky. One day she decided to go for a walk in the forest. As she went through, she noticed a lump of coal. It was smooth and shiny. She picked it up and gazed at it. It was very pretty and made her feel happy. She decided to keep it in her pocket for safekeeping. After she had been walking for a while she came to a river. She stopped and looked around. The sun was setting and the sky was ablaze with orange and gold. She took out the coal and began to gaze at it while the sky changed colour. She felt so peaceful and content. When she got home she put the coal in her bulb bed so she could gaze at it every night before she slept. She loved it and knew it would keep her safe.

One day, a reindeer was walking in the woods. He felt very bored. He wanted to play with his friends, but they all said no. They said they had things to do. The reindeer was sad. He saw a big tree and wanted to climb it. His friends said, "No, don't do it!" But the reindeer insisted. He climbed the tree and saw something in the sky. It was a big, bright star! It was so pretty. The reindeer was happy he climbed the tree. He showed the star to his friends. They all looked at the star and smiled. They were not bored anymore. They all played together under the star.

One day, a little dog named Max was very excited. He saw a big ice cream cone on the table. Max wanted to eat the ice cream cone. He jumped and tried to reach it, but he was too small. Max's friend, a bird named Sam, saw Max trying to get the cone. Sam said, "Max, you should take a nap. You are too excited." Max thought about it and decided to listen to Sam. He went to his bed and took a nap. When Max woke up, he saw the ice cream cone on the floor. He was so happy! But then, he noticed that the ice cream was all gone. Sam said, "Max, while you were napping, I ate the ice cream. I'm sorry." Max was sad, but he learned that it's important to share with friends.

Ben and Mia were playing in the backyard. They saw a big ladder leaning on the wall. They wanted to climb it and see what was on the roof. "Let's go up the ladder!" Ben said. "It looks fun!" "I don't know, Ben. It looks high and scary," Mia said. "What if we fall?" "Don't be afraid, Mia. It's harmless. I'll go first and show you," Ben said. He started to climb the ladder, step by step. Mia watched him from below. She saw him reach the top of the ladder and look around. He saw a bird's nest, a ball, and a ruler. "Wow, look what I found!" Ben shouted. "A ruler! We can measure things with it!" "What can we measure, Ben?" Mia asked. "We can measure the ladder, the roof, the bird's nest, anything!" Ben said. "Come on, Mia, join me!" Mia felt curious. She wanted to see what Ben saw. She decided to be brave and climb the ladder too. She followed Ben's steps and reached the top. "See, Mia, it's not so bad!" Ben said. He handed her the ruler. "Here, you can measure first." Mia smiled. She took the ruler and measured the ladder. It was very long. She measured the roof. It was very wide. She measured the bird's nest. It was very small. "This is fun, Ben!" Mia said. "Let's measure more things!" "Okay, Mia!" Ben said. "But be careful. Don't drop the ruler!" They measured more things on the roof. They had a lot of fun. They forgot about their fear of heights. They only remembered their joy of discovery.
