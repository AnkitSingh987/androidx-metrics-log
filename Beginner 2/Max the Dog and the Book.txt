Category: Beginner
Title: Max the Dog and the Book

Once upon a time, there was a big dog named Max. Max loved to play and run all day long. He liked to behave and be a good dog. One day, Max found a book in the park. The book had a spell inside. Max wanted to try the spell, so he barked the words out loud. Suddenly, something unexpected happened! Max turned into a tiny ant. He was so small that nobody could see him. Max was scared and did not know what to do. Max thought hard and remembered the spell. He barked the words again, but this time he behaved even better. The spell worked, and Max turned back into a big dog. He was so happy to be big again. From then on, Max knew that behaving well was important, and he never played with spells again.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. He was a very nervous boy. One day, he went to the park with his mommy. At the park, he saw other kids playing with a ball. Tim wanted to play too, but he was too scared to ask. Tim's mommy saw him looking sad and said, "Why don't you go play with the other kids, Tim?" Tim replied, "I am too nervous, mommy." His mommy smiled and said, "It's okay to be nervous, but you can be brave too. Just go and ask if you can play with them." So, Tim took a deep breath and walked to the other kids. He asked, "Can I play with you?" The kids smiled and said, "Yes, of course!" They gave Tim the ball, and he threw it to one of the kids. They all played happily together. At the end of the day, Tim was so happy he made new friends. He learned that even though he was nervous, he could still be brave and try new things. And that is how Tim learned the importance of being brave and making friends.

Sam and Jen liked to play in the park. They liked to run, swing, slide and climb. But their favorite thing was the big statue of a lion. The lion was very old and had a long mane and sharp teeth. It looked like it could roar and jump any time. One day, Sam and Jen had a new idea. They wanted to see if they could fit inside the lion's mouth. They thought it would be fun and scary. They climbed on the lion's back and crawled to its head. They saw the big hole where the mouth was. Sam went first. He put his head inside and said, "Look, Jen, I'm a lion cub!" Jen laughed and followed him. She put her head inside too and said, "Me too, me too!" They giggled and pretended to roar. They did not see the man who was walking by. He was a worker who came to fix the statue. He had a big tool in his hand. He saw the lion's mouth was open and thought it was broken. He said, "Oh, no, this won't do. I have to close it." He used his tool to push the mouth shut. He did not know Sam and Jen were inside. He heard a loud thud and then some muffled screams. He was confused and looked around. He saw two pairs of feet sticking out of the lion's mouth. He realized what had happened and felt very sorry. He quickly opened the mouth again and helped Sam and Jen out. They were scared and crying. He said, "I'm so sorry, kids. I didn't see you. Are you okay?" Sam and Jen nodded and hugged each other. They were not hurt, but they were very shaken. They did not want to play with the lion anymore. They ran to their mom who was sitting on a bench. She saw their faces and asked, "What happened?" They told her everything. She was shocked and angry. She scolded them for being foolish and dangerous. She thanked the worker for saving them. She said, "You two are very lucky. You could have been hurt or worse. Don't ever do that again. Do you understand?" Sam and Jen said, "Yes, mom. We're sorry." They learned their lesson. They never tried to fit inside the lion's mouth again. They still liked to play in the park, but they stayed away from the statue. They found other things to have fun with. They were happy and safe.
