Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and the Park

Sara liked to play chess with her dad. She learned how to move the pieces and how to say checkmate. She felt smart and happy when she played chess. One day, Sara went to the park with her dad. She saw a big chess board on the ground, with big pieces. She wanted to play with them. She asked her dad if they could play. He said yes, and they went to the board. Sara picked the white pieces and her dad picked the black pieces. They started to play. Sara moved her pieces carefully, trying to trap her dad's king. She was doing well, until she made a mistake. She moved her queen too far, and her dad took it with his rook. Sara felt sad and angry. She cried out loud. "Hey, what's wrong?" a boy asked. He was watching them play. He had brown hair and blue eyes. He looked nice, but Sara did not know him. Sara felt embarrassed. She did not want the boy to see her cry. She wiped her tears and said, "Nothing. I just lost my queen." The boy smiled and said, "That's okay. Chess is hard. I play chess too, but I lose sometimes. Do you want to play with me? Maybe we can help each other." Sara looked at her dad. He nodded and said, "That's a sweet idea. Why don't you play with him, Sara? I'll watch you." Sara felt better. She liked the boy's offer. She said, "Okay. Thank you. What's your name?" The boy said, "I'm Ben. What's yours?" Sara said, "I'm Sara. Nice to meet you, Ben." They shook hands and smiled. They moved the pieces back and started a new game. Sara and Ben played chess and had fun. They did not care who won or lost. They just enjoyed the game and each other's company. Sara was glad she met Ben. She made a new friend.

Tom and Lily were twins who liked to play outside. They had a red bike that they shared. Sometimes they took turns, and sometimes they rode together. One day, they decided to speed down the hill near their house. They felt the wind in their hair and the sun on their faces. They laughed and shouted with joy. But at the bottom of the hill, there was a cat. The cat was black and white, and it was sleeping on the road. Tom and Lily did not see the cat until it was too late. They tried to stop, but they crashed into the cat. The bike fell over, and Tom and Lily scraped their knees. The cat yowled and ran away. Tom and Lily started to cry. They were hurt and scared. They wanted their mom and dad. But then, they heard a voice. It was Mrs. Lee, their neighbor. She was a kind and compassionate lady who loved animals. She saw what happened, and she came to help. She picked up the bike and carried it to her porch. She took Tom and Lily inside her house. She cleaned their wounds and gave them band-aids. She made them some juice and cookies. She hugged them and told them stories. Tom and Lily felt better. They thanked Mrs. Lee for her help. They said they were sorry for hurting the cat. Mrs. Lee said the cat was fine, and that it was an accident. She said she was glad they were not badly hurt. She said they were brave and good children. She said they could come and visit her anytime. Tom and Lily smiled. They felt peace in their hearts. They said goodbye to Mrs. Lee and walked back to their house. They held hands and sang a song. They learned a lesson that day. They learned to be careful and to be compassionate. They learned that speed is fun, but peace is better.

Once upon a time, there was a smart girl named Lily. She had a little penny that she loved very much. One day, she went to the store with her mom. She wanted to buy candy with her penny. At the store, Lily showed her penny to the store man. "Can I have candy, please?" she asked. The store man looked at the penny and said, "Oh no, this is not enough. You need more pennies." Lily was sad, but she did not cry. She knew she had to remain calm. On the way home, something unexpected happened. A kind old lady saw Lily and her mom. She smiled and gave Lily a big bag of candy. "Here you go, dear," she said. "You can keep your penny and have this candy too." Lily was so happy! She thanked the old lady and shared the candy with her mom. And they all lived happily ever after.
