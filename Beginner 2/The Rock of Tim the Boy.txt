Category: Beginner
Title: The Rock of Tim the Boy

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play with his toy rock. He would throw it, roll it, and sometimes just hold it in his hand. One day, Tim went to visit his grandpa. His grandpa had a big, shiny watch on his wrist. Tim liked to look at it and see the little hands go around and around. Tim's grandpa was very healthy. He liked to eat good food and go for walks. One day, Tim and his grandpa went for a walk in the park. Tim brought his toy rock with him. They saw a big tree and decided to sit under it. Tim's grandpa took off his watch and let Tim hold it. Tim looked at the watch and then at his toy rock. He had an idea. Tim put the watch on the ground and placed his toy rock on top of it. He gently rocked the rock back and forth on the watch. The watch made a soft ticking sound that made Tim smile. His grandpa laughed and said, "You found a new way to play with your rock and my watch!" They both laughed and played together until it was time to go home. And from that day on, whenever Tim visited his grandpa, they would always play with the rock and the watch under the big tree.

Once upon a time, there was a big, hairy dog named Fuzzy. Fuzzy liked to play with his friends in the park. One day, Fuzzy saw a small bug on the ground. The bug had a pretty stamp on its back. Fuzzy wanted to be friends with the bug, but the bug was scared. Fuzzy tried to show the bug he was nice by giving it a toy. The bug liked the toy and started to play. They played together and had lots of fun. While they were playing, Fuzzy found a stick. He picked it up and tried to break it. Fuzzy used his strong teeth and snap! The stick broke in half. The bug was so happy and clapped its tiny hands. Fuzzy and the bug with the stamp became best friends and played together every day.

Once upon a time there was a leopard. He was blue and liked to have adventures. One day he thought of something fun to do. He suggested going to the park to swing on the swings and play hide-and-seek. The leopard jumped and hopped his way to the park. When he arrived, he saw lots of other children playing. He asked the children if he could join them. The kids said yes and started having fun! They suggested playing tag and the leopard said yes. They chased each other around and laughed. Then they suggested building a fort with the playground blocks. The leopard helped make a blue fort with a roof of leaves. They all went in and pretended to be pirates hunting for treasure. They made up a story of a blue leopard with treasure. When it was time to go, the leopard said goodbye with a smile. He hopped back home happy and excited to come back to the park and play soon again.

Sara and Tom are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they find a long bean on the ground. It is green and shiny. "Wow, look at this bean!" Sara says. "It is so long and pretty. I want it!" "No, I want it!" Tom says. "I saw it first. It is mine!" They both pull on the bean. It is very strong. They cannot break it. "Stop it!" Sara says. "You are hurting the bean. Let go!" "No, you let go!" Tom says. "You are being mean. Share!" They keep pulling and pulling. They are not happy. They are angry. Then, they hear a voice. It is Mama. She is looking for them. "Sara, Tom, where are you?" Mama says. "It is time to go home. Come here!" Sara and Tom drop the bean. They run to Mama. They hug her. "Mama, we found a long bean!" Sara says. "But we could not share it. We fought over it." "Mama, we are sorry!" Tom says. "We were not nice to each other. We hurt the bean." Mama smiles. She picks up the bean. She looks at it. "This is a special bean," Mama says. "It is a magic bean. It can grow into a big plant. Do you want to see?" Sara and Tom nod. They are curious. They want to see. Mama takes them to a pot. She digs a hole. She puts the bean in the hole. She covers it with dirt. She waters it. "Let's wait and see what happens," Mama says. "Maybe tomorrow, we will see something." Sara and Tom look at the pot. They look at each other. They feel sorry. "Tom, I am sorry I was mean to you," Sara says. "You can have the bean. I don't need it." "Sara, I am sorry I was mean to you," Tom says. "You can have the bean. I don't need it." They hug each other. They are friends again. "Mama, can we share the bean?" Sara and Tom say. "We want to see the magic together." Mama nods. She is proud of them. "Of course you can share the bean," Mama says. "Sharing is caring. And caring is magic."
