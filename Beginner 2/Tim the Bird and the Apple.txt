Category: Beginner
Title: Tim the Bird and the Apple

Once upon a time, there was a little bird named Tim. Tim lived in a big tree with many branches. One day, Tim saw a brilliant red apple on a high branch. He wanted to eat the apple, but he was scared. He had never flown so high before. Tim's mom saw him looking at the apple. She wanted to help him. She said, "Tim, you can do it! I will encourage you. Just try your best." Tim felt better with his mom's help. He tried to fly up to the branch with the red apple. At first, Tim was scared. But his mom kept encouraging him. She said, "You can do it, Tim! Keep trying!" Tim tried harder and harder. Finally, he reached the branch with the apple. He was so happy! He ate the apple and felt proud of himself. The moral of the story is that with encouragement and trying your best, you can do things you never thought you could do.

Ben and Mia were playing with their toy trains on the track. They liked to make the trains go fast and slow, and make noises like choo-choo and beep-beep. They had fun with their trains, but they also shared them with each other. One day, a big dog came into the yard where they were playing. The dog saw the trains and thought they were toys for him. He ran towards the track and grabbed one of the trains with his mouth. He shook it and tossed it in the air. Ben and Mia were scared and sad. They wanted their train back. "Hey, dog, that's our train!" Ben shouted. "Give it back, please!" But the dog did not listen. He ran away with the train, leaving the track broken and the other trains scattered. Ben and Mia cried. They did not know what to do. Then they saw their neighbor, Lily, who was older and kind. She saw what happened and felt sorry for them. She was compassionate. She had a leash and a treat in her hand. She walked towards the dog and called him. "Come here, boy, I have something for you!" she said. "You don't need that train, you need a friend!" The dog heard her voice and smelled the treat. He dropped the train and ran to her. She gave him the treat and put the leash on him. She brought him back to Ben and Mia. "Here you go, kids, I rescued your train!" she said. "I'm sorry the dog took it. He was lonely and curious. He didn't mean to hurt your toys. Do you want to pet him and say hello?" Ben and Mia wiped their tears and nodded. They were happy to have their train back, but they also felt sorry for the dog. They petted him and said hello. The dog wagged his tail and licked their faces. He was happy to have friends. "Thank you, Lily, you are very nice!" Ben and Mia said. "Can we play with you and the dog?" "Sure, you can!" Lily said. "But first, let's fix the track and put the trains back. Then we can have more fun together!" Ben and Mia agreed. They helped Lily fix the track and put the trains back. Then they played with Lily and the dog. They learned that sharing and caring are important, and that sometimes a rescue can lead to a friendship.

Once upon a time, there were two friends, Will and Carson. Will thought it was lots of fun to make motions with his hands. So he started to move his hands fast to make shapes in the air. Carson watched and said, “Why are you doing that?” Will said, “I'm making lights! I step back and it makes a cool shape.” Carson said, “That looks like a lot of fun! Can I try it?” Will said, “Of course! Just repeat what I do.” Carson followed Will's every move. He moved his hands in the same way and noticed a light in the shapes he made. The two friends laughed and kept making motions in the air. At the end of the day, Will said to Carson, “Making motions with our hands teaches us that when we do the same thing, we get the same result.” Carson nodded in agreement. He had learned a valuable lesson about the power of repeating something over and over again.
