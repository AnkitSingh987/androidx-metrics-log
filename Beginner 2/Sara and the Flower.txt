Category: Beginner
Title: Sara and the Flower

Sara and her mom went to the shop. They needed to buy shampoo for their hair. Sara liked the shampoo that smelled like flowers. Her mom liked the shampoo that was good for dry hair. "Can we buy both?" Sara asked her mom. "No, we only need one," her mom said. "We have to be honest and not waste money." "But I want the flower shampoo," Sara said. "It is so pretty and nice." Her mom smiled and hugged her. "I know, sweetie. But sometimes we have to choose. How about we buy the dry hair shampoo this time, and next time we buy the flower shampoo?" Sara thought for a moment. She wanted to make her mom happy. She nodded and said, "OK, mom. Let's buy the dry hair shampoo. But can we smell the flower shampoo first?" "Of course we can," her mom said. "Let's go and smell it together." They went to the shelf and picked up the flower shampoo. They opened the cap and sniffed it. It smelled like roses and lilies. Sara smiled and said, "It smells so good, mom. Thank you for letting me smell it." "You're welcome, Sara. You are a very honest and kind girl. I love you very much," her mom said. They put the flower shampoo back and took the dry hair shampoo. They paid for it and left the shop. Sara and her mom were happy. They had bought shampoo and they had been honest.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to play outside with his friends. They would run, jump, and laugh all day long. Tim's mom always told him to be safe when playing near the street. One day, Tim's friend, Sally, came over to play. They were having so much fun! Sally mentioned that they should play on the other side of the street. Tim remembered his mom's words about being safe near the street. Tim and Sally looked left and right for any traffic. They saw cars going fast, so they waited. When it was safe, they held hands and crossed the street together. Now they could play and have fun on the other side. Tim was happy they were safe and listened to his mom.

Once upon a time, there was a sweet little girl named Sarah who loved to bathe. Every morning and evening she would bounce into the bathroom and draw a bath. She would giggle as she put bubbles into the water, and she loved to splash around. One day, Sarah's mom called her into the kitchen. "Sarah, I have a surprise for you!" said her mom. Sarah smiled and walked over. To her delight, she saw a big bowl of ice-cream. "Oh wow, ice-cream!" exclaimed Sarah. "Yes, but don't forget to bathe first," her mom said. "Ok," Sarah said, feeling slightly disappointed. Sarah went back to the bathroom and hopped into the bath. She was so excited about the ice-cream that she only stayed in the bath for a few seconds before dashing back into the kitchen. "Mom, I'm done with my bath!" she said, trying to hide her weak smile. Her mom smiled back. "That's good, you can now enjoy your ice-cream!" Sarah was so happy that she started to dance around with joy. She went back to the kitchen and started to eat her delicious treat. Sarah was so glad that she remembered to bathe before enjoying her ice-cream!

Sam and Mia like to play treasure hunt. They use a map that Sam's dad made for them. The map has pictures of things they can find in the park. The first one to find all the things on the map wins. One day, they go to the park with the map and a basket. They start to look for the things on the map. They see a big tree, a red flower, a yellow ball, and a blue bird. They put them in the basket. "Look, Mia, I found a green frog!" Sam says. He shows Mia the frog in his hand. "It is the last thing on the map. I win!" Mia looks at the map. She sees that there is one more thing on the map that Sam did not find. It is a star. She looks around and sees a shiny star on a swing. She runs to the swing and grabs the star. "Wait, Sam, you did not win!" Mia says. She shows Sam the star in her hand. "This is the last thing on the map. I win!" Sam looks at the map again. He sees that Mia is right. He did not see the star on the map. He feels sad. He likes to win. "I'm sorry, Sam. I did not mean to make you sad," Mia says. She gives Sam a hug. "You are a good friend. You are reliable. You always share and help me. Do you want to share the star with me?" Sam smiles. He hugs Mia back. He does not feel sad anymore. He likes to share and help Mia too. He says, "Yes, Mia, I want to share the star with you. You are a good friend too. You are reliable. You always play and listen to me. Let's play treasure hunt again tomorrow." They put the star in the basket with the other things. They hold hands and walk back to Sam's dad. They show him the things they found. They tell him they both win. Sam's dad smiles. He is proud of them. He says, "You are both winners. You are both reliable. You are both good friends."
