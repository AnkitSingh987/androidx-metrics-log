Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim and the Drawing

One hot day, a boy named Tim went to play outside. He saw his friend, Sam, who was drawing on his arm with a marker. Tim was curious and asked, "What are you doing, Sam?" Sam said, "I'm making marks on my arm. It's fun! Do you want to try?" Tim thought about it, but he knew his mom would not like it. He said, "No, thank you. My mom says not to draw on our arms." Later, Sam's arm started to hurt. It was red and hot. He cried and went to his mom. His mom was not happy. She said, "I told you not to draw on your arm. Now it is hurt. Let's clean it and make it better." Sam learned his lesson. He knew that he should listen to his mom and not draw on his arm. Tim was glad that he did not draw on his arm too. From that day on, they both played with markers only on paper.

Once upon a time, there was a little dog named Spot. He lived in a hot place with many cactuses. Spot loved to follow his friend, a big cactus named Mr. Green. Mr. Green was very tall and had many arms. One day, Spot saw a small ball near Mr. Green. "I want to play with the ball," said Spot. "Can you help me, Mr. Green?" Mr. Green could not talk, but he moved one of his arms to help Spot get the ball. Spot was very happy to play with the ball. Spot was a persistent dog. He played with the ball all day, always following it when it rolled away. He even played near other cactuses. At the end of the day, Spot was tired but happy. He thanked Mr. Green for helping him and went to sleep.

Once upon a time, there was a big place called University. Many people went there to learn new things. It was a fun place to be. In University, people played games. The games were competitive. They had to try their best to win. But they also had fun and made friends. At the end of the day, everyone at University was happy. They had learned new things and played games. They knew that being at University was a good place to be.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She had a big room with a wardrobe. The wardrobe was full of pretty clothes. Lily loved her wardrobe very much. One day, Lily played with her toys all day. She was very tired. It was time for her to sleep. She put on her cozy pajamas and went to bed. Her mom gave her a hug and a kiss goodnight. As Lily slept, she had a dream. In her dream, her wardrobe came to life. It danced and twirled around the room. The pretty clothes inside the wardrobe danced too. Lily woke up with a big smile. She knew it was just a dream, but it was a very fun dream.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl called Maria. She liked to stack things, like boxes and books. One day, Maria went to her room to get some of her blocks to stack. When Maria arrived in her room, she saw the biggest stack she had ever seen! But the stack was so tall that it was about to fall over. "Oh no!" Maria said. "I shouldn't have made this stack so tall. It looks so silly and stupid!" Suddenly, a voice came from behind her. "Don't worry, Maria," the voice said. "I can help you. Here, let me get it for you." Maria turned around and saw her big brother, smiling. He fixed the stack back up and made it balanced and stable. He ruffled Maria's hair and said, "Don't be stupid and make the stack too tall next time. Ok?" Maria smiled and nodded. She thanked her brother and said, "I won't do it again!"

Once upon a time, there lived two best friends, Rusty and Lucy. They played together every day and shared a special bond. One day, Rusty decided he wanted to try something new. 'Let's make a video!' said Rusty with a big smile. 'Yes let's!' replied Lucy, excitedly. They both got to work, collecting leaves and stones to make their video. After a few minutes, they had part of their video ready. 'We need more things to add to the video though', said Rusty. Lucy had just the thing. 'My lucky penny!' she said, holding up her lucky penny. After adding the penny to the video, they were both happy with the finished result. 'We did it!' shouted Rusty, high-fiving Lucy. 'We sure did!', said Lucy, 'Now let's show our friends'.
