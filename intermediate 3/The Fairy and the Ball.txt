Category: Intermediate
Title: The Fairy and the Ball

Once upon a time, in a big, green forest, there lived a little fairy. The fairy had a small house made of pretty flowers. She loved to fly around the trees and play with her friends, the birds and the bugs. One day, the fairy saw a big, red ball. She did not know what it was, so she went closer to look at it. She started to wonder if it was something fun to play with. But the ball was very hot and dangerous. A wise, old owl saw the fairy and called out to her, "Be careful, little fairy! That ball is hot and not safe to touch." The fairy listened to the owl and did not touch the ball. She said "Thank you, wise owl, for telling me. I will not play with it." The fairy went back to her house and played with her friends, happy that she was safe. The dangerous ball did not hurt her, and she had many more fun days in the big, green forest.

Tim and Lily like to play in the garden. They find some mud and sticks and stones. They want to design something with them. "What are you making, Tim?" Lily asks. "I am making a castle for the king and queen," Tim says. He puts some mud on a big stone and sticks some sticks on top. "See? This is the tower." "That is interesting," Lily says. She looks at her mud and thinks. She wants to make something different. She decides to make a cake for the king and queen. She rolls some mud into a ball and puts some stones on it. "See? This is the cake." "That is interesting, too," Tim says. He looks at his castle and her cake. He thinks they are both good. He wants to show them to the king and queen. He says, "Let's go and find them." They leave their mud things in the garden and run to the house. They look for the king and queen. They are their mom and dad. They find them in the kitchen. "Mom, dad, we made something for you in the garden," Tim says. "Come and see." "What did you make, sweeties?" Mom asks. "We made a castle and a cake with mud and sticks and stones," Lily says. "They are interesting." Mom and dad smile. They think their children are creative. They say, "Okay, let's go and see." They follow Tim and Lily to the garden. They see the mud things. They are not happy. They are angry. The mud and sticks and stones are dirty and messy. They make the garden look bad. "Tim, Lily, what have you done?" Mom says. "This is not a castle and a cake. This is a pile of mud and trash. You have ruined the garden." "We are sorry, mom," Tim says. "We wanted to design something for you." "We are sorry, dad," Lily says. "We thought they were interesting." Mom and dad shake their heads. They are not pleased. They say, "You have to clean up this mess. Now. And no more playing with mud and sticks and stones. Ever." Tim and Lily are sad. They have to throw away their mud things. They have to wash their hands and clothes. They have to say sorry again. They have no fun. They have a bad ending.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim was scared of the loud thunder that came with the big rain. One day, the sky was dark and the thunder was very loud. Tim felt hurt in his heart because of his fear. Tim's mom saw that he was scared. She wanted to help him not be afraid. She had an idea. "Tim, let's attach a soft pillow to your ears. It will make the thunder sound quiet," she said. Tim and his mom attached the soft pillow to his ears. The thunder was not so loud anymore. Tim felt happy and safe. He said, "Thank you, Mom. I'm not scared of the thunder now." They both smiled and enjoyed the sound of the rain together.

Once upon a time, there was a small girl named Lily. She loved to eat waffles. She had a friend named Sam. Sam was very competitive. They liked to play games together. One day, Lily said, "Sam, let's invite our friends to a waffle party!" Sam thought that was a great idea. They invited all their friends to come and eat waffles. Lily and Sam also planned a fun game. At the waffle party, everyone was happy and eating waffles. Then, Lily said, "Let's play the waffle game!" In the game, the kids had to stack waffles on top of each other. The one with the tallest waffle tower would win. They all knew Sam was competitive, so they were excited to see who would win. The game started, and everyone was having fun. The waffle towers grew taller and taller. At the end, Lily and Sam had the tallest towers. Everyone cheered for them. Sam was happy, but he was not the only winner. Lily and Sam learned that playing together was more fun than being competitive.
