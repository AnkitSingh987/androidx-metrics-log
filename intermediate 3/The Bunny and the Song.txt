Category: Intermediate
Title: The Bunny and the Song

Once upon a time, there was a little bunny who loved to hop. One day, he heard a happy song. The song was so fair and nice. The bunny wanted to find out who was singing the song. He hopped and hopped to find the song. He found a big bird singing the song. The bunny asked the bird, "Can you teach me your fair song?" The bird said, "Yes, but you must give me your food." The bunny thought and thought. He knew he would be hungry, but he wanted to learn the song. So, he gave the bird his food. The bird taught the bunny the song. The bunny was so happy. But then, the bunny was very hungry. He hopped and hopped to find more food, but there was no food. The bunny was sad and hungry. He should not have given away his food. The moral of the story is to think before you give away something important.

One day, a playful lady named Jane went for a walk in the park. She saw a long line of ants marching on the ground. Jane thought it was fun to watch them move in a row. She decided to march like the ants, too. As Jane marched, she met a little girl named Sue. Sue saw Jane marching and thought it looked fun. Sue asked, "Can I march with you?" Jane smiled and said, "Yes, let's march together!" So, Jane and Sue marched like the ants in the park. They had a lot of fun and laughed as they played together. The ants kept marching, and Jane and Sue followed them. It was a happy day for everyone in the park.

One day, a dog named Spot went to work with his friend, a girl named Sue. They went to a big lab where Sue's dad worked. In the lab, they saw many things like big tables, chairs, and lots of things to play with. In the lab, Spot saw a wild bird fly in through the window. The bird was very fast and made a lot of noise. Spot wanted to play with the bird, so he ran and jumped, trying to catch it. Sue laughed and said, "Spot, you are so silly!" Sue's dad came into the lab and saw Spot and the wild bird. He opened the window wide and the bird flew away. Spot was sad, but Sue's dad said, "Don't worry, Spot. You did a good job helping us in the lab today." Spot wagged his tail and felt happy again.

Once upon a time, there was a small, honest dog named Max. Max loved to play with his favorite toy, a soft triangle. Max would rub the triangle with his paws and carry it around in his mouth. One sunny day, Max was playing with his triangle in the park. A little girl named Lily saw Max and wanted to play with him. Lily asked Max, "Can I play with your triangle, please?" Max wagged his tail and shared his triangle with Lily. Together, Max and Lily played with the triangle. They would rub it and toss it in the air. They laughed and had lots of fun. Max and Lily became best friends, and they always played with the triangle in the park. And they lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a polite elephant named Ella. Ella lived in a big forest with many friends. Every day, she would play with her friends and eat yummy leaves from the trees. One sunny day, Ella and her friends decided to have a big picnic. They all brought their favorite food to eat. Ella brought her favorite leaves, and her friends brought fruits and nuts. They all sat down to eat and share their food. As they were eating, something unexpected happened. A tiny mouse named Max came to join them. Max was very hungry, but he didn't have any food. Ella and her friends were surprised, but they decided to share their food with Max. Max was so happy and thankful. From that day on, Max the mouse and Ella the elephant became best friends.

Once upon a time, there was a great driver named Tom. He drove a big, red bus. Tom was very nice and always said "please" when he talked to people. One day, Tom met a little girl named Sue. Sue wanted to ride the big, red bus. "Please, may I ride the bus?" Sue asked. Tom smiled and said, "Of course! Please, come in and find a seat." Sue sat by the window and looked at all the pretty things outside. They saw trees, flowers, and birds. Sue was very happy. Tom was a great driver and made sure Sue got home safe. From that day on, Sue always rode the big, red bus with her friend, the great driver Tom.
