Category: Intermediate
Title: Ben the Baby and the Ball

Once upon a time, in a small house, there was a baby named Ben. Ben was a very happy baby. He liked to play with his toys and laugh all day long. One sunny day, Ben went outside to play with his ball. He threw the ball up in the air and watched it rise high. But then, something unexpected happened. Ben started to feel dizzy. He sat down on the grass and rubbed his eyes. When Ben opened his eyes, he saw that his ball had changed into a big, colorful balloon. The balloon was so big that it could lift him off the ground. Ben held onto the balloon, and it made him rise up, up, up into the sky. He was not dizzy anymore. He laughed and waved at the birds flying by. And that's how Ben had the most fun and surprising day ever.

One day, a little girl named Lucy went for a walk in the park. She saw a big tree with a zip on it. Lucy was very curious about the zip. She thought, "What could be inside the tree?" She wanted to find out, so she went closer and put her hand on the zip. Lucy felt a little frightened. She was scared of what might come out of the tree. But she was also very excited. So, she took a deep breath and pulled the zip down. The tree opened up, and out came a friendly squirrel. The squirrel said, "Hi, I'm Sam! I love to dance!" Lucy smiled and said, "I love to dance too!" So, Lucy and Sam danced together in the park. They had lots of fun, and Lucy was not frightened anymore. She was very happy that she had opened the zip on the tree and found a new friend.

Once upon a time, there was a calm port. One day, a ship sailed into the port. A boy jumped onto the dock and shouted out with joy. He had been on the ship for many days and was so happy to be back on dry land. The boy started to explore the port. He climbed onto a big boat and shouted louder than ever. He was having so much fun discovering the port. Suddenly, a seagull flew over to the boat. The boy smiled and shouted hello to the bird. The bird didn't reply, but the boy was still happy. Then, they heard a loud bell sound. The boy looked up at the bell tower and saw a man ringing it. He shouted out with excitement. The boy ran back to his ship and said goodbye to the port. It was time to set sail again. He waved goodbye to the seagull as the ship sailed away. Everything was calm again at the port.

Anna liked to play with her toys. She had many toys: dolls, cars, blocks, books and more. She kept them in a big box in her room. She was very organized. She put each toy in its place when she was done playing. One day, her brother Ben came to her room. He was bored. He saw the big box of toys. He wanted to play with them too. He did not ask Anna. He just grabbed the toys and threw them around. He made a big mess. He broke some of the toys. He made a wreck. Anna heard the noise. She ran to her room. She saw Ben and the toys. She was very angry. She yelled at Ben. She told him to stop. She told him to go away. She told him he was bad. She cried. Ben felt sorry. He did not mean to make Anna sad. He did not know she was so organized. He tried to fix the toys. He tried to put them back in the box. He said he was sorry. He hugged Anna. Anna calmed down. She saw Ben was sorry. She forgave him. She helped him fix the toys. She showed him how to be organized. She said it was okay. She hugged Ben. They played together. They were happy.

Once there was a duck called Dan. He was a very impatient duck and he wanted to succeed at everything he attempted. He was the only duck who had swimming lessons because he was determined to learn to swim. Dan practiced very hard, but sometimes things didn't go as well as he wanted. One day, Dan was in the water practicing as usual. His teacher, Ms. Swan, was watching him. “Dan, you're trying really hard. But I can see you're a bit impatient. I have a plan for you to help you succeed," she said. Dan listened with interest. “What is the plan?" he asked. Smiling, Ms. Swan said “If you stay very still in the water and don't move, one of your friends will come swimming along. Then you can follow them and you'll be able to swim just like they do.” Dan was excited. He did exactly as Ms. Swan suggested and waited in the water. Within minutes, one of his friends came along and Dan quickly followed. Before long, Dan was swimming like a pro. The lesson was a success! Dan had used the plan that Ms. Swan had given him to finally succeed at swimming.
