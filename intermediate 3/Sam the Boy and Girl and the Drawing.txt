Category: Intermediate
Title: Sam the Boy and Girl and the Drawing

Once there was a boy and a girl named Sam and Susie. They were best friends and did lots of fun things together like playing hide and seek and drawing pictures. One day they were outside and Sam got a match from his pocket. Susie asked if she could have it and Sam said no. They began to quarrel, but it wasn't very loud. Although it was a mild quarrel, Susie still didn't get the match. She was very sad and started to cry. Sam felt sorry and said, "Let's play a game. I'll give you the match if you can win." But Susie shook her head. "No, I don't want to do that. I just wanted the match," Susie said. Sam thought for a moment and then grinned. He handed Susie the match and said, "Okay, I'll give it to you as a present. Now you have a match." Susie was so happy and gave Sam a big hug. From that day on, they never quarreled again.

Once upon a time there was a pine tree. All day it seemed a bit sad, and all night it was very lonely. One day, a friendly magic fairy showed up, determined to fix the problem. The fairy flew around the tree and sprinkled some magical dust on its branches. Immediately, the pine tree felt better. The magic fairy was so happy. The next day, the fairy came to the tree again, this time with a basket of sour apples. She hung it from one of the tree's branches so that the pine tree could feed on them. The tree was so thankful and it felt so much happier. It also had many more fun friends around. From then on, the pine tree was very content and never lonely. The tree and the fairy stayed friends forever. The tree's happiness was fixed and it would never be sad again!

One day, a boy named Tim went to the park. He saw a big, dry tree. Under the tree, there was a cardboard box. Tim was happy to find the box. He thought it would be fun to play with. Tim called his friend, Lily. "Lily, come to the park! I found a fun cardboard box!" he said. Lily ran to the park to see the box. She was excited too. They both played with the box under the dry tree. They pretended the cardboard box was a car. Tim and Lily took turns driving the car around the park. They laughed and had so much fun. After playing, they sat under the dry tree and talked. They agreed it was the best day at the park.

Once upon a time, there was a noisy train. The train went on a rail every day. The train loved to go fast and make loud sounds. The train was big and strong. One day, something new happened. The noisy train saw a little bird on the rail. The train did not want to scare the little bird. So, the train went slow and quiet this time. The little bird was happy and flew away. From that day on, the noisy train learned to be quiet sometimes. The train still loved to go fast, but it also liked making new friends. The train and the little bird became good friends, and they played together every day.

Once upon a time, in a big zoo, there was a big rhinoceros. He was orange and very nice. All the kids loved to see him. One day, the orange rhinoceros got a letter. The letter said, "We will send you a friend." The rhinoceros was very happy. He wanted a friend to play with. Soon, a big truck came to the zoo. The truck had a new friend for the rhinoceros. The friend was a big, blue elephant. The orange rhinoceros and the blue elephant played all day. They were very happy. And all the kids loved to see them play.

Once there was a little boy named Tim. He loved to play with water. One day, his mom asked him to wash his hands. Tim went to the bathroom and saw the faucet. He turned it on, but the water was hot. Tim did not like hot water. "Mom, the water is hot. I don't want to wash my hands," Tim called out. His mom came in and felt the water. She agreed it was hot. She turned the faucet to make the water cold. "Thank you, mom," Tim said. He was happy to accept the cold water. He washed his hands and splashed a little. His mom laughed and dried his hands with a towel. From that day on, Tim always checked the water before washing his hands. He learned how to turn the faucet to make the water just right. Tim and his mom were both happy.
