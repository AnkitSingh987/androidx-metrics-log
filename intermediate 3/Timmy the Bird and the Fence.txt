Category: Intermediate
Title: Timmy the Bird and the Fence

Once upon a time, there was a friendly little bird named Timmy. Timmy lived in a big tree with his family. They were all very happy. One day, Timmy saw a man with a hammer. The man was fixing a broken fence. Timmy flew down and said, "Hello, Mr. Man! What are you doing with that hammer?" The man replied, "Hi, Timmy! I am fixing this fence so it will be strong again." Timmy watched the man work and thought about how he used to hate hammers because they were loud and scary. But now, he saw that hammers could also be helpful. Timmy learned that even things we don't like can be good sometimes. And that was the moral of the story: don't hate something just because you don't understand it.

Lily and Ben were playing with their toys in the living room. Lily had a laser that made red lights and sounds. Ben had a normal car that he could push and roll. Lily liked her laser a lot, but she also wanted to play with Ben's car. "Can I have your car, Ben?" Lily asked. "No, it's mine. You have your laser," Ben said. "Please, Ben. Just for a little bit," Lily begged. "No, no, no. Go away, Lily," Ben said. He pushed her with his hand. Lily felt sad and angry. She wanted to make Ben sorry for being mean. She had an idea. She pointed her laser at Ben's car and pressed the button. The laser made a loud noise and a red light flashed on the car. "Hey, stop that! You're breaking my car!" Ben shouted. Lily laughed. She teased Ben with her laser. She moved it up and down, left and right, making the car light up and beep. "Ha ha, Ben. Your car is a laser car now. It's not normal anymore," Lily said. Ben was scared and mad. He grabbed his car and ran to his mom. He told her what Lily did. Mom was not happy. She took the laser from Lily and scolded her. "Lily, that was not nice. You should not tease your brother with your laser. You could hurt him or his car. You have to say sorry to Ben and share your toys," Mom said. Lily felt bad. She knew she was wrong. She said sorry to Ben and gave him a hug. She let him play with her laser for a while. Ben forgave her and let her play with his car. They played nicely together and had fun. Mom smiled and gave them a snack.

Tommy liked to examine things. He liked to look at them very closely and see what they were made of. He had a magnifying glass that his dad gave him. He used it to examine leaves, rocks, bugs and anything else he could find. One day, he saw a spider on the wall. It was black and shiny and had eight legs. Tommy was eager to examine it. He ran to get his magnifying glass and came back to the wall. He held the glass close to the spider and said, "Hello, spider. What are you doing?" The spider did not answer. It moved a little bit and spun a thin thread from its back. Tommy watched in wonder as the spider made a web. He thought it was very clever and beautiful. He said, "Wow, spider. You are very good at making webs. Can I touch it?" The spider did not like Tommy's question. It felt scared and angry. It did not want Tommy to touch its web. It thought Tommy was too big and loud and rude. It hissed and showed its fangs. It said, "No, you cannot touch it. Go away, or I will bite you!" Tommy was surprised and scared. He did not know that spiders could talk. He did not know that spiders could bite. He dropped his magnifying glass and ran away. He said, "Sorry, spider. I did not mean to bother you. Please do not bite me!" The spider watched Tommy go. It felt relieved and proud. It finished its web and waited for some flies. It said, "That was a close one. I do not like humans. They are too curious and nosy. They should leave me alone."
