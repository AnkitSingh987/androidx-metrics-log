Category: Intermediate
Title: The Curious Prince and the Ball

Once upon a time, there was a curious prince. He lived in a big castle with his mom and dad. The prince liked to play with his toys and look around his big home. One day, the prince found a box in his room. He said, "What is inside?" He opened the box and found a big, red ball. He was very happy and wanted to play with it. The prince threw the ball and it went very far. But then, the ball hit a vase and it broke. The prince was sad and scared. He said, "Mom, I did not mean to break the vase." His mom came in and saw the broken vase. But she was not mad. She said, "It is okay, my prince. That vase was not special. It was just old and we were going to throw it away." The prince was happy again. He knew that sometimes things break, but it is okay. He played with the big, red ball all day and had lots of fun.

Once upon a time, there was a kind and compassionate bunny named Bob. Bob loved to eat celery. One day, he went to the garden to find some. As Bob was eating his celery, he noticed a little bird who was sad. The bird could not find any food. Bob wanted to help the bird, so he shared his celery with him. The bird was very happy and thanked Bob. From that day on, they became best friends. They always helped each other and shared their food. And they lived happily ever after.

One day, a cute bird was flying in the sky. The bird was very tired. It wanted to sleep. It saw a tree and thought, "I will sleep on that tree." The bird sat on a branch and said, "Hello, tree! I am tired. Can I sleep here?" The tree said, "Yes, cute bird! You can sleep on me. I will keep you safe." The bird started to sleep. It was happy and safe on the tree. But then, a big wind came. The wind was very strong. The cute bird tried to hold on, but it could not. The wind blew the bird away from the tree. The bird was very sad. It wanted to sleep, but now it could not. The bird flew away, looking for a new place to sleep.

Tom and Anna were twins who liked to play outside. One day, they saw a big cloud in the sky that looked like a funnel. It was a tornado, and it was coming closer to their house. "Tom, Anna, come inside now!" their mom shouted. "There is a tornado and it is very dangerous!" Tom ran to the house, but Anna was stubborn. She wanted to see the tornado better. She did not listen to her mom. "Anna, please, come inside!" Tom said. "The tornado can hurt you!" But Anna did not move. She watched the tornado spin and roar. It was scary, but also exciting. The tornado was getting closer and closer. It picked up trees and cars and threw them in the air. Anna saw a big truck flying towards her. She was very afraid. She wished she had listened to her mom. "Help!" she screamed. Tom heard her scream. He ran out of the house and grabbed her hand. He pulled her inside just in time. The truck crashed into the fence, but it did not hit them. They hugged each other and cried. Their mom hugged them too. She was very relieved. "I'm sorry, mom," Anna said. "I was stubborn and I did not listen to you. I will never do that again." "I'm glad you are safe, Anna," mom said. "But you should always listen to me when I tell you something. I love you and I want to protect you." "I love you too, mom," Anna said. "And I love you, Tom. Thank you for saving me." "I love you too, Anna," Tom said. "But don't be stubborn again. It is not fun to visit a tornado."

Once upon a time, there was a wise old lady. She lived near a station, at the end of a village. One day, the lady happened to spot a little boy walking around the station. He had a toy but it didn't seem to be working. The woman approached the boy and asked, "What is wrong, my child?" The boy replied, "My toy isn't working. Can you help me?" The wise old lady thought of a solution. She said, "Why don't you borrow my toy? It will make you much happier!" The little boy smiled and thanked the woman. He was so happy to be able to borrow the toy. The old lady was happy too, to see the little boy happy. From that day on, the wise old lady and the little boy were friends. Whenever the little boy needed a toy, he would borrow it from the wise old lady.
