Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Storm

One day, a boy named Tim went outside to play. He saw some weird clouds in the sky. They were big and dark. Tim was not scared. He knew what the clouds meant. They meant a storm was coming. Tim saw a flash of lightning in the sky. He knew he had to count to see how far away the storm was. He started counting: one, two, three, four, five. Then he heard a big boom! The storm was five seconds away. Tim was excited to see the storm. As the storm got closer, Tim counted the lightning again. One, two, three, four. The storm was even closer now! Tim ran inside to tell his mom about the storm. They watched the lightning together and counted the seconds. Tim loved watching the storm with his mom.

Once upon a time, there was a big oak tree. It was very stubborn and did not like to share its space with others. One sunny day, a little boy named Tim came to play near the oak tree. Tim wanted to step on the roots of the oak tree to climb up and see the sky. But the stubborn oak did not want Tim to step on its roots. The oak tree shook its branches to scare Tim away. Tim felt sad and did not know what to do. Tim had an idea. He asked his friends, the birds, to help him. The birds flew up and sang a sweet song to the oak tree. The stubborn oak tree liked the song and stopped shaking its branches. Tim was happy and stepped on the roots to climb the oak tree. They all became friends and played together every day.

Once upon a time, there was a big bear named Bobo. He loved to play with his blocks. One day, he made a tall stack of blocks. It was very important to him. Bobo's friend, a little bird named Lulu, came to visit. "Wow, that's a big stack!" said Lulu. Bobo was proud of his stack. They played together, but Bobo didn't want to surrender his stack. He was afraid it would fall. Lulu had an idea. "Let's make a bigger stack together!" she said. Bobo agreed. They worked together and made the biggest stack ever. Bobo was happy to surrender his old stack for the new one. They learned that playing together was more important and fun.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sally. She was playing in her backyard one sunny day. Suddenly, she heard a voice call to her, "Sally! Hurry! Come to the land!" Sally was very worried. She hadn't been to this land before. Who was calling her? She wished her mom was there to help her. Sally was so scared but she was brave. She held her breath and ran towards the voice. The voice was coming from a tall tree. When Sally reached the tree, she saw a small animal. It was a chipmunk! The chipmunk said to Sally, "I'm very sorry to hurry you. My home is in danger! We need to hurry and save my family!" Sally was so happy she could help the chipmunk! They ran together towards the chipmunk's home. Sally was so relieved when she arrived safe and sound with the chipmunk's family. Everyone was happy and Sally made a new friend.

One day, a little girl named Sue went to her bookcase. She wanted to get her favorite book. But the book was stuck! Sue tried to pull it out, but it did not move. Sue thought of a plan. She would squeeze the book out. She pushed the books on both sides. The book started to come out. Sue was happy. But then, she felt something cold on her foot. It was water! The water was coming from the window. Sue ran to get a towel. She cleaned the water and closed the window. Now, she could read her favorite book in her warm, dry room.

One day, a little tiger named Tim went for a walk in the forest. He saw a big tree with no leaves. The tree looked sad. Tim wanted to help the tree, so he thought of a fun idea. Tim said to the tree, "I will decorate you and make you pretty." The tree smiled and said, "Thank you, little tiger." Tim found some leaves, flowers, and colorful berries. He put them on the tree's branches. The tree was very happy with its new look. It said, "Thank you, little tiger Tim. You made me feel special." Tim was happy too. He knew he had made a new friend. From that day on, Tim and the tree played together in the forest, and they were always happy.
