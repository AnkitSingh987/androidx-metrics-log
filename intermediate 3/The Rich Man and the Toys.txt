Category: Intermediate
Title: The Rich Man and the Toys

Once upon a time, there was a rich man. He lived in a big house with an attic. The attic was full of old toys and games. One day, the rich man went up to the attic to find a toy for his little boy. In the attic, he found a toy gun. The rich man thought, "My little boy will love to play with this toy gun and shoot it." So, he took the toy gun down to his little boy. The little boy was very happy to see the toy gun. He said, "Thank you, Daddy! I will shoot the toy gun and have fun!" The rich man and his little boy played with the toy gun all day. They had a great time shooting the toy gun and laughing together. And they lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Sue. She had a colorful napkin. She loved her napkin very much. It had red, blue, and yellow colors on it. Sue took her napkin everywhere she went. One day, Sue wanted to improve her napkin. She thought it needed more colors. So, she took her paint and started to paint on the napkin. She painted green, orange, and purple. Sue was happy with her improved napkin. But, Sue made a big mess with the paint. The paint went all over her room. Her mom came in and saw the mess. She was very sad and angry. Sue's mom took away her colorful napkin. Sue was sad and cried. The end.

Once upon a time, in a small village, there was a girl named Ann. Ann loved to learn new words. One day, she met a boy named Tom. Tom knew a very impressive word. Ann wanted to learn it too. Tom said, "I will teach you the word 'spell.' To spell is to say the letters in a word. Like 'c-a-t' spells 'cat'." Ann was very happy to learn this new word. She said, "Thank you, Tom. Can I borrow your word 'spell' and use it when I talk to my friends?" Tom smiled and said, "Of course, Ann! You can borrow the word 'spell' and share it with your friends. Sharing words is fun!" Ann was very happy. She went to her friends and taught them the impressive word 'spell.' They all had fun spelling words together. And they all became better friends.

One day, a boy named Tim went to school. He had a black eraser. It was big and soft. Tim liked his eraser a lot. He used it to fix his drawings. At school, Tim met his friend, Sam. Sam saw the black eraser and said, "Can you lend me your eraser, Tim? I need to fix my drawing too." Tim said, "Yes, Sam. I can lend you my eraser. But please give it back when you are done." Sam used the black eraser to fix his drawing. But then, something unexpected happened. The eraser started to talk! The eraser said, "Hello, Sam! I am a magic eraser. I can help you draw very well." Sam was very surprised. He told Tim about the magic eraser. Tim and Sam used the black eraser to make beautiful drawings. They were very happy. And they always shared the magic eraser with their friends.

Once upon a time, there was a kid named Tim. Tim wanted to be tough like his big brother, Sam. Sam was big, strong, and always helped people. Tim thought being tough was cool. One day, Tim saw a cat stuck in a tree. He wanted to help the cat, but he was too small to reach it. Tim felt sad. Then, he had an idea. He found a long stick and used it to help the cat come down. The cat was happy, and Tim felt tough. Tim learned that being tough was not just about being big and strong. It was also about helping others and being brave. Tim was happy to be a tough kid, just like his big brother.

Anna was a regular girl who liked to play with her toys and watch cartoons. She had a big brother, Ben, who was very smart and went to university. Anna wanted to be like Ben and learn many things, but she was too little to go to university. One day, Ben came home with a big book and put it on the table. Anna was curious and wanted to see what was in the book. She climbed on a chair and tried to reach the book, but it was too far. She stretched her arm as much as she could, but she still could not touch the book. "Anna, what are you doing?" Ben asked, when he saw her on the chair. "That book is not for you, it is for my homework. It has hard words and pictures that you won't understand." "But I want to see, I want to learn!" Anna said, and stretched her arm more. She felt the chair wobble and lose balance. She screamed as she fell to the floor, along with the book and the chair. Ben ran to help her, but it was too late. Anna had hit her head on the corner of the table and was bleeding. She was crying and hurting. Ben called their mom and dad, who came home quickly and took Anna to the hospital. The doctor said Anna had a bad injury and needed surgery. She might not wake up again. Ben felt very sad and guilty. He wished he had let Anna see the book, or put it somewhere safe. He wished he had been nicer to his sister, and played with her more. He wished he could say sorry to her, and tell her he loved her. But he could not. He had lost his regular girl, and his best friend.
