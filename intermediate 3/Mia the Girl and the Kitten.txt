Category: Intermediate
Title: Mia the Girl and the Kitten

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Mia. She loved to play outside and explore new places. One sunny day, Mia went on an adventure to an unknown place. She was very excited to see what she could find. As Mia walked and walked, she saw a big tree. Behind the tree, there was something hiding. Mia was curious and wanted to reveal what it was. She slowly peeked behind the tree and found a cute, little kitten. Mia was so happy to find the kitten in the unknown place. She picked up the kitten and took it back to her home. Mia and the kitten became best friends and had lots of fun adventures together. And they lived happily ever after.

Once upon a time, there was a boy named Tom. Tom was a foolish boy. He liked to play and laugh all day. One day, Tom went to the shop to buy some rice for his mom. At the shop, Tom met a man. The man said, "Give me your rice and I will give you a toy." Tom was happy and gave the man the rice. The man gave Tom a toy and went away. Tom went home and showed his mom the toy. His mom was angry. She said, "Tom, we need rice for dinner, not a toy! Go back to the shop and pay for more rice." Tom felt sad and went back to the shop. He paid for the rice and brought it home. From that day, Tom learned not to be foolish.

John was walking in the park with his mom and dad. He loved to observe all the animals, plants and people around him. He saw a large tree with big leaves and a funny looking red fruit hanging from it. "Look, Daddy! That's a radish! What is it doing there?" he asked his daddy. "That's a very special radish tree, John. It doesn't grow these funny red fruits down here, but it does in a distant land," his father explained. John was so excited to see the special radish tree in the park. He learned that radishes can only grow in certain places. He smiled and was happy to observe the distant radish tree in the park.

Once upon a time, in a small town, there was a big bridge. The bridge was very old and not ready for people to walk on it. But the people in the town needed to cross the river to get to the other side. One day, a little boy named Tim wanted to play with his friends on the other side of the river. He went to his mom and said, "Mom, I want to go to the other side. Please help me!" His mom knew the bridge was not safe, so she said, "No, Tim, the bridge is not ready. We must wait." Tim was sad, but he knew his mom was right. The next day, the people in the town came together and demanded a new, safe bridge. They worked hard and built a strong bridge. Now, Tim could play with his friends on the other side of the river, and everyone was happy. The town had a new, safe bridge that was ready for everyone to use.

Lily and Ben are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a lady doing yoga on the grass. She bends and twists her body in funny ways. "Look at her!" Ben says. "She is so ugly!" Lily feels bad. She thinks the lady is not ugly. She thinks the lady is strong and happy. "Why do you say that?" Lily asks. "She is just doing yoga. Yoga is good for you. It makes you calm and healthy." Ben laughs. "Yoga is silly. It is for girls and babies. You are a baby, Lily. I dare you to do yoga with her. Then you will be ugly too!" Lily is not a baby. She is brave and kind. She does not like Ben's dare. She does not want to make fun of the lady. But she also does not want to be a chicken. "Okay, I will do yoga with her," Lily says. "But you have to do it too. Or you are a chicken." Ben does not want to do yoga. He thinks it is hard and boring. But he does not want to be a chicken either. He agrees to do yoga with Lily. They go to the lady and ask if they can join her. The lady smiles and says yes. She teaches them some yoga poses. Lily and Ben try to copy her. They stretch and balance and breathe. Lily likes yoga. She feels good and relaxed. She thinks the lady is nice and beautiful. Ben does not like yoga. He feels tired and sore. He thinks the lady is weird and annoying. After a while, the lady says goodbye and leaves. Lily and Ben go back to their bikes. "Did you like yoga?" Lily asks. "No, I hated it," Ben says. "It was stupid and hard. You are still ugly, Lily. And you are still a baby." Lily does not care. She knows Ben is wrong. She knows she is not ugly or a baby. She knows she is strong and happy. She smiles and says, "I liked yoga. It was fun and easy. You are still a chicken, Ben. And you are still mean." She rides away and leaves Ben alone. He feels bad and lonely. He wishes he was not a chicken or mean. He wishes he was more like Lily.
