Category: Intermediate
Title: The Story of the Bear and the Bird

One day, a big, heavy bear went to the park. He saw his friends, a tiny bird and a small cat, playing a game. They were trying to do a race to see who could win. The heavy bear wanted to play too. He asked his friends, "Can I do the race too? I want to win!" The tiny bird and the small cat said, "Yes! Let's all do the race together!" They all ran as fast as they could. The heavy bear was slow, but he did not give up. In the end, the tiny bird won the race. The heavy bear and the small cat clapped for their friend. They were all happy to play together.

One day, a girl named Sue went to the park. She took a basket with her. In the basket, she had spicy food to eat. Sue liked spicy food a lot. At the park, she saw her friend, Tim. "Hi, Tim!" said Sue. "I have spicy food in my basket. Do you want to try some?" Tim said, "Yes, I like spicy food too!" They sat down and started to eat. But then, something unexpected happened. A big wind came and blew the basket away! "Oh no!" said Sue. "Our spicy food is gone!" Tim had an idea. "Let's mix some new spicy food!" So, they went to Sue's house and mixed new spicy food together. They had a fun time and were happy that they could still eat spicy food.

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim had many toys, but his favorite toy was a big box of stamps. He loved to put stamps on paper and make pretty pictures. One day, Tim saw a new girl named Sue. Sue looked sad and unknown to Tim. He wanted to make her feel happy. Tim thought about what he could do to make Sue feel better. He remembered his stamps and thought, "I can share my stamps with Sue!" So, Tim went to Sue with his box of stamps and asked her if she wanted to play with him. Sue's face lit up, and she said, "Yes, I would love to play with your stamps!" As they played with the stamps, Tim and Sue became good friends. They shared the stamps and made beautiful pictures together. Tim felt happy that he shared his stamps with Sue, and Sue felt happy because she had a new friend. The moral of the story is that sharing can bring happiness to others and create new friendships.

Once upon a time, there was a new toy named Tim. Tim was a tiny toy who could shrink. One day, Tim went out to play in the sun. He saw a big wet puddle and jumped in it. The water made him very wet. Tim wanted to get dry, so he went to sit in the sun. But the sun was so hot, Tim started to shrink. He got smaller and smaller. Tim was scared, but he knew he needed to find a way to get big again. Tim had an idea. He went back to the wet puddle and jumped in it. The water made him grow back to his normal size. Tim was so happy to be big again. From that day on, Tim learned to be careful when playing in the sun.

Once upon a time, in a big tree, there was a little dove. The dove had a small nest where it lay every night. The nest was warm and fit just right for the dove. The dove loved its nest very much. One sunny day, the dove saw a big bird trying to lay in a small nest. The big bird did not fit in the small nest. The big bird was sad. The little dove wanted to help the big bird. The dove talked to the big bird and they became friends. Together, they built a nest that was big enough for the big bird. The big bird was very happy. The little dove and the big bird learned that helping others can make everyone happy.

Tommy was so excited, he couldn't wait to go to the store. As he jumped up and down, his mom smiled. "Today, you're going to learn to sign," Mom said. Tommy clapped his hands together with glee. He had heard that signing was a way to communicate without speaking. At the store, Mom showed Tommy all the bright signs around them. She told him to pick the one he liked best. Tommy chose one with a friendly motor on it. They put the sign in the car and drove home. Once they arrived home, Mom gave Tommy a special book to practice. Tommy was busy flipping through the pages, but he was still having some trouble understanding. After a few minutes, Mom said with a smile, "You already know how to sign with your hands, don't you?" Tommy looked at his hands, and realized that he already knew how to make the motor sign with them. He smiled with pride, and repeated the sign over and over. Now, he knew how to communicate without speaking!
