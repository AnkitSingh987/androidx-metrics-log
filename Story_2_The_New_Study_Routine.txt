Title: The New Study Routine

Category: Intermediate

Elena sat at her small desk, staring blankly at the massive pile of textbooks, loose papers, and empty coffee cups. The clock on her laptop showed that it was 2:15 in the morning. She rubbed her tired eyes and tried to read the same paragraph for the fourth time, but the words just looked like meaningless shapes on the page. She had a major economics exam in two days, and she felt completely unprepared.

Elena was a second-year university student studying business administration. She was intelligent and ambitious, but she was also incredibly overwhelmed. Besides taking five difficult classes, she worked part-time at a local cafe to help pay for her living expenses. Her daily schedule was chaotic. She usually attended classes during the day, went to work in the late afternoon, and then tried to study late at night when she was already exhausted. 

When she received the results of her midterm exams earlier that week, she was shocked and deeply disappointed. She had gotten poor grades in almost all of her subjects. She knew she was smart enough to understand the material, but her current methods were clearly failing her. 

The next afternoon, Elena was sitting in the university library, staring sadly at her terrible economics grade. A shadow fell over her desk. She looked up and saw David, an older student who was known for being one of the top students in the business program. He was also a teaching assistant for one of her classes.

“Rough midterm?” David asked gently, noticing the red marks on her test paper.

Elena sighed and nodded. “It’s a disaster. I don’t understand what I’m doing wrong. I study all the time! I stay up until three in the morning reading the textbook, but when I get to the exam, my mind just goes completely blank. I can't remember anything.”

David pulled up a chair and sat down next to her. “I used to have the exact same problem during my first year,” he said kindly. “I thought that spending more hours studying meant I would get better grades. But it’s not about how many hours you study; it’s about how you use those hours. What does your current study routine look like?”

Elena laughed bitterly. “Routine? I don't really have one. I just try to read as much as possible the night before a test. I usually sit on my bed, drink a lot of energy drinks, and try to memorize the chapters.”

David smiled sympathetically. “Well, that’s your first problem. Studying on your bed tells your brain it's time to sleep, not time to focus. And staying up all night ruins your memory. If you want to improve, you need to build a realistic, effective study routine.”

“I want to, but I don't know where to start,” Elena admitted, feeling a little hopeful. “Between classes and my job at the cafe, I feel like I never have enough time.”

“You have more time than you think, but you have to manage it better,” David explained. He pulled a blank piece of paper from his notebook and drew a simple weekly calendar. “First, you need to schedule your study time just like you schedule your work shifts. Don't just wait until you have free time, because you will never have free time. Write down exactly when and where you will study.”

Elena watched as David filled in blocks of time. 

“Second,” David continued, “you have to stop trying to read for five hours straight. Your brain can't maintain focus for that long. Have you ever heard of the Pomodoro technique?”

Elena shook her head. “No, what is that?”

“It's a time management method,” David explained. “You choose a task, set a timer for twenty-five minutes, and work on it without any distractions. No phones, no social media, no chatting. When the timer goes off, you take a five-minute break. After you do this four times, you take a longer break, maybe twenty or thirty minutes. It keeps your mind fresh and prevents you from burning out.”

“That actually sounds manageable,” Elena said, feeling a small spark of motivation. 

“And finally, you need to practice active recall. Don't just passively read the book. Close the book and try to write down everything you remember. Test yourself. It's much harder, but it forces your brain to actually learn the information,” David concluded. 

Elena thanked David for his advice and decided to try his method immediately. She went home, cleaned all the trash and old cups off her desk, and put her phone in another room. She opened her planner and scheduled two hours of studying for that evening, before she got too tired. 

At seven o'clock, she sat down at her clean desk, opened her economics notes, and set a timer on her laptop for twenty-five minutes. 

The first few minutes were incredibly difficult. She felt a strong urge to check her phone, to get a snack, or to look out the window. Her mind kept wandering. But every time she got distracted, she looked at the timer ticking down and forced herself to focus back on the notes. When the timer finally went off, she felt a small sense of accomplishment. She stood up, stretched her legs, and drank some water for five minutes. Then, she started the next twenty-five-minute session.

To her surprise, the second session was easier than the first. By the third session, she was deeply focused on the material. She used active recall, covering her notes and explaining the economic theories out loud to her empty room. She realized that she actually understood the concepts much better when she was rested and focused. 

However, building a new routine was not always easy. Two days later, on a Thursday night, Elena’s roommate, Chloe, came into her room holding two concert tickets.

“Elena! My favorite band is playing downtown tonight, and my friend gave me free tickets. You have to come with me!” Chloe said excitedly. 

Elena looked at the tickets, then at her open textbook. She really wanted to go. She loved live music, and she was tired of studying. But she remembered her terrible midterm grades. She knew that if she went out, she would be exhausted the next day and would fall behind on her schedule. 

“I’m so sorry, Chloe,” Elena said, feeling a heavy weight in her chest. “I would love to go, but I scheduled tonight for studying. I have a huge history assignment due on Monday, and I can't leave it for the last minute again.”

Chloe looked disappointed but nodded. “I understand. You’ve been working really hard lately. We’ll go out next weekend to celebrate when your assignments are done.”

Saying no was painful, but the next morning, Elena woke up feeling rested and proud of herself. She had finished her assignment on time, and she didn't have to spend the weekend panicking. 

Over the next two months, Elena stuck to her new routine strictly. She studied in small, focused blocks. She stopped studying on her bed and only used her designated desk. Most importantly, she started going to sleep at midnight every night, ensuring she got at least seven hours of rest. 

The real test came during the final exam week. Usually, this was a time of intense anxiety, tears, and energy drinks for Elena. But this time, it was different. She had been reviewing the material gradually over the past weeks. When she walked into the examination hall, she felt a strange sensation: she felt calm. 

She sat down, took a deep breath, and opened the test booklet. The questions didn't look like a foreign language anymore. She recognized the concepts, and she knew the answers. She wrote steadily, feeling confident in her knowledge. 

A week later, Elena received an email from the university with her final grades. Her heart was beating fast as she clicked the link to open the document. She scrolled down to the bottom of the page. 

Economics: A-. History: B+. Marketing: A. 

Elena couldn't believe her eyes. She stared at the screen for a long time, making sure she was reading it correctly. A huge smile spread across her face. She had done it. 

She realized that success wasn't about being the smartest person in the room, and it wasn't about suffering through sleepless nights. It was about discipline, working smart, and having a realistic routine. As she closed her laptop, she felt ready to face whatever challenges the next semester would bring.
