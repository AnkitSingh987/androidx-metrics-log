Title: The Helpful Teacher
Category: Beginner

It is three o'clock. The school bell rings loudly. Ring, ring, ring! 
The school day is over. All the children are very happy. They pack their bags quickly. They talk and laugh. They run out of the classrooms. They run out of the school. They want to go home and play. 

In Room 10, it is very quiet. The children are gone. 
Only two people are in the room. There is Miss Davis, the English teacher. And there is a young boy named Peter. 
Peter is sitting at his desk. He is not packing his bag. He is looking at a piece of paper. He looks very sad. He looks like he wants to cry. 

Miss Davis is at her desk. She is marking some tests with a red pen. She looks up. She sees Peter. 
"Peter," she says softly. "The bell rang. Why are you still here? It is time to go home."
Peter does not look up. He keeps his head down. "I know, Miss Davis," he says. "But I have a problem."

Miss Davis puts her red pen down. She stands up. She walks to Peter's desk. She pulls up a chair and sits next to him. 
"What is the problem, Peter?" she asks. Her voice is very kind. "Are you feeling sick?"
"No, I am not sick," Peter says. He pushes the piece of paper towards her. 

Miss Davis looks at the paper. It is the English homework for tonight. It is a worksheet about verbs. 
"I do not understand the homework," Peter says. "I did not understand the lesson today. It is too hard. I cannot do it."
A tear falls from his eye and lands on the paper. 

Miss Davis smiles a gentle smile. She puts her hand on Peter's shoulder. 
"Oh, Peter," she says. "Please do not cry. Verbs can be difficult. It is okay if you do not understand it the first time."
"But everyone else understands," Peter says. "Only I do not know."
"That is not true," Miss Davis says. "Many students find it difficult. You are just brave enough to say it."

"Can you help me?" Peter asks. 
"Of course I can help you," Miss Davis says. "That is why I am a teacher. I am here to help you learn."
She looks at the worksheet. 
"Okay, let's look at the first question," she says. "The sentence is: 'The dog ___ in the garden'. We need an action word. What does a dog do in a garden?"

Peter thinks for a minute. "A dog runs," he says. "A dog plays. A dog barks."
"Exactly!" Miss Davis says. "Run, play, bark. These are all action words. They are verbs. The word tells us what the dog is doing."
"Oh," Peter says. He stops crying. 

"Let's try one of your words," Miss Davis says. "Let's use 'run'. But the dog is doing it now. So we add 's'. The dog runs in the garden."
Peter takes his pencil. He writes the word 'runs' in the empty space. 
"The dog runs in the garden," Peter reads out loud. "That sounds right."
"It is right," Miss Davis says. "Very good."

"Let's do the next one," she says. "The sentence is: 'I ___ an apple for lunch'. What do you do with an apple?"
"I eat an apple," Peter says quickly. 
"Yes," Miss Davis says. "So 'eat' is the verb. It is the action."
Peter writes the word 'eat' on the paper. 

They work together for twenty minutes. The empty classroom is quiet and peaceful. Miss Davis explains the rules very simply. She uses easy words. She is very patient. 
Peter asks many questions. Miss Davis answers all of them. 
Slowly, Peter begins to understand. The worksheet does not look difficult anymore. It looks easy. 

He finishes the last question. He puts his pencil down. He smiles a big smile. 
"I did it!" Peter says. "I understand verbs now. They are just action words."
"Yes, you did it," Miss Davis says. She is proud of him. "You are a very smart boy, Peter. You just needed a little extra time."

Peter stands up. He puts his finished homework in his folder. He puts his folder in his bag. 
"Thank you, Miss Davis," Peter says. "Thank you for helping me."
"You are very welcome, Peter," she says. "Always ask for help if you need it. Do not be afraid to ask."
"I will," Peter promises. 

He puts his bag on his back. He walks to the door. 
"Have a good evening, Peter," Miss Davis says. 
"You too, Miss Davis," he answers. 
He walks out of the classroom. He feels light and happy. The difficult homework is finished. He has a very helpful teacher, and he knows he can always learn new things.
