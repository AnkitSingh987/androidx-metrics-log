Title: Helping with the Boxes
Category: Beginner

An hour later, the massive white moving truck was still parked in the driveway next door. The two men in blue uniforms were working very hard, carrying heavy beds and dressers into the house. 

Lily watched the young boy. He was not just standing around anymore. He was carrying small, square cardboard boxes from the back of the truck to the front porch. He was trying to be helpful, but the boxes looked awkward to carry. 

"Mom, can I go over and help him?" Lily asked. "I can carry the small boxes. It will make their work go faster."
Her mother smiled approvingly. "That is a very kind and neighborly idea, Lily. Yes, you can go over. Just be careful not to get in the way of the professional movers."

Lily jogged across the grass that separated the two houses. She walked up the driveway and approached the boy. 
He was struggling to lift a box that had 'Books' written on the side in black marker. Books are always the heaviest things to move. 

"Hi," Lily said loudly, smiling friendly. "Do you need some help with that?"
The boy dropped the box back onto the floor of the truck and looked at her. He looked a little surprised, but then he smiled back. 
"Hi," he said, wiping sweat from his forehead. "Yes, actually. That would be great. This box is heavier than I thought."

"I'm Lily," she said, holding out her hand. "I live right next door, in the house with the blue door."
"I'm Jack," the boy replied, shaking her hand. "Nice to meet you. We just drove for six hours from another city. I am exhausted."
"Well, welcome to the neighborhood, Jack," Lily said brightly. "Let me help you with these boxes."

Lily picked up a box that said 'Toys and Games'. It was much lighter than the box of books. Jack picked up his heavy box again, and they walked together towards the front porch. 
"Where do these go?" Lily asked. 
"Just leave them on the porch for now," Jack instructed. "My mom says we will carry the small things inside after the men finish with the large furniture."

They placed the boxes on the porch and walked back to the truck. They worked together as a team. They carried boxes labeled 'Kitchen Plates', 'Winter Clothes', and 'Bathroom Supplies'. 
As they walked back and forth, they started to talk and get to know each other. 
"How old are you?" Jack asked. 
"I am twelve," Lily answered. "I will be starting the seventh grade at the middle school down the street next month."
"Really?" Jack sounded relieved. "I am twelve too. I am going into the seventh grade as well. I was worried I wouldn't know anyone at my new school. Is the school nice?"
"It is great," Lily assured him. "The teachers are nice, and we have a very good football field."

Jack's mother walked out of the front door, carrying an empty coffee cup. She saw Jack and Lily carrying a large box together. 
"Well, look at this," she smiled warmly. "I turn my back for five minutes, and you have already hired an assistant, Jack."
Jack laughed. "Mom, this is Lily. She lives next door, and she offered to help me."

"It is a pleasure to meet you, Lily," Jack's mother said. "I am Mrs. Davis. Thank you so much for helping Jack. Moving is very tiring, and any help is greatly appreciated."
"You are very welcome, Mrs. Davis," Lily said politely. 

After another thirty minutes, the back of the moving truck was completely empty. The two men closed the metal door with a loud bang, got into the front cabin, and drove away. 
The driveway looked empty without the huge truck, but the front porch was covered in a mountain of cardboard boxes. 

"Thank you for your hard work, Lily," Jack said, sitting down on a box to rest. 
"No problem," Lily smiled. "I will let you guys unpack in peace now. But if you need anything else, you know where I live!"
She waved goodbye and jogged back across the grass to her own house. Helping Jack with the boxes was a great idea. She had not only been a good neighbor, but she was also pretty sure she had just made a new friend.
