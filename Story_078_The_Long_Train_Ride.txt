Title: The Long Train Ride
Category: Beginner

It was dark when the alarm clock rang on Saturday morning. It was six o'clock. The whole house woke up quickly. Nobody complained about being tired because they were too excited. They ate a fast breakfast of toast and cereal, put on their warm coats, and grabbed their heavy bags by the front door. 

Mr. Miller ordered a taxi to take them to the central train station. The streets were very quiet and empty so early in the morning. When they arrived at the station, however, it was a completely different story. The large building was full of people. People were running with suitcases, drinking coffee, and looking up at the large black departure boards. The station was loud and busy. 

"Stay close together," Mr. Miller told his family. He held his large suitcase in one hand and the tickets in the other. "We need to find Platform 9."
They walked through the crowded hall. Thomas looked around in amazement. He loved the high glass ceiling and the deep sounds of the trains blowing their horns. 

They found Platform 9 just in time. A long, silver train was already waiting there. The doors were open. 
"This is our train," Mr. Miller said, checking the number on his ticket. "Carriage number four. Let's go."
They climbed up the metal steps into the train carriage. It was warm inside. They walked down the narrow aisle, looking at the seat numbers. 
"Here we are," Mrs. Miller said. "Seats 45, 46, 47, and 48. We have a table seat."

There were two seats facing forward and two seats facing backward, with a small wooden table in the middle. Thomas and Sophie quickly sat next to the large window. They wanted the best view. Mr. and Mrs. Miller put the heavy luggage on the racks above their heads and sat down in the aisle seats. 

At exactly eight o'clock, the train blew a loud whistle. Slowly and smoothly, the heavy train began to move. 
"We are going!" Sophie said happily. 
They watched through the window as the busy train station slowly disappeared behind them. The train moved faster and faster as it left the city center. It passed by tall apartment buildings, old factories, and empty streets. 

After thirty minutes, a man in a dark blue uniform walked down the aisle. He had a small machine around his neck. He was the train conductor. 
"Tickets, please. Show your tickets," he called out politely. 
Mr. Miller handed the four paper tickets to the conductor. The conductor looked at them, smiled at the children, and used a small metal tool to punch a hole in each ticket. 'Click, click, click, click.'
"Thank you very much," the conductor said. "Enjoy your journey to the countryside."

The journey was going to take three hours. For the first hour, Thomas and Sophie were very interested in everything. They watched the city slowly turn into suburbs, with smaller houses and larger gardens. 
But soon, the movement of the train made them feel a little sleepy. 

Mrs. Miller opened her bag of food. She brought out four ham and cheese sandwiches, some apples, and a large thermos filled with hot tea. 
"Who is hungry?" she asked. 
"I am!" Thomas and Sophie answered at the same time. 
Eating on the train was fun. The small table was perfect for their indoor picnic. They ate their sandwiches and drank the warm tea while the train rushed forward. 

After breakfast, Thomas pulled his comic book out of his blue bag. He started to read about superheroes. Sophie took out a small notebook and started to draw a picture of the train. Mr. Miller read a book, and Mrs. Miller simply closed her eyes and rested. 

The train ride was long, but it was very comfortable. The gentle rocking motion and the rhythmic sound of the wheels on the tracks—'clack-clack, clack-clack'—made the time pass pleasantly. They were on their way, leaving the busy city far behind them, moving steadily towards their weekend adventure.
