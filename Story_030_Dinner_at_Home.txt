Title: Dinner at Home
Category: Beginner

It is seven o'clock in the evening. It is time for dinner. The Robinson family is gathering in the dining room. 
The Robinson family has four people. There is Mr. Robinson, the father. There is Mrs. Robinson, the mother. There is their son, Mark, who is fourteen. And there is their daughter, Sarah, who is eleven. 

Mrs. Robinson is carrying a large dish from the kitchen. It is very hot. She is wearing oven gloves to protect her hands. 
"Dinner is ready, everyone!" she calls out. "Please come to the table."

Mr. Robinson walks in from the living room. He was reading the news. 
Mark comes down the stairs from his bedroom. He was playing a computer game. 
Sarah comes from the garden. She was playing with the dog. She washes her hands quickly in the bathroom before she sits down. 

The dining table is made of dark wood. It is set beautifully. There is a white tablecloth. There are four white plates, four forks, four knives, and four glasses. In the center of the table, there is a small vase with yellow flowers. 
Everyone sits in their usual chairs. Mr. Robinson sits at one end of the table. Mrs. Robinson sits at the other end. Mark and Sarah sit across from each other. 

Mrs. Robinson puts the large dish in the middle of the table. 
"What is for dinner?" Mark asks. He looks at the dish. He is a growing boy, and he is always hungry. 
"It is roast chicken with potatoes and green beans," Mrs. Robinson says. 
"My favorite!" Sarah says happily. 

Mr. Robinson takes a large spoon and a large fork. He starts to serve the food. He puts a large piece of chicken on Mrs. Robinson's plate. He gives her some potatoes and green beans. Then he serves Sarah, then Mark, and finally himself. 
"Thank you, dear," Mrs. Robinson says. 
"Thank you, Dad," the children say. 

They all pick up their forks and knives. They start to eat. The food is hot and delicious. The chicken is soft. The potatoes are crispy on the outside. The green beans are fresh. 
For a few minutes, nobody talks. They are too busy eating. The only sound is the clinking of knives and forks on the plates. 

Then, Mr. Robinson takes a drink of water from his glass. He puts the glass down. 
"So, how was everyone's day?" he asks. This is their tradition. They always talk about their day during dinner. 
"My day was good," Mrs. Robinson says. "I went to the supermarket. I bought all the food for the week. And I planted some new flowers in the garden."
"The yellow flowers in the vase?" Sarah asks. 
"Yes, exactly," her mother smiles. 

"How was school today, Mark?" Mr. Robinson asks his son. 
Mark finishes chewing a piece of potato. "It was okay," he says. "We had a big math test today. I think I did well. And in sports class, we played basketball."
"Did your team win?" his father asks. 
"Yes, we won by two points," Mark says proudly. 
"Well done," Mr. Robinson says. 

"And what about you, Sarah?" Mrs. Robinson asks. "What did you learn today?"
"We learned about space in science class," Sarah says eagerly. "We learned about the planets. Did you know Jupiter is the biggest planet?"
"Yes, I did know that," her father says. "But it is very interesting to learn."
"I want to be an astronaut when I grow up," Sarah says. "I want to fly to space."
"You will have to study hard in science class then," her mother says. 
"I will!" Sarah promises. 

They continue to eat and talk. Mr. Robinson tells a funny story about his work in the office. Everyone laughs. 
It is a warm and happy time. They are a close family. They enjoy eating together. They enjoy listening to each other. 

Soon, the plates are empty. Mark ate all his chicken. Sarah ate all her green beans. Mr. Robinson finishes the last potato. 
"That was a wonderful dinner," Mr. Robinson says. He pats his stomach. "I am very full."
"Thank you for cooking, Mom," Mark says. 
"You are welcome," Mrs. Robinson says. 

Now, the meal is over, but the work is not finished. The table must be cleared. 
"Okay, team," Mr. Robinson says. "Who is helping with the cleaning tonight?"
"It is Mark's turn to clear the table," Sarah says quickly. 
Mark sighs. "Okay, okay. I will clear the table."
"And I will wash the dishes," Mr. Robinson says. "Your mother cooked, so she can rest."

Mrs. Robinson smiles. "Thank you both," she says. She stands up and goes to the living room to relax. 
Mark stands up. He takes his plate and Sarah's plate. He carries them to the kitchen. He comes back and takes his parents' plates. He takes the empty chicken dish. He takes the empty glasses. 
Mr. Robinson stands at the kitchen sink. He turns on the hot water. He puts soap on a sponge. He starts to wash the plates. Mark helps by drying the plates with a towel. 

The dining room is empty and quiet again. The dinner is finished. The family is full and happy. Tomorrow evening, they will sit at the table and do it all again.
