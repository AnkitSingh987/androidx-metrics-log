Title: The Missing Notebook
Category: Beginner

It is ten o'clock in the morning. It is time for Mathematics class in Room 6. 
Mr. Davies is the teacher. He is writing numbers on the blackboard. 
"Okay, class," Mr. Davies says. "Please take out your math notebooks. We are going to copy these problems from the board."

Lucy is ten years old. She sits at a desk near the front. She opens her green school bag. She looks for her math notebook. Her math notebook is yellow. It has her name written on the front in big letters. 

She pulls out her blue English book. That is not it. 
She pulls out her red Science book. That is not it. 
She pulls out her pencil case. 
She looks inside the bag again. It is empty. There is no yellow notebook. 

Lucy feels a cold feeling in her stomach. She always brings her notebook. She did her homework in it last night. She remembers putting it in her bag. 
She looks under her desk. Maybe it fell on the floor. It is not on the floor. 
She looks in her desk drawer. There are some old papers and a broken pencil. But no yellow notebook. 

"Mr. Davies," Lucy says. She raises her hand. 
"Yes, Lucy?" the teacher asks. 
"I cannot find my math notebook," she says. She sounds a little upset. 
"Are you sure, Lucy?" Mr. Davies asks. "Please check your bag one more time."
Lucy checks her bag again. "I am sure," she says. "It is not here. But I did my homework. I promise."

Mr. Davies nods. "I believe you, Lucy. You are a good student. But you need paper to copy the problems today. Here, take this piece of paper."
He gives Lucy a clean piece of white paper. 
"Thank you," Lucy says. She takes her pencil and starts to write the numbers from the board. But she is still thinking about her yellow notebook. Where is it? 

The boy sitting behind Lucy is named Oliver. Oliver is sometimes a little messy. His bag is always full of many things. 
Oliver is trying to find his own math notebook. He opens his bag. He pulls out a sweater, an apple, and three different books. 
Finally, he pulls out a yellow notebook. 
He opens it to the first page. He looks at the name. 
He is surprised. The name does not say 'Oliver'. The name says 'Lucy'. 

Oliver looks at the back of Lucy's head. He taps her on the shoulder. 
Lucy turns around. "What is it, Oliver?" she asks quietly. 
Oliver holds up the yellow notebook. "Is this yours?" he whispers. 
Lucy's eyes open wide. "Yes! That is my math notebook! Where did you find it?"
"It was in my bag," Oliver says. He looks confused. "I don't know how it got there."

Then, Lucy remembers. 
Yesterday, at the end of the day, they had reading time. Lucy and Oliver were reading at the same table. The bell rang. They had to pack their bags very quickly to catch the school bus. 
They were in a hurry. Their bags were next to each other on the table. 
"We packed our bags too fast yesterday," Lucy says. "You must have picked up my notebook by mistake. You thought it was yours."

Oliver thinks about it. "Yes, my math notebook is also yellow. I just saw a yellow book and pushed it into my bag."
He reaches into his bag again. He searches at the very bottom. He pulls out another yellow notebook. This one is dirty and bent. 
"Ah," Oliver says. "Here is my notebook. I had two yellow notebooks in my bag."

Oliver hands Lucy's notebook to her. 
"Here you go," he says. "I am sorry for the mistake."
"That is okay," Lucy says with a smile. "Thank you for finding it. I was very worried."

Lucy raises her hand again. 
"Mr. Davies," she says. 
"Yes, Lucy?" 
"I found my notebook," she says happily. "Oliver had it in his bag by mistake. Our notebooks look exactly the same."
Mr. Davies smiles. "I am glad you found it. Mystery solved. Now, please continue with your work."

Lucy puts the piece of white paper inside her desk. She opens her yellow notebook. She finds the page with her finished homework. She turns to a new, clean page. 
She takes her pencil. She looks at the blackboard. She starts to copy the math problems. 
She is very happy. Her homework is safe, and her notebook is not missing anymore. Tomorrow, she will be more careful when she packs her bag.
