Title: Looking for My Shoes
Category: Beginner

It is a beautiful Saturday afternoon. The sky is blue. There are no clouds. 
Emily is a young girl. She is ten years old. She is in her house. She wants to play outside. She wants to ride her bicycle. Her bicycle is red and shiny. It is in the garden. 

Emily is wearing a yellow T-shirt and blue shorts. She is wearing white socks on her feet. But she cannot ride her bicycle in socks. She needs her shoes. Her favorite shoes are pink sneakers. They are very comfortable for riding a bicycle. 

She goes to the front door. There is a shoe rack near the door. It is made of wood. Her father's big brown shoes are on the rack. Her mother's black shoes are on the rack. Her brother's dirty boots are on the rack. 
Emily looks for her pink sneakers. She looks on the top shelf. They are not there. She looks on the bottom shelf. They are not there. 

"That is strange," Emily says. "I always leave my shoes here."
She looks around the hallway. She looks under the small table. She looks behind the umbrella stand. No pink sneakers. 

Emily walks into the living room. Her older brother, Ben, is on the sofa. He is fourteen years old. He is reading a comic book. 
"Ben," Emily says. "Did you see my pink shoes?"
Ben does not look up from his comic book. "No," he says. "I did not see your shoes."
"Are you sure?" Emily asks. "Maybe you moved them."
"I did not touch your shoes, Emily," Ben says. "Go look in your room."

Emily thinks that is a good idea. Sometimes she takes her shoes off in her bedroom. 
She walks up the stairs. She goes into her bedroom. Her bedroom has pink walls. Her bed has a white blanket. 
She looks under her bed. It is dark under the bed. She gets down on her knees. She looks closely. She sees a dusty box. She sees an old doll. She sees one green sock. But there are no pink sneakers. 

She stands up. She goes to her closet. She opens the closet doors. She looks at the bottom of the closet. She sees her black school shoes. She sees her winter boots. She sees her summer sandals. The pink sneakers are not there. 
Emily sighs. She is frustrated. She really wants to ride her bicycle. The weather is so nice. 

She walks back downstairs. She goes to the kitchen. Her mother is in the kitchen. She is washing apples at the sink. 
"Mother," Emily says. Her voice is sad. 
Her mother turns around. She turns off the water. "What is wrong, Emily?" she asks. 
"I cannot find my shoes," Emily says. "I want my pink sneakers. I want to ride my bike. But they are lost."

Her mother dries her hands on a towel. "They are not lost, Emily. They are just in the wrong place. Where did you look?"
"I looked on the shoe rack. I looked in the living room. I asked Ben. I looked in my bedroom. I looked under my bed. I looked in my closet," Emily says. "They are nowhere."

Her mother smiles. "Let's think," she says. "When did you wear them last?"
Emily thinks. "I wore them yesterday," she says. 
"Where did you go yesterday?" her mother asks. 
"I went to the park with my friends," Emily says. "We played on the grass."
"And then you came home," her mother says. "What did you do when you came home?"
"I was very thirsty," Emily says. "I ran into the house. I came straight to the kitchen to get juice."

Her mother points her finger at Emily's feet. "Did you take your shoes off in the kitchen?"
Emily looks at the kitchen floor. She looks near the fridge. She looks under the table. 
There, under the kitchen table, are two pink sneakers. They are sitting quietly near a chair. 

"My shoes!" Emily shouts. She is very happy. 
She runs to the table. She crawls under the table. she takes her shoes. 
"I remember now," Emily says. "I sat at the table to drink my juice. My feet were hot. So I took my shoes off under the table."
"And you forgot to put them on the shoe rack," her mother says. 
"Yes, I forgot," Emily says. "I am sorry."
"That is okay," her mother says. "Next time, put them in the right place. Then you will not have to look for them."

"I will," Emily promises. 
She sits on the kitchen chair. She puts on her left shoe. She ties the white laces. She puts on her right shoe. She ties the white laces. 
She stands up. She stomps her feet on the floor. Her pink sneakers feel good. 
"Thank you for helping me, Mother," Emily says. 
"You are welcome, dear," her mother says. "Have fun on your bicycle."
"I will!" Emily says. 

She runs out of the kitchen. She runs down the hallway. She opens the front door. She runs out into the warm sun. Her red bicycle is waiting for her. Now she has her shoes, and she is ready to ride.
