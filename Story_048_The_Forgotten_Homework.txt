Title: The Forgotten Homework
Category: Beginner

It is a bright Thursday morning. Ben is walking to school. He is walking with his friend, Sam. They are talking about a new movie. Ben is very happy. He likes school, and he likes Thursdays. 

They arrive at the school gate. They walk into the building. They go to their classroom, Room 9. 
Ben sits at his desk. He takes off his jacket and puts it on the back of his chair. 
The teacher, Mr. Harris, walks into the room. 
"Good morning, class," Mr. Harris says. 
"Good morning, Mr. Harris," the students reply. 

"Please take out your math homework," Mr. Harris says. "Put it on your desk. I will come and collect it."
Ben reaches into his school bag. He looks for his red math folder. His math homework is inside the red folder. 
He looks in the big pocket. There is his English book, his history book, and his pencil case. But there is no red folder. 

Ben frowns. He opens the middle pocket. There is his lunch box and his water bottle. No red folder. 
He opens the small front pocket. There are some keys and tissues. No red folder. 

Ben's heart starts to beat faster. He feels hot. He searches the bag again. He pulls out all the books. He looks at the bottom of the bag. The red folder is not there. 
"Oh no," Ben whispers. 
He remembers now. Last night, he finished his math homework. He closed the red folder. But he did not put it in his bag. He left it on the kitchen table. He went to watch television and he forgot. 

His homework is finished, but it is sitting on the kitchen table at home. 
Mr. Harris is walking down the aisle between the desks. He is collecting the homework papers. 
He takes Sam's homework. "Thank you, Sam," he says. 
Then, he stops at Ben's desk. Ben's desk is empty. There is no paper. 

"Ben," Mr. Harris says. "Where is your math homework?"
Ben looks up at the teacher. He feels very embarrassed. His face is red. 
"I am sorry, Mr. Harris," Ben says. His voice is quiet. "I do not have my homework."
"Did you not do it?" Mr. Harris asks. He does not look angry, but he looks disappointed. 

"I did do it, Mr. Harris. I promise I did it," Ben says quickly. "I finished all the questions last night. But I forgot to put it in my bag. I left it on the kitchen table at home."
Mr. Harris looks at Ben for a moment. Ben is usually a very good student. He always brings his homework. 

"I see," Mr. Harris says. "It is very important to bring your work to school, Ben. Doing the homework is only the first step. Bringing it to class is the second step."
"I know, sir," Ben says. "It was a mistake. I will not do it again."
"I believe you," Mr. Harris says. "But I cannot give you a mark for homework I cannot see."

"I will bring it tomorrow morning," Ben promises. "I will give it to you before the first bell."
"Okay, Ben," Mr. Harris says. "I will write a note in my book. You can bring it tomorrow. But remember, you must be responsible for your own things."
"Yes, Mr. Harris. Thank you," Ben says. 

Mr. Harris walks away to the next desk. 
Ben lets out a long breath. He feels a little better. The teacher was not angry. The teacher understood. 
But Ben is still annoyed with himself. He did the hard work, but he forgot the easy part. 

When the school day is over, Ben walks home quickly. He opens the front door of his house. He walks straight to the kitchen. 
There it is. The red math folder is sitting quietly on the kitchen table. 
Ben picks it up immediately. He opens his school bag. He puts the red folder into the big pocket. He zips the bag closed. 

"You are not staying on the table tonight," he says to the folder. 
Tomorrow, he will give his homework to Mr. Harris. And from now on, he will always pack his bag at night, before he goes to sleep. That is the best way to not forget anything.
