Title: Managing a Monthly Budget

Category: Intermediate

Clara sat at her small kitchen table and stared at her laptop screen. She refreshed the banking website for the third time, but the number on the screen did not change. Her account balance was only forty-five dollars. The problem was that her next payday was still seven days away. She sighed heavily and put her head in her hands. She did not understand where all her money had gone.

Clara was twenty-four years old, and she had recently started her first full-time job as a graphic designer. When she got the job, she was incredibly excited. The salary seemed like a lot of money to her. She had immediately decided to move out of her parents' house and rent a small, bright apartment in the city center. She loved her new independence. She loved buying her own groceries, decorating her living room, and inviting her friends over for dinner. However, this independence came with a high price.

In the first two months, Clara had spent her money freely. If she saw a nice jacket in a shop window, she bought it. If she felt tired after work, she ordered a pizza instead of cooking. She often went to cafes to buy expensive coffee, and she went out for drinks with her colleagues every Friday evening. She thought that because she had a good salary, she did not need to worry about the cost of things. Now, she realized that she had made a big mistake.

Her phone buzzed on the table. It was a message from her friend, Leo.
"Hey Clara! Are we still going to the cinema tonight? We can grab dinner beforehand."
Clara looked at her bank balance again. Forty-five dollars. If she went to the cinema and bought dinner, she would have almost nothing left for the rest of the week. She still needed to buy groceries, and she had to pay for her bus ticket to get to work.

She picked up her phone and typed a reply. "Hey Leo. I’m really sorry, but I can't come tonight. I'm completely broke."
A few minutes later, Leo called her. "What do you mean you are completely broke?" he asked. "You just got paid two weeks ago!"
"I know," Clara said, feeling embarrassed. "I don't know what happened. I paid my rent, I bought some groceries, and I bought a few other things. Now the money is gone. I only have forty-five dollars to last until next Friday."
Leo laughed softly. "It sounds like you need to start managing your monthly budget. Have you ever made a budget before?"
"No," Clara admitted. "I never needed one when I lived with my parents. I just spent what I had. But now, with the rent, the electricity, the internet, and everything else... it is too complicated."
"It is not as complicated as you think," Leo told her. "I am coming over right now. I will bring some snacks, and we will figure this out together."

Half an hour later, Leo knocked on her door. He brought a bag of chips and a notebook. He sat down opposite Clara at the kitchen table and opened the notebook.
"Okay," Leo said, clicking his pen. "The first step of making a budget is to know exactly how much money you earn. What is your monthly income after taxes?"
Clara told him the amount, and Leo wrote it at the top of the page.
"Great," Leo said. "Now, the second step is to write down all your fixed expenses. These are the bills that you absolutely have to pay every month, and the amount usually stays the same. What are they?"
Clara thought for a moment. "My rent, of course. Then there is my internet bill, my phone bill, and my health insurance."
Leo wrote them down and subtracted the total from her income. "Alright. Now we have your remaining money. The next step is variable expenses. These are things you need, but the amount can change. For example, groceries, electricity, and transportation."

Clara opened her banking app and looked at her spending from the previous month. She was shocked when she saw how much she had spent on food. "I spent over four hundred dollars on groceries and restaurants," she said, her eyes wide. "That is way too much."
"It happens," Leo said kindly. "When you don't track your spending, it is very easy to buy lunch every day and forget about it. Five dollars here, ten dollars there... it adds up quickly."
They decided that Clara should limit her food budget to two hundred and fifty dollars a month. She would have to cook at home more often and stop ordering takeout when she was tired.
"If I cook a large meal on Sunday, I can take the leftovers to work for lunch," Clara suggested.
"Exactly," Leo agreed. "That will save you a lot of money."

Finally, Leo explained the last category of the budget. "This is the fun money," he said. "You still need to enjoy your life. You need money for hobbies, clothes, going out with friends, and maybe saving for a vacation."
When Clara subtracted her fixed and variable expenses from her income, she saw that she actually had a decent amount of money left for fun and savings. The problem was not that she didn't earn enough; the problem was that she was spending her money blindly.

"I see it now," Clara said, looking at the numbers on the page. "I have been spending my 'fun money' on silly things like expensive coffee and impulsive shopping. If I am careful, I can actually save money every month."
"That is the goal," Leo smiled. "A budget does not mean you cannot spend money. It just means you tell your money where to go, instead of wondering where it went."

For the next week, Clara was very careful. She went to the supermarket and bought cheap but healthy ingredients, like rice, beans, chicken, and vegetables. She cooked all her meals at home. Instead of buying a coffee at the cafe, she made coffee in her apartment and put it in a travel mug. She did not go shopping for clothes. It was a little difficult at first. She felt a strong temptation to order a pizza on Wednesday night after a long day at the office, but she looked at her budget and decided to make a simple pasta dish instead.

When Friday finally arrived, Clara received her salary. She immediately sat down at her kitchen table with her notebook. She transferred a portion of the money into a savings account. Then, she paid her rent and her internet bill. She looked at the remaining amount and felt a deep sense of relief. She was in control.

A few days later, she met Leo for coffee. This time, she knew exactly how much she could spend.
"How is the new budget going?" Leo asked as they sat down.
"It is going surprisingly well," Clara replied. "I feel much less stressed. I know exactly how much I can spend on this coffee without feeling guilty. It is a very good feeling."
Clara realized that managing a monthly budget was not about punishing herself. It was about creating freedom. By planning her expenses carefully, she was ensuring that she would always have enough for the things that truly mattered to her. She thanked Leo for his help and promised herself that she would never go back to her old spending habits again.
