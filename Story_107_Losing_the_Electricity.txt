Title: Losing the Electricity
Category: Beginner

The winter storm raged violently outside. It was now eight o'clock in the evening. The Clark family had just finished eating a warm dinner of tomato soup and grilled cheese sandwiches. They were sitting in the living room, feeling full and comfortable. The television was on, showing a comedy movie, and the central heating hummed softly, keeping the house perfectly warm. 

Outside, the wind was incredibly loud. It rattled the window frames and made the heavy front door shake on its hinges. 

"I have never heard the wind blow this hard before," Mark said, looking nervously at the ceiling. He felt like the roof might fly away. 
"It is a very strong blizzard," his father replied calmly. "But this house is old and strong. We are perfectly safe."

Suddenly, there was a loud 'CRACK!' from the street outside. It sounded like a massive wooden stick breaking in half. 
A split second later, the television screen went completely black. The soft humming of the central heating stopped instantly. The lamps in the living room died. 

The house plunged into absolute, pitch-black darkness. 

"Oh no!" Mrs. Clark gasped in surprise. 
"Nobody move," Mr. Clark said quickly. His voice was loud and clear in the sudden silence of the house. "Don't walk around in the dark. You might trip over the furniture and hurt yourselves. I know exactly where the flashlights are."

Mark sat perfectly still on the sofa. The sudden silence inside the house made the noise of the storm outside seem much louder and more frightening. Without the lights, the house felt very different. It felt large, empty, and cold. 

He heard his father moving slowly across the carpet. He heard the sound of hands touching the wall, finding the doorway to the kitchen. A few seconds later, a bright beam of white light cut through the darkness. 
"I have them," Mr. Clark announced, carrying the three metal flashlights from the kitchen counter. 

He walked back into the living room and handed one flashlight to Mark and one to his wife. 
Mark turned his flashlight on. He pointed the beam of light around the room. It cast long, strange shadows on the walls. The familiar living room looked slightly spooky in the harsh white light. 

"Well, the meteorologist was right," Mrs. Clark said with a sigh. "A tree branch must have fallen on the power lines. The whole street is dark."
She walked to the window and pointed her flashlight outside. There were no streetlights shining. The houses across the road were completely black blocks against the falling white snow. 

"How long do you think it will last, Dad?" Mark asked. He was already feeling a little bored without the television or the internet. 
"It is hard to say," his father answered honestly. "In a bad storm like this, it could be a few hours, or it could be a few days. The repair crews cannot fix the power lines while the wind is this strong."

"A few days?" Mark repeated in shock. "But what will we do? How will we cook food? And the heating is off!"
"Don't panic, Mark," his mother said soothingly. "We are prepared for this. We have thick blankets to keep us warm, and we have a gas stove in the kitchen. The gas still works, so we can boil water and cook hot food. We will be absolutely fine."

Mark nodded, but he still felt a bit uneasy. He realized how much he relied on electricity for everything in his life. Without it, he couldn't play video games, he couldn't listen to music, and he couldn't even see his own hands clearly. 

"Okay, team," Mr. Clark said, clapping his hands once. "Flashlights are good for walking around, but they use up batteries very quickly. We need a more permanent light source for the living room. It is time to bring out the candles."
"I will get them," Mrs. Clark said, aiming her flashlight towards the hallway. 
The house was dark and quiet, but the family was calm. The electricity was gone, but their preparations were about to pay off.
