Category: Intermediate
Title: Bob the Dog and the Bird

Once upon a time, there was a chubby dog named Bob. Bob loved to play with his hose in the yard. Every day, he would run and jump in the water that came out of the hose. He liked to get wet and cool down on hot days. One day, while Bob was playing with the hose, he saw a little bird. The bird looked very hot and tired. Bob thought the bird might want to play with the hose too. So he took the hose and sprayed some water near the bird. The bird jumped in the water and splashed around. It looked very happy. The bird said, "Thank you, Bob, for sharing your hose with me." Bob was happy that he could help the bird. They played together all day and became good friends. From that day on, Bob and the bird always played with the hose together. And they always said "thank you" to each other for sharing and being kind.

Once upon a time, there was a pale bunny. The bunny liked to play in a maze. The maze was in a big park. The bunny had many friends. They played together every day. One day, the bunny was in the maze. He saw a small bird. The bird was lost. The bunny said, "I will help you." The bird was happy. They walked and talked. The bird reminded the bunny of his friend, the squirrel. The bunny and the bird found the way out of the maze. They saw the squirrel. The squirrel was happy to see them. The bird said, "Thank you, bunny." The bunny was happy to help. They all played together in the park.

Tom and Amy were playing in the garden. They liked to splash in the pool and make mud pies. But today, the sun was very hot and the pool was almost dry. Amy was sad. She wanted to swim and cool down. "Let's go inside and find something else to play," Tom said. "Maybe we can watch a movie or draw some pictures." "No, I want to stay here," Amy said. "Maybe we can find some water in the hose or the tap." Tom shrugged and followed Amy. They looked for water, but they could not find any. The hose was empty and the tap was broken. They were very thirsty and tired. "Look, there is a big bottle over there," Amy said, pointing to a corner of the garden. "Maybe it has water inside. Let's go and see." Tom and Amy ran to the bottle. It was green and shiny and had a strange label on it. Tom could not read it, but he saw a picture of a monster. He felt scared. "Amy, I don't think this is water," he said. "This looks like something bad. Maybe we should leave it alone." "Don't be silly, Tom," Amy said. "It's just a picture. Maybe it's juice or soda. I'm so thirsty, I want to try it." She grabbed the bottle and opened the cap. She smelled it and made a face. It smelled funny, but she was too curious to stop. She took a sip and swallowed. "Yuck, this is gross," she said. "It tastes like dirt and soap. Bleh." She spat it out and threw the bottle away. But it was too late. The liquid was already in her body. She felt a strange tingling in her mouth and throat. She looked at Tom and saw his eyes widen in horror. "Amy, what's happening to you?" he screamed. "You look different. You look like a monster!" Amy touched her face and felt something hard and scaly. She looked at her hands and saw claws and fur. She looked at her feet and saw hooves and spikes. She was turning into a monster. She wanted to cry, but she could only roar. Tom was terrified. He wanted to run away, but he was too slow. Amy was faster and stronger. She was hungry and angry. She did not remember Tom as her friend. She only saw him as food. She jumped on him and bit him. He screamed and bled. He was gone. Amy was alone. She was a monster. She was not happy. She was not attractive. She was dry.
