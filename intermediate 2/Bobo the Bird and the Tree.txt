Category: Intermediate
Title: Bobo the Bird and the Tree

Once upon a time, there was a little bird named Bobo. Bobo loved to fly and play with his friends. One day, while they were playing, Bobo saw a wire. He didn't know what it was, but he wanted to play with it. Bobo went near the wire and tried to touch it. Suddenly, he felt a little pain. He was very sorry that he touched the wire. Bobo's friends came and told him to be careful. They said, "Don't touch things you don't know. It can hurt you." Bobo learned his lesson and never touched the wire again. Later, he took a nice nap under a big tree. When he woke up, he played with his friends again, but this time, he was careful. He knew that it is important to be safe and listen to his friends.

One day, a little boy went to the zoo with his mom. He was very excited to see all the animals. He saw a big tiger in a cage. The tiger had pretty stripes and a long tail. The little boy wanted to watch the tiger all day. His mom said, "We need to see the other animals too." But the little boy was stubborn. He did not want to leave the tiger. He liked the tiger too much. At last, the little boy said goodbye to the tiger. He went to see the other animals with his mom. He saw a funny monkey and a tall giraffe. He had a fun day at the zoo.

---

One day, a little girl named Mia went to the park with her mom. They saw a big tree with a hole in it. Mia heard a sound coming from the hole. She wanted to know what was making the sound, so she went closer to the tree. Mia looked into the hole and saw a tiny bird. The bird looked alert and said, "Peek! Peek!" Mia was happy to find the bird. She asked her mom, "What language does the bird speak?" Her mom smiled and said, "The bird speaks bird language. When it says 'Peek! Peek!' it is saying hello to you." Mia laughed and said hello back to the bird. They played together until it was time to go home.

Once upon a time, in a green town, there was a boy named Tim. Tim had a dog named Max. Max was not feeling well. Tim wanted to help Max feel better. Tim took Max to the veterinarian. The veterinarian was a nice lady who took care of all the animals in the green town. When they arrived, there was another boy named Sam with his cat, Whiskers. Sam and Tim began to quarrel about who would see the veterinarian first. "Max is sicker," said Tim. "No, Whiskers is sicker," said Sam. The veterinarian heard the quarrel and came out. She said, "Don't quarrel, boys. I will help both Max and Whiskers." The veterinarian took care of Max and Whiskers. Soon, both pets were feeling better. Tim and Sam stopped quarreling and became friends. They all played together in the green town, happy and healthy.

Tom is a boy who likes to learn new things. He goes to school with his friends and his teacher. His teacher is nice and helps him learn. Today, his teacher has a big white board. On the board, she writes some numbers. She says, "These are numbers. Numbers help us count and measure things. Can you say the numbers with me?" Tom and his friends look at the board. They see one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, and ten. They try to say the numbers with their teacher. Some of them say them well, some of them make mistakes, but they all have fun. Tom likes the number four. He thinks it looks like a chair. He says, "Four, four, four!" His teacher smiles and says, "Good, Tom! You are learning the numbers. Do you know what else you can do with the number four?" She picks up four crayons and shows them to Tom. She says, "You can make four lines, or four circles, or four squares. You can also make a picture with four colors. Do you want to try?" Tom nods and takes the crayons. He makes four lines on a paper. Then he makes four circles. Then he makes four squares. He thinks it is fun to make shapes with the number four. He also makes a picture of a house with four colors. He uses red, blue, yellow, and green. He shows his picture to his teacher and his friends. They all say, "Wow, Tom! You are very good at numbers and colors!" Tom feels happy and proud. He says, "Thank you, teacher! Thank you, friends! I like to learn numbers and colors. They are fun and pretty!" He puts his picture on the wall and looks at it. He thinks, "I wonder what else I can learn with numbers and colors." He can't wait for the next day of school.
