Category: Intermediate
Title: Bob and the Park

Once upon a time, a wealthy man named Bob had to go to the airport. He had a big plane to catch. Before he left, he wanted to print a picture for his little girl, Sue. He printed a picture of them at the park. Sue was very happy. At the airport, Bob saw a big plane flying in the sky. He thought about Sue and the fun they had at the park. He wished Sue could see the big plane too. He had an idea. When Bob came back home, he told Sue about the big plane at the airport. He said, "I want to take you to see the big plane too!" Sue was so excited. They went to the airport, and Sue saw the big plane. She was very happy, just like when she got the picture. And they took a new picture together with the big plane in the background.

Once upon a time, a little dog had a flea. The flea liked to jump and play on the dog's back. The dog did not like the flea. The flea made the dog feel itchy and bad. The dog tried to be nice, but the flea was harsh. One day, the dog saw a big bow. The dog had an idea. The dog took the bow and put it on the flea. The flea looked very funny with the big bow. The dog laughed and laughed. The flea did not like the bow. The flea felt silly. Then, something unexpected happened. The flea started to laugh too. The flea liked the bow now. The dog and the flea became friends. They played and laughed together. The flea was not harsh anymore. The dog was happy, and they lived happily ever after.

Molly was a very happy little girl. Every day she went out and played with her friends in the park. One day, Molly went to the park with her dad. He wanted to teach her how to load up her wagon with fun things and take them to the pitch. Molly was very curious, so she quickly loaded the wagon with toys and everything else she needed. She looked to her dad and asked, “Are we ready? Can we go to the pitch now?” Molly’s dad smiled and said, “Yes, we are ready! Let’s go!” So the two of them went to the pitch and had great time together. Molly was so glad that she had learned how to load up the wagon and take it with her to the pitch. Happy and tired, Molly went home with her dad, grinning from ear to ear.

Once upon a time there was a little bird. His name was Tom. Tom had a beautiful voice and loved to sing. Every day he would fly high in the sky and sing his sweet songs. But one day, Tom came upon a saw. It was lying in the grass, all by itself. Tom thought it was a toy and he wanted to play with it. But the saw was selfish. It didn't want to share. It said, "Go away little bird! I don't want you to play with me!" Tom was sad. He had never met a selfish saw before. So he sang a goodbye song and flew away. Tom never saw the saw again. But he never forgot the lesson the saw had taught him: sometimes things don't want to share.

Sam wanted to choose something from the tray. He had seen it before on the table. It was a blue tray full of surprises. He carefully looked at what was on the tray. He saw a red car, some brown blocks and a blue bear. Sam looked carefully back and forth at each of them. He stretched out his arm and pointed at the blue bear. He chose the blue bear with its bright eyes and soft fur. He wanted to hug it. Sam picked up the blue bear and hugged it close. He was so happy to have chosen it and he knew he would keep it forever.

Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a brave little boy named Tim. Tim loved to count. One day, his mom gave him a big bag of beans. She said, "Tim, can you count all these beans for me?" Tim was very happy. He sat down and started counting the beans. "One, two, three..." he counted. He was very careful not to miss any beans. It took him a long time, but he did not give up. Finally, he counted all the beans. When he was done, he ran to his mom and said, "Mom, I counted all the beans! There are one hundred beans in the bag!" His mom smiled and said, "Good job, Tim! You are very brave for not giving up, and you learned that counting takes time and patience." From that day on, Tim always remembered to be brave and patient when he counted. And everyone in the village knew that Tim was the best bean counter around. The moral of the story is to always be brave and patient when learning something new.
