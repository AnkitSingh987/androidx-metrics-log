Category: Intermediate
Title: True the Girl and the Cat

One day, a girl named True went to play in the park. She felt lucky because it was a sunny day. True saw a big tree and wanted to climb it. She started to go up, but then she heard a noise. True looked down and saw a little cat. The cat was stuck in the tree and scared. True said, "Don't worry, cat. I will help you." She tried to reach the cat, but it was too far. True had an idea. She found a big stick and started to stir the branches near the cat. The cat moved closer to True. Finally, True was able to grab the cat. She held the cat tight and climbed down the tree. The cat was happy and safe. True felt lucky that she could help the cat. They both went to play in the park, and they became the best of friends.

Tom and Lily went to the park with their mom. They saw many pigeons on the grass. They wanted to feed them some bread. Mom gave them each a piece of bread and told them to break it into small bits. Tom and Lily broke the bread and threw it to the pigeons. The pigeons flew to the bread and ate it. They made loud noises and flapped their wings. Tom and Lily laughed and clapped their hands. "Look, mom, the pigeons are happy!" Lily said. "Yes, they are, sweetie. They like the bread you gave them." Mom said. Tom saw a big pigeon with a shiny green neck. He wanted to touch it. He walked slowly to the pigeon and reached out his hand. The pigeon saw him and got scared. It flew away and scattered the other pigeons. "Hey, come back!" Tom shouted. He ran after the pigeon, but it was too fast. He tripped and fell on the grass. "Ouch!" Tom cried. He scraped his knee on a rock. It hurt and bled a little. Mom and Lily ran to Tom and helped him up. Mom wiped his knee with a smooth cloth and put a band-aid on it. "It's okay, Tom. It's just a small boo-boo. The pigeon didn't mean to scare you. It was just shy." Mom said. "I'm sorry, mom. I just wanted to pet it. It was so pretty." Tom said. "I know, honey. But pigeons don't like to be touched. They like to be free and fly. Maybe you can draw a picture of the pigeon later. Would you like that?" Mom said. Tom nodded and smiled. He hugged mom and said, "Thank you, mom. You are the best." Lily hugged Tom and said, "I love you, Tom. You are my brother." They all hugged and kissed. Then they went to the playground and had more fun. The pigeons watched them from a tree and cooed softly.

Once upon a time there was a brave sailor named Annie. Annie was the captain of a small boat. She was on a journey to explore uncharted waters. One day, Annie came across a large anchor. It was as tall as a house and as strong as a giant. The anchor was purple and it sparkled in the sunshine. Annie was fascinated by the anchor and decided to investigate it. She jumped onto the anchor and started to explore. As she jumped around the deck of the boat, she noticed something strange. With a closer look, Annie realized the boat was slowly drifting. It was as if the anchor was holding her boat in place. The moral of Annie's adventure is that one should never underestimate their strengths. The anchor she found was much bigger and stronger than she was, but it was also holding her back. Annie was brave enough to explore and jump around, and this curiosity ultimately saved her from the powerful anchor's grip.

Once upon a time there was a young girl called Ann. Ann was very clever and loved solving puzzles. One day Ann went to the park with her mum. As she was walking around, she spotted something shining in the grass. It was an ordinary note. Ann picked it up and was so excited to see that it was a puzzle that needed solving. With the help of her mum, Ann read the note and realised it was a challenge. It said that whoever could solve the puzzle, would be given a big surprise. Ann looked around the park for clues and started to try and solve the puzzle. Ann's mum watched as Ann tried very hard to solve the puzzle but it was very difficult. Ann worked on it for a long time, but eventually she just couldn't solve it. Ann was very disappointed. Just then, her mum gave her a comforting hug and said “Ann, it’s okay. Not everything can be solved, sometimes it’s better to enjoy the fun of trying to solve the puzzle. That’s where the real joy lies.” Ann hugged her mum and really understood the moral of the story. From then on, she no longer felt disappointed when she could not solve a puzzle, she just enjoyed the fun of trying to solve it.
