Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily the Girl and the Dog

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She had a notebook that she loved very much. She would draw pictures in it every day. One day, she saw a big, fearful dog near her house. She was scared, but she wanted to be brave. Lily thought of a plan to make the dog nice. She took her notebook and drew a big, happy smile on a page. She showed the picture to the dog. The dog looked at the picture and wagged its tail. It was not fearful anymore. Lily and the dog became friends. They played together every day. When it was Lily's birthday, they had a big party to celebrate. They ate cake, played games, and had a lot of fun. And Lily drew a picture of her and the dog in her notebook, so they would always remember their special day.

Once upon a time, there was a little fish named Fin. Fin loved to dive deep in the water. One day, a big tornado came to the sea. All the fish were scared. Fin wanted to help his friends. He thought of a plan. He told all the fish to swim in a tight circle. They all listened to Fin and started to swim together. As they swam, the tornado got smaller and smaller. Finally, the tornado went away. All the fish were happy and thanked Fin. From that day on, they all loved to dive and play together.

One day, a little girl named Sue and her dog, Max, went for a walk. They saw a big pole in the park. Sue said, "Max, let's go see what we can do with that pole!" As they got close to the pole, they saw a little boy named Tim trying to climb it. Sue asked, "Tim, is it safe to climb the pole?" Tim said, "I don't know, but I want to try." Sue and Max watched as Tim climbed the pole. Suddenly, a little bird flew out of a hole in the pole! Tim got scared and slipped down. Max barked and ran to catch Tim. Max did a good job, and Tim was safe. They all laughed and played together in the park.

---

Tom and Lily were playing in the park. They liked to dig in the sand and look for treasures. Sometimes they found shiny stones, or shells, or coins. They put them in a bucket and pretended they were rich. One day, they saw a big hole in the sand. Someone had dug it and left it there. Tom and Lily were curious. They ran to the hole and looked inside. They saw something sparkling at the bottom. It was a diamond! "Wow!" Tom said. "Look, Lily, a diamond! It's so pretty and bright! Let's take it!" Lily nodded. She reached for the diamond, but then she stopped. She had a question. "Tom, where did the diamond come from?" she asked. "Who dug the hole? Why did they leave it here?" Tom shrugged. He didn't know. He didn't care. He just wanted the diamond. "Maybe someone lost it," he said. "Or maybe they forgot it. Or maybe they wanted us to have it. It doesn't matter. It's ours now!" He grabbed the diamond and put it in his pocket. He smiled. He felt very happy. But then, they heard a loud voice behind them. "Hey, you kids! What are you doing with my diamond? Give it back to me, now!" Tom and Lily turned around. They saw a man standing over them. He was wearing a modern suit and a hat. He looked angry and scary. He had a shovel in his hand. Tom and Lily were afraid. They realized they had made a mistake. The diamond was not theirs. It was his. He had dug the hole and hidden the diamond there. He had come back to get it. They dropped the bucket and ran away. They left the diamond behind. They didn't want it anymore. They learned a lesson. They should not take things that don't belong to them. They should always question what they find.

One day, a loud chicken went to the store. The chicken wanted to buy a toy. The store had many toys. The chicken saw a big, red ball. The chicken liked the ball. The chicken said, "I want the ball, please." The store man said, "You need to pay for the ball." The chicken looked sad. The chicken did not have any money to pay for the ball. Then, a nice boy saw the sad chicken. The boy had money. The boy said, "I will pay for the ball." The chicken was happy. The chicken and the boy played with the big, red ball together. They became good friends.
