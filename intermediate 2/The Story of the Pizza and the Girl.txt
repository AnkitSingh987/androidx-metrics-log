Category: Intermediate
Title: The Story of the Pizza and the Girl

Once upon a time, there was a little girl, who was very sad. She was walking down the street and stopped outside a pizza shop. The pizza shop owner saw her and said, "Welcome, little lady! Would you like a pizza?" But the little girl was too sad to answer. The pizza shop owner was curious. "Why are you so sad?" he asked her. The little girl replied, "I don't have any money to buy a pizza." The pizza shop owner smiled and said, "I will give you a pizza. Welcome!" The little girl was overjoyed and said, "Thank you!" She was no longer sad. She enjoyed the pizza and went on her way.

Once there was a large bear. He was walking in the summer sun when he saw a rabbit. The bear called out to the rabbit and asked if it wanted to be his friend. The rabbit looked up and asked what it would have to do. The bear replied that it would have to play games and go for long walks together. The rabbit thought about it for a minute and then agreed. The two became the best of friends and spent all of their time together in the bright summer days. They loved to go on long walks and explore the large, green fields. The rabbit would always reply with a happy nod when the bear asked if it wanted to go on an adventure. The summer days were always filled with fun and laughter, and the pair never wanted them to end.

Anna and Ben are playing with blocks. They want to build a big tower. They put one block on top of another block. They are very careful. They do not want the tower to fall. "Look, Anna, our tower is almost complete!" Ben says. He is happy. "Yes, Ben, it is very tall and pretty!" Anna says. She is happy too. They need one more block to finish the tower. They look around. They see a block on the table. It is red and shiny. They both want the block. "I want the block!" Ben says. He runs to the table. "No, I want the block!" Anna says. She runs to the table too. They reach the table at the same time. They both grab the block. They pull and tug. They do not share. "Let go, Ben!" Anna says. She is angry. "Let go, Anna!" Ben says. He is angry too. They pull and tug harder. The block slips from their hands. It flies in the air. It lands on the tower. The tower wobbles and shakes. It falls down. The blocks scatter everywhere. They make a loud noise. Anna and Ben are shocked. They look at the mess. They look at each other. They feel sad and sorry. They hear a voice. It is Mom. She is in the living room. She is having a meeting with her boss. She is on the phone. "What was that noise?" Mom says. She is worried. Anna and Ben do not know what to say. They are scared. They hear footsteps. It is Mom. She comes to the playroom. She sees the mess. She sees Anna and Ben. She is not happy. "What happened here?" Mom says. She is stern. Anna and Ben lower their heads. They do not look at Mom. "We were playing with blocks," Anna says. She is quiet. "We wanted to build a tower," Ben says. He is quiet too. "But we fought over the last block," Anna says. She is ashamed. "And we made the tower fall," Ben says. He is ashamed too. "We are sorry, Mom," Anna and Ben say together. They are sincere. Mom sighs. She is not angry anymore. She is gentle. "I know you are sorry, Anna and Ben," Mom says. She is kind. "But you should not fight over toys," Mom says. She is firm. "You should share and take turns," Mom says. She is wise. "And you should be careful and quiet when I have a meeting," Mom says. She is serious. "Do you understand?" Mom says. She is hopeful. "Yes, Mom, we understand," Anna and Ben say together. They are respectful. They hug Mom. They say sorry again. Mom hugs them back. She says it is okay. They help Mom clean up the mess. They put the blocks back in the box. They say goodbye to Mom's boss on the phone. They apologize for the noise. Mom smiles. She is proud. Anna and Ben smile. They are happy. They learn a lesson. They do not fight over toys anymore. They share and take turns. They are careful and quiet when Mom has a meeting. They are good children.
