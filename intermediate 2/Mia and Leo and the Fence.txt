Category: Intermediate
Title: Mia and Leo and the Fence

Mia and Leo went to the zoo with their mom. They saw many animals, like lions, monkeys, and giraffes. But their favorite was the panda. The panda was black and white, and had thick fur. It was eating bamboo and looked very happy. "Can we go closer, mom?" Mia asked. "I want to see the panda better." "Sure, honey, but we have to be quiet and gentle. Pandas are shy and need space," mom said. They walked to the fence, where there was a sign that said "Yield to the panda". Mia and Leo did not know what "yield" meant, but they guessed it was something good. "Hello, panda!" Leo said softly. "You are very cute. Do you like bamboo?" The panda looked at Leo and Mia with its big eyes. It seemed curious, but also a bit scared. It moved away from the fence and hid behind a rock. "Oh, no, did we scare the panda?" Mia asked, feeling sad. "No, sweetie, you didn't. The panda is just not used to people. It needs time to feel safe and comfortable. Yield means to let the panda have its way, and not bother it too much. If we yield to the panda, maybe it will come out again and play with us," mom explained. Mia and Leo nodded. They understood. They backed away from the fence and sat on a bench. They watched the panda quietly, and waited for it to come out. After a while, the panda peeked from behind the rock. It saw that Mia and Leo were not too close, and not too loud. It felt more relaxed. It came out of its hiding place, and walked to the fence. It reached out its paw, and touched Leo's hand. Leo smiled, and gently stroked the panda's fur. Mia joined him, and the panda let them. The panda liked Mia and Leo. They were kind and respectful. They yielded to the panda, and the panda yielded to them. They became friends.

Once upon a time, there was a lazy aunt named Sue. She liked to sit on her big, red chair all day. Aunt Sue did not like to play or run, she just liked to be still and watch her favorite show on TV. One sunny day, a little girl named Amy came to visit Aunt Sue. Amy wanted to play outside, but Aunt Sue did not want to leave her big, red chair. Amy was sad and asked Aunt Sue, "Why are you so lazy?" Aunt Sue thought for a moment and said, "I don't know, Amy. Maybe I should try to be more fun." So, Aunt Sue got up from her big, red chair and went outside to play with Amy. They played catch and ran around the yard. Aunt Sue was not lazy anymore. She was happy to be a fun aunt.

One day, a little star named Twinkie lived in the sky. Twinkie was a fair star. All the other stars liked Twinkie. They played and laughed together. One night, Twinkie felt a big sneeze coming. "Achoo!" Twinkie sneezed so hard that it fell from the sky. Down, down, down it went. Twinkie landed in a soft bed of grass. A little girl named Lily saw Twinkie and said, "Wow! A star! Are you okay?" Twinkie said, "Yes, I am okay. But I miss my friends in the sky." Lily had an idea. She took a big balloon and tied it to Twinkie. The balloon lifted Twinkie up, up, up, back to the sky. Twinkie was so happy to be with its friends again. The stars all said, "Thank you, Lily!" The moral of the story is to help others when they need it. And sometimes, a sneeze can bring new friends.

One day, a little bird named Tim went on vacation. He was very excited to see new places and make new friends. Tim was a happy bird who loved to whistle. He would whistle all day long. On his vacation, Tim met a big, bossy cat named Max. Max liked to tell everyone what to do. "Hey, bird, stop whistling!" Max said. Tim stopped whistling, but he felt sad. He wanted to whistle and be happy. Later, Tim met a kind dog named Bella. Bella liked Tim's whistling. "Why did you stop whistling, Tim?" she asked. Tim told her about the bossy cat. Bella said, "Don't listen to Max. You can whistle if you want to." So Tim started to whistle again, and they both had a great vacation.
