Category: Intermediate
Title: Sarah and the Book

Sarah was three years old and very careful. She always made sure to be aware of her surroundings. Today she was visiting the library and looking through the shelves. As she read the titles she spotted a novel. She flipped through it and felt excited. She had never seen a book this big before. Suddenly, Sarah noticed an older woman walking over to her. The woman asked Sarah in a gentle voice, "What is so fascinating about this book?" Sarah replied, "This novel looks very interesting. I can already see that it has a lot of words and stories." The woman smiled. "I think you value reading, just like me. This book is a great one to start with. I hope that you enjoy it." Sarah was delighted to have found such a nice novel. She thanked the woman and promised to take care as she read it. As Sarah returned home, she knew that she had valued something special.

Lily liked to grow things. She had a small garden with flowers and carrots and beans. She watered them every day and watched them get bigger and bigger. She was proud of her garden. One day, she saw a shiny buckle in the dirt. She picked it up and looked at it. It was silver and had a star on it. She wondered who it belonged to. Maybe it was a cowboy's buckle, she thought. She put it in her pocket and went to show her mom. "Look, mom, what I found in the garden!" she said, holding out the buckle. "Wow, that's a pretty buckle, Lily. Where did you find it?" her mom asked. "In the dirt, near the beans. Can I keep it?" Lily asked. "Sure, you can keep it. But maybe you should wash it first. It's a bit dirty," her mom said. "Okay, I'll wash it. Can I wear it?" Lily asked. "Of course, you can wear it. But you'll need a belt to put it on. Do you have a belt?" her mom asked. Lily shook her head. She didn't have a belt. She only had a long ribbon that she sometimes used to tie her hair. "Can I use this?" she asked, holding up the ribbon. "Sure, you can use that. It might be a bit loose, but you can try," her mom said. Lily ran to her room and put the ribbon around her waist. She fastened the buckle on the ribbon and looked at herself in the mirror. She smiled. She looked like a cowboy. She decided to go outside and play. She put on her hat and boots and grabbed her toy horse. She ran to the garden and pretended to ride her horse. She said, "Yee-haw! I'm a cowboy!" She had fun playing in the garden. She didn't notice that the ribbon was getting loose. She didn't notice that the buckle was sliding down. She didn't notice that the buckle was about to fall off. She was too busy having fun. Until she heard a loud clink. She looked down and saw the buckle on the ground. She gasped. She had lost her buckle. She felt sad. She bent down to pick it up. But before she could, she saw something else. Something green and curly and long. Something that looked like a bean. A very big bean. A bean that had grown around her buckle. She blinked. She couldn't believe it. She reached out and touched the bean. It was real. It was a buckle bean. She smiled. She had grown a buckle bean. She felt happy. She picked up the bean and the buckle and ran to show her mom. "Look, mom, what I grew in the garden!" she said, holding out the bean and the buckle. "Wow, that's amazing, Lily. You grew a buckle bean. How did you do that?" her mom asked. "I don't know. I just planted the buckle and watered it and it grew," Lily said. "That's very cool, Lily. You have a magic garden. And a magic buckle. You can keep both of them. But maybe you should find a better belt," her mom said. "Okay, I'll find a better belt. But can I wear the buckle bean?" Lily asked. "Sure, you can wear the buckle bean. It's very pretty. And very unique. Just like you," her mom said. Lily hugged her mom and thanked her. She put the buckle bean around her neck and wore it like a necklace. She felt proud of her buckle bean. She felt proud of her garden. She felt proud of herself. She was a cowboy. And a gardener. And a buckle bean grower. She was Lily. And she liked to grow things.
