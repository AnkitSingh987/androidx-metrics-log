Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Clever Boy and the Cake

Once upon a time, there was a clever little boy named Tim. Tim loved to help his mom in the kitchen. One day, Tim's mom was making a cake. She used a big knife to cut the cake. Tim wanted to help, so he asked, "Mom, can I help cut the cake?" His mom smiled and said, "Yes, Tim, you can help. But be careful with the knife." Tim took the knife and carefully cut the cake. He felt very clever and proud. The next morning, Tim woke up early to see the sun rise. He saw the sky change colors as the sun slowly rose. It was very pretty. Tim ran to his mom and said, "Mom, come see the sun rise! It's so beautiful!" His mom smiled and they watched the sun rise together, feeling happy and loved.

Lila and Ben were playing with their toys in the living room when they heard a loud noise outside. They ran to the window and saw water rushing down the street. It was a flood! "Oh no, what will we do?" Lila asked, scared. "Maybe we should go upstairs," Ben suggested. They grabbed their toys and ran to the stairs, but they saw that the water was already coming into the house. It was wet and dirty and smelled bad. "Mom! Dad!" they shouted, hoping their parents would hear them. They heard their mom's voice from upstairs. "Lila! Ben! Come up here, quickly!" They climbed the stairs as fast as they could and found their mom and dad in their bedroom. They had a big suitcase and some blankets. "Are you okay, kids?" their mom asked, hugging them. "Yes, mom, we're okay," Lila said. "What's happening, dad?" Ben asked. "The rain was too much and the river overflowed. It's a flood, son. We have to leave the house and go somewhere safe," their dad explained. "Where will we go, dad?" Lila asked. "I don't know, sweetie. We'll have to wait and see," their dad said. They looked out the window and saw that the water was still rising. The sky was dark and gloomy. They felt sad and scared. They heard a loud honk from outside. It was a big truck with a sign that said "Rescue". A man with a yellow hat and a loudspeaker was calling out. "Anyone in there? We're here to help. Come out and get on the truck!" Their dad opened the window and waved. "We're here! We're coming!" He grabbed the suitcase and told his family to follow him. They went downstairs, careful not to slip on the water. The man with the yellow hat helped them get on the truck. There were other people on the truck, too. Some of them were crying, some of them were smiling. "Are you all right?" the man with the yellow hat asked them. "Yes, thank you," their dad said. "Where are you taking us?" their mom asked. "To a shelter. It's a place where you can stay until the flood is over. They have food and water and beds and toys," the man with the yellow hat said. "Will our house be okay?" Lila asked. "I don't know, honey. It depends on how much water there is and how long it lasts. But don't worry, we'll do our best to help you," the man with the yellow hat said. "Can we go back to our house when the flood is over?" Ben asked. "Maybe, son. Maybe," the man with the yellow hat said. He shrugged and started the truck. They drove away from their house, leaving behind their toys and their memories. They hoped the flood would end soon and they could go back to their home. But they were glad they had each other and they were safe.

Once upon a time, there was a wild comet. It was shooting around the sky, so fast that no one could catch it. One day, a little boy said he wanted to ride the comet. His mom said, "But that's too dangerous! You'll have to quit trying to ride the comet, or you're going to get hurt." But the little boy wouldn't listen. He kept running around, trying desperately to catch the wild comet. Eventually, the comet was too fast for the little boy and with a final burst of speed, it flew away into the night sky. The little boy sighed and said, "I guess I'll have to quit trying to catch the comet." His mom sighed in relief and said, "Oh, thank goodness! I'm so glad you quit trying to catch that wild comet!" The little boy had learned his lesson, and he never tried to ride wild comets again.
