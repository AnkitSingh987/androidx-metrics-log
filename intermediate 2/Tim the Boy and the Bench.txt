Category: Intermediate
Title: Tim the Boy and the Bench

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Tim. Tim loved to eat popcorn. One day, Tim went to the park with his mother. At the park, Tim saw a big bag of popcorn on a bench. It was filthy, but he wanted to eat it. Tim asked his mother, "Can I eat the popcorn?" His mother said, "No, Tim. It's filthy. You will suffer if you eat it." But when his mother was not looking, Tim ate the popcorn. Soon, Tim's tummy hurt. He cried and told his mother, "I ate the popcorn and now I suffer." His mother hugged him and said, "I told you not to eat the filthy popcorn. The next time, you must listen. That is how we learn." From that day on, Tim always listened to his mother and never ate filthy food again. He learned that it's important to listen to our parents because they know what's best for us.

Once upon a time, there was a big race. All the kids wanted to win the race. They wanted to get the medal. The medal was shiny and pretty. One little boy was very serious. He wanted to win the race more than anyone. He took big steps and ran very fast. He passed all the other kids. At the end of the race, the serious boy won. He got the shiny medal. All the kids clapped and cheered for him. The serious boy was very happy. He took the medal home to show his mom and dad.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Mia. She had an unusual toy named Mr. Brain. Mr. Brain was not like other toys. He could talk and he liked to help Mia. One day, Mia wanted to organize her toys. Mr. Brain said, "I can help you!" Mia was happy. They started to put the toys in their right places. Everything was going well. Then, something unexpected happened. Mr. Brain started to organize the toys in a funny way. He put the dolls in the car box and the cars in the dollhouse. Mia was surprised and laughed. Mr. Brain made organizing fun for Mia. They had a great time together.

Once upon a time, there was a hippo who was feeling very envious. Everywhere he looked, other animals were sleeping peacefully. Mice, koalas, antelopes - all of them seemed to be resting. Everywhere except for hippo. Hippo wanted to sleep too, but no matter how hard he tried, he just couldn't seem to do it. He watched and waited, trying to stay still, but it didn't matter - he just couldn't relax. Finally, the hippo had an idea. He took a deep breath and started counting, humming a lullaby he'd heard a mama bird singing to her eggs. He soon felt himself feeling sleepy and finally, he was able to doze off. The hippo slept and slept until morning. He woke up feeling refreshed and more relaxed than ever before. Hippo was no longer envious at all. He was delighted to have gotten his sleep, just like the other animals.

Sara and Tom are friends. They like to play in the park. One day, they see a big horse in the park. The horse is brown and has a long tail. "Can we pet the horse?" Sara asks Tom. "OK, but we have to be nice and quiet," Tom says. They walk to the horse and say hello. The horse is friendly and lets them touch his nose and ears. Sara and Tom are happy. "Look, I have some oats in my bag," Sara says. "Maybe the horse is hungry." "Good idea," Tom says. "Let's give him some oats." They take out some oats from Sara's bag and hold them in their hands. The horse smells the oats and eats them from their palms. He makes a funny sound and wags his tail. "Wow, he likes the oats," Sara says. "Yes, he does," Tom says. They give him more oats until the bag is empty. Then they see a rope on the ground. "Hey, what is this?" Tom asks. "It's a rope," Sara says. "Maybe we can tie it to the horse and ride him." "That sounds fun," Tom says. "But how do we tie it?" Sara thinks for a moment. She remembers how her mom ties her shoes. "I know how to tie it," she says. "You make a loop and then another loop and then you put one loop through the other loop and pull." She shows Tom how to do it. Then they tie the rope to the horse's neck. "OK, now we can ride him," Sara says. They climb on the horse's back. The horse is big and strong. He walks around the park with them. "Yay, this is so fun," Sara says. "Yes, it is," Tom says. They feel the wind in their hair and the sun on their faces. They are fit and happy. They love the horse and the horse loves them. They are the best friends ever.
