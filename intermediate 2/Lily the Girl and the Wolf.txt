Category: Intermediate
Title: Lily the Girl and the Wolf

Once upon a time, in a small bright house, there lived a little girl named Lily. She had a big, soft bed where she would settle in every night. Lily always felt safe in her room with her toys and her night light. One day, Lily went outside to play. She saw a bright, pretty butterfly. She wanted to catch it, so she ran after it. The butterfly led her far away from her safe home. Lily got lost and couldn't find her way back. She started to cry. The sun went down and it got dark. She tried to settle under a tree, but she didn't feel safe like she did in her bed. A big, scary wolf heard her cry and came to find her. The wolf found Lily under the tree. She was very scared. The wolf took her away, and she never saw her bright, safe home again.

Tim and Tom were twins who liked to play outside. One day, they found a big beetle in the grass. Tim wanted to keep it as a pet, but Tom wanted to tear it apart and see what was inside. "Give me the beetle!" Tom said, pulling on one of its legs. "No, leave it alone!" Tim said, holding on to another leg. They both pulled hard, and the beetle made a loud noise. It was scared and hurt. A woman saw them and came over. She was their neighbor, and she was very kind. She had a mild voice and a mild smile. "What are you doing, boys?" she asked. "We found a beetle!" Tim said. "We want to see what's inside!" Tom said. The woman shook her head. She took the beetle gently from their hands and put it on a leaf. "Beetles are living things, just like you and me," she said. "They have feelings and needs. They don't like to be torn apart or hurt. They like to be free and happy. How would you feel if someone did that to you?" Tim and Tom looked at each other. They felt sorry for the beetle. They had not thought about how it felt. "We're sorry, beetle," they said together. The woman smiled. She gave them each a hug. "That's good, boys," she said. "You learned a valuable lesson today. Always treat others the way you want to be treated. That's called the golden rule. Can you remember that?" Tim and Tom nodded. They thanked the woman and said goodbye. They watched the beetle fly away. They felt happy too. They had learned to be kind and respectful.

Once there was a little caterpillar. He was very small. He was yellow too. He wanted to make some friends, so he went for a walk. He met a bee. "Hello bee", cried the little caterpillar. But the bee didn't answer. He just flew away. The caterpillar was sad. He felt very alone. He wanted to cry. So he did, and he cried and cried. Suddenly, he heard a voice. It was coming from a butterfly. "Don't cry," said the butterfly, "I'm here now. I'm your friend". The caterpillar was happy. He stopped crying. He was no longer alone. He smiled and the two new friends flew away together.

Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lisa. She had a pair of heavy scissors. Lisa liked to use the scissors to cut paper and make fun shapes. One day, Lisa wanted to make a big star. She found some yellow paper and used her heavy scissors to cut out a star shape. It was hard work, but she did it. Lisa was so happy with her big yellow star. She showed it to her mom and dad, and they were proud of her. Lisa put the star on her wall, and it made her room bright and happy.

Sara was hungry. She wanted a sandwich. She asked her mom, "Mom, can I have a sandwich, please?" Mom smiled and said, "Of course, Sara. What kind of sandwich do you want?" Sara thought for a moment. She liked cheese and ham and lettuce and tomato. But she knew her brother Ben liked peanut butter and jelly. Ben was at school. He would come home soon. Sara was thoughtful. She said, "Mom, can you make two sandwiches? One for me and one for Ben?" Mom was proud of Sara. She said, "That's very kind of you, Sara. You are a good sister. I will make two sandwiches for you and Ben." Mom took out the bread and the fillings. She cut the bread with a knife. She put cheese and ham and lettuce and tomato on one slice. She put peanut butter and jelly on another slice. She put the slices together. She cut the sandwiches in half. She put them on a plate. Sara said, "Thank you, Mom. The sandwiches look yummy. Can I help you with anything?" Mom said, "You can help me by setting the table. Get two napkins and two cups. And a glass of milk for you and a glass of juice for Ben." Sara nodded. She got the napkins and the cups. She poured the milk and the juice. She set the table. She waited for Ben to come home. She was happy. She had a sandwich. And she had a surprise for Ben.
