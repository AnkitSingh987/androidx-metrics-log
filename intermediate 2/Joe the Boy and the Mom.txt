Category: Intermediate
Title: Joe the Boy and the Mom

Once upon a time, there was a little boy named Joe. He was three years old. He loved looking at the colorful pictures in the original magazines in his room. One day, Joe found something interesting in a magazine. With a huge smile on his face, he quickly said to his mom, “Mommy, mommy look what I found!”. His mom said “What is it Joe?” Joe replied “I don’t know! I guess it’s some sort of game. Can I play it?” His mom said “It looks like fun, let’s try it!” So Joe and his mom played the game together. They had a lot of fun playing it. They laughed and cheered when Joe won. At the end of the game, Joe shouted “I won! I won! Can I play it again?” His mom said “Of course you can, you did a great job. You are a real winner!” Joe smiled and said “Yay! I love this original magazine game!”

Anna liked to play in the garden. She liked to look at the flowers and the insects. She saw a bee, a butterfly, a ladybug and a worm. She was happy. But then she saw a spider. She did not like spiders. She was scared. She ran to her mom and cried. "Mom, there is a spider in the garden! It is big and hairy and has many legs!" Her mom hugged her and comforted her. "Don't be afraid, Anna. The spider is not bad. It is just an insect, like the others. It does not want to hurt you. It just wants to make a web and catch flies. It is compassionate. It helps the garden by eating the pests." Anna looked at her mom. She trusted her mom. She calmed down. "Really, mom? The spider is compassionate?" "Yes, Anna. The spider is compassionate. And so are you. You are kind and gentle to the other insects. You do not harm them. You appreciate them. You are a good girl." Anna smiled. She felt better. She thanked her mom and gave her a kiss. Then she went back to the garden. She saw the spider again. She was not scared anymore. She said hello to the spider and wished it a good day. She played with the other insects and enjoyed the sun. She was happy.

Once upon a time, there was a small ball that could bounce very high. It lived in a park with many kids. One day, a new infant came to the park with her big sister. The infant saw the ball and wanted to play with it. But the ball did not want to play with the infant. It was afraid the infant would not be able to bounce it high. The infant was very persistent and kept trying to play with the ball. The ball tried to bounce away, but the infant kept following it. Finally, the ball let the infant play with it. To the ball's surprise, the infant could bounce it high, just like the big kids. The ball learned that even small kids can do big things. The moral of the story is to not judge someone by their size.

Once upon a time, there was a blue bird named Billy. Billy loved to fly high in the sky. One day, at noon, he saw a big, blue cloud. He wanted to touch it. So, he flew up and up. But, a mean bird named Tom saw Billy. Tom did not want Billy to touch the blue cloud. Tom said, "You can't touch the cloud, Billy!" Billy felt sad. He wanted to touch the blue cloud so much. He tried to imagine how soft it would feel. Then, a nice bird named Lucy heard Billy and Tom. Lucy said to Tom, "Why can't Billy touch the cloud?" Tom thought for a moment. He said, "I don't know. Maybe we can all touch the cloud together." So, Billy, Tom, and Lucy flew up and up. They touched the big, blue cloud. It was so soft and fluffy. They all became friends and played in the sky until the sun went down.

Sara and Tom are twins. They like to play in the park. They have a big bucket of water and some cups. They splash each other with water and laugh. "Look, Sara, a fancy book!" Tom says. He sees a shiny book on a bench. It has pictures of animals and flowers. He runs to the bench and picks up the book. "Tom, wait!" Sara says. She follows him. She wants to see the book too. But the book is not theirs. It belongs to a lady who is sitting on the grass. She is reading a magazine and does not see Tom and Sara. Tom opens the book and shows it to Sara. "Wow, so pretty!" Sara says. She points to a picture of a butterfly. But then, oh no! Tom drops the book in the bucket of water. The book gets wet and soggy. The pages stick together. The pictures fade. "Tom, what did you do?" Sara says. She is scared and sad. She likes the book. The lady hears them and looks up. She sees her book in the water. She is angry and shocked. She runs to the bench and grabs the book. "Hey, you naughty kids! This is my book! You ruined it!" she says. She shakes the book and water drips from it. She glares at Tom and Sara. Tom and Sara are sorry and ashamed. They did not mean to hurt the book. They did not know it was hers. "We are sorry, lady. We like your book. We just want to see it," Tom says. "Sorry is not enough. This book is very special to me. It was a gift from my friend. It is very fancy and expensive. You have to pay for it!" she says. Tom and Sara do not have any money. They only have water and cups. They do not know what to do. They start to cry. The lady sees them crying and feels a little sorry for them. She remembers that they are just kids. They made a mistake. Maybe she was too harsh. "Okay, okay, stop crying. I forgive you. But you have to promise me something. You have to promise me that you will never touch other people's things without asking. And you have to promise me that you will take good care of books. Books are precious and beautiful. They are not toys. Do you understand?" she says. Tom and Sara nod. They understand. They promise. "Good. Now, go and play. But be careful and respectful. And here, take this magazine. It has some pictures too. Maybe you will like it," she says. She gives them the magazine and smiles. Tom and Sara smile back. They say thank you and sorry again. They take the magazine and run to the slide. They are happy and relieved. The lady looks at her book and sighs. It is ruined. But she is not angry anymore. She is glad that she taught Tom and Sara a lesson. Maybe they will learn to love books as much as she does. She hopes so. She loves books.
