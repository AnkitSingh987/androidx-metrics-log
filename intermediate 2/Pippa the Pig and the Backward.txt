Category: Intermediate
Title: Pippa the Pig and the Backward

Once upon a time, there was a little pig named Pippa. Pippa was very excited to play with her friends. She loved to run, jump, and play in the mud. One day, Pippa and her friends decided to play a new game called "reverse." In the game, they had to do everything backward. They walked backward, talked backward, and even ate backward. It was a lot of fun, and everyone laughed and played all day. But soon, Pippa and her friends got tired and wanted to go home. As they started to walk home, they realized they were lost. They had been playing reverse all day and didn't know how to get back. Pippa thought hard and decided to reverse their steps. They walked backward, retracing their steps, and soon found their way home. Pippa and her friends learned the moral value of being careful and paying attention to where they were going. They promised to be more careful next time they played.

Lily and Ben are playing with their dolls. They have a fancy tea party with cups and plates and cookies. Lily likes to count how many cookies they have. "One, two, three, four, five, six," she says, pointing to each cookie. "We have six cookies. That is a lot of cookies!" Ben nods and picks up a cookie. He takes a big bite and smiles. "Yum, yum, yum," he says. "This cookie is very good. How many cookies do I have now?" Lily looks at his cookie. It has a big hole in it. She thinks for a moment. "You have half a cookie," she says. "Half is not a whole. Half is less than a whole." Ben frowns. He does not like half. He likes whole. He wants another cookie. He reaches for the plate, but Lily stops him. "Wait, wait, wait," she says. "You have to share. We have to share the cookies. How many cookies do we each get if we share?" Ben thinks for a moment. He knows how to count, but he does not know how to share. He looks at Lily and the plate. He sees six cookies, but one is half. He is confused. "I don't know," he says. "How do we share?" Lily smiles. She knows how to share. She learned it from her mom. She picks up the plate and shows it to Ben. "Look, look, look," she says. "We have six cookies, but one is half. We can make two piles of cookies. One pile for you and one pile for me. Each pile has three cookies, but one is half. Do you see?" Ben looks at the plate. He sees two piles of cookies. He sees three cookies in each pile, but one is half. He nods. He sees. "I see, I see, I see," he says. "We each get three cookies, but one is half. That is fair. That is sharing." Lily and Ben smile. They are happy. They have learned how to share. They pick up their cookies and eat them. They use their mouths to say "yum, yum, yum". They have a fancy tea party with their dolls. They have fun.

One day, a boy named Tim had a toy car. He loved to make the car zoom all around his room. The car was red and very tough. Tim played with the car every day. One day, Tim found a picture in a big book. The picture showed a car just like his toy car. Tim was very happy. He showed the picture to his mom and dad. They smiled at the picture. The next day, when Tim went to play with his car, it was gone. He looked everywhere, but he could not find it. Then, Tim looked at the picture in the book. The car in the picture was now red, just like his toy car. Tim was very surprised. His toy car had zoomed into the picture!

Once upon a time, there was a hungry caterpillar named Carl. Carl loved to eat leaves. One day, he saw a big tree with many leaves. Carl said, "Yummy! I will eat these leaves!" Carl ate and ate until his tummy was full. Then, he saw some pretty things on the ground. They were colorful and shiny. Carl thought, "I will decorate my tree with these pretty things!" Carl picked up the pretty things and put them on the tree. The tree looked very nice. Carl was happy. He said, "I am a good helper. My tree is so pretty now!" A bird saw the tree and said, "Wow! Your tree is very pretty, Carl!" Carl smiled and said, "Thank you! I decorated it with the pretty things I found." And so, the hungry caterpillar Carl made the tree beautiful and made a new friend. They played together and lived happily ever after.
