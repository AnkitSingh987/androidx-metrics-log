Category: Intermediate
Title: The Story of the Stars and the Bed

Once upon a time, there was a little girl who was three. One night when she was ready for bed, her mommy made her a nice warm bath. To make the bath even nicer, mommy poured a special bubble bath in. The bubbles were big and smelled nice and made the water feel soft. The little girl enjoyed her warm bath and when she was all clean and ready for bed, mommy helped her get out of the tub and put some comfy pajamas on. Then she hopped into bed and Mommy pulled the covers over her. The little girl was so happy and warm in her soft bed. But the room was a bit dull. So, Mommy grabbed a bucket of special stars and poured them all around her bed. The stars twinkled and glowed, making the room look so bright and fun. The little girl smiled and snuggled down into her bed. She felt so cozy and glad to be there. Before long, she was fast asleep, dreaming of her special twinkling stars.

One day, a girl named Sue wanted to mail a unique thing to her friend, Tom. She put the thing in a box and went to the mail place. She was very happy because she knew Tom would like this special thing a lot. On the way, a big wind came and blew the box away. Sue was sad and didn't know what to do. She looked for the box, but it was gone. Sue thought Tom would never get his unique thing now. But then, something unexpected happened. Tom found the box near his house! He opened it and saw the unique thing inside. He was so happy and surprised. Tom knew it was from Sue and he couldn't wait to tell her that he got the special thing.

Once upon a time, there was a unique little girl named Lily. She loved to collect pretty rocks. One sunny day, she went to the park to find more rocks for her collection. She walked along the path, looking down for shiny stones. At the park, she saw a boy named Tim. Tim was also collecting rocks. They both wanted the same shiny rock. They began to fight over it. "No, it's mine!" said Lily. "No, it's mine!" said Tim. Then, they stopped and looked at each other. They decided to share the shiny rock and become friends. Together, they collected many more unique rocks. And they were both very happy.

One day, a little girl named Lucy went to the bakery with her mom. She saw a twisty pastry in the glass case. Lucy pointed and said, "Mommy, I want that twisty one!" Her mom smiled and asked the baker for the twisty pastry. As they walked home, Lucy held her twisty pastry tight. She couldn't wait to eat it. Suddenly, a crazy bird flew down and tried to take her pastry. Lucy yelled, "No, bird! This is mine!" The bird flew away, and Lucy felt happy. She went home and ate her twisty pastry. It was very yummy. From that day on, Lucy always asked for twisty pastries at the bakery.

Lily and Ben were twins who liked to play outside. One day, they saw a big puddle of mud in the yard. Lily wanted to jump in it and make splashes. Ben wanted to stay on the grass and read his book. "Come on, Ben, let's have some fun!" Lily said. "The mud is soft and wet and squishy. We can make mud pies and mud castles and mud monsters." "No, thank you, Lily," Ben said. "The mud is dirty and sticky and icky. It will soak our clothes and shoes and hair. We will have to take a bath and wash them all. I like my book better. It has pictures and words and stories." Lily thought Ben was lazy and boring. She ran to the puddle and jumped in it with a big splash. She laughed and giggled and rolled in the mud. She made mud pies and mud castles and mud monsters. She had a lot of fun. Ben thought Lily was silly and messy. He stayed on the grass and read his book. He learned about animals and plants and stars. He had a lot of fun. But then, the sun went behind a cloud and the wind blew cold. Lily felt wet and cold and shivery. She wanted to get out of the mud and go inside. She called to Ben for help. "Ben, Ben, please help me!" Lily said. "The mud is cold and heavy and hard. I can't get out of it. I'm stuck and I'm cold and I'm scared." Ben heard Lily and put down his book. He ran to the puddle and saw Lily covered in mud. He felt sorry for her and wanted to help her. He reached out his hand and pulled her out of the mud. He hugged her and comforted her. "Lily, Lily, it's okay," Ben said. "I'm here, I'm here. Don't be scared. We'll go inside and get warm and dry. We'll take a bath and wash the mud off. We'll have some hot chocolate and cookies. It will be okay." Lily smiled and thanked Ben. She was glad he was her brother. She hugged him back and apologized. "Ben, Ben, I'm sorry," Lily said. "I was silly and messy. I should have listened to you. You were right. The mud was dirty and sticky and icky. I don't like it anymore. I like your book better. It has pictures and words and stories." Ben smiled and forgave Lily. He was glad she was his sister. He shared his book with her and taught her some new words. They walked inside together, holding hands. They had a lot of fun.
