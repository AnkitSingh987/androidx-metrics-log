Title: The First Time I Used a Bank

Category: Intermediate

Leo was twenty-two years old, and he had a secret. While most of his friends managed their money using colorful apps on their smartphones, Leo kept his savings in a metal box under his bed. He had always worked part-time jobs at restaurants and cafes where he received his wages in cash. For Leo, holding physical money felt safe and simple. He always knew exactly how much he had because he could count the paper bills.

However, things were changing. Leo had recently graduated from a vocational college and secured his first professional job as an IT assistant at a large technology company. During his orientation on Monday morning, the human resources manager handed him a stack of paperwork. 

"We need your banking details by the end of the week, Leo," the manager said with a polite smile. "Our company only pays salaries through direct deposit. We do not issue paper checks or pay in cash."

Leo nodded, although his heart started beating a little faster. He didn't have banking details because he didn't have a bank account. He realized that his days of keeping money in a metal box were officially over. He needed to enter the modern financial world, and he needed to do it quickly.

The next day during his lunch break, Leo decided to visit the main branch of City Trust Bank. It was a tall, impressive building with huge glass windows and shiny floors. As he pushed the heavy glass doors open, he felt a wave of cold air conditioning and nervousness. The interior looked completely different from the casual cafes where he used to work. People in smart business suits were walking purposefully, checking their watches and looking at their phones.

Leo stood near the entrance, unsure of what to do. There were several different lines and a machine that dispensed numbered tickets. Luckily, a woman wearing a blue uniform and a friendly smile noticed him looking confused. 

"Good afternoon! Welcome to City Trust Bank," she said, stepping forward. "How can I help you today? Are you looking to make a deposit, or do you have an appointment with a personal banker?"

"Hi," Leo replied, clearing his throat. "I don't have an appointment. Actually, this is my first time here. I need to open a bank account for my new job. They said I need something called a direct deposit."

"Congratulations on the new job!" the woman said warmly. "My name is Sarah, and I can definitely help you with that. You don't need an appointment to open an account. Please take a seat in the waiting area over there, and I will be with you in just a moment."

Leo sat down on a comfortable leather chair and waited. He watched the tellers behind the long counter counting cash and typing quickly on their computers. Five minutes later, Sarah called his name and led him to a small, quiet desk in the corner of the branch. 

"So, let's get started," Sarah said as she sat down and clicked her mouse. "To open an account, I will need to see some identification. Do you have your passport or your driver's license with you? I will also need a document that proves your current address, like an electricity bill or a rental agreement."

Leo reached into his backpack. He had researched this part on the internet the night before. "Yes, I have my passport and my apartment lease," he said, handing the documents across the desk. 

"Perfect," Sarah smiled, inspecting the papers. "Now, we have a few different options for you. Most people in your situation open a standard current account. This is designed for your daily expenses. You can use it to pay your rent, buy groceries, and receive your salary. It comes with a debit card, so you can withdraw cash from an ATM or pay for things in stores."

Leo listened carefully. "What about a savings account? My mother always told me it's important to save money."

"That is excellent advice," Sarah agreed. "A savings account is a separate account where you keep money that you don't plan to spend immediately. The advantage of a savings account is that it earns interest. This means the bank pays you a small percentage of extra money just for keeping your savings with us. Many of our customers open both. They have their salary paid into the current account, and then they transfer a portion of it into the savings account every month."

"That sounds like a good system," Leo said. "Let's do both. Are there any monthly fees?"

"If you set up a direct deposit for your salary, we waive the monthly maintenance fee for the current account," Sarah explained. "This means it will be completely free for you to use. However, you must be careful not to spend more money than you have in your account. If your balance goes below zero, you will be charged an overdraft fee, which can be quite expensive."

"I understand," Leo said. He was used to only spending the cash he actually had in his pocket, so he didn't plan on spending money he didn't own. 

Sarah spent the next fifteen minutes typing Leo's information into the system. She asked him a few routine questions about his employment and his contact details. Then, she printed out several pages of terms and conditions.

"Please read through these documents and sign at the bottom of the last page," she instructed. 

Leo read the documents. Some of the legal vocabulary was confusing, but he understood the main points. He signed his name with a black pen. 

"Congratulations, Leo! Your accounts are now officially open," Sarah announced cheerfully. She handed him a folder containing his new account numbers and a temporary debit card. "Your permanent debit card will arrive in the mail in about five to seven business days. In the meantime, you can use this temporary card at our ATMs."

"Thank you," Leo said, feeling a sense of relief. 

"One more thing," Sarah added. "I highly recommend downloading our mobile banking application. You can check your balance, transfer money between your current and savings accounts, and even pay your bills directly from your phone. It is very convenient."

"I will definitely do that," Leo promised. 

As Leo walked out of the bank and back onto the busy street, the heavy metal box under his bed suddenly seemed very old-fashioned. He felt proud of himself. He had successfully navigated his first banking experience. He finally had an account number to give to his human resources manager, and he was ready to receive his very first direct deposit. The modern financial world wasn't so scary after all, as long as you asked the right questions.
