Title: The Grandparents' House
Category: Beginner

The drive from the small train station took only fifteen minutes. Grandpa drove the old green car carefully along a narrow, winding road. Thomas and Sophie looked out the back windows. There were no streetlights, and the sky was already getting dark, but they could see thick hedges and large, open fields on both sides of the road. 

"Here we are," Grandpa announced cheerfully. He turned the steering wheel, and the car bumped onto a gravel driveway. The sound of the stones crunching under the tires was very loud in the quiet countryside. 

When the car stopped, the children opened the doors and stepped out. In front of them stood a beautiful, old stone house. It was completely different from their modern apartment building in the city. The house had two floors, a dark grey slate roof, and a large front porch made of wood. Warm, yellow light shone brightly from the windows, making the house look very inviting against the cold November evening. 

"Welcome to our new home!" Grandma said proudly, walking up the wooden steps to the front door. She pushed the heavy wooden door open, and the family followed her inside. 

As soon as they entered the hallway, a wonderful smell hit them. It smelled like roasting chicken, fresh bread, and a warm wood fire. Thomas took a deep breath. 
"It smells amazing in here, Grandma," he said, taking off his heavy winter coat. 

The inside of the house was cozy and comfortable. The floors were made of dark, polished wood, and there were colorful rugs in every room. Grandma led them into the living room. In the corner, a real fire was burning brightly in a stone fireplace. The orange flames danced and crackled, throwing warm light across the comfortable armchairs and the large sofa. 

"It is so warm in here," Sophie said happily. She walked close to the fireplace and held out her cold hands to feel the heat. In their city apartment, they only had white radiators on the walls. A real fire felt like magic. 

"Let me show you to your rooms," Grandpa said, picking up the two small bags. "You can put your things away before dinner."
He led Thomas and Sophie up a narrow wooden staircase. The stairs creaked softly under their feet. At the top of the stairs, there was a long hallway with three doors. 

Grandpa opened the first door. "This is your room, Thomas," he said. 
Thomas walked inside. The room was not very large, but it was perfect. It had a single bed with a thick blue quilt, a small wooden desk, and a window that looked out over the back garden. 
"Thank you, Grandpa," Thomas said, putting his messy blue bag on the floor. 

Grandpa opened the second door. "And this is your room, Sophie."
Sophie's room had yellow walls and a bed covered with a flower-patterned blanket. She immediately loved it. 
"It is beautiful," she smiled, placing her pink suitcase on the bed. 

"Wash your hands and come downstairs quickly," Grandma called from the kitchen. "The chicken stew is ready, and it is very hot!"
The children quickly unpacked their toothbrushes and pyjamas. They washed their hands in the small bathroom at the end of the hall and ran back down the creaky stairs. 

They found their parents and grandparents sitting around a large, round wooden table in the kitchen. The kitchen was the heart of the house. It was large and bright, with many pots and pans hanging from the ceiling. 
In the center of the table was a huge bowl of steaming chicken stew, surrounded by plates of fresh bread and a bowl of green salad. 

"Sit down, sit down," Grandma said, pointing to two empty chairs. 
They all sat together. Grandpa served the stew into deep bowls. It was full of soft potatoes, orange carrots, and tender pieces of chicken. Thomas and Sophie were very hungry after their long train journey. They ate quickly, dipping the fresh bread into the rich soup. 

"Do you like the new house?" Mrs. Miller asked her children. 
"I love it," Sophie answered with her mouth full. "I like my yellow room."
"I like the fireplace," Thomas added. "It is much better than the city."

"Tomorrow, I will show you the garden," Grandpa promised, taking a sip of his water. "It is very big. We have apple trees, a small shed, and plenty of space to run around. But for tonight, we will just stay inside, stay warm, and rest."
After dinner, the family moved into the living room. They sat around the crackling fire, drinking hot tea and talking. Thomas and Sophie felt very safe and comfortable in the old stone house. It was the perfect place for a weekend adventure.
