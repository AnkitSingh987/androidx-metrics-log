Title: A Small Study Goal
Category: Beginner

It is Saturday afternoon. The weather outside is raining and cold. It is a good day to stay inside the house. 
Sarah is thirteen years old. She sits on her bed. She has a thick book in her hands. It is a book for her English class. The book is very long. It has two hundred pages. Sarah must read the whole book before next Friday. 

Sarah looks at the thick book. She feels overwhelmed. Two hundred pages is a lot of reading. She does not like reading long books. It feels like a mountain she has to climb. 
"I cannot do this," she says to herself. "It is too much. I will never finish it."

Her father walks past her open bedroom door. He sees Sarah looking sad at her book. He walks into the room. 
"What is the matter, Sarah?" he asks. 
"This English book," Sarah complains. "It is too long. I have to read it all before next week. It is impossible."
Her father looks at the book. "Two hundred pages," he says. "Yes, that is a long book. But it is not impossible."
"It feels impossible," Sarah says. 

"You are looking at the whole mountain," her father says. "You cannot jump to the top of a mountain. You must take small steps."
Sarah looks confused. "What do you mean?"
"Do not think about reading two hundred pages," he explains. "That is a big goal. You need a small goal. A small study goal is easy to reach."
"Like what?" Sarah asks. 

"How many pages can you read in twenty minutes?" her father asks. 
Sarah thinks about it. "Maybe ten pages?"
"Okay," her father says. "That is your small goal. Read ten pages. Do not think about the other one hundred and ninety pages. Just read ten pages. Then, close the book and take a break."

Sarah looks at the book again. Ten pages. That does not sound scary. That sounds very easy. 
"I can read ten pages," she says. 
"Good," her father says with a smile. "I will leave you to your reading. Good luck with your ten pages." He walks out of the room. 

Sarah opens the book to page one. She looks at the clock on her wall. It is two o'clock. 
She starts to read. The story is actually very interesting. It is about a girl who finds a secret garden. Sarah likes the story. She reads page one. She turns to page two. 
She reads steadily. She does not look at how many pages are left. She only focuses on the page she is reading. 

She reads page five. She reads page eight. 
She finishes page ten. 
She looks at the clock. It is twenty past two. It took her exactly twenty minutes. 

Sarah closes the book. She achieved her small goal. She read ten pages. 
She feels a little burst of happiness. It was not difficult at all. She did it easily. 
She stands up and stretches her arms. She walks to the kitchen. She pours a glass of orange juice. She drinks it slowly. She enjoys her break. 

She thinks about the book. She wants to know what happens next in the secret garden. 
She walks back to her bedroom. She looks at the book on her bed. 
"Ten pages was easy," she says to herself. "I think I can read ten more pages."

She sits on the bed. She opens the book to page eleven. She starts to read again. She sets a new small goal. Ten more pages. 
She reads until page twenty. She closes the book. She takes another break. 
Then she reads ten more pages. 

By dinner time, Sarah is on page fifty. 
Fifty pages! She read a quarter of the book in one afternoon. She is very proud of herself. 

At the dinner table, her father asks, "How is your English book going, Sarah?"
Sarah smiles broadly. "It is going very well, Dad," she says. "I read fifty pages."
"Fifty pages!" her father says. "That is fantastic. How did you do it?"
"I just took small steps," Sarah says. "I read ten pages, and then ten more, and then ten more. The small goals made it very easy."

"That is the secret to doing big things," her father says. "Small steps."
Sarah nods. She is not afraid of the thick book anymore. She knows she will finish it before Friday. She just needs to read ten pages at a time. The big mountain is not scary when you know how to climb it.
