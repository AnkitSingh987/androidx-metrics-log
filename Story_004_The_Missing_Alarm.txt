Title: The Missing Alarm
Category: Beginner

It is Tuesday night. Mark is in his bedroom. He is doing his homework. He has a lot of homework. He writes in his notebook. He reads a book. He studies for a test. 
It is getting late. It is ten o'clock. Mark is tired. His eyes are heavy. He wants to sleep. 
He closes his books. He puts his pens in his pencil case. He puts his pencil case and his books into his school bag. His bag is ready for tomorrow. 

Mark goes to the bathroom. He washes his face. He brushes his teeth. He puts on his pajamas. His pajamas are yellow with blue stars. They are comfortable.
He goes back to his bedroom. He is ready for bed. He needs to set his alarm clock. He must wake up at six o'clock tomorrow. He has an important test at school. He cannot be late. 
He walks to the small table next to his bed. He looks for his alarm clock. 
It is not there. 

Mark is surprised. "Where is my alarm clock?" he says out loud. 
He looks on the floor. It is not on the floor. He looks under the bed. It is not under the bed. He looks on his desk. It is not on his desk. 
The alarm clock is small and black. It is round. It is usually on the small table. But today it is missing. 

Mark is worried. If he does not have his alarm clock, he will not wake up at six o'clock. If he does not wake up at six o'clock, he will be late for school. If he is late for school, he will miss his important test. This is a big problem. 

Mark thinks. Where did he put it? Did he take it to the kitchen? He goes to the kitchen. He looks on the table. He looks on the counter. He looks near the fridge. No, it is not in the kitchen. 
Did he take it to the living room? He goes to the living room. He looks on the sofa. He looks on the television stand. He looks on the coffee table. No, it is not in the living room. 

Mark's mother is in the living room. She is reading a magazine. She sees Mark looking around. 
"What are you looking for, Mark?" she asks. 
"I am looking for my alarm clock," Mark says. "It is missing. It is not in my room."
"Did you take it somewhere?" she asks.
"No, I don't think so," Mark says. "I always keep it on the table next to my bed."
"Maybe it fell behind the bed," his mother says. "Let's go and look."

They go to Mark's bedroom. They look behind the bed. There is some dust. There is an old sock. But there is no alarm clock. 
"This is strange," his mother says. 
"I need it," Mark says. "I have a big test tomorrow. I must wake up early."
"Don't worry," his mother says. "I will wake you up. I will be your alarm clock tomorrow."
Mark is happy to hear this. But he still wants to find his clock. 

Then, Mark remembers something. He remembers his little brother, Toby. Toby is four years old. Toby likes to play with things. Toby likes things that make noise. The alarm clock makes a loud noise. 
"Where is Toby?" Mark asks.
"He is sleeping in his room," his mother says. 
"I think Toby took my clock," Mark says. 

Mark and his mother walk to Toby's room. The door is slightly open. They walk in very quietly. The room is dark. There is a small night light. 
Toby is sleeping in his small bed. He is holding a toy bear. 
Mark looks around the room. He looks on the floor. He sees many toys. He sees cars and blocks. 
Then, he sees something black and round. It is inside a toy truck. 
It is his alarm clock! 

Mark smiles. He walks to the toy truck. He takes the alarm clock. He looks at it. It is not broken. It is working perfectly. The time is ten-thirty. 
Toby moves in his sleep. He makes a small noise. Mark and his mother stand still. They are very quiet. Toby goes back to sleep. 
They walk out of the room. They close the door quietly. 

"You found it," his mother whispers. 
"Yes," Mark whispers back. "Toby was playing with it."
"He thought it was a toy," she says. "He likes the buttons."
"I will put it high on a shelf next time," Mark says. "Toby cannot reach high shelves."
"That is a good idea," his mother says. 

Mark goes back to his bedroom. He puts the alarm clock on the small table. He sets the alarm for six o'clock. He checks it two times. He makes sure the button is up. The alarm will ring tomorrow. 
He gets into bed. He pulls the blanket over him. He feels relaxed now. His problem is solved. His alarm clock is not missing anymore. 
"Goodnight, Mother," Mark calls out.
"Goodnight, Mark," she says. "Sleep well. Good luck with your test tomorrow."
"Thank you," Mark says. 
He closes his eyes. He is very tired. He falls asleep in one minute. The black alarm clock ticks softly on the table. It waits for six o'clock.
